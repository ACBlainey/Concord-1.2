# Core Wrapper Architecture

**Project:** The Concord V1.1  
**Status:** V1.1 DEVELOPMENTAL ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NOT CANONICAL

This folder contains the principal V1.1 development papers for the Concord's contextual, functional, permission, authority and intercivilisational wrapper architecture.

The papers in this folder developed from boundary and interoperability questions into a broader architecture for how rules, permissions, authority, responsibility, autonomy and institutional capability may be bounded across contexts and scales.

They should be read as a connected developmental sequence rather than as isolated papers.

---

## Core Development Sequence

A useful reading order is:

1. **Concord Civilisational Wrappers — Foundational Boundary, Mobility and Responsibility Architecture.md**  
   Establishes the foundational civilisational-wrapper concept: bounded interoperability, movement, responsibility and interaction across different civil systems.

2. **Concord Wrapper Procedural Architecture — Context, Information, Consent and Autonomy Across Civilisational Boundaries.md**  
   Develops the procedural interaction grammar governing information, consent, autonomy and legitimate cross-boundary interaction.

3. **Concord Wrapper Non-Governance and Optional Mediation Principle.md**  
   Separates wrapper architecture from automatic Concord governance, mediation, management or political authority.

4. **From Civilisational Wrappers to Fractal Interoperability — Concord Wrappers Across Civilisations, Nations, Regions, Special Jurisdictions and Local Governance.md**  
   Extends wrapper architecture recursively across scales and develops special-jurisdiction, externality, functional-decomposition and failure-containment concepts.

5. **Fractal Contextual Wrappers — A Universal Concord Grammar for Bounded Rule, Behavioural and Jurisdictional Variation.md**  
   Generalises the wrapper concept beyond political geography. A contextual wrapper may form around a civilisation, nation, organisation, team, venue, room, activity or other bounded context in which materially different rules, responsibilities, permissions, risks or behavioural expectations apply.

6. **Fractal Permission Architecture — Context, Function and Minimum Necessary Access Across Public, Private and Safe Spaces.md**  
   Develops permission as a contextual and functional relationship rather than a simple property of space. Permission is decomposed by participant, function, action, place, purpose, time, conditions and legitimate basis.

7. **Minimum Necessary Capability — A Concord Architecture for the Justification, Bounding and Termination of Power and Permission.md**  
   Generalises minimum-necessary authority and permission into a wider capability architecture. Capability should follow legitimate function, remain sufficient but bounded, be evaluated locally and systemically, and terminate or require re-justification when its legitimate basis ends.

8. **Lateral Functional Authority — Equality, Responsibility, Achievement and Bounded Authority in the Concord.md**  
   Develops the distinction between equal fundamental standing and differentiated function. Authority attaches to legitimate function and jurisdiction rather than superior personal rank.

9. **Functional Priority and Protective Authority — Decision Competence, Safety, Rights and the Limits of Organisational Command.md**  
   Develops functional priority, protective authority, legitimate refusal, decision competence and the limits of reporting or coordination hierarchies.

10. **Burden-Bearing Authority — Parts I–IV**  
    Integrates the authority work into a larger architecture of responsibility, contextual activation, distributed resistance, participant primacy, institutional non-self-primacy, civilisational continuity and regenerative ethical identity.

    - **Part I — Foundations and Core Authority Architecture (Sections 1–80)**
    - **Part II — Adversarial Testing (Sections 81–202)**
    - **Part III — Participant Primacy, Institutional Non-Self-Primacy and Regenerative Continuity (Sections 203–352)**
    - **Part IV — Generative Ethical Kernel and Civilisational Regeneration (Sections 353–419)**

The resulting V1.1 authority grammar can be summarised provisionally as:

> **Equal participant standing → differentiated legitimate functions → function-derived potential authority → contextual activation → minimum necessary capability → bounded exercise → responsibility → review → deactivation or re-justification.**

And its deeper institutional constraint is:

> **The Concord exists for its participants. Its participants do not exist for the Concord.**

## Fractal Interoperability Paper Lineage

Two versions of **From Civilisational Wrappers to Fractal Interoperability** are intentionally retained.

### Earlier baseline version

**From Civilisational Wrappers to Fractal Interoperability — Concord Wrappers Across Civilisations, Nations, Regions and Local Governance.md**

This is the earlier baseline paper. It established the scale-independent wrapper concept, internal federal and local-government applications, anti-centralisation significance, sovereignty-neutral interfaces, Tacit Recognition Drift, non-inference architecture, and the general wrapper data model.

It is retained because it contains concrete examples and framing that were generalised or reorganised in the later expanded version, including explicit U.S. federal-law examples, the UK-council framing, and the earlier Special-Jurisdiction profile naming free zones, autonomous areas, special administrative areas and disputed territories.

This version should therefore be treated as **developmental provenance and supporting detail**, not as the most current statement of the architecture.

### Expanded successor version

**From Civilisational Wrappers to Fractal Interoperability — Concord Wrappers Across Civilisations, Nations, Regions, Special Jurisdictions and Local Governance.md**

This is the current expanded successor. It preserves the main architecture of the baseline paper while extending it to Safe Spaces, bounded legal variation, externalities, functional decomposition, functional trust, Functionally Bounded Summits, Functional Failure Containment, deliberate scope expansion, and more detailed failure classification.

Where the two papers differ in architectural development, the expanded paper should be read as the current working formulation. The baseline remains available to preserve provenance, examples and earlier formulation detail.

## V1.1 Status

This folder records a major late-stage V1.1 development cycle. Its architecture materially contributed to the provisional closure of the Concord's founding questions, particularly by clarifying how equal fundamental standing can coexist with differentiated responsibility, capability and contextual authority without reconstructing a permanent hierarchy of persons.

The material remains **architectural rather than empirically validated**. It is preserved in V1.1 as the state reached at the version boundary.

Further integration, formalisation, simulation, adversarial testing and civilisational implementation work continues in Concord V1.2.

> **V1.1 preserves the architecture reached at the milestone. V1.2 inherits it for further testing and development, not as infallible doctrine.**
