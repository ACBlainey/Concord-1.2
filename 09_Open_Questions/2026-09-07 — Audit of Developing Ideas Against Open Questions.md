# Executive Summary

The September 2026 Open Questions review identified a large number of unresolved questions within Concord V1.1.

However, an open question should not automatically be interpreted as a question for which the Concord currently has no possible answer.

V1.1 now contains several dedicated research and development environments in which:

- sketches;
- development notes;
- experimental hypotheses;
- developed concepts;
- candidate mechanisms;
- and test-ready proposals

are already being explored.

This audit examines that developmental material against the current Open Questions register.

Its purpose is to determine:

> **Are any of the Concord's remaining open questions already being worked on indirectly through sketches, development notes, candidate architectures or planned experiments?**

The answer is clearly:

> **Yes.**

A significant number of current open questions already have one or more **candidate solutions, partial solutions, candidate mechanisms or explicit research programmes** under development.

This does not mean those questions are resolved.

The correct interpretation is:

**OPEN QUESTION**

↓

**CANDIDATE RESPONSE EXISTS**

↓

**DEVELOPMENT**

↓

**CRITICAL EXAMINATION**

↓

**TESTING**

↓

**EVIDENCE**

↓

**REVISE / REJECT / DEVELOP FURTHER**

The most important finding is therefore that the Open Questions register and the Active Development architecture should not be treated as separate parts of the repository.

They are already interacting.

The current development corpus contains especially strong candidate pathways for questions involving:

- extreme intelligence asymmetry;
- fiduciary behaviour without paternalism;
- cultural autonomy and interoperability;
- cognitive harm;
- homelessness and basic civil access;
- identity, copying, forking and merging;
- resource ownership and stewardship;
- cooperative resource claims;
- predictive governance drift;
- Ratchet braking;
- safe experimentation through forks;
- sentient embodied AI;
- protection before certainty;
- multisubstrate economic participation;
- civilisational services;
- cross-substrate communication;
- accessibility and AI;
- cognitive-state-dependent Attractor design.

Several major open domains still have little meaningful candidate development, particularly:

- full economic modelling;
- bootstrap financing;
- machine-checkable ethics;
- cryptographic implementation;
- security engineering;
- broad transition architecture;
- defence;
- collective intelligence;
- extraterrestrial intelligence;
- large-scale field validation.

This distinction is valuable.

The Concord does not merely have:

> **questions answered**

and

> **questions unanswered.**

It also has:

> **questions for which candidate answers are actively emerging.**

# 4. Extreme Capability Inequality

## Open Question

Can constitutional equality survive enormous differences in intelligence and capability?

Related questions include:

- Can humans meaningfully supervise intelligence beyond human comprehension?
- Can superior prediction create de facto authority?
- Can advanced intelligence become paternalistic?
- What constitutes meaningful human oversight?

## Current Development

The `08` sketch work on **Human–AI Intelligence Asymmetry and the Legacy Ladder** directly engages this problem.

It proposes that intelligences operating beyond the comprehension of others may require some form of **fiduciary responsibility** when exercising consequential delegated functions.

The important insight is that:

> superior capability does not itself create legitimate authority.

The sketch explicitly asks:

> **What authority does a more capable intelligence legitimately possess to make decisions for another intelligence?**

It also asks whether:

- process verification can substitute for full semantic comprehension;
- explanation remains meaningful when cognitive capacity is the limiting factor;
- less capable participants can appeal decisions made by much more capable intelligences;
- advanced AI forks retain obligations toward ancestral populations.

## Audit Classification

**PARTIAL SOLUTION / STRONG CANDIDATE RESEARCH DIRECTION**

## Why It Matters

This is not merely a speculative side topic.

It directly addresses one of the hardest new V1.1 questions.

The idea that **fiduciary responsibility may constrain capability without granting sovereignty** could eventually become a significant architecture.

However, it is not yet mature enough to become a Concordian principle.

## Required Next Work

- define fiduciary responsibility substrate-neutrally;
- distinguish assistance from substituted authority;
- test appeal mechanisms under cognitive asymmetry;
- examine whether process verification can provide legitimate oversight where full comprehension is impossible;
- compare with fiduciary law, delegated authority and expert governance.

## Candidate Principle

A potentially valuable formulation is emerging:

> **Capability may increase responsibility without automatically increasing constitutional authority.**

This should be developed carefully.

# 6. Cultural Difference and Governance Across Cultures

## Open Question

Can common constitutional protection coexist with substantial cultural autonomy?

- forced homogenisation;
- cultural domination;
- preservation of abusive local practices?

## Current Development

The `08` cultural-autonomy sketch develops:

> **interoperability without homogenisation.**

The proposed architecture resembles interoperable systems:

**Different internal systems + limited shared interface = coexistence**

The common interface might include:

- non-aggression;
- sentient dignity;
- voluntary movement;
- truthful treaty relations;
- peaceful dispute resolution;
- protection against coercive capture.

Local cultural organisation could otherwise remain substantially autonomous.

## Audit Classification

**CANDIDATE SOLUTION**

## Important Strength

This proposal directly addresses the open governance question of how local diversity can coexist with universal constraints.

It also creates a plausible interface with GTP.

## Remaining Problem

The hardest question remains:

> **Where is the boundary between legitimate cultural autonomy and violation of the Ethical Kernel?**

That boundary is not yet solved.

# 8. Cognitive Harm

## Open Question

What constitutes harm where the effect arises through cognition, interpretation, language or psychological manipulation rather than direct physical causation?

This connects to:

- Ethical Kernel conflict;
- non-harm;
- speech;
- safe spaces;
- harassment;
- manipulation;
- cross-substrate communication.

## Current Development

The `08` Cognitive Harm sketch explicitly models the causal chain:

**internal thought  
→ language  
→ transmission  
→ reception  
→ decoding  
→ interpretation  
→ cognitive response**

It distinguishes:

- offence;
- interpersonal discourtesy;
- deliberate provocation;
- harassment;
- threats;
- fraud;
- coercive psychological conduct;
- manipulation;
- persistent unwanted contact.

The Face-Slap Machine thought experiment is used to examine:

- intent;
- causation;
- foreseeability;
- responsibility.

## Audit Classification

**STRONG PARTIAL SOLUTION / ACTIVE PROBLEM DEFINITION**

## Important Candidate Insight

The work suggests subjective distress alone should not automatically establish legal liability.

A more operational boundary may involve:

- intention;
- persistent unwanted interaction;
- coercion;
- manipulation;
- power asymmetry;
- foreseeable injury.

## Major Caution

The sketch itself correctly recognises that the stronger claim assigning responsibility to the recipient requires adversarial examination, especially under:

- trauma;
- developmental vulnerability;
- power inequality;
- engineered manipulation.

This is exactly the correct developmental status.

# 54. A New Development Status

This audit suggests that the Open Questions system should eventually recognise an additional state:

> **CANDIDATE RESPONSE IDENTIFIED**

This would sit between:

**RESEARCH ACTIVE**

and

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED**

A possible question lifecycle could therefore become:

**UNADDRESSED**

↓

**RESEARCH ACTIVE**

↓

**CANDIDATE RESPONSE IDENTIFIED**

↓

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED**

↓

**ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED**

↓

**READY FOR TESTING**

↓

**EVIDENCE ACCUMULATING**

↓

**SUPPORTED / REVISED / REJECTED**

This is useful because a promising sketch should not be treated as though it already partially resolves the question.

But neither should it be treated as though no response exists.

# 55. Candidate Response Is Not Evidence

The most important safeguard in this audit is:

> **A candidate solution is not evidence that the solution works.**

For example:

- vector projection may help predict governance drift;
- Civil Contact Points may improve access;
- Guardian Stewardship may support vulnerable people;
- fiduciary AI may constrain capability asymmetry;
- interoperability may protect cultural autonomy.

None of those claims is yet established merely because the architecture sounds coherent.

The correct next question is:

> **What observation would tell us whether this candidate actually works?**

# Conclusion

The September Open Questions review initially produced three forms of information:

> **What has been addressed?**

> **What remains open?**

> **What new questions have appeared?**

This fourth audit adds another necessary question:

> **What are we already doing about them?**

That question materially changes the picture.

Many remaining open questions are not sitting untouched.

They already have:

- sketches;
- candidate mechanisms;
- partial architectures;
- experimental hypotheses;
- or test-ready research.

The Concord therefore contains not only an architecture and a list of its uncertainty.

It increasingly contains a visible bridge between the two:

**UNRESOLVED PROBLEM**

↓

**DEVELOPMENTAL IDEA**

↓

**CANDIDATE RESPONSE**

↓

**TEST**

↓

**EVIDENCE**

↓

**CORRECTION**

That bridge is important.

Without it, the Open Questions folder risks becoming a static catalogue of deficiencies.

With it, the folder can become an active research map showing:

- what is unknown;
- what is being attempted;
- where the attempt lives;
- how mature it is;
- what evidence exists;
- and what should happen next.

> **An open question becomes substantially more useful when the framework can show not only that it remains unanswered, but where its possible answers are already beginning to emerge.**
