> **Which questions previously classified as open have now acquired sufficient candidate architecture that they should be reclassified, decomposed, reframed or narrowly closed?**

The governing principle remains:

\[

\]

and:

\[
ConceptualAdvance \neq EmpiricalValidation
\]

The objective is therefore not to maximise closure.

It is to maintain an accurate representation of the Concord's current uncertainty.

# 3. Major Development Since the 10 September Reconciliation

The 10 September reconciliation identified the following as a central unresolved problem:

> **How can a civilisation determine that a function genuinely needs power without allowing power itself to determine the answer?**

Recent work has not solved this problem.

It has, however, substantially decomposed the architecture surrounding it.

The emerging chain is now approximately:

\[
Reality
\rightarrow

\rightarrow

\rightarrow
RequiredFunction
\rightarrow
AuthorityNeed
\rightarrow

\rightarrow

\rightarrow

\rightarrow

\]

The major advance is upstream of authority.

The Concord now possesses substantially clearer candidate architecture for determining:

- how conditions become observable;
- how different observations are compared;
- how participant evidence enters civil knowledge;
- how evidence provenance is preserved;
- how common dependencies are exposed;
- how local and system-level observations interact;
- how anomalies become inquiries rather than automatic commands;
- how threat assessment remains distinct from governance;
- how functional need can be supported by evidence without allowing evidence-producing institutions to become sovereign.

This has created what may be understood provisionally as a:

> **Civil Epistemic–Authority Interface**

This interface is not itself a new sovereign institution.

It is the collection of processes through which claims about reality can eventually become legitimate inputs into civil action.

\[

# 8. FN-03 — Unexpressed Need

## Previous question

> **How should legitimate needs be represented for participants who cannot presently identify or communicate them?**

## Development

Recent work partially advances this question.

\[

- incapacity;

- suppression;
- reporting friction;
- lack of awareness;
- inaccessible reporting systems;
- developmental state;

Formal observation, local stewardship, infrastructure evidence and cross-scale comparison can therefore provide evidence of possible unmet need.

\[

# 9. FN-04 — Aggregation

## Previous question

> **How should conflicting participant goods be aggregated without collapsing into average-based policy?**

## Previous status

**OPEN**

## Development

The entire aggregation problem remains unresolved.

However, one major component has advanced considerably.

Triadic Epistemics and GTP now support:

> **Aggregate normality must not automatically invalidate credible concentrated local or minority harm.**

A candidate response is:

```text
Aggregate Normality
        +
Credible Local Anomaly
        ↓
Preserve the Anomaly
        ↓
Increase Resolution
        ↓
Examine Relevant Child Regions,
Populations, Substrates or Networks
        ↓
Investigate
```

GTP provides a particularly useful mechanism because a region can simultaneously be:

- a whole;
- part of a larger parent region;
- composed of smaller child regions;
- compared with neighbouring regions.

A higher-level average therefore need not erase lower-level structure.

This closely aligns with the Concord's existing rejection of average-person policy exemplified by the Size 9 Shoe Problem.

## Narrow conceptual closure

The following question can now reasonably be closed at the conceptual level:

> **May higher-level aggregate normality, by itself, invalidate credible evidence of serious local or minority harm?**

**CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO**

This does not solve conflicting-goods aggregation generally.

## Replacement question

> **How should genuinely conflicting participant goods, rights and burdens be reconciled when increasing observational resolution does not remove the conflict?**

## Revised disposition

**BROAD QUESTION DECOMPOSED / AGGREGATE-ERASURE PREMISE CLOSED / CONFLICTING-GOODS AGGREGATION REMAINS OPEN**

# 13. Recursive GTP Epistemic Mesh

A major new development is recognition that Triadic Epistemics should not be modelled merely as isolated three-node measurement systems.

GTP supplies a recursive spatial mesh.

Within a GTP region:

```text
        Metric A
          /\
         /  \
        /    \
Metric B ---- Metric C
```

but the region also exists within:

```text
LATERAL COMPARISON
Region ↔ Neighbouring Regions

VERTICAL COMPARISON
Children ↔ Region ↔ Parent
```

and can additionally compare:

- participant evidence;
- functional networks;
- infrastructure;
- historical states;
- independent external evidence.

Importantly, GTP recursive subdivision is not itself triadic.

Each triangular GTP cell subdivides into four child triangles.

The distinction should therefore remain explicit:

> **Triadic Epistemics describes a candidate comparison architecture operating within and across civil observation systems. GTP supplies recursive triangular spatial topology and scale abstraction.**

The two architectures interact but are not identical.

# 14. GTP as Epistemic Resolution Control

The recursive GTP structure creates a candidate civil equivalent of changing observational resolution.

For example:

```text
Region R
↓
Unusual Food-Availability Signal
↓
Neighbouring Regions Normal
↓
Descend into R's Child Regions
```

Suppose:

```text
R.1 — Normal
R.2 — Normal
R.3 — Severe
R.4 — Normal
```

The system can descend again:

```text
R.3.1 — Normal
R.3.2 — Shortage
R.3.3 — Shortage
R.3.4 — Normal
```

This does not establish the cause.

It localises the structure of the anomaly.

Possible explanations might include:

- genuine local shortage;
- distribution failure;
- transport failure;
- warehouse failure;
- contamination;
- economic exclusion;
- sensor fault;
- calibration drift;
- software failure;
- data corruption;
- deliberate manipulation;
- cyberattack;
- participant/formal-evidence mismatch;
- an early signal not yet visible elsewhere.

Thus:

> **Do not average a credible anomaly away. Change resolution until its structure becomes visible or the limits of observation become explicit.**

\[

**CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO**

The appropriate question is:

> **Independent with respect to which plausible failure?**

# 17. Anomaly Classification

Recent work suggests that the word **anomaly** itself requires decomposition.

At least four forms should be distinguished.

## 17.1 Civil Anomaly

Something unusual appears to be occurring in the world or civil system.

## 17.2 Epistemic Anomaly

Different representations or evidence channels disagree.

## 17.3 System-Health Anomaly

The measurement or knowledge-producing system itself may be malfunctioning.

## 17.4 Security Anomaly

There is evidence consistent with deliberate interference, manipulation or capture.

These categories may overlap.

They should not automatically imply one another.

For example:

\[
EpistemicAnomaly
\not\Rightarrow
CivilEmergency
\]

and:

\[
SystemHealthAnomaly
\not\Rightarrow
DeliberateAttack
\]

This decomposition should improve future threat and epistemic experiments.

# 18. Epistemic Attention — Newly Exposed Major Question

The direct adversarial attack on Triadic Epistemics exposed an important problem not adequately represented in the previous Open Questions register.

Plural observation may generate more anomalies than civilisation can investigate.

Therefore:

\[
ObservationCapacity
>
InvestigationCapacity
\]

may become normal.

Someone or something must determine which anomalies receive scarce attention.

This creates a new potential concentration of power.

A civilisation could possess distributed measurement but still become epistemically centralised through control of:

- investigation queues;
- anomaly ranking;
- resource allocation;
- escalation thresholds;
- expert access;
- computational resources;
- public visibility.

## EQ-01 — Epistemic Attention Allocation

> **How should scarce investigative attention be distributed without creating an unelected epistemic gatekeeper capable of determining which contradictions become civil knowledge?**

**Status:** NEW / HIGH-PRIORITY OPEN QUESTION

GTP subsidiarity may provide part of the answer:

```text
Local Anomaly
↓
Local Verification
↓
Neighbour / Child / Parent Comparison
↓
Escalate Only if Material or Unresolved
```

This distributes some filtering.

It does not completely solve attention allocation.

# 22. Mesh Compression

Distributed epistemics creates another difficult interface.

Governance cannot necessarily inspect every raw signal produced by a planetary epistemic mesh.

Information must therefore be summarised.

But summarisation itself creates power.

```text
Distributed Evidence
↓
Compression
↓
Governance Representation
```

Whoever controls compression may indirectly control what governance sees.

This problem becomes particularly important where AI systems summarise large civil evidence streams.

## EQ-05 — Mesh Compression

> **How can rich distributed evidence be communicated to decision systems without collapsing the epistemic mesh into a hidden hierarchical summarisation authority?**

**Status:** NEW / HIGH-PRIORITY AI–GOVERNANCE INTERFACE**

Possible requirements include preserving:

- provenance;
- dissent;
- material outliers;
- confidence;
- uncertainty;
- shared dependencies;
- unresolved contradictions;

# 24. Architectural Consistency Versus Structural Independence

The Triadic Epistemics audit produced a principle with wider Concordian relevance:

> **Architectural consistency must not be mistaken for structural independence.**

Using similar patterns across a civilisation can reduce complexity.

But excessive structural uniformity may produce:

- common attack strategies;
- shared blind spots;
- repeated incentive failures;
- predictable coalition structures;
- correlated institutional behaviour.

The relevant design problem is therefore not simply:

\[
Consistency = Good
\]

or:

\[
Diversity = Good
\]

but:

\[
UsefulConsistency
+
FailureRelevantDiversity
\]

This should become a recurring criterion in future Concordian system audits.
