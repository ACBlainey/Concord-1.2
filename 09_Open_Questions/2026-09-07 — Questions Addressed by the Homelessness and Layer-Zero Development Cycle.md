# Document Purpose

This document records open questions within Concord V1.1 that have been materially advanced through the development of:

- *Homelessness as a Layer-Zero Systems Failure*;
- predictive and vectoral support architecture derived from that case;
- Layer −0 Digital Twin and counterfactual modelling;
- Civil Contact Points;
- citizen data ownership, access and contestability;
- recursive Metrics auditing;
- and the associated Concordian Method case study.

The purpose is not to claim that these questions have been solved.

The relevant distinction remains:

> **Architecture is not proof.**

> **Formalisation is not validation.**

> **Internal consistency is not external evidence.**

> **A simulation is not reality.**

> **Development is not adoption.**

The homelessness development cycle has produced candidate principles, mechanisms, safeguards, operational relationships and testable propositions.

These constitute **developmental progress**.

They do not constitute empirical validation.

This audit therefore asks:

> **Which previously open questions have received materially new responses, what has been learned, what status should now apply, and what remains unresolved?**

# 3. Autonomy Versus Protection from Harm

## Previous Open Question

The Ethical Kernel identifies a fundamental potential conflict between:

- respect for autonomy;
- and protection from harm.

A civilisation may recognise that a participant is deteriorating while simultaneously lacking legitimate authority to impose assistance.

This creates a particularly difficult problem where failure to act may eventually produce circumstances in which the participant has substantially fewer meaningful choices.

## Development from the Homelessness Case

The homelessness work developed a staged response rather than treating support as a binary choice between:

**DO NOTHING**

and:

**INTERVENE.**

The emerging architecture is approximately:

**ENVIRONMENTAL AND SYSTEMIC SUPPORT**

↓

**INFORMATION AND ACCESS**

↓

**VOLUNTARY OFFER OF SUPPORT**

↓

**PARTICIPANT CHOICE**

↓

**SUPPORTED DECISION-MAKING WHERE REQUIRED**

↓

**GUARDIAN STEWARDSHIP UNDER HIGHER THRESHOLDS**

↓

**EMERGENCY INTERVENTION UNDER STILL HIGHER THRESHOLDS**

The degree of legitimate intrusion should not automatically increase merely because a predictive system forecasts a negative future.

This generated the candidate principle:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

It also generated:

> **Where appropriate, early voluntary support may preserve autonomy by reducing the probability that later circumstances require more intrusive intervention.**

The objective is therefore not merely to avoid intervention.

It is to preserve the conditions under which meaningful autonomous choice remains possible.

## Additional Safeguard

The case also established:

> **Refusing help is not automatically evidence that help should be imposed.**

A participant may understand the circumstances and deliberately choose a course of action that differs from the system's preferred outcome.

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — CANDIDATE OPERATIONAL RESPONSE IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- What exact evidential thresholds justify movement between support stages?
- How should capacity be assessed?
- Who determines when supported decision-making becomes Guardian Stewardship?
- What judicial review is required?
- How are emergency exceptions constrained?
- How does this architecture translate across substrates?
- Can early-support systems unintentionally create dependency?
- Can voluntary support become coercive through economic or social pressure?
- How well does the architecture work in real cases?

# 4. Paternalism and Caretaker Capture

## Previous Open Problem

A recurring Concord problem is:

> **Can support, stewardship or protection gradually become paternalistic control?**

A system may begin with benevolent intent while progressively replacing participant judgement with institutional judgement.

## Development from the Homelessness Case

Predictive homelessness intervention exposed this danger directly.

A system might observe:

**Risk indicator detected**

and incorrectly infer:

**Intervention authorised.**

The developed architecture rejects this transition.

Instead:

**SIGNAL**

↓

**REVIEW**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**CASE-SPECIFIC EVALUATION**

↓

**POSSIBLE EXPLANATIONS**

↓

**PROPORTIONATE OPTIONS**

↓

**VOLUNTARY OFFER**

↓

**ENGAGEMENT OR NON-ENGAGEMENT**

↓

**FURTHER OBSERVATION WHERE LEGITIMATE**

The predictive system should normally increase the participant's available choices rather than reduce them.

This generated:

> **Predictive civil systems should use evidence of possible deterioration primarily to expand a participant's available choices, not to reduce them.**

## Anti-Paternalistic Failure Mode

A particularly dangerous recursive failure was identified:

**SYSTEM:** You are deteriorating.

**PARTICIPANT:** I am not.

**SYSTEM:** Your disagreement demonstrates that you do not understand your deterioration.

This architecture would make the model effectively unfalsifiable.

The derived safeguard is:

> **Disagreement with a predictive model must not automatically be interpreted as evidence that the model is correct.**

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — SIGNIFICANT CANDIDATE SAFEGUARDS IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- How is genuine incapacity distinguished from legitimate disagreement?
- How should systems respond where participant choices create serious risks to third parties?
- How can subtle coercion be detected?
- Can service dependence become an indirect mechanism of control?
- What appeals are required?
- How should AI or collective intelligences express disagreement?
- What evidence demonstrates that the safeguards work in practice?

# 5. Privacy Versus Accountability

## Previous Open Question

The Ethical Kernel identifies a conflict between:

- privacy;
- accountability;
- and the information required for effective civilisational coordination.

A sophisticated Metrics system could easily become a surveillance architecture.

## Development from the Homelessness Case

The homelessness work required personal information for legitimate individual support while simultaneously requiring aggregate information for civilisational planning.

This produced an architectural separation between:

**PERSONAL IDENTIFIABLE DATA**

and:

**CIVILISATIONAL INFORMATION.**

For individual support, identity may legitimately matter.

Examples include:

- healthcare;
- housing support;
- financial support;
- employment support;
- personal Digital Twins;
- vectoral trajectory analysis.

For civilisational coordination, individual identity frequently does not matter.

The candidate rule therefore became:

> **Where individual identity is not necessary for the civilisational purpose, individual identity should not accompany the data.**

And:

> **Civilisational metrics should use the minimum data resolution necessary for their legitimate purpose.**

## Wider Proposition

The work generated a broader architectural proposition:

> **A civilisation can become highly measurable without requiring its citizens to become highly observable as individuals.**

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — CANDIDATE DATA-SEPARATION ARCHITECTURE IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- What technical anonymisation standards are sufficient?
- How should re-identification risk be measured?
- When may Judiciary access identifiable information?
- What emergency access rules are legitimate?
- How should third-party information be handled?
- What cryptographic architecture is required?
- How are distributed AI and collective entities anonymised?
- What data-retention periods are appropriate?
- How can anonymised data remain useful without becoming indirectly identifiable?

# 6. Personal Data Ownership

## Previous Open Question

The Property, Stewardship and Ownership programme asks, among other questions:

> **Can information be owned?**

The homelessness case exposed a narrower and more immediately operational version:

> **Who should own identifiable information derived from an individual participant?**

## Candidate Response

The resulting proposition is:

> **Individuals should retain primary ownership and control of identifiable personal data derived from them.**

Civilisational need for information does not automatically establish civilisational ownership of that information.

This produces the conceptual relationship:

**PARTICIPANT**

↓

**IDENTIFIABLE PERSONAL DATA**

↓

**PARTICIPANT-OWNED / PARTICIPANT-CONTROLLED PERSONAL RECORD**

while legitimate civilisational learning occurs through appropriately anonymised or aggregated derivatives.

## New Status

**CANDIDATE RESPONSE IDENTIFIED FOR PERSONAL INFORMATION**

The broader philosophical and legal question of information ownership remains open.

## Remaining Questions

- What precisely does data ownership legally mean?
- Does ownership include deletion?
- Can some records have multiple legitimate owners?
- What happens where one person's data necessarily contains information about another?
- What rights survive anonymisation?
- Can derived statistical information remain personal property?
- What happens to data after death?
- How does ownership work for AI copies, forks and merged intelligences?
- How does this interact with legal requirements in existing jurisdictions?

# 7. Data Access

## Newly Developed Question

Ownership without access may be largely nominal.

The homelessness development therefore expanded the personal-data problem beyond ownership.

## Candidate Response

A participant should normally be able to inspect consequential personal information maintained about them.

This may include:

- source data;
- measurements;
- provenance;
- major derived metrics;
- significant classifications;
- major predictive conclusions;
- and, where appropriate, access history.

This generated:

> **Where civilisation maintains a consequential model of an individual, that individual should normally have meaningful access to the model and the information from which it was derived.**

## New Status

**CANDIDATE RESPONSE IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- Which information may legitimately be withheld?
- How are third-party rights protected?
- What security exceptions exist?
- How is access made understandable rather than merely technically available?
- How should complex AI-derived predictions be explained?
- What standards apply to children or participants lacking decision-making capacity?

# 8. Data Contestability and Correction

## Newly Developed Question

What happens when civilisational data is wrong?

The homelessness case demonstrated that incorrect data could produce incorrect predictions and potentially inappropriate intervention.

## Candidate Architecture

A participant should be able to flag personal data as disputed.

Possible outcomes include:

- **DATA CONFIRMED**
- **DATA CORRECTED**
- **DATA UNCERTAIN**
- **INTERPRETATION CORRECTED**
- **CONTEXT ADDED**
- **DISPUTE UNRESOLVED**

The historical record should not necessarily be silently erased.

Instead:

**ORIGINAL MEASUREMENT**

↓

**PARTICIPANT CHALLENGE**

↓

**AUDIT**

↓

**CORRECTION OR CONFIRMATION**

↓

**CURRENT RECORD**

with appropriate provenance retained.

## Candidate Principle

> **A participant should normally be able to contest personal data and consequential interpretations derived from it.**

## New Status

**CANDIDATE OPERATIONAL ARCHITECTURE IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- Who adjudicates disputed records?
- What evidence standard applies?
- How are malicious correction attempts handled?
- How are conflicting authoritative sources reconciled?
- What happens while a dispute remains unresolved?
- When should disputed data cease influencing consequential decisions?

# 9. Contextualisation of Metrics

## Problem Revealed

A datum may be correct while its interpretation is wrong.

The homelessness case generated the example of an older couple who:

- reduce working hours;
- reduce income;
- move to a smaller home;
- reduce expenditure.

A simplistic Metrics system might interpret these simultaneously as socioeconomic deterioration.

Yet the actual explanation may be:

- voluntary retirement;
- financial security;
- deliberate downsizing;
- reduced consumption;
- increased leisure;
- lifestyle simplification.

The measurements may all be correct.

The interpretation may still be wrong.

## Candidate Architecture

The system should distinguish:

**RECORDED DATUM**

from:

**SYSTEM INTERPRETATION**

from:

**PARTICIPANT CONTEXT.**

The participant should normally be able to add contextual information without requiring accurate source data to be erased.

This generated:

> **A participant should be able to add relevant context without requiring accurate original observations to be erased.**

and:

> **Measurement, interpretation and participant context should remain distinguishable.**

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — CANDIDATE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- How much evidential weight should participant context receive?
- How should conflicting context be represented?
- Can contextual information itself be manipulated?
- How can automated systems reason over context?
- How should contextual meaning survive aggregation?

# 10. Metrics Reliability

## Previous Problem

Metrics may:

- measure the wrong thing;
- use bad proxies;
- contain faulty source data;
- create false positives;
- create false negatives;
- or become targets for gaming.

## Development from the Homelessness Case

Citizen correction provides an additional source of evidence about Metrics reliability.

The emerging recursive cycle is:

**PERSONAL METRIC**

↓

**PARTICIPANT CHALLENGE**

↓

**LOCAL REVIEW**

↓

**CORRECTION / CONTEXT**

↓

**ANONYMISED ERROR SIGNAL**

↓

**AGGREGATE ERROR PATTERN**

↓

**METRICS SYSTEM AUDIT**

↓

**MEASUREMENT OR MODEL IMPROVEMENT**

↓

**BETTER FUTURE METRICS**

One error may represent an isolated mistake.

Repeated similar errors may indicate:

- measurement failure;
- sensor failure;
- administrative failure;
- inappropriate proxy selection;
- systematic bias;
- missing contextual variables;
- or flawed inference.

## Candidate Principle

> **Participants can function as distributed auditors of civilisational measurement.**

This creates a reciprocal architecture:

**SYSTEM OBSERVES PARTICIPANT**

and:

**PARTICIPANT OBSERVES SYSTEM.**

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — RECURSIVE AUDIT MECHANISM IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- How are error reports aggregated safely?
- How are malicious reports detected?
- What level of disagreement triggers system audit?
- Who audits the audit process?
- How are different measurement systems compared?
- What thresholds justify replacement of a metric?

# 12. Predictive Governance

## Previous Problem

The Concord increasingly uses Metrics and trajectory analysis.

This creates a dangerous question:

> **Does predicting a harmful future justify intervention before that future exists?**

## Candidate Response

The homelessness work produces a clear answer:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

Predictive information may legitimately:

- trigger further examination;
- generate alternative hypotheses;
- reveal possible support needs;
- improve service availability;
- generate voluntary offers;
- inform aggregate planning.

It does not automatically justify coercion.

The appropriate chain is:

**POSSIBLE DETERIORATION DETECTED**

↓

**CONTEXT EXAMINED**

↓

**UNCERTAINTY RETAINED**

↓

**ALTERNATIVES GENERATED**

↓

**SUPPORT OPTIONS IDENTIFIED**

↓

**VOLUNTARY OFFER**

↓

**PARTICIPANT DECIDES**

rather than:

**RISK DETECTED → INTERVENTION AUTHORISED.**

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — STRONG CANDIDATE CONSTRAINT IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- Are there circumstances where prediction legitimately contributes to authority?
- What probability and severity thresholds matter?
- How should irreversible low-probability risks be treated?
- What happens where third parties are endangered?
- How should prediction interact with emergency powers?
- What evidential standards should Judiciary require?

# 13. Vectoral Prediction

## Development

The homelessness case applied existing vectoral reasoning to individual civil trajectories.

Rather than asking only:

> **What is the participant's current state?**

the system may ask:

> **In what direction is the participant's situation changing?**

Potential signals might include combinations of:

- employment instability;
- absenteeism;
- financial deterioration;
- housing instability;
- health deterioration;
- social isolation;
- traumatic exposure;
- service disengagement.

However:

> **An indicator is a reason to ask a question, not an answer to that question.**

Population-level correlations should not automatically become individual diagnoses.

## New Status

**CANDIDATE APPLICATION IDENTIFIED / REQUIRES TESTING**

## Remaining Questions

- Which indicators are genuinely predictive?
- What are false-positive rates?
- What are false-negative rates?
- How should uncertainty accumulate across indicators?
- What prediction horizon is useful?

- How should participant consent operate?

# 14. Layer −0 and Digital Twins

## Previous Problem

Digital Twins and modelling existed as developing Concord concepts, but their precise civilisational role required further definition.

## Development from the Homelessness Case

The case generated a clearer distinction:

**LAYER ZERO**

= the participant's actual lived condition.

**LAYER −0**

= the modelling environment in which possible trajectories and interventions may be explored before consequential real-world action.

The conceptual architecture is:

**REAL PARTICIPANT — LAYER ZERO**

↑ observation / legitimate metrics ↓

**DIGITAL TWIN — LAYER −0**

↓

**VECTORAL ANALYSIS**

↓

**REALITY TREES**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE INTERVENTIONS**

↓

**SIMULATED / ESTIMATED OUTCOMES**

↓

**RETURN TO LAYER ZERO THROUGH APPROPRIATELY CONSTRAINED ACTION**

## Candidate Purpose

Layer −0 therefore begins to function as a:

> **counterfactual safety layer.**

It creates space between prediction and action.

## Candidate Principle

> **Where significant uncertainty or autonomy risk exists, alternative interventions should, where practical, be explored in Layer −0 before consequential action is applied at Layer Zero.**

## New Status

**ARCHITECTURAL PURPOSE MATERIALLY ADVANCED / CANDIDATE ARCHITECTURE IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- How accurate must Digital Twins be?
- How are model errors represented?
- What data may they contain?
- Who controls them?
- How are they audited?
- How should participant access work?
- What level of simulation is proportionate?
- How should multiple competing models be compared?
- What decisions should never be delegated to Digital Twins?

# 15. Digital Twin Ownership and Contestability

## Development

The data architecture produced a more specific proposition:

> **An individual Digital Twin should be treated as an extension of the participant's protected personal data rather than as general civilisational property.**

A Digital Twin should also be a contestable model.

A participant may reasonably state:

- the data is correct;
- the data is wrong;
- the model is incomplete;
- context is missing;
- an inference is unreasonable;
- a plausible branch has been omitted.

The appropriate cycle is:

**MODEL**

↓

**PARTICIPANT REVIEW**

↓

**CORRECTION / CONTEXT**

↓

**UPDATED MODEL**

↓

**BETTER PREDICTION**

rather than:

**MODEL → ASSUMED CORRECT → ACTION.**

## New Status

**CANDIDATE RESPONSE IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- Can a Digital Twin have shared ownership?
- What happens when models are generated by third parties?
- Can participants delete their twin?
- What happens to historical models?
- How are forks handled?
- What rights apply to AI participants whose model may resemble or duplicate their actual cognitive architecture?

# 16. Layer −0 and Individual Control

## Problem Revealed

A predictive civilisation could use Digital Twins to optimise people rather than support them.

The homelessness case therefore distinguished:

> **steering a system**

from:

> **controlling a participant.**

If modelling suggests that mental-health support would reduce future homelessness, the system can:

- increase availability;
- increase capacity;
- reduce waiting times;
- improve accessibility;
- improve information;
- improve geographic distribution.

It does not follow that a particular participant must be forced to use that service.

## Candidate Principle

> **Where possible, predictive civil architecture should steer by improving the availability and accessibility of beneficial options before attempting to steer through restrictions on individual autonomy.**

## New Status

**CANDIDATE CONSTRAINT IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- When is system steering itself manipulative?
- Can choice architecture become covert coercion?
- Who determines what counts as a beneficial option?
- How should competing models of wellbeing be represented?

# 17. Counterfactual Punishment

## Problem Revealed

Predictive models can represent futures in which a participant performs harmful acts.

This creates a major constitutional danger if simulated behaviour is treated as actual behaviour.

## Candidate Principle

> **A simulated future must never be treated as though the participant has already chosen, caused or committed the acts represented within it.**

Layer −0 predictions therefore cannot themselves establish guilt.

## New Status

**CANDIDATE CONSTITUTIONAL SAFEGUARD IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- Can predictions ever contribute to preventative restrictions?
- How should credible threats be distinguished from model-generated possibilities?
- What evidential status should predictive models have in Judiciary proceedings?
- How should extremely high-consequence risks be treated?

# 23. Outlier-First Design

## Newly Developed Method

The homelessness case suggests that edge conditions can function as architectural probes.

Rather than testing only typical participants, Concord can deliberately examine:

- homelessness;
- disability;
- severe poverty;
- unusual cognitive profiles;
- displaced persons;
- participants without conventional residence;
- unusual substrates;
- distributed intelligence;
- participants with conflicting needs;
- people refusing assistance.

The principle is not that outliers should automatically determine all architecture.

It is that:

> **Outliers can reveal assumptions hidden by normal operation.**

## New Status

**CANDIDATE CONCORDIAN DEVELOPMENT METHOD**

## Remaining Questions

- How should test cases be selected?
- How can overfitting to rare cases be avoided?

# 24. Ratchet and Metric Gaming

# 25. Recursive Oversight

## Previous Problem

A system that observes civilisation may itself become a source of unchecked authority.

## Development

The Metrics work generated an important recursive relationship.

The Metrics system measures participants and civilisation.

Participants can:

- inspect their records;
- challenge measurements;
- challenge interpretations;
- provide context;
- generate correction signals.

Aggregate correction signals can then measure the Metrics system itself.

Therefore:

> **The observer is also observed.**

The architecture becomes:

**METRICS SYSTEM → CIVILISATION**

and:

**CITIZENS + OUTCOMES + AUDITS → METRICS SYSTEM.**

This reduces dependence on:

> **The Metrics system says that the Metrics system is accurate.**

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — NEW RECURSIVE OVERSIGHT MECHANISM IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

- Who audits aggregate Metrics?
- Can audit institutions be captured?
- How are citizen reports independently verified?
- Can recursive oversight become another authority centre?
- What happens when different audit systems disagree?

# 32. Authoritarian Drift

## Existing Open Problem

A major recurring Concord question is:

> **Could the Concord become authoritarian while believing itself ethical?**

The homelessness cycle does not solve this problem.

It does, however, produce several independent constraints against one possible path toward authoritarianism.

These include:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

> **Refusing help is not automatically evidence that help should be imposed.**

> **Disagreement with a predictive model must not automatically be interpreted as evidence that the model is correct.**

> **A simulated future must never be treated as though the participant has already committed the acts represented within it.**

> **Predictive support data should not silently become predictive authority data.**

> **Where individual identity is unnecessary for a legitimate civilisational purpose, it should not accompany the data.**

> **Personal models should remain contestable.**

> **Support and punishment should remain distinguishable.**

> **Increasing intervention should require increasing justification.**

> **Layer −0 modelling should normally create deliberative space before consequential action rather than automatically authorise that action.**

Together these constitute a meaningful architectural response to one pathway through which benevolent systems can become coercive.

## New Status

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — MULTIPLE CANDIDATE ANTI-AUTHORITARIAN SAFEGUARDS IDENTIFIED**

## Remaining Questions

The overarching question remains open.

The architecture requires:

- adversarial testing;
- institutional testing;
- simulation;
- historical comparison;
- red-team analysis;
- real pilot evidence.
