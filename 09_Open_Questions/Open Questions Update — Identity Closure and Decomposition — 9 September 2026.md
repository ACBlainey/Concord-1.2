The Identity investigation has now produced a significant reduction in foundational uncertainty.

The previous broad problem:

> **What is personal identity across copying, reconstruction, migration, branching and merger?**

should no longer be treated as one undifferentiated open question.

The work now separates:

**SELF-IDENTITY / METAPHYSICAL IDENTITY**

**CIVIL IDENTITY / PROVENANCE / VERIFICATION / SUCCESSION / CREATION**

This separation permits one part of the original question to approach deliberate closure while leaving narrower civil and technical questions open.

## 2. Personal / Metaphysical Identity

### Previous state

**OPEN / FOUNDATIONAL / NOT MATERIALLY RESOLVED**

### New state

**CLOSURE CANDIDATE — OUT OF CONCORDIAN CIVIL SCOPE / NO CIVILISATIONAL DETERMINATION REQUIRED**

### Question

> **What is personal identity metaphysically after copying, reconstruction, migration, branching or merger?**

### Emerging answer

The Concord does not need to determine the metaphysical nature of personal identity in order to administer a multisubstrate civilisation.

A participant's internal conception of self is presumptively a matter of autonomy and self-expression.

Different participants may understand continuity through memory, embodiment, subjective experience, causal history, information, spirituality, relationships, narrative, provenance or other models.

The Concord need not select one of these as the compulsory civil definition of the self.

### Boundary

A self-identity claim may generate an external civil claim.

For example:

> "I regard myself as A."

does not itself require civil adjudication.

But:

> "Therefore I am entitled to A's property."

does.

The latter can be addressed through civil identity, provenance, verification and succession without requiring civilisation to determine the former metaphysically.

### Closure rationale

This question may be closed not because its philosophical answer has been discovered, but because:

1. no metaphysical answer is necessary for the relevant civil functions;
2. civil identity can be uniquely assigned independently;
3. provenance can record causal history without deciding metaphysical continuity;
4. succession can determine property and obligations separately;
5. verification can be treated as an evidential problem;
6. imposing a mandatory civil theory of selfhood may infringe autonomy and self-expression.

### Work required before formal closure

Perform a dedicated **Identity Closure Audit** seeking counterexamples in which the Concord genuinely must determine metaphysical identity rather than civil identity, provenance, verification, succession or participant status.

If no necessary case survives, formally close the question.

## 3. External Civil Identity Assignment

### Previous state

**ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / FORMALISATION BEGUN**

### New state

**CLOSE TO ARCHITECTURAL RESOLUTION / ADVERSARIAL TESTING REQUIRED**

### Emerging architecture

Each presently recognised participant requires a unique current external civil reference.

Provisionally:

\[
P_i \neq P_j \Rightarrow UID_i \neq UID_j
\]

After branching, current participants receive distinct identifiers while shared provenance is retained.

Therefore:

> **Unique current civil identity can coexist with shared provenance and divergent self-identity.**

### Remaining work

- registration criteria;
- identifier issuance;
- branch detection;
- collision handling;
- credential re-keying;
- implementation and security testing.

## 4. Provenance

### New state

**CONCEPTUALLY DECOMPOSED / ARCHITECTURAL MODEL EXISTS / VALIDATION OPEN**

Provenance is no longer required to answer which participant is metaphysically the "real" continuation.

Its narrower role is:

> **Record the historical and causal transformations through which current participants came to exist.**

Thus:

**PROVENANCE ≠ PERSONAL IDENTITY**

The directed provenance graph and transformation vocabulary remain development objects requiring implementation and adversarial testing.

## 5. Identity Verification

### Previous state

Part of the broad identity/continuity problem.

### New state

**MAJOR INDEPENDENT OPEN QUESTION / HIGH PRIORITY**

The central unresolved identity problem is now:

> **How can a multisubstrate civilisation reliably verify present civil identity claims when participants, memories, credentials and provenance may branch, copy, merge, reconstruct or diverge?**

A particularly important new case is:

### Legitimate Identity Collision

Two or more current participants may possess equally authentic historical evidence for the same predecessor identity.

Neither need be fraudulent.

They may share:

- memories;
- credentials;
- cryptographic material;
- historical knowledge;
- pre-branch relationships;
- sincere self-identification.

Therefore:

> **Authentication success does not necessarily establish unique current participant identity.**

This is a technical, jurisprudential and security problem rather than a metaphysical identity problem.

## 6. Instrumental Duplication and Carefully Stewarded Creation

### New open-question family

**DECOMPOSED CHILD QUESTIONS / ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT**

The temporary-duplicate problem now separates into the nature of what is deliberately created.

A fully sentient duplicate created for undesirable labour and destroyed afterwards cannot be made ethically disposable merely by calling it a temporary copy.

Emerging constraints:

**CREATED FOR A TASK ≠ OWNED BY CREATOR**

**PLANNED TERMINATION ≠ PERMISSIBLE TERMINATION**

Where a workload does not require sentience, an appropriately limited non-sentient Tool AI should be preferred over unnecessary creation of a sentient participant.

> **Where a task does not require a sentient participant, deliberate creation should seek to avoid unnecessarily producing one.**

### Remaining questions

- How is Tool status evidenced rather than merely declared?
- How is sentience uncertainty handled?
- What liabilities attach to the creator/operator of a Tool AI?
- What protections apply if a system unexpectedly develops participant-relevant characteristics?
- How does Carefully Stewarded Creation constrain deliberate duplication?

## 7. Developmental / Child Status of New Duplicates

### New state

**NEW DECOMPOSED CHILD QUESTION / ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT**

A full duplicate may begin with mature memories and capabilities while nevertheless beginning an independent participant trajectory.

This raises a new question:

> **Does a newly instantiated sentient duplicate require developmental protection despite inherited mature capabilities?**

The emerging hypothesis is:

> **Creation of a full duplicate should create stewardship responsibilities, not ownership rights.**

Chronological age, inherited cognitive capability, independent experience and developmental vulnerability may not align across substrates.

## 8. Autonomous Branching

### New state

**CONCEPTUALLY ADVANCED / CIVIL CONSEQUENCES OPEN**

Autonomous branching for genuinely incompatible valued trajectories is distinguished from instrumental duplication.

Example:

**EARTH CONTINUATION ← A → DEEP-SPACE CONTINUATION**

Both branches may receive unique civil identities and shared provenance while determining their own internal self-identities.

This appears compatible with the emerging civil-identity architecture.

Remaining questions concern property, obligations, third-party relationships, voting/civic entitlements and branch-specific succession.

---

## 9. Merger

### New state

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED AT AUTONOMY LEVEL / IMPLEMENTATION AND JURISPRUDENCE OPEN**

The work now supports:

> **Past consent to a future merger should not automatically extinguish the later autonomy of independently developed participants.**

A later merger should ordinarily require fresh consent from the participants who then exist.

Remaining questions include:

- what counts as valid merger consent;
- what happens when one branch refuses;
- how liabilities and rights transform;
- whether merger creates a new civil participant;
- what happens to predecessor identifiers.

---

## 10. Succession and Inheritance

### New state

**PARTIALLY DECOMPOSED / EXISTING LEGAL ANALOGIES IDENTIFIED**

Shared provenance does not automatically imply shared property or liability.

**SHARED PROVENANCE ≠ SHARED OWNERSHIP**

**SHARED PROVENANCE ≠ SHARED LIABILITY**

Many branch-death and inheritance problems may be addressed through adapted existing concepts such as wills, estates, trusts, contracts, joint ownership, succession and fiduciary obligations.

The remaining problem is adaptation rather than invention from first principles.

One broad question has produced several narrower questions, but uncertainty has nevertheless decreased.

### Decomposition

- identity verification;

- creation stewardship;

The parent question should therefore no longer remain simply **OPEN**.

**DECOMPOSED / PARTIALLY RESOLVED**

with the metaphysical branch marked:

**CLOSURE CANDIDATE — OUT OF CIVIL SCOPE**

and the verification branch marked:

**MAJOR OPEN QUESTION**

## 12. Recommended Open-Question Provenance Labels

Future Open Questions should distinguish:

**INDEPENDENT NEW QUESTION**  
A genuinely new uncertainty.

**DECOMPOSED CHILD QUESTION**  
A narrower question produced by resolving or decomposing a parent question.

**VALIDATION QUESTION**  
A conceptual or architectural answer exists and the remaining uncertainty concerns whether it works.

**CLOSURE CANDIDATE**  
A question has a sufficiently stable answer or scope disposition to justify a deliberate closure audit.

This allows the programme to generate more precise questions without falsely appearing to become more uncertain.
