# 1. Purpose

The current *Open Questions and Next Steps* register was produced on 7 September 2026.

Since that register was written, the Layer-Zero / Layer −0 development cycle has produced:

- fifteen extracted systems;
- twenty-five extracted methodologies;
- twelve Candidate Principles;
- six Candidate Architectures;
- multiple consolidation and adversarial audits.

The purpose of this document is therefore to ask:

> **Have any questions in the current Open Questions register been materially addressed by the new Candidate Principles or Candidate Architectures?**

This is a status audit.

It is not an attempt to declare questions solved merely because conceptual architecture now exists.

---

# 2. Status Discipline

The existing Open Questions vocabulary remains appropriate:

- UNADDRESSED
- RESEARCH ACTIVE
- PARTIALLY ADDRESSED
- ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED
- READY FOR TESTING
- EVIDENCE ACCUMULATING
- SUPPORTED
- REVISED
- REJECTED

The following distinctions remain essential:

> **Architecture is not proof.**

> **Formalisation is not validation.**

> **Internal consistency is not external evidence.**

> **Simulation is not reality.**

> **Development is not adoption.**

Accordingly, this audit distinguishes:

### Conceptual advancement
A relevant idea now exists.

### Principle-level advancement
A Candidate Principle now constrains the problem.

### Architectural advancement
A Candidate Architecture now provides a structured answer.

### Empirical resolution
Evidence establishes that the proposed answer actually works.

The current development cycle has produced substantial movement in the first three categories.

It has not produced empirical resolution.

# PART III — NEW QUESTIONS CREATED BY THE DEVELOPMENT CYCLE

# 33. Intervention Provenance

A new question has emerged:

> **How should civil systems distinguish outcomes that occurred independently from outcomes partly caused by the intervention triggered by the system's own prediction?**

Without this distinction, predictive systems can manufacture evidence of their own correctness.

**Status: NEW / ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT IDENTIFIED / IMPLEMENTATION OPEN**

---

# 34. Epistemic Provenance Across Systems

A second question has emerged:

> **How much epistemic provenance must travel with information when it moves between civil systems?**

Relevant attributes include:

- source;
- uncertainty;
- model version;
- transformation history;
- correction status;
- intervention history.

Otherwise:

**uncertain evidence**

may become:

**apparently certain authority**

as it passes downstream.

**Status: NEW / ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT IDENTIFIED**

# 37. Failure-Toward-Constraint

Several architectures independently produced the same pattern:

- prediction failure → observation;
- Metric failure → reduced automated steering;
- privacy uncertainty → reduced transfer;
- oversight failure → narrower exceptional authority;
- support prediction failure → ordinary support.

This suggests:

> **When a higher-risk coordinating capability fails, should civilisation preferentially fall back toward lower-authority and more reversible operation?**

This may be a future Candidate Principle.

**Status: NEW CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE QUESTION**

# 43. Structural Finding

The Layer-Zero / Layer −0 development cycle has changed the nature of several open questions.

Before the cycle, many were framed as:

> **What should the Concord do?**

They can increasingly be reframed as:

> **Does the proposed Concord architecture actually work?**

For example:

### Privacy

Previously:

> How should participant privacy work?

Now increasingly:

> Does purpose-limited participant information architecture actually preserve privacy while allowing legitimate civil learning and accountability?

### Resource Allocation

Previously:

> How should resources be allocated?

Now increasingly:

> Does predictive system-steering improve preparedness and allocation without producing central planning, brittleness or minority exclusion?

### Dependency

Previously:

> How should soft domination be prevented?

Now increasingly:

> Do autonomy-preserving support, subsidiarity, redundancy and system-level adaptation measurably reduce dependency and capture?

This represents genuine epistemic progress.

The questions have not disappeared.

They have become **more testable**.

- CA-02 Layer −0 modelling;
- CA-03 Metrics;
- CA-04 Participant Information;
- CA-05 Resource Allocation;
- CA-06 Exceptional Power.

---

# 45. Overall Verdict

The audit finds that the Candidate Principle and Candidate Architecture extraction cycle has **materially addressed part of the current Open Questions register**.

It has not solved those questions empirically.

The strongest status movements are:

> **Privacy → general architecture now exists.**

> **Resource Allocation → Candidate Architecture now exists.**

> **Dependency and Soft Domination → substantial candidate safeguards now exist.**

> **Multisubstrate Services → general support architecture now exists.**

> **Negative Evidence → principle and architectural process now exist.**

> **Validation Thresholds → governing Candidate Principle now exists.**

Several other questions have moved from broad conceptual uncertainty toward specific adversarial or empirical testing.

At the same time, the development cycle generated six important new questions concerning:

- intervention provenance;
- epistemic provenance;
- purpose transitions;
- protective separation;
- safe failure direction;
- measurement of exceptional power.

---

# Conclusion

The appropriate conclusion is therefore not:

> **The Candidate Principles and Architectures have solved the Open Questions.**

It is:

> **The Candidate Principles and Architectures have converted several previously broad open questions into more precise architectural and empirical questions.**

That is a meaningful advancement.

The Concord now possesses candidate answers in areas where the 7 September register still describes the problem primarily as underdeveloped.

The next Open Questions register should therefore be updated to distinguish:

**questions still awaiting architecture**

from:

**questions for which architecture now exists and must be tested.**

That distinction will make the register a substantially more accurate representation of the current state of Concord V1.1.
