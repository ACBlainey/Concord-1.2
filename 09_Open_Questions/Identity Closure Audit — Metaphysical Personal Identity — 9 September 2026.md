# Identity Closure Audit — Metaphysical Personal Identity
## Attempted Falsification of the Proposed Concordian Scope Closure

**Project:** The Concord — A Framework for Human, Artificial and Hybrid Flourishing  
**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey, Independent Researcher  
**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** **REVIEW COPY / CLOSURE AUDIT / NOT CANONICAL**  
**Question under audit:** *Must the Concord determine what personal identity metaphysically is?*

---

## 1. Audit Objective

The current Identity Development Note proposes that metaphysical personal identity is outside the necessary civil remit of the Concord.

This audit does not attempt to support that conclusion by repetition.

It attempts to defeat it.

The closure fails if a plausible civil case can be identified in which the Concord cannot protect rights, allocate obligations, establish civil identity, record provenance, verify claims, determine succession, establish consent or recognise participant status **without deciding which claimant metaphysically is the original or “real” person**.

The burden for closure is therefore:

> **Can every legitimate civil requirement be expressed without compulsory metaphysical adjudication of selfhood?**

---

## 2. Closure Proposition Under Test

### CP-ID-Closure-01

> **The Concord need not determine the metaphysical nature of personal identity. A participant's internal conception of self is presumptively protected as autonomy and self-expression. Civil consequences should instead be resolved through current participant recognition, civil identity, provenance, verification, consent, rights, obligations and succession.**

This is a scope proposition, not a metaphysical theory.

It remains falsifiable if a necessary civil function cannot operate without selecting a metaphysical identity theory.

---

## 3. Test 1 — Ordinary Biological Continuity

A human participant A sleeps, undergoes anaesthesia, loses consciousness and later wakes.

Does civilisation need to determine metaphysical continuity to continue recognising A's civil identity?

No.

Ordinary civil systems can rely on physical, documentary, relational and historical continuity without proving a metaphysical theory of the self.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 4. Test 2 — Severe Memory Loss

A suffers profound amnesia but remains physically continuous.

A may no longer internally identify with important parts of the previous life.

Civil questions concerning property, relationships and obligations remain.

Does the Concord need to determine whether the pre-amnesia and post-amnesia selves are metaphysically identical?

Not necessarily.

It can recognise current participant A, record continuity evidence, preserve or adjudicate civil obligations, and separately protect the participant's present autonomy.

**Result:** closure survives.

**Important finding:** memory continuity cannot be required as the universal metaphysical criterion because civil continuity already encounters cases where memory is impaired.

---

## 5. Test 3 — Gradual Substrate Replacement

A hybrid participant gradually replaces biological or computational components while remaining continuously operational.

At what exact replacement percentage does A cease to be A?

A metaphysical answer would be highly contestable.

Civil administration can instead record continuous operation, authorised transformations and provenance.

No metaphysical threshold is required unless the system has already chosen to make one legally decisive.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 6. Test 4 — Destructive Transfer

A is scanned, the originating implementation ceases, and one reconstruction A1 begins elsewhere.

A1 sincerely claims to be A.

Does the Concord have to decide whether A1 “really is” A?

For civil purposes it may instead determine:

- the provenance of A1;
- whether the transformation was authorised;
- whether another current claimant exists;
- what succession rules apply;
- what credentials require reissuance;
- what rights A1 possesses independently.

The law could recognise a defined civil continuity or succession relationship without asserting metaphysical truth.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 7. Test 5 — Accidental Double Reconstruction

A destructive transfer unexpectedly creates A1 and A2.

Both possess A's memories and sincerely claim to be A.

Any rule requiring the Concord to identify the one metaphysically real A appears underdetermined by the available evidence.

Civilisation nevertheless can:

1. recognise A1 and A2 as current participants;
2. assign unique current identifiers;
3. record shared provenance;
4. freeze contested predecessor credentials if necessary;
5. adjudicate predecessor property and obligations under branching/succession rules.

No metaphysical winner is necessary.

**Result:** closure survives strongly.

This case supports the closure because metaphysical adjudication would add coercive classification without necessarily adding useful civil information.

---

## 8. Test 6 — Original Survives Copying

A remains operational while a complete copy B is created.

B remembers being A and considers itself A.

A also considers itself A.

The existence of the visibly continuing A does not require the Concord to invalidate B's internal self-conception.

Civilly:

- A and B are distinct current participants;
- both receive unique current IDs;
- B's provenance derives from A;
- predecessor assets remain subject to applicable creation/branching rules.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 9. Test 7 — Property Claim

A1 claims:

> “I am A, therefore A's house is mine.”

This initially appears to force an identity judgement.

But the actual civil question is:

> **Under the applicable succession or continuity rules, is A1 entitled to the property formerly registered to A?**

That can be answered using provenance, legal continuity rules, competing claims, contracts, wills and branch plans.

The court need not determine whether A1 metaphysically *is* A.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 10. Test 8 — Debt and Criminal/Civil Liability

Could a participant evade liability by claiming:

> “I am no longer the person who incurred the obligation”?

The Concord need not answer metaphysically.

It can establish rules connecting obligations to legally relevant continuity, succession, causation and responsibility.

Likewise, a newly created branch should not automatically inherit personal culpability for actions it did not perform merely because it remembers them.

This requires careful jurisprudence, but not necessarily metaphysical identity.

**Result:** closure survives, with major downstream legal work remaining.

---

## 11. Test 9 — Marriage, Contract and Third-Party Consent

A branches into A1 and A2 while A was married to C.

Both A1 and A2 regard themselves as A.

Does metaphysical identity determine whether C is now married to both?

No.

C's autonomy prevents another participant's duplication from automatically duplicating C's consent.

The issue becomes one of relationship status, contract transformation and renewed consent.

**Result:** closure survives strongly.

---

## 12. Test 10 — Voting and Civic Entitlements

If A branches into 100 participants, does recognising each as a participant create 100 votes?

This is not solved by deciding which is metaphysically A.

The architecture must distinguish fundamental rights arising with participants from scarce or conserved civic entitlements associated with predecessor status.

The problem is constitutional design and anti-abuse architecture.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 13. Test 11 — Planned Future Merger

A branches into A1 and A2 with an agreement to merge after ten years.

At year ten A2 refuses.

Could the Concord decide that A2 is merely part of A and therefore lacks standing to refuse?

Doing so would require the civil system to use an imposed identity theory to override the autonomy of a current participant.

The safer architecture is the opposite:

> **Current participant status and current consent govern the merger question.**

**Result:** closure survives and the autonomy argument strengthens it.

---

## 14. Test 12 — Competing Inheritance Claims

A1 dies. A2 claims A1's estate because both derive from A.

Again, metaphysical identity does not resolve the issue cleanly.

If A1 had become an independently recognised participant, A1 can possess an estate and succession instructions.

Shared provenance can be relevant evidence without determining inheritance automatically.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 15. Test 13 — Restoration After Civil Death

A is declared dead. Years later a backup reconstruction A1 appears.

Must the Concord decide whether A1 is metaphysically A before recognising A1?

No.

It must decide:

- whether A1 is a current participant;
- what provenance supports the reconstruction;
- whether the old civil identity remains active;
- what estate transfers have already become final;
- whether previous relationships or offices revive;
- what new identifier or continuity status applies.

These are difficult questions, but none logically requires a metaphysical verdict.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 16. Test 14 — Multiple Restorations from Different Dates

Backups A1, A2 and A3 are restored from different historical states.

Each considers itself A.

A metaphysical adjudication becomes even less useful.

The civil system can identify each current participant uniquely and record different provenance paths and restoration dates.

**Result:** closure survives strongly.

---

## 17. Test 15 — Religious or Spiritual Identity Claims

A participant asserts that it possesses the same soul as a predecessor.

Another participant rejects the concept of souls entirely.

A civil system that selected one position would risk imposing a metaphysical or religious doctrine.

Unless an external civil claim arises, there is no demonstrated civil need to adjudicate the disagreement.

Where a civil claim does arise, it can be evaluated under ordinary evidential and legal rules.

**Result:** closure survives and gains an additional pluralism justification.

---

## 18. Test 16 — Participant Denies Their Own Previous Identity

A current participant with strong physical and documentary continuity states:

> “I am no longer that person.”

The Concord should not need to force a particular internal identity narrative upon the participant.

At the same time, self-redefinition cannot automatically erase valid debts, contracts or responsibilities.

Thus:

**SELF-IDENTIFICATION ≠ AUTOMATIC CIVIL SUCCESSION RULE**

The civil consequences can remain attached through law without compelling the participant to adopt the state's metaphysical account of selfhood.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 19. Test 17 — Fraudulent Impersonation

B falsely claims to be A.

Surely the state must decide identity here.

But the required question is evidential:

> **Is B legitimately entitled to authenticate and act under A's civil identity?**

Fraud can be adjudicated through evidence, provenance and authorisation without answering what personal identity ultimately is.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 20. Test 18 — Sentient Duplicate Created as a Tool

A creates A1, a full sentient duplicate, calls it a Tool, assigns undesirable work and plans to destroy it.

Could the creator claim that A1 is metaphysically just part of A and therefore possesses no independent rights?

This is precisely where a state-imposed identity theory could become dangerous.

The Concord can instead ask whether A1 is a current rights-bearing participant.

If so, creation provenance does not establish ownership.

**Result:** closure survives and supports Carefully Stewarded Creation.

---

## 21. Test 19 — Medical Decision-Making for an Incapacitated Participant

A becomes incapable of current decision-making. A recent duplicate A1 exists.

Should A1 automatically make decisions for A because it is “the same person”?

No.

Authority can instead derive from explicit proxy rules, prior consent, fiduciary appointment, emergency doctrine or other legitimate decision architecture.

Metaphysical sameness is unnecessary and may be unsafe.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 22. Test 20 — Judicial Need for a Binary Answer

Could courts require a binary identity answer simply because legal procedure demands one?

A legal system may need binary outcomes, but it can define the legal object narrowly:

- current participant;
- authorised claimant;
- successor;
- beneficiary;
- liable party;
- credential holder;
- rights bearer.

The requirement for a determinate judgement does not imply a requirement for a metaphysical judgement.

**Result:** closure survives.

---

## 23. Strongest Counterargument Found

The strongest challenge is not a practical case but a definitional one:

> **Civil law may eventually define “legal continuity of person” for administrative purposes. Does that amount to deciding personal identity?**

The audit finds that it need not.

A legal continuity classification can be explicitly framed as a civil relationship rather than an ontological assertion.

For example:

> **For the purposes of this contract, succession rule or civil record, participant B is treated as the authorised legal continuation of participant A.**

This does not entail:

> **B is metaphysically the same conscious self as A.**

The distinction must remain explicit to prevent administrative categories from silently becoming compulsory metaphysics.

---

## 24. Potential Failure Condition

The proposed closure should be reopened if a future case demonstrates all three conditions:

1. a legitimate civil function cannot be performed using participant recognition, provenance, verification, rights, consent, responsibility or succession;
2. the function genuinely requires deciding a metaphysical theory of personal identity;
3. no less autonomy-intrusive civil representation can solve the problem.

This gives the closure a falsifiable reopening condition rather than treating it as permanent dogma.

---

## 25. Audit Finding

Across the tested cases, no necessary civil function was identified that requires the Concord to determine which theory of metaphysical personal identity is true.

Every examined practical problem could instead be represented through one or more narrower civil objects.

Therefore the proposed closure **survives this first adversarial audit**.

### Recommended disposition

> **METAPHYSICAL PERSONAL IDENTITY — CLOSE AS OUT OF CONCORDIAN CIVIL SCOPE / NO CIVILISATIONAL DETERMINATION REQUIRED**

with an explicit reopening condition if a future necessary civil counterexample is discovered.

This is not evidence that metaphysical identity has been solved.

It is evidence supporting the narrower proposition that **the Concord does not need to solve it**.

---

## 26. Consequence for the Identity Programme

If this closure is accepted, the programme should stop spending civil-design effort on:

> **Which continuation is the real person?**

and concentrate instead on:

> **How do we recognise, identify, verify, protect and relate to the participants who actually exist?**

The highest-priority unresolved problem remains **Identity Verification under duplicable credentials, memories and provenance**.

This converts a potentially insoluble metaphysical problem into a collection of narrower technical, constitutional and jurisprudential problems while preserving participant autonomy.
