# 3. Major Change Since the Previous Open Questions Update

The most important development since the previous update is the emergence of a substantially clearer theory of **civil authority**.

The relevant chain is now approximately:

\[
EthicalKernel
\rightarrow
Rights
\rightarrow
RightsRelevantProblem
\rightarrow
LegitimateCivilObjective
\rightarrow
LegitimateFunction
\rightarrow
FunctionalNeed
\rightarrow
MinimumProportionateAuthority
\rightarrow
BoundedContextualAuthority
\]

This materially changes the authority question.

The problem is no longer simply:

> **Who should possess authority?**

The stronger question is:

> **What legitimate function requires this power now, why is the function legitimate, why does it require authority, and what is the minimum proportionate authority necessary to perform it?**

This produces a direction of justification:

\[
Legitimacy \rightarrow Function \rightarrow Need \rightarrow Authority
\]

rather than:

\[
Authority \rightarrow Function \rightarrow Legitimacy
\]

and establishes several important negative propositions:

\[
AuthorityExists \not\Rightarrow FunctionIsLegitimate
\]

\[
AuthorityExists \not\Rightarrow AuthorityIsNecessary
\]

\[
PastAuthority \neq PresentNeed
\]

\[
TechnicalCapability \neq CivilAuthority
\]

\[
Role \neq PermanentUniversalPrivilege
\]

This does not solve authority.

It changes the open problem from **inventing a general basis for authority** to **testing the legitimacy, evidence, proportionality and meta-governance of the emerging basis**.

# 4. Authority — Revised Status

## Previous state

Authority was distributed across Governance, emergency architecture, proportionality, exceptional access, oversight, refusal and anti-capture questions.

## Current state

Bounded Contextual Authority now supplies a candidate general operator:

> **Authority should attach to a legitimate function within a defined context rather than to status alone.**

The Authority Justification Chain supplies the missing upstream direction:

> **Authority should primarily derive from a legitimate functional need. Authority itself does not make a function legitimate and does not prove that the authority is necessary.**

The BCA adversarial audit further establishes that BCA cannot operate alone. It depends on:

\[
EthicalKernel + Rights + LegitimacyRules + Proportionality + Evidence + ConflictResolution + Review + Provenance
\]

## Revised disposition

**ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED AT GENERAL PRINCIPLE LEVEL / DIRECT FALSIFICATION AND META-AUTHORITY QUESTIONS OPEN**

The broad question “what is authority based on?” should therefore be retired in favour of more precise questions.

### AQ-01 — Legitimate Function

> **What makes a proposed civil function legitimate independently of the authority or institution that wishes to perform it?**

**Status:** HIGH-PRIORITY OPEN QUESTION / INITIAL ARCHITECTURE EXISTS.

### AQ-02 — Functional Need Evidence

> **What evidence is sufficient to establish that a legitimate function is actually needed?**

**Status:** HIGH-PRIORITY OPEN QUESTION.

### AQ-03 — Authority Necessity

> **When does performing a legitimate function genuinely require special authority rather than ordinary consent, voluntary coordination, public access or information?**

**Status:** DECOMPOSED / ARCHITECTURAL TEST EXISTS / VALIDATION OPEN.

### AQ-04 — Minimum Proportionate Authority

> **How can the minimum reasonable authority required for a function be identified without making the function ineffective?**

**Status:** OPEN / FORMALISATION AND ADVERSARIAL TESTING REQUIRED.

### AQ-05 — Current Justification

> **What evidence demonstrates that a previously legitimate authority remains necessary now?**

**Status:** ARCHITECTURALLY IDENTIFIED / OPERATIONAL TEST REQUIRED.

### AQ-06 — Meta-Authority

> **Who or what legitimately determines that a function exists, continues, expands or conflicts with another function without allowing that validating authority itself to become captured?**

**Status:** MAJOR OPEN QUESTION.

This is currently the deepest unresolved problem exposed by BCA.

# 5. Functional Need — New Decomposition

The newly preserved sketch on bottom-up functional need adds a significant upstream question:

> **Where does the evidence that a civil function is needed originate?**

A provisional distinction has emerged.

## 5.1 Ordinary civil function

Where practicable:

\[
ParticipantNeeds
\rightarrow
RightsRelevantProblem
\rightarrow
LegitimateCivilObjective
\rightarrow
LegitimateFunction
\]

This is a bottom-up Layer 0 route.

## 5.2 Civilisation-level protective function

Some system-level threats may be detected from a higher architectural level:

\[
SystemLevelThreat
\rightarrow
ProtectiveObjective
\rightarrow
ProtectiveFunction
\]

but the legitimacy of that function should still return to:

\[
EthicalKernel + Rights + ParticipantGoods
\]

Thus:

> **Top-down threat recognition does not imply top-down moral legitimacy.**

## Revised status

**NEWLY DECOMPOSED / SKETCH ARCHITECTURE EXISTS / REQUIRES ADVERSARIAL EXAMINATION**

The major derived questions are:

### FN-01 — Need Discovery

How can participant needs be identified without building intrusive surveillance?

### FN-02 — Need Versus Preference

What distinguishes a rights-relevant need from preference, demand, convenience or optimisation opportunity?

### FN-03 — Unexpressed Need

How should legitimate needs be represented for participants who cannot presently identify or communicate them?

### FN-04 — Aggregation

How should conflicting participant goods be aggregated without collapsing into average-based policy?

### FN-05 — Civilisational Protective Function

What qualifies as a legitimate civilisation-level protective function rather than institutional self-preservation?

### FN-06 — Exceptional Threat Recognition

Who may identify war, black-swan or existential threats, and what evidence is required?

### FN-07 — Rights Under Existential Pressure

Which rights, if any, may be temporarily restricted under genuine civilisation-level threat, and which should remain non-derogable?

These questions should remain separate from ordinary Governance design for now.

# 7. Civil Space — Major Architectural Advance

The civil-space work now distinguishes three primary classes:

\[
CivilSpace
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
PublicSpace\\
ProtectedSpace\\
RestrictedSpace
\end{cases}
\]

with Unclassified/Transitional as a temporary epistemic or administrative state rather than an ordinary fourth class.

Important distinctions now include:

\[
AccessClass \neq Ownership \neq Provision \neq Purpose \neq CurrentState
\]

\[
FreedomOfMovement \neq UniversalRightOfEntry
\]

\[
Protected \rightarrow BoundaryControl
\]

\[
Restricted \rightarrow ConditionedEligibility
\]

and:

> **Restricted Space is not fundamentally a category of exclusion. It is a category of justified conditional access.**

## Revised disposition

**ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED / CLASSIFICATION VALIDATION AND EDGE CASES OPEN**

The old broad question of how safe/public/private space should work should be retired in favour of narrower tests:

### CS-01 — Classification Reliability

Can independent users classify real spaces consistently using the three-class architecture?

### CS-02 — Nested Spaces

Does the architecture remain understandable where Public, Protected and Restricted spaces are nested?

### CS-03 — Dynamic State

Can temporary occupancy and state changes be represented without unnecessary reclassification?

### CS-04 — Hazard Compatibility

Can hazard-based restrictions remain participant-neutral while still accounting for individual capability?

### CS-05 — Digital and Hybrid Space

Does the architecture generalise to digital, virtual, distributed and mixed physical/digital environments?

### CS-06 — Classification Capture

Can authorities manipulate classification to produce de facto exclusion while claiming merely to classify space?

# 24. Questions That Should Not Be Immediate Priorities

This does not mean these questions are unimportant.

They should remain open, but immediate work may have lower dependency value:

- complete metaphysical theories of identity;
- universal theory of artificial subjective experience;
- speculative artificial leisure without experimental design;
- full economy redesign before authority and participation foundations stabilise;
- civilisation-scale implementation detail before core architectures survive falsification;
- additional Governance expansion before authority legitimacy is better tested.

This reflects the Concordian principle of finding the correct layer.

> **Before asking which answer is correct, establish what question is actually being answered.**

# 25. Recommended Immediate Research Question

The strongest immediate question is:

> **Can the Authority Justification Chain survive direct adversarial attack without allowing institutions, authorities or validators to manufacture the conditions that justify their own power?**

This question is preferable to immediately extending BCA because BCA already survived a first broad adversarial audit.

The unresolved vulnerability is upstream.

The chain may still fail if:

- the observed condition is selectively described;
- a non-civil problem is declared rights-relevant;
- institutional interest is laundered into a civil objective;
- the objective is legitimate but the chosen function is not necessary;
- the function is legitimate but special authority is unnecessary;
- “minimum authority” is defined by the authority holder;
- the validator is captured;
- repeated locally proportionate decisions create systemic domination.

The next research paper should therefore be a **direct falsification audit of the Authority Justification Chain**.

Its purpose should not be to defend the chain.

Its purpose should be to try to break it.
