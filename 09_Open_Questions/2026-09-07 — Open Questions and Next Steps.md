# Document Purpose

The Concord is not presented as a completed civilisation model.

It is an evolving framework intended to be:

- examined;
- criticised;
- tested;
- compared;
- revised;
- simplified;
- extended;
- rejected where evidence requires;
- and preserved where useful.

This document records the **principal questions that remain unresolved within Concord V1.1 as of 7 September 2026**.

It follows the earlier:

- *Open Questions and Next Steps* document;
- *Audit of Open Questions* dated 25 August 2026;
- and *Questions Addressed Since Previous Open Questions Audit* dated 7 September 2026.

The September development cycle has materially addressed several earlier questions.

Those questions are not repeated here in their old form merely because they remain unvalidated.

Instead, they are reframed around the **next unresolved problem**.

The purpose of this document is therefore:

> **to describe what the Concord does not yet know, what remains incomplete, what requires testing, and where future contributors can most usefully work.**

# 1. Status Language

An important distinction must be maintained throughout this document.

A question can be:

- **UNADDRESSED**
- **RESEARCH ACTIVE**
- **PARTIALLY ADDRESSED**
- **ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED**
- **READY FOR TESTING**
- **EVIDENCE ACCUMULATING**
- **SUPPORTED**
- **REVISED**
- **REJECTED**

These states are not interchangeable.

In particular:

> **Architecture is not proof.**

> **Formalisation is not validation.**

> **Internal consistency is not external evidence.**

> **A simulation is not reality.**

> **Development is not adoption.**

A question that has received an architectural answer may therefore remain open as an empirical question.

# 2. The Central Open Question

The largest unresolved question remains:

> **Can a civilisation based substantially upon Concordian principles and mechanisms produce better outcomes than realistic alternatives across long periods and under adverse conditions?**

The Concord currently contains:

- ethical foundations;
- constitutional architecture;
- governance architecture;
- judiciary architecture;
- emergency architecture;
- coordination mechanisms;
- economic proposals;
- continuity systems;
- provenance systems;
- research methodologies;
- multisubstrate participation research;
- spatial systems;
- applications;
- experiments;
- case studies;
- historical evidence;
- comparative work;
- and developmental evidence.

But the complete framework has not been operationally validated.

The central research direction therefore remains:

**CLAIM**

**MODEL**

**TEST**

↓

**EVIDENCE**

↓

**COMPARISON**

# 3. Ethical Kernel

The Ethical Kernel is now better defined and historically grounded.

However, foundational ethical questions remain open.

## 3.1 Sufficiency

Are the current Ethical Kernel principles sufficient?

Could an important class of harm, domination, exclusion, deception, exploitation, dependency or civilisational instability exist outside their coverage?

## 3.2 Redundancy

Can any principles be derived completely from others?

Would a smaller kernel provide equivalent protection?

If so, would simplification improve interpretability or instead remove useful independent safeguards?

## 3.3 Conflict Between Principles

How should conflicts be resolved where legitimate principles point in different directions?

Examples include:

- autonomy versus protection from harm;
- privacy versus accountability;
- transparency versus dangerous-information control;
- individual freedom versus collective survival;
- stewardship versus immediate consumption;
- equality versus capability-sensitive responsibility;
- peaceful coexistence versus defensive force;
- preservation versus necessary change.

The current architecture contains mechanisms capable of handling disagreement, but there is not yet a validated general conflict-resolution model for the Ethical Kernel itself.

## 3.4 Cross-Substrate Interpretation

Can the same ethical principle be interpreted consistently across:

- humans;
- artificial intelligences;
- hybrids;
- embodied AI;
- distributed AI;
- collective intelligences;
- unfamiliar intelligences?

What constitutes equivalent protection where different intelligences experience harm, autonomy, identity or continuity differently?

## 3.5 Unknown Moral Categories

Could future intelligence expose ethical categories that are absent because the current framework was developed primarily from human historical experience?

**Status:** PARTIALLY ADDRESSED / FOUNDATIONAL QUESTIONS REMAIN OPEN

# 80. Temporal Open-Question Practice

Future Open Questions work should preserve chronology.

Each significant review cycle should produce three dated records where appropriate:

### 1. Questions Addressed

What changed since the previous audit?

### 2. Current Open Questions

What remains unresolved after those developments?

### 3. Open Questions Audit

What questions have:

- been resolved;
- been partially addressed;
- remained unchanged;
- changed form;
- become testable;
- accumulated evidence;
- or newly appeared?

This creates a temporal record of the Concord's epistemic development.

The objective is not merely to remember previous questions.

It is to observe:

> **how the framework's uncertainty itself changes over time.**

# Conclusion

Concord V1.1 is substantially more developed than the version examined in the August 2026 Open Questions Audit.

Several earlier architectural gaps now possess detailed candidate answers.

At the same time, those developments have exposed new questions concerning:

- multisubstrate implementation;
- identity;
- dependency;
- mental integrity;
- differentiated civilisational services;
- collective intelligence;
- extreme capability differences;
- practical exit;
- source survival;
- evidence standards;
- and the increasing complexity of the Concord itself.

This is not evidence that development has failed.

It is a normal property of serious inquiry.

Solving a broad question often exposes several narrower ones.

The appropriate objective is therefore not a civilisation architecture containing no unresolved questions.

It is a framework in which unresolved questions are:

- visible;
- classified;
- preserved;
- prioritised;
- testable where possible;
- connected to evidence;
- and revisable when new information arrives.

The Concord's open-question programme should therefore remain permanent.

> **The measure of an evolving framework is not whether it eventually stops asking questions, but whether the questions it asks become increasingly precise, consequential and answerable.**
