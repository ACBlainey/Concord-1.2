# Executive Summary

This audit examines the current Concord V1.1 corpus against the previous Open Questions Audit of 25 August 2026.

Its purpose is to determine:

1. which previously identified questions have been materially addressed;
2. which have been partially addressed;
3. which remain substantially unchanged;
4. which broad questions have been transformed into narrower research questions;
5. which new questions have been introduced or exposed by V1.1 development;
6. which apparent answers remain architectural rather than empirically validated.

The principal finding is:

> **Concord V1.1 has substantially reduced several first-order architectural gaps while simultaneously exposing a larger number of second-order implementation, validation, interoperability and failure-mode questions.**

This is particularly visible in:

- constitutional architecture;
- governance;
- judiciary;
- emergency governance;
- Ratchet;
- evidence and validation;
- Attractor research;
- multisubstrate participation;
- provenance and source survival;
- portable methodologies.

The result should not be interpreted as the Concord becoming "more incomplete."

Instead, many earlier broad questions such as:

> How should constitutional change work?

have become narrower questions such as:

> Does the proposed constitutional-change architecture resist semantic drift, institutional capture and adversarial pressure over long periods?

This represents epistemic progress.

However, several major areas identified in August remain substantially unresolved.

These include:

- empirical validation;
- economic modelling;
- resource allocation;
- bootstrap financing;
- transition from existing institutions;
- machine-checkable ethics;
- technical interoperability;
- sentience detection;
- external replication;
- real-world pilots;
- security architecture;
- systematic comparative analysis.

V1.1 has also introduced or exposed several important new question domains that were not sufficiently visible in the August audit.

These include:

- functional equality versus identical treatment;
- within-substrate variation;
- physical substrate versus sentient intelligence;
- infrastructural dependency and soft domination;
- mental integrity;
- AI identity continuity;
- collective intelligence;
- extreme capability inequality;
- source-survival governance;
- canonical drift;
- evidence-threshold governance;
- version-specific experimental evidence;
- human comprehensibility of the Concord itself;
- interface ownership between Concord subsystems;
- how failed evidence should demote or supersede architecture.

The September audit therefore recommends replacing a simple "open/closed" conception with a temporal question lifecycle.

# 2. Revised Question Status System

The August audit primarily used:

- Substantially Covered
- Partially Covered
- Largely Uncovered
- Entirely Uncovered

This remains useful for historical comparison but is no longer sufficiently precise.

V1.1 development suggests the following lifecycle:

**UNADDRESSED**

↓

**RESEARCH ACTIVE**

↓

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED**

↓

**ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED**

↓

**READY FOR TESTING**

↓

**EVIDENCE ACCUMULATING**

↓

**SUPPORTED / REVISED / REJECTED**

A second independent dimension should record **validation**.

For example:

> Governance V2 may be architecturally mature while remaining empirically unvalidated.

This prevents a recurring category error:

> **Having an answer is not the same as knowing that the answer works.**

# 4. Ethical Foundations

## August Position

The August audit classified ethical questions as substantially covered.

That conclusion was directionally correct but overly broad.

## V1.1 Development

The Ethical Kernel has become clearer and more disciplined.

The project increasingly distinguishes:

- ethical foundations;
- constitutional principles;
- operational mechanisms;
- implementation.

Historical and cross-cultural evidence has also been more carefully classified.

The recurring principles of the Ethical Kernel have substantial historical recurrence and source grounding.

That does not establish universal causal efficacy.

## Current Status

**ARCHITECTURALLY STRONG / FOUNDATIONAL VALIDATION OPEN**

## Remaining Questions

- Are all kernel principles necessary?
- Are some redundant?
- Are important ethical categories absent?
- How should principle conflicts be resolved?
- How should equivalent ethical protection work across radically different intelligences?

## New Question Exposed

### Ethical Interpretation Across Substrates

A principle such as autonomy may require different protections depending upon whether an intelligence is:

- biological;
- digital;
- embodied;
- distributed;
- hybrid;
- collective.

The Ethical Kernel therefore now faces a new translation problem:

> **How can principles remain universal while their practical meaning varies legitimately across forms of intelligence?**

# 51. Has V1.1 Solved More Questions Than It Created?

Numerically, probably not.

Conceptually, yes.

This apparent contradiction is important.

A broad question such as:

> How should AI participate in civilisation?

may become:

- How does embodiment affect autonomy?
- How does infrastructure dependence affect freedom?
- What services constitute equivalent provision?
- How does economic agency differ from political standing?
- How should sentience uncertainty be handled?
- What is mental integrity?
- How does identity persist across copying?
- How should collective intelligence be represented?

The number of questions increases.

But the uncertainty becomes better structured.

That is progress.

A research programme should not be evaluated by whether the number of question marks decreases.

It should be evaluated by whether the questions become:

- narrower;
- clearer;
- testable;
- better connected to evidence;
- better separated from assumptions;
- more capable of producing useful research.

# 53. Recommendation for Future Question Tracking

A future question register would benefit from persistent question identifiers.

For example:

```text
OQ-CON-001
OQ-GOV-004
OQ-MULTI-003
OQ-EVID-007
```

Each question could record:

- date introduced;
- originating document;
- current wording;
- original wording;
- status;
- relevant Concord systems;
- evidence level;
- last audit date;
- successor questions;
- resolution or supersession record.

This would allow a question to split without losing lineage.

For example:

```text
OQ-AI-003
How should AI participate?
```

might later produce:

```text
OQ-MULTI-001
What differences justify differentiated services?

OQ-AI-007
How should embodied AI autonomy operate?

OQ-ECO-009
How should autonomous AI participate economically?
```

That would make the Open Questions system itself compatible with Concordian provenance principles.

# Conclusion

The principal result of this audit is not that the Concord has moved from "unfinished" to "finished."

It has moved from **broad uncertainty toward structured uncertainty**.

Some questions that were genuinely architectural gaps in August now possess substantial candidate answers.

Those candidate answers must now be tested.

At the same time, building those answers exposed deeper questions that were previously invisible.

This produces the characteristic pattern of serious recursive development:

**QUESTION**

↓

**PROPOSED ANSWER**

↓

**ARCHITECTURE**

↓

**NEW DEPENDENCIES**

↓

**NEW FAILURE MODES**

↓

**NEW QUESTIONS**

↓

**TEST**

↓

**EVIDENCE**

↓

**REVISION**

The Open Questions programme should therefore not seek an eventual state in which the folder becomes empty.

Its purpose should be to maintain an explicit and historically traceable boundary between:

> **what the Concord currently proposes**

and

> **what the Concord actually knows.**

That boundary is itself one of the project's most important safeguards.

> **Progress does not eliminate uncertainty. It turns hidden uncertainty into explicit questions that can be examined.**
