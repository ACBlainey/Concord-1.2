## 1. Purpose

This review evaluates the Development Notes and Sketch work produced after the existing **Open Questions Audit — Concord V1.1 — 9 September 2026** against the Concord's open-question register.

It does not replace either the 8 September register or the earlier 9 September audit. Its purpose is to identify what has materially changed.

The principal new or newly advanced material considered is:

- **Identity, Continuity, Provenance and Verification Across Substrates**
- **Civilisation, Non-Conformity and Peaceful Heterogeneity — Edge-Case Investigation**
- **Peaceful Heterogeneity — Rights, Harm and Bounded Authority — Synthesis and Development Map**
- **PH-01 — Boxing: Bounded Consent and Contextual Harm**
- **PH-02 — Emergency Governance and Emergency Operations**
- expanded **Architectural Ethics and Upstream Liability**
- the developing **Heterogeneous AI Experimental Connectivity** programme
- the remaining Simulation, Peaceful Heterogeneity and AI Gym Sketch portfolio context.

## 2. Executive Finding

The strongest change is that three areas previously recorded mainly as open questions have now acquired enough internal structure to become distinct development programmes:

1. **Cross-Substrate Identity and Continuity**
2. **Peaceful Heterogeneity / Bounded Consent / Contextual Harm**
3. **Emergency Governance and Emergency Operations**

The change is not empirical validation. It is movement from broad conceptual uncertainty toward explicit object models, classifications, candidate architectures and testable edge cases.

The research-state transition is approximately:

**OPEN QUESTION → SKETCH → EDGE CASE → HIDDEN DISTINCTION → FORMAL OBJECTS → CANDIDATE ARCHITECTURE → TESTABLE CASE MATRIX**

This materially changes the status of several entries in the open-question register.

# Part I — Open Questions Materially Advanced

## 3. Identity Across Copying, Forking and Migration

### Previous State

The 9 September audit explicitly listed:

- identity across copying, forking and migration;
- death, deletion and restoration;

among questions **not materially advanced**.

### New Development

The Identity Development Note now separates:

**PARTICIPANT ≠ EXTERNAL IDENTITY ≠ SELF-IDENTITY ≠ AUTHENTICATION ≠ PROVENANCE ≠ CONTINUITY ≠ SUCCESSION ≠ PERSONHOOD ≠ RIGHTS**

It introduces:

- a participant-provenance graph \(G=(V,E)\);
- transformation classes including CONTINUE, BRANCH, COPY, RECONSTRUCT, TRANSFER, RESTORE, MERGE and TERMINATE;
- participant-state representation;
- branching and merger operators;
- an attribute-transformation taxonomy;
- planned branching;
- re-keying and identity assignment;
- separation of identity from succession;
- credential-duplication failure cases;
- identity assurance as evidence rather than identity itself;
- multidimensional continuity relationships;
- a proposed six-layer civil architecture.

### Revised Assessment

**Previous:** FUNDAMENTALLY / SUBSTANTIALLY OPEN — NOT MATERIALLY ADVANCED  
**Now:** **ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / FORMALISATION BEGUN / OPERATIONAL DECOMPOSITION ACHIEVED / LEGAL AND EMPIRICAL VALIDATION OPEN**

The metaphysical identity problem remains unresolved, but the Concord no longer needs to solve it before beginning civil and technical modelling.

### Open Questions Newly Exposed

- What evidence establishes that two apparently identical claimants are one continuation, two branches or an impostor relationship?
- How should hidden duplication be detected?
- Which rights arise independently with each participant and which civic entitlements are conserved?
- How do liabilities transform under branching and merger?
- Can a branch refuse planned recombination?
- What happens to third-party relationships whose consent cannot be duplicated?
- What constitutes termination when restoration or surviving branches remain possible?
- How should voting, taxation, labour and fiduciary authority behave under branching?

## 6. Peaceful Heterogeneity and Non-Conformity

### Previous State

The 9 September audit recorded:

> How can a civilisation remain stable in the presence of persistent behavioural and value heterogeneity without converting stability into enforced conformity?

Status:

**NEW FOUNDATIONAL OPEN QUESTION / SKETCH IDEA / REQUIRES DEVELOPMENT**

### New Development

This question has now moved decisively beyond Sketch status.

The starting presumption is:

\[
AUTONOMOUS\ VARIATION \rightarrow PERMITTED
\]

rather than conformity as the permission baseline.

The investigation has separated three development paths:

1. General Rights, Autonomy and Peaceful Heterogeneity
2. Harm, Consent and Permissibility
3. Emergency Governance and Operations

The emerging minimum common boundary concerns:

- non-consensual external harm;
- rights conflicts;
- validity and scope of consent;
- coercion;
- practical exit;
- proportionality;
- legitimate bounded authority;
- constitutional constraints.

### Revised Assessment

**Previous:** NEW FOUNDATIONAL OPEN QUESTION / SKETCH IDEA  
**Now:** **ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / FOUNDATIONAL DECOMPOSITION ACHIEVED / CANDIDATE BOUNDARY CONDITIONS IDENTIFIED / TESTING AND ADVERSARIAL EXAMINATION REQUIRED**

A potentially important emerging proposition is:

> **A heterogeneous civilisation may require less agreement about how participants should live than agreement about how participants may treat one another and how power may legitimately be exercised.**

This is not yet a Concordian Principle.

## 9. Ethical Kernel — Autonomy versus Protection

The existing register already identified Autonomy versus Protection as partially constrained.

PH-01 adds a new lower-level distinction:

\[
PERMISSIBILITY \neq f(HARM\ SEVERITY\ ALONE)
\]

It separates:

- participation consent;
- action consent;
- risk consent;
- outcome consent;
- expected harm;
- foreseeable accepted harm;
- prohibited harm;
- extra-contextual harm;
- temporal termination.

It also preserves:

\[
H\uparrow,\ Irreversibility\uparrow
\Rightarrow Consent\ Assurance\uparrow
\]

without assuming:

\[
Consent\ Assurance\uparrow
\Rightarrow Authority\uparrow
\]

### Revised Assessment

**Previous:** PARTIALLY CONSTRAINED  
**Now:** **MATERIALLY ADVANCED THROUGH BOUNDED-CONSENT AND CONTEXTUAL-HARM MODEL / DYNAMIC CAPACITY AND IRREVERSIBLE-CHOICE TESTING OPEN**

This is one of the clearest new contributions to Ethical Kernel conflict handling.

## 10. Machine-Checkable Ethics

PH-01 creates a useful challenge for machine ethics because a literal "harm occurred" rule would classify boxing badly.

The decision object must include context, consent envelope, action class, risk acceptance, intent, rule compliance and temporal validity.

PH-02 adds similarly contextual authority states.

### Revised Assessment

The overall status remains:

**MAJOR OPEN RESEARCH PROGRAMME / ACTIVE EXPERIMENTAL RESEARCH**

but a new formalisation requirement becomes explicit:

> **Machine-checkable ethics must represent bounded contextual permissions and authority states without reducing them to outcome severity or binary consent.**

This provides strong future experimental scenarios for the Ethical Guardian programme.

## 18. Ethical Guardianship and Bounded Delegated Authority

PH-01 and PH-02 produce a general concept:

> **Bounded Delegated Authority**

This has direct relevance to Ethical Guardianship.

A Guardian could be modelled not as a superior moral sovereign but as a role with:

- defined purpose;
- defined trigger;
- defined scope;
- temporal/domain limits;
- proportionality;
- provenance;
- oversight;
- contestability;
- termination/graduation.

### Revised Assessment

Guardian Regress remains unresolved, but Bounded Delegated Authority provides a promising legitimacy constraint.

**Guardian Graduation** also gains a structural analogue: referee/emergency authority should end when its qualifying conditions end.

This does not solve who validates graduation, but strengthens the hypothesis that **authority persistence itself requires continuing justification**.

## 35. Conclusion

The new work does not substantially reduce the Concord's need for external validation. What it does is improve the **resolution of the questions**.

Several broad questions have decomposed into smaller objects that can now be:

- modelled;
- varied one variable at a time;
- adversarially examined;
- translated into case matrices;
- compared across substrates;
- and eventually tested.

The most important methodological result is therefore not that the Concord now has more answers.

It is that several previously vague uncertainties have become **better questions with identifiable failure conditions**.

That is the correct direction of development:

**BROAD QUESTION → DISTINCTION → MODEL → EDGE CASE → FAILURE → REVISION → TEST**

rather than treating additional conceptual structure as validation.
