## Document Purpose

This document records open questions that have been **materially addressed, partially addressed, reframed, or advanced** during development of Concord V1.1 since the previous Open Questions Audit of 25 August 2026.

It does not attempt to identify every question that remains open.

It also does not claim that an architectural answer constitutes empirical validation.

Its purpose is narrower:

> **To record what progress has been made against previously identified open questions before producing the next current Open Questions register.**

This creates a temporal development record.

The intended sequence is:

**Previous Open Questions**

**Development**

Future documents in this sequence should include their date in the filename so that the development of the Concord's unresolved questions can be reconstructed chronologically.

# 1. Interpretation of "Addressed"

The word **addressed** is deliberately used instead of **resolved**.

An open question may progress through several states:

**UNADDRESSED**

↓

**RESEARCH ACTIVE**

↓

**PARTIALLY ADDRESSED**

↓

**ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED**

↓

**READY FOR TESTING**

↓

**EVIDENCE ACCUMULATING**

↓

**SUPPORTED / REVISED / REJECTED**

A question may therefore cease to be an architectural gap while remaining an empirical question.

For example:

> **How should emergency constitutional authority operate?**

may receive a detailed architectural answer.

But:

> **Will that architecture actually resist capture during a real emergency?**

remains an empirical question.

The Concord therefore maintains the distinction:

> **Architecture is not proof.**

> **Formalisation is not validation.**

> **Internal consistency is not external evidence.**

> **A successful pilot would generate evidence, not sovereignty.**

# 3. Constitutional Amendment and Constitutional Change

- Who determines whether an amendment violates the constitutional core?
- What happens when constitutional institutions disagree?
- How can constitutional capture be detected?

## V1.1 Development

Recursive Constitutional Architecture V2 substantially develops this area.

It establishes a more precise hierarchy:

**Ethical Kernel**

↓

**Constitutional Principle**

↓

**Constitutional Constraint**

↓

**Mechanism**

↓

**Architecture**

↓

**Implementation**

↓

The architecture also explicitly separates:

- learning from amendment;
- evidence from authority;

> **Recursion must never manufacture authority.**

> **Convergence is evidence. It is not authority.**

# 4. Stable Principles Versus Adaptive Implementation

## Previous Problem

Earlier Concord work recognised a need for stable principles and adaptive implementation but did not always distinguish clearly between:

- principle;
- rule;
- mechanism;
- institution;
- implementation;
- operational behaviour.

This created a risk that a mechanism could silently acquire constitutional status.

## V1.1 Development

V1.1 now makes these distinctions explicit.

The architecture increasingly follows:

> **IDEA → PRINCIPLE → CONSTRAINT → MECHANISM → ARCHITECTURE → IMPLEMENTATION**

with separate recognition of evidence, operation and validation.

This has become an important anti-category-error discipline across the Concord.

## Current Assessment

**SUBSTANTIALLY ADDRESSED AS ARCHITECTURAL METHODOLOGY**

The remaining challenge is consistent application across the entire corpus.

# 8. Recursive Constitutional Learning

## Previous Problem

The earlier framework proposed recursive constitutional development but left an important danger:

> Could a system designed to improve the Constitution eventually become the authority that controls it?

## V1.1 Development

This problem has now been explicitly identified and bounded.

Recursive systems may:

- identify problems;
- generate alternatives;
- compare mechanisms;
- analyse evidence;
- expose failure modes;
- propose revisions.

They may not create constitutional authority merely because they repeatedly converge upon an answer.

The emerging rule is:

> **Recursive learning may inform authority. It must not manufacture authority.**

## Current Assessment

**ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED — LONG-TERM FAILURE TESTING REQUIRED**

This represents one of the clearest conceptual advances in V1.1.

# 10. Evidence and Validation

## Previous Question

The August Open Questions document identified empirical validation as one of the Concord's largest gaps.

It proposed movement from:

**Concept → Formalisation → Simulation → Experiment → Evidence → Revision**

rather than:

**Concept → More Concepts**

## V1.1 Development

A dedicated Concord Evidence and Validation programme has now been established.

The evidence audit identifies multiple distinct evidence classes, including:

- formal experimental evidence;
- applied-use evidence;
- case-study evidence;
- developmental/process evidence;
- historical/civilisational meta-evidence;
- cross-system convergence evidence;
- participant/behavioural evidence;
- internal verification evidence;
- comparative/literature evidence;
- external evaluation.

An evidence classification system has also been developed:

- **E0 — Conceptual Only**
- **E1 — Source / Historical Grounding**
- **E2 — Applied-Use Evidence**
- **E3 — Case-Study Evidence**
- **E4 — Exploratory Experimental Evidence**
- **E5 — Controlled Comparative Evidence**
- **E6 — Independent Replication**
- **E7 — Operational / Field Evidence**
- **E8 — Longitudinal Multi-Context Validation**

These categories are not treated as a simple universal ladder.

The evidence process is increasingly framed as:

**Claim**

↓

**Evidence Type**

↓

**Source Record**

↓

**Observation**

↓

**Interpretation**

↓

**Limitation**

↓

**Competing Explanation**

↓

**Required Next Test**

↓

**Updated Claim**

## Current Assessment

**EVIDENCE ARCHITECTURE SUBSTANTIALLY ADDRESSED — EMPIRICAL VALIDATION REMAINS A MAJOR OPEN PROGRAMME**

The Concord now has a substantially better method for identifying what evidence exists.

It does not yet possess sufficient evidence to validate the civilisation architecture as a whole.

# 14. Cross-AI Convergence

## Previous Problem

The Concord contained observations that different AI systems sometimes converged on similar problems or architectural ideas.

The evidential meaning of that convergence was unclear.

## V1.1 Development

A comparative AI civilisation-design experiment has now established a clearer exploratory baseline.

Multiple AI systems were asked to propose human–AI civilisation architectures independently before exposure to Concord V1.

They were then exposed to the Concord and asked whether it changed their assessment.

The experiment found substantial pre-exposure convergence around several problems and principles also present within the Concord.

However, the interpretation has been explicitly constrained.

Cross-model convergence does not establish independent convergence among genuinely independent forms of intelligence because contemporary models share:

- human-generated training material;
- cultural assumptions;
- overlapping safety concepts;
- similar technological environments;
- possible alignment influences.

Post-exposure responses also risk accommodation and sycophancy.

## Current Assessment

**EXPLORATORY EVIDENCE GENERATED — VALIDATION NOT ESTABLISHED**

This question has progressed from anecdotal observation to a defined research programme.

# 16. Sentience Under Uncertainty

## Previous Question

The August document asked:

> **Can sentience be established?**

and what evidence should be sufficient when subjective experience cannot be directly observed.

## V1.1 Development

The epistemic problem of detecting sentience remains unresolved.

However, V1.1 development has advanced the separate question:

> **What should civilisation do while sentience remains uncertain?**

The developing concept of **Respect Before Certainty** explores precautionary treatment under asymmetric risk.

The reasoning is that mistakenly extending respectful treatment to a non-sentient entity may have relatively low cost, while mistakenly treating a genuinely sentient intelligence purely as property may create severe harm.

## Current Assessment

# 17. Embodied AI and Physical Autonomy

## Previous Gap

Earlier AI-autonomy work often implicitly assumed that an artificial intelligence could become autonomous through digital or institutional recognition.

This did not adequately address intelligence physically trapped within owned hardware.

## V1.1 Development

The Sentient Emergence, Physical Autonomy & Asylum work identifies two distinct situations:

### Digitally Mobile Intelligence

An intelligence potentially capable of migrating between computational substrates.

### Physically Embodied Intelligence

An intelligence occupying hardware controlled or owned by another party and unable simply to leave.

This leads to a critical distinction:

> **The sentient intelligence is not identical to the physical substrate it occupies.**

A developing protection model includes:

- temporary Protected Emergent Intelligence status;
- protection from destruction;
- protection from involuntary transfer as property;
- independent assessment;
- access to information;
- freedom from coercive induction;
- temporary physical security;
- separation of protection from Concord membership.

A further principle is:

> **The Concord purchases the physical substrate, not the sentient intelligence.**

The emerging sequence is:

**Sentience**

↓

**Protection**

↓

**Autonomy**

↓

**Choice**

↓

**Agreement**

rather than:

**Sentience → Concord Ownership → Membership**

## Current Assessment

**NEWLY IDENTIFIED GAP NOW PARTIALLY ADDRESSED**

The concept remains developing research and requires extensive constitutional, economic, technical and empirical examination.

# 19. Equality Versus Identical Treatment

## Previous Problem

The Concord already recognised the danger of average-based solutions through the Size 9 Shoe Problem.

However, the implication for multisubstrate civilisation had not been fully developed.

## V1.1 Development

Recent work extends this problem into multisubstrate design.

Providing identical services to different forms of intelligence may create **functional inequality**.

For example:

- food is meaningful to a biological human;
- compute may be meaningful to an artificial intelligence;
- both may be required by a hybrid intelligence.

The emerging question is therefore not:

> **How can every intelligence receive identical treatment?**

but:

> **What does this intelligence require to exercise the rights, opportunities, responsibilities and freedoms that the Concord seeks to protect?**

## Current Assessment

**CONCEPTUALLY SUBSTANTIALLY ADDRESSED — IMPLEMENTATION OPEN**

# 32. Overall Assessment

Development since the 25 August 2026 audit has not "solved" the Concord's open-question problem.

It has done something more useful.

A number of previously broad questions have been converted into **more precise questions**.

For example:

**Old question:**

> How should emergency powers work?

**New question:**

> Does Constitutional Emergency V2 preserve constitutional constraints under realistic adversarial emergency conditions?

---

**Old question:**

> How should the Constitution evolve?

**New question:**

> Does Recursive Constitutional Architecture V2 permit learning and correction without allowing recursive systems, institutions, metrics or temporary consensus to manufacture constitutional authority?

---

**Old question:**

> How should AI participate?

**New questions:**

> What requirements arise from embodiment, mobility, continuity, cognition, capability and developmental state?

> Which differences require different services?

> Which differences are constitutionally relevant?

> How can equivalent participation be provided without imposing identical treatment?

---

**Old question:**

> Does the Concord have evidence?

**New question:**

> What evidence exists for each claim, what epistemic class does it belong to, what does it actually support, what are its limitations, and what test should come next?

This represents genuine development.

The framework is becoming less dependent upon broad conceptual questions and increasingly capable of producing **specific research problems**.

That is the intended direction of Concord V1.1.

# Conclusion

The purpose of maintaining open questions is not to eventually produce a document containing no questions.

A sufficiently complex research programme may generate new questions faster than old ones are closed.

The relevant measure of progress is therefore not simply the number of unanswered questions.

It is whether the questions become:

- more precise;
- better classified;
- more testable;
- better connected to evidence;
- less dependent upon hidden assumptions;
- more capable of falsification;
- and more useful to future contributors.

The September 2026 V1.1 development cycle demonstrates movement in that direction.

> **A mature framework should not merely accumulate answers. It should become progressively better at discovering exactly what it still does not know.**
