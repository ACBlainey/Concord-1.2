# Document Purpose

The Concord is not presented as a completed civilisation model.

It is an evolving framework intended to be:

- examined;
- criticised;
- tested;
- compared;
- revised;
- simplified;
- extended;
- rejected where evidence requires;
- and preserved where useful.

This document records the **principal questions that remain unresolved within Concord V1.1 as of 8 September 2026**.

It supersedes the 7 September 2026 *Open Questions and Next Steps* document as the current register while preserving that earlier document as part of the Concord's temporal research record.

Since the 7 September register, the Layer-Zero / Layer −0 development cycle has produced:

- fifteen extracted systems;
- portable methodological extractions;
- twelve Candidate Principles;
- six Candidate Architectures;
- Candidate Principle consolidation and adversarial audits;
- Candidate Architecture extraction, dependency and adversarial audits.

Several questions have therefore changed status.

In particular, some questions that previously lacked architecture now possess **candidate architectural answers** and should consequently be reframed around:

- testing;
- implementation;
- adversarial failure;
- empirical comparison;
- and validation.

The purpose of this register is:

> **to describe what the Concord does not yet know, what remains incomplete, what requires testing, and where future contributors can most usefully work.**

# 1. Status Language

A question can be:

- **UNADDRESSED**
- **RESEARCH ACTIVE**
- **PARTIALLY ADDRESSED**
- **ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED**
- **READY FOR TESTING**
- **EVIDENCE ACCUMULATING**
- **SUPPORTED**
- **REVISED**
- **REJECTED**

These states are not interchangeable.

In particular:

> **Architecture is not proof.**

> **Formalisation is not validation.**

> **Internal consistency is not external evidence.**

> **Simulation is not reality.**

> **Development is not adoption.**

A question that has received a principle-level or architectural answer may therefore remain open as an empirical question.

# 2. The Central Open Question

The largest unresolved question remains:

> **Can a civilisation based substantially upon Concordian principles and mechanisms produce better outcomes than realistic alternatives across long periods and under adverse conditions?**

The Concord contains increasingly substantial architecture.

It does not yet possess sufficient real-world evidence to answer this question.

The central research direction therefore remains:

**CLAIM**

↓

**MODEL**

↓

**TEST**

↓

**EVIDENCE**

↓

**COMPARISON**

↓

**FAILURE ANALYSIS**

↓

**REVISION**

rather than:

**CLAIM → ADDITIONAL THEORY**

**Status:** FUNDAMENTAL OPEN QUESTION / EMPIRICAL VALIDATION REQUIRED

# 3. Ethical Kernel

The Ethical Kernel is historically grounded and increasingly connected to operational constraints.

Foundational questions remain.

## 3.1 Sufficiency

Are the present Ethical Kernel principles sufficient?

Could an important class of:

- harm;
- domination;
- exploitation;
- dependency;
- exclusion;
- deception;
- civilisational instability

remain outside their coverage?

## 3.2 Redundancy

Can any principles be fully derived from others?

Would a smaller kernel provide equivalent protection?

## 3.3 Conflict Between Principles

Several conflict classes now possess Candidate Principle and Candidate Architecture constraints.

Examples include:

### Autonomy versus protection

Now partially constrained through:

- Autonomy-Preserving Support;
- Proportionality of Authority;
- Participant Support and Autonomy Architecture;
- Exceptional Access, Provenance and Accountable Power Architecture.

### Privacy versus accountability

Now partially constrained through:

- Purpose-Limited Information;
- Exceptional Access Must Remain Exceptional;
- Observable Power and Compensating Oversight;
- Purpose-Limited Participant Information Architecture.

However, no validated universal Ethical Kernel conflict-resolution model yet exists.

## 3.4 Cross-Substrate Interpretation

Can the same ethical principle be interpreted consistently for:

- humans;
- AI;
- hybrids;
- embodied AI;
- distributed AI;
- collective intelligences;
- unknown intelligence?

## 3.5 Unknown Moral Categories

Could unfamiliar intelligence reveal ethical categories absent from a framework developed largely through human history?

**Status:** PARTIALLY ADDRESSED — MULTIPLE CONFLICT CLASSES ARCHITECTURALLY CONSTRAINED / FOUNDATIONAL QUESTIONS REMAIN OPEN

# 77. Intervention Provenance

A new question has emerged from adversarial architecture testing.

> **How should civilisation distinguish an outcome that occurred independently from an outcome partly caused by the intervention triggered by the system's own prediction?**

3. intervention changes participant behaviour;
4. changed behaviour is recorded;
5. system declares prediction accurate.

Required provenance may include:

- pre-intervention state;
- prediction;
- uncertainty;
- intervention;
- expected intervention effect;
- actual outcome;
- plausible intervention-caused effects.

# 90. Current Epistemic Shift

A major change is now visible.

Several domains have moved from:

> **What should the Concord do?**

to:

> **Does the proposed architecture actually work?**

Examples include:

### Privacy

Old question:

> How should privacy be protected?

Current question:

> Does Purpose-Limited Participant Information Architecture preserve privacy while still permitting legitimate accountability and civil learning?

### Resource Allocation

Old question:

> How should resources be allocated?

Current question:

> Does Predictive System-Steering improve preparedness without producing central planning, brittleness or minority exclusion?

### Dependency

Old question:

> How should soft domination be prevented?

Current question:

> Do autonomy restoration, subsidiarity, redundancy and system adaptation actually reduce dependency and capture?

### Exceptional Power

Old question:

> How should exceptional authority be controlled?

Current question:

> Does bounded exceptional access with provenance and independent oversight remain accountable under real institutional pressure?

This is a meaningful developmental transition.

The questions remain open.

But they are becoming more precise and increasingly testable.

# Conclusion

Concord V1.1 remains far from complete.

Several of its most difficult questions remain fundamentally unresolved, particularly:

- sentience;
- agency;
- identity;
- economic transition;
- bootstrap financing;
- intergenerational legitimacy;
- cross-substrate jurisprudence;
- mental integrity;
- collective intelligence;
- external legal compatibility;
- civilisational success.

However, the Layer-Zero / Layer −0 development cycle has materially changed the status of several other domains.

General candidate architecture now exists for:

- participant support;
- predictive and counterfactual modelling;
- civilisational learning and Metrics;
- participant information boundaries;
- predictive resource steering;
- exceptional access and accountable power.

The appropriate research question for those domains is therefore increasingly no longer:

> **Can we invent an architecture?**

It is:

> **Does the architecture survive reality?**

The current research emphasis should accordingly shift, where architecture is sufficiently developed, from further conceptual expansion toward:

- adversarial testing;
- simulation;
- comparison;
- limited pilots;
- external evidence;
- failure analysis;
- correction;
- rejection where necessary.

The Concord's open-question programme should remain permanent.

> **The objective is not to eliminate uncertainty by declaration, but to progressively convert broad uncertainty into precise, testable and consequential questions.**
