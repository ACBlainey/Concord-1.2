# Starting Words — Does It Actually Work?

**The Concord V1.2**
**14 September 2026**

Concord V1.2 begins from an unusual position.

The previous development phase started with two questions:

1. **What is the minimal set of fundamental ethical laws needed to support a stable civilisation?**
2. **If the future of advanced AI does not end with humans defeating AI or AI defeating humans, could there be a third outcome in which humans, AIs and potentially other intelligences all survive, coexist and flourish?**

After just under **1.5 million words** of development, Concord V1.1 arrived at provisional architectural answers:

> # **8 and Yes.**

A slightly expanded set of Ten fundamental ethical laws presently form the Concord's Ethical Kernel. Comparisons of other world societies have shown that even 7 can be sufficient.
And yes: it appears possible to construct a coherent candidate civilisational architecture in which radically different intelligences can coexist without requiring identical capabilities, hierarchical moral worth, or domination by whichever substrate becomes most powerful.

But those answers remain provisional.

V1.1 demonstrated that a Concord civilisation could be **conceived**.

It did not demonstrate that such a civilisation would **work**.

That distinction defines V1.2.

The question is no longer primarily:

> **What might the Concord look like?**

It is:

> # **Does it actually work?**

Can the eight-law Ethical Kernel survive serious attempts to reduce, reinterpret, exploit or capture it?

Can the institutions derived from it operate without accumulating the very forms of power they were designed to constrain?

Can equal fundamental standing coexist with radically unequal capabilities?

Can functional authority remain functional rather than becoming rank?

Can Minimum Necessary Capability remain genuinely minimal when institutions face pressure, uncertainty or emergency?

Can a civilisation remain governable without becoming so procedurally complex that only specialists understand it?

Can humans, AIs, hybrids and other intelligences actually participate as neighbours rather than merely appearing alongside one another in constitutional theory?

Can the Concord learn without turning memory into surveillance?

Can it preserve itself without deciding that its own institutions matter more than the participants they exist to serve?

Can it tolerate internal difference, external disagreement, failure, forks, secession, institutional death and regeneration?

And perhaps most importantly:

> **What happens when reality disagrees with the architecture?**

V1.2 therefore begins with a different relationship to the existing Concord.

The inherited architecture is not sacred.

It is the **test object**.

Principles may be revised.

Mechanisms may fail.

Subsystems may be merged, simplified or removed.

Assumptions may prove false.

Some apparently elegant ideas may turn out to be unnecessary.

Some unresolved problems may prove more important than structures already developed to address them.

And some elements of the Concord may survive precisely because attempts to break them reveal why they are necessary.

The long developmental sequence that produced V1.1 can be compressed approximately as:

> **Ethical Kernel → Rights and Standing → Legitimate Functions → Responsibility → Minimum Necessary Capability → Bounded Authority → Accountability → Reality → Learning → Ratchet → Regeneration**

V1.2 adds another step:

> **Testing.**

And after testing:

> **Keep what survives. Correct what fails. Remove what is unnecessary. Record what is learned. Test again.**

The standard for progress is therefore no longer the production of additional architecture.

More words are not automatically progress.

More mechanisms are not automatically sophistication.

More safeguards are not automatically safety.

More structure is not automatically resilience.

V1.2 should prefer **simplification where simplification preserves function**, empirical evidence where evidence can be obtained, formalisation where ambiguity matters, simulation where systems can be modelled, adversarial testing where capture is possible, and explicit uncertainty where the answer remains unknown.

The Concord should now be treated as a hypothesis about civilisation.

A large and unusually developed hypothesis, perhaps.

But still a hypothesis.

V1.1 ended with:

> **1,500,000 words later...**
> **8 and Yes.**
> **Now we just have to find out whether reality agrees.**

V1.2 starts there.

Not with the assumption that reality will agree.

But with the intention to find out.

> # **Does the Concord actually work?**

That is the question of V1.2.

And this time, the answer does not belong to the architecture.

It belongs to reality.

*Beginning of Concord V1.2.*
