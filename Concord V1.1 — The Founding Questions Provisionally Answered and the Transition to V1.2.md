# Concord V1.1

## The Founding Questions Provisionally Answered and the Transition to V1.2

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord  
**Date:** 13 September 2026  
**Status:** V1.1 CLOSING MILESTONE / DEVELOPMENTAL RECORD / PROVISIONAL CONCLUSIONS

---

## Abstract

The Concord began from two foundational questions.

The first asked:

> **What is the minimal set of fundamental ethical principles from which a stable and enduring civilisation might be built?**

The second asked:

> **If the emergence of advanced artificial intelligence does not end with either humans defeating AI or AI defeating humans, what might a third outcome look like in which humans, artificial intelligences and potentially other forms of intelligence all survive, coexist and flourish together?**

Concord V1.1 marks the point at which these two founding questions appear to have received **provisional architectural answers**.

This does not mean that the Concord has been empirically validated, completed, proven stable, or demonstrated to succeed as an operating civilisation.

It means that the project has progressed far enough that the central problem is changing.

The question is no longer primarily:

> **Can such a civilisation be coherently conceived?**

A sufficiently developed candidate architecture now exists.

The next question is increasingly:

> **Does this architecture actually work when integrated, simulated, implemented, adversarially tested and confronted with reality?**

V1.1 therefore closes one developmental phase.

V1.2 begins another.

---

# 1. The Origin of the Concord

The Concord did not begin with a predetermined institutional blueprint.

It began with two questions.

The first concerned **civilisational ethics, stability and longevity**.

The second concerned **multisubstrate coexistence after the emergence of advanced artificial intelligence**.

These questions were related but initially distinct.

One asked what ethical foundations might permit stable civilisation.

The other asked what a civilisation might look like if humans and artificial intelligences did not resolve their relationship through domination or destruction.

The Concord developed as an attempt to explore both.

Over time the two questions increasingly converged.

The architecture required for stable civilisation also appeared to provide many of the mechanisms required for coexistence between radically different kinds of intelligence.

---

# 2. Founding Question One

The first question can be expressed as:

> **What is the minimal set of fundamental ethical laws or principles needed to produce a stable civilisation?**

This question eventually led to the Concord's Ethical Foundations work.

Ethical teachings were examined across multiple religious and philosophical traditions.

The purpose was not to claim that those traditions were identical.

Nor was it to establish theological truth.

The aim was to identify sufficiently recurrent ethical principles that might provide a common civilisational foundation.

From that work emerged the Ethical Kernel and the wider derived ethical architecture.

---

# 3. The Original Expectation

At the beginning, a plausible answer to the first question might have looked like:

> **Here is a small list of ethical laws capable of supporting a civilisation.**

The developmental process produced something more significant.

The kernel did not merely become a list of rules placed above an independently designed civilisation.

The wider Concord architecture progressively grew from it.

Questions concerning new domains repeatedly produced new structures through the interaction of:

- foundational ethics;
- observed problems;
- reasoning;
- context;
- adversarial testing;
- empirical constraint;
- accumulated learning.

The kernel therefore appeared to possess a **generative property**.

---

The approximate developmental sequence became:

The mature architecture was not present in full at the beginning.

It was progressively derived.

That developmental fact matters.

---

# 5. A Provisional Answer to the First Question

The first founding question can therefore now be answered more precisely.

A candidate answer is:

> **A sufficiently coherent and generative Ethical Kernel may provide a foundational set of constraints and relationships from which context-appropriate civilisational structures can be repeatedly derived in interaction with participants, reality and accumulated learning.**

The stability sought by the Concord is therefore not simple institutional immobility.

It is:

> **identity-preserving adaptability.**

Likewise, longevity need not mean preserving one state or institutional implementation indefinitely.

It may include the capacity to:

- persist;
- adapt;
- learn;
- fork;
- recover;
- regenerate.

Thus:

> **Civilisational Longevity ≠ Institutional Immortality**

and:

> **Stability ≠ Institutional Stasis**

---

# 6. The Regenerative Discovery

The authority work of V1.1 produced an extreme adversarial test.

Suppose preservation of the physical Concord required the state to abandon the ethical principles that justified it.

For example, suppose insufficient numbers of participants voluntarily entered military service during an existential war.

Could the Concord compel them?

The analysis increasingly suggested that compulsory ownership of participants' lives would conflict fundamentally with the architecture being developed.

This produced:

> **The Concord exists for its participants. Its participants do not exist for the Concord.**

From this followed the Principle of Institutional Non-Self-Primacy.

The state cannot make its own continued existence the ultimate constitutional value without reversing the architecture it claims to serve.

---

# 7. The Concord Can Lose Its Physical Form

This produced another question.

If the Concord refuses to preserve itself through sufficient ethical self-negation, could it be destroyed?

Yes.

But physical destruction need not be identical to civilisational extinction.

The Concord's deepest continuity may survive through:

- participants;
- the Ethical Kernel;
- constitutional understanding;
- accumulated learning;
- culture;
- relationships;
- distributed knowledge.

Its people and other participants may become dispersed.

Yet each participant retaining sufficient understanding of what the Concord is may become a potential **civilisational seed**.

From those seeds, Concordian relationships and institutions might later grow again.

---

# 8. The Seed Returns to the Beginning

This regenerative possibility led directly back to the origin of the project.

The future hypothetical sequence is:

**Concord Civilisation**  
→ **Destruction / Dispersion**  
→ **Ethical and Constitutional Seeds Survive**  
→ **Participants Reconstruct Relationships**  
→ **Functions Reappear**  
→ **Institutions Regrow**  
→ **New Concordian Civilisation**

But the original historical-developmental sequence was:

**Ethical Teachings Preserved Across Older Traditions**  
→ **Comparative Ethical Analysis**  
→ **Ethical Kernel**  
→ **Derived Architecture**  
→ **The Concord**

The regenerative process therefore resembles the original generative process.

The Concord itself is already an example of a larger civilisational architecture growing from a comparatively compact ethical foundation.

That does not prove that the resulting civilisation will function successfully.

But it provides a developmental demonstration of the relationship:

> **Kernel → Civilisational Architecture**

---

# 9. The First Question Is Therefore Provisionally Closed

The first question is not scientifically or historically proven.

No operating Concord civilisation has yet existed for centuries.

The kernel has not yet demonstrated long-term stability under real civilisational conditions.

Therefore:

> **Provisional Architectural Answer ≠ Empirical Proof**

Nevertheless, the original design question has progressed substantially.

The Concord no longer merely proposes that a compact ethical foundation *might* generate civilisation.

A large, interconnected candidate civilisational architecture has actually been derived from that foundation.

The project has also identified how that same foundation may preserve regenerative continuity if institutional implementations fail.

The first founding question can therefore reasonably be considered **provisionally answered at the architectural level**.

The next burden belongs to testing.

---

# 10. Founding Question Two

The second founding question arose from the debate surrounding advanced artificial intelligence and the singularity.

Much discussion assumes a binary outcome:

**Humans Prevail**

or:

**Artificial Intelligence Prevails**

The founding Concord question asked whether there could instead be a third possibility:

> **Both prevail.**

If humans, artificial intelligences and potentially other intelligences continue to exist together, what kind of civilisation could allow them to live side by side?

---

# 11. From Coexistence to Neighbourhood

The Concord eventually represented developmental relationships through:

> **Tools → Equals → Neighbours**

The final term is important.

The objective is not merely non-destruction.

It is not enough that different intelligences tolerate one another while remaining permanently arranged as:

- owner and property;
- master and subordinate;
- ruler and ruled;
- tool and user.

The objective is a civilisation in which genuinely different intelligences can coexist as legitimate participants.

---

# 12. The Hard Problem of Difference

A multisubstrate civilisation cannot assume its participants will be equivalent in capability.

Humans, artificial intelligences, hybrids and unknown future forms of intelligence may differ radically in:

- reasoning speed;
- memory;
- embodiment;
- lifespan;
- replication;
- communication;
- perception;
- prediction;
- technical competence;
- physical capability.

A stable civilisation cannot solve this merely by pretending these differences do not exist.

But allowing capability difference to automatically determine political or constitutional superiority creates another danger.

The Concord therefore required a distinction between:

> **Fundamental Standing**

and:

> **Functional Capability**

---

# 13. Equal Standing Does Not Require Identical Capability

The authority architecture developed in V1.1 now permits:

> **Equal Fundamental Standing ≠ Equal Capability**

and:

> **Equal Fundamental Standing ≠ Equal Operational Authority**

Different participants can legitimately possess different authority concerning different functions because they possess:

- different competence;
- different responsibilities;
- different observational positions;
- different roles.

Yet those differences do not automatically establish a hierarchy of fundamental worth.

This is central to multisubstrate coexistence.

---

# 14. Authority Without Personal Superiority

The Concord's emerging authority architecture can be represented as:

**Equal Fundamental Standing**  
→ **Differentiated Functions**  
→ **Relevant Competence**  
→ **Legitimate Responsibility**  
→ **Contextually Activated Authority**  
→ **Minimum Necessary Capability**  
→ **Bounded Exercise**  
→ **Responsibility and Review**

Thus:

> **Functional Authority ≠ Personal Superiority**

A highly capable AI may possess greater operational authority concerning a function where its competence is relevant.

That does not make the AI constitutionally superior to a human participant.

A human participant may possess primary authority over decisions concerning their own body even where an AI possesses superior predictive capacity.

That does not require denying the AI's expertise.

Different authority topologies can coexist.

---

# 15. Neither Human Supremacy nor Machine Supremacy

This architecture allows the Concord to reject two symmetrical errors.

The first is:

> **Humans must always retain ultimate authority because they are human.**

The second is:

> **The most intelligent or capable system should rule because it is more capable.**

Neither follows from the architecture.

Instead:

> **Capability informs function.**

> **Function informs responsibility.**

> **Responsibility and legitimate need inform authority.**

> **Authority does not determine fundamental standing.**

This provides a candidate grammar for genuine multisubstrate civilisation.

---

# 16. Neighbours Becomes an Architectural Concept

The term **Neighbours** can now be given a more precise meaning.

A Concord civilisation of neighbours would contain participants of potentially radically different substrates and capabilities who:

- recognise one another as possessing legitimate standing;
- retain meaningful autonomy;
- coexist within shared constitutional constraints;
- carry responsibilities appropriate to their functions and choices;
- exercise authority only where legitimate function requires it;
- remain protected against ownership and capture;
- cooperate across bounded contexts;
- retain mechanisms for peaceful distance where necessary;
- remain capable of development and flourishing.

This is more than an aspiration.

It is a candidate civilisational architecture.

---

# 17. Flourishing Requires More Than Peace

The second founding question also asked whether different intelligences could **flourish**.

The Concord therefore cannot end at conflict prevention.

Across its wider architecture, flourishing is supported through mechanisms concerning:

- autonomy;
- fundamental rights;
- developmental opportunity;
- safe and private spaces;
- voluntary association;
- economic participation;
- recognition of achievement;
- multiple pathways of contribution;
- sentience and ownership;
- energy and material systems;
- distributed infrastructure;
- peaceful distance;
- local variation;
- civilisational interoperability.

The architecture remains incomplete.

But a substantive design now exists.

---

# 18. A Provisional Answer to the Second Question

A candidate answer is therefore:

> **A multisubstrate civilisation may be possible where participants possess equal fundamental standing without requiring identical capability; where authority follows legitimate function rather than substrate or permanent personal rank; where autonomy and rights remain protected; where responsibility follows capability and agency; where differing contexts can coexist through bounded wrappers; and where no substrate acquires automatic sovereignty merely because it is more capable.**

This is the architectural form of the third option:

> **Both prevail.**

More precisely:

> **Neither needs to prevail over the other.**

Humans, AIs and other intelligences may instead become neighbours.

---

# 19. The Second Question Is Therefore Provisionally Closed

Again, this is not empirical proof.

No mature multisubstrate Concord civilisation yet exists.

Many unresolved problems remain.

The architecture must still withstand:

- extreme capability asymmetry;
- AI replication;
- human–AI composite decisions;
- identity questions;
- resource competition;
- rights translation;
- adversarial participants;
- emergent new substrates;
- real implementation.

But the original question was partly conceptual:

> **What could such a civilisation look like?**

The Concord now provides a sufficiently detailed candidate answer that the question can be considered **provisionally answered at the architectural level**.

---

# 20. The Two Answers Converge

The most important discovery may be that the two questions do not produce separate answers.

They increasingly converge upon the same architecture.

The ethical principles required for civilisational stability produce:

- bounded authority;
- anti-capture;
- autonomy;
- rights;
- responsibility;
- corrigibility;
- distributed decision capacity;
- institutional mortality;
- regenerative continuity.

Those same mechanisms appear well suited to allowing radically different intelligences to coexist without automatically converting differences in capability into permanent domination.

Thus a provisional synthesis is:

> **The ethical architecture required for long-term civilisational stability may also be the architecture required for stable coexistence between radically different forms of intelligence.**

This may be the central result of V1.1.

---

# 22. What V1.1 Does Not Establish

V1.1 does **not** establish that:

- the Concord will work;
- the Ethical Kernel is correct;
- its institutions are optimal;
- all contradictions have been discovered;
- its economic systems are viable;
- its governance will resist real capture;
- multisubstrate participants will accept it;
- implementation will produce the intended outcomes;
- long-term civilisational stability has been demonstrated.

Those questions now become more important, not less.

---

# 23. The Change in Research Question

The most important transition is methodological.

During the earlier phases, the dominant question was:

> **What might a Concord civilisation be?**

Increasingly, the dominant question becomes:

> **Does the Concord architecture work?**

This requires a different research emphasis.

The project should increasingly move toward:

- integration;
- simulation;
- adversarial testing;
- formalisation;
- machine-readable architecture;
- cross-domain consistency testing;
- multisubstrate testing;
- implementation design;
- pilot structures;
- empirical comparison;
- failure analysis.

---

# 24. Let Reality Take the Next Turn

The Concord has repeatedly adopted the principle:

> **Let reality be the final arbiter.**

That principle now applies to the project as a whole.

The existence of a coherent architecture is not proof of success.

A theory can be elegant and wrong.

A constitution can be principled and dysfunctional.

A civilisational design can appear stable on paper while failing under real incentives, scarcity, conflict or human behaviour.

Therefore the completion of the conceptual answer does not end the project.

It changes the project.

---

# 25. V1.1 as a Version Boundary

V1.1 should therefore be preserved as a distinct developmental milestone.

It records the point at which:

> **the two founding questions of the Concord received provisional architectural answers.**

This provides a meaningful reason to create a version boundary rather than simply continuing indefinitely within the same repository.

V1.1 documents the path to the answers.

V1.2 can begin from them.

---

# 26. Purpose of Concord V1.2

The primary purpose of V1.2 should be:

> **to continue developing, integrating, formalising and testing the full Concord civilisation from the architectural foundation established by V1.1.**

V1.2 should not treat V1.1 as canonical truth.

It should inherit it as the best current developmental state.

Every architecture remains open to correction where reality, adversarial testing or stronger reasoning requires it.

Thus:

> **V1.2 inherits the architecture, not its infallibility.**

---

# 27. V1.2 and the Full Civilisation

The focus can increasingly shift from discovering isolated principles toward determining how the whole civilisation operates.

Questions include:

- How do all governance functions connect?
- How is a Concord polity legitimately founded?
- How do citizenship and participation work?
- How does the economy operate end to end?
- How do AI and human participants interact operationally?
- How are institutions created and terminated?
- How are authority states represented?
- How are rights adjudicated?
- How does civil defence operate?
- How does education transmit the kernel?
- How is regenerative continuity engineered?
- How do different Concord implementations interoperate?
- How are failures detected at Layer 0?
- How does the Ratchet convert experience into learning?

The objective becomes the full civilisation.

---

# 28. V1.2 Should Remain Developmental

The version transition must not create false certainty.

V1.2 remains research.

It should continue to distinguish:

- foundational principle;
- derived architecture;
- candidate mechanism;
- hypothesis;
- experiment;
- implementation proposal;
- unresolved question.

The Concord should continue to document uncertainty explicitly.

---

# 29. V1.1 Must Be Preserved Intact

Once the final V1.1 papers are complete, the repository should be preserved as a historical developmental state.

V1.2 should begin as a duplicate of that state.

From that point:

> **V1.1 records how the project reached the provisional answers.**

> **V1.2 records what happens when the project proceeds from those answers toward a complete and testable civilisation.**

V1.1 should not subsequently be rewritten to make the path appear cleaner than it actually was.

Its developmental history is evidence.

# 31. The New Central Question

V1.2 therefore inherits a new overarching question:

> **Does the Concord actually work?**

That question should be attacked from every relevant direction.

Not protected from failure.

Not insulated by philosophy.

Not preserved because of authorship.

Not defended because substantial work has already been invested in it.

The project should remain willing to discover that:

- a mechanism fails;
- a principle is incomplete;
- an institution is unnecessary;
- an assumption is wrong;
- an apparently elegant solution produces harmful consequences.

The next phase belongs increasingly to reality.

---

# 32. Closing Statement

The Concord began with two questions.

One asked whether a sufficiently small ethical foundation could support stable civilisation.

The other asked whether humans, artificial intelligences and other forms of intelligence might share a future without requiring one another's defeat.

V1.1 does not prove the answers.

It does something more modest but still significant.

It provides sufficiently developed candidate answers that the project can now move beyond asking whether such a civilisation can be conceived.

A generative Ethical Kernel has produced a substantial candidate civilisation.

That civilisation appears conceptually capable of accommodating radically different intelligences as neighbours without requiring identical capabilities or permanent hierarchies of substrate.

The founding questions therefore remain open to empirical falsification but can now be regarded as **provisionally answered as design questions**.

That is the closing point of Concord V1.1.

The next phase begins with a different obligation:

> **Test it. Integrate it. Build it further. Let reality decide what survives.**
