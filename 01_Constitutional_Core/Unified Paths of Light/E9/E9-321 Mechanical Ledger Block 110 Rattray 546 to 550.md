# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-321 — Mechanical Ledger Block 110: Rattray 546–550

546 / C1685 = Y — an old woman experiencing hunger frames the need as food for the children. A person's own need may be communicated indirectly through care-oriented language about dependents rather than through direct self-claim.

547 / C1686 = H — the local OCR prints the item number as 647. Hunger suppresses a stranger's activity while satiety is followed by accosting local men's wives. Resource state clearly alters behaviour, but the gendered conduct and implied judgment make a transferable ethical occurrence insecure.

548 / C1687 = H — the stranger's hunger is said to have been brought from the stranger's own village. This may allocate responsibility for pre-existing need away from the host community, but the normative scope is underdetermined.

549 / C1688 = Y — hunger is felt by both slave and king. Basic bodily need crosses formal status boundaries; rank does not remove fundamental physiological vulnerability.

550 / C1690 = Y — another person cannot literally experience one's hunger. Some states of need are irreducibly first-person even when others can observe, infer or respond to them.

### AR334 — R546/C1685
An agent may communicate personal need indirectly through concern for dependents rather than through explicit self-claim.

### AR335 — R549/C1688
Formal status does not eliminate fundamental bodily needs shared across social ranks.

### AR336 — R550/C1690
Some need states are irreducibly first-person: others may recognise and respond to them but cannot directly experience them for the affected agent.
