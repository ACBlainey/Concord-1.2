# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-324 — Mechanical Ledger Block 113: Rattray 561–565

561 / C1021 = Y — plants may have medicinal value that an uninformed observer dismisses as useless. Lack of knowledge about a capability is not evidence that the capability is absent.

562 / C1972 = Y — Rattray explicitly explains that demanding medicine immediately prevents the herbalist from having time to search for the suitable plant or root. Quality work can require adequate time for expert search and selection; urgency imposed on the provider can degrade suitability.

563 / C3124 = Y — “fear him who is near you.” Proximate agents can present practical risks that distant or abstract threats do not; threat assessment should include accessibility/proximity.

564 / C3125 = Y — the local OCR prints the item number as 664. A declared intention to take from an agent is more threatening than a declared intention to give. Threat assessment can rationally distinguish adverse from beneficial stated intent.

565 / C3126 = Y — fear of being killed produces an adaptive bodily posture intended to reduce exposure. Rattray's extended note describes historical execution and deliberate prevention of oath-swearing by the condemned. The admitted occurrence is that credible lethal threat can alter behaviour toward self-protection; the execution practices remain historical context, not normative endorsement.

### AR345 — R561/C1021
Lack of knowledge about a capability is not evidence that the capability is absent or useless.

### AR346 — R562/C1972
Expert search and selection may require adequate time; imposed urgency can reduce the quality or suitability of the resulting intervention.

### AR347 — R563/C3124
Threat assessment should account for practical proximity or accessibility, not merely abstract harmful capacity.

### AR348 — R564/C3125
Stated adverse and beneficial intentions provide different evidence for threat assessment and should not be treated as equivalent.

### AR349 — R565/C3126
Credible threat of severe harm can alter behaviour toward protective adaptation.
