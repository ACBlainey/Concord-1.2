### OCR numbering exception
R574 is displayed as 674 in the local OCR. It occurs between visible R573 and R575 and carries C432. The ledger records the Rattray identity as 574 while preserving the OCR defect as provenance debt.

### R567 extended commentary
Rattray explicitly glosses the proverb as warning that a weak person who seeks cheap popularity is imposed upon. He then expands this into broad colonial-era generalisations about “Ashanti character” and advice to those who rule them.

E9 extracts only the narrow proverb-supported boundary/exploitation relation. Rattray's population-level and colonial governance claims are retained as mediation history, not accepted as ethical or empirical conclusions.

### R574 extended note
Rattray discusses stigma attached to body marks, their association with slave origin, and scarification of a child after previous sibling deaths. These practices are preserved as historical context; R574 remains H.
