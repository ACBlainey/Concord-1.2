### R491 OCR numbering exception
The local OCR omits the printed item number before the proverb carrying C2068. However, this is not a merely inferred sequential assignment:
- Rattray's earlier kinship note explicitly cites Nos. 487, 488, 491 and 492;
- R488 explicitly cross-references No. 492;
- the C2068 proverb appears directly between visible R490 and R492;
- the earlier note explicitly identifies 491 as one of this kinship sequence.

R491 is therefore recorded as source-reconciled but with OCR-numbering debt retained.

### R496 extended note
The long note describes:
- truth oaths;
- transfer of private disputes to a chief's court;
- investigation;
- open-court hearing when the oath is answered;
- fees and consequences;
- appeal by oath to higher courts;
- historical execution practices and a separate curse custom.

Only the securely relevant procedural structure is extracted into AR304. The more violent and institution-specific details remain source context.

### Gendered mediation
R499–504 contain strong gender norms/stereotypes and, in R501, explicitly racialised editorial language from Rattray. These are not silently normalised into ethical propositions.
