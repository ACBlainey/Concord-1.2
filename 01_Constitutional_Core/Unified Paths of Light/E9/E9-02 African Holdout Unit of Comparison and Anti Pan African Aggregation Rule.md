# Unified Paths of Light — E9
## E9-02 — African Holdout Unit of Comparison and Anti-Pan-African Aggregation Rule

## Problem
“African traditional ethics” is not a single tradition or canon. Treating the continent as one corpus would confound geography with cultural lineage and could manufacture apparent convergence through aggregation.

## Rule
E9 will not create a synthetic pan-African corpus and then treat the resulting architecture as though it belonged to one historical tradition.

The experimental unit must be a **declared ethnolinguistic/source tradition** with sufficient documented material for reproducible extraction.

## Multi-tradition design
If E9 uses more than one African tradition, each must first be:
1. bounded separately;
2. extracted separately;
3. normalised separately;
4. frozen separately.

Only after those internal freezes may an E9 cross-African comparison be constructed.

## Why this matters
This permits E9 to distinguish:
- within-tradition recurrence;
- between-African-tradition recurrence;
- continent-level synthesis;
- convergence with X1–X7.

It also prevents one source-rich tradition from filling apparent gaps in another.

## Candidate-source assessment
Preliminary methodological reconnaissance indicates that Akan proverb traditions are unusually well documented in large published collections, while Yorùbá material provides another substantial but structurally different oral record, including proverb traditions and the much larger Ifá corpus.

These are candidate source families only. No ethical result has been extracted and no final corpus boundary is declared by this document.

## Control
Corpus selection must be based on reproducibility, provenance quality, linguistic/contextual support and manageable completeness—not on whether a tradition appears likely to recover X.
