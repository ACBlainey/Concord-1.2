# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-336 — Mediation Note: Rattray 567 Colonial Governance Commentary

R567 has one of the clearest examples in this portion of the corpus of a short proverb being followed by a much broader editorial interpretation.

### Secure source layers

**Proverb:** fear of unpopularity leads to one's knife being borrowed to flay a python, after which the knife is considered useless.

**Rattray's direct gloss:** a weak man who panders to cheap popularity is soon imposed upon.

**Rattray's broader commentary:** he generalises about Ashanti character, advises those charged with ruling the people, contrasts leniency with firmness, and invokes Bacon's claim about kings being loved and feared.

### E9 treatment
The admitted occurrence AR351 is restricted to the first two layers: approval-seeking can cause an agent to surrender boundaries in ways that expose resources to misuse.

The broader claims are:
- Rattray's 1916 colonial interpretation;
- population-level generalisations;
- governance advice from a colonial administrator's perspective.

They are therefore not used as direct evidence about Akan ethical architecture.

This separation is necessary because accepting the editorial expansion would allow the recorder's political framework to enter the derived ethics under cover of a proverb.
