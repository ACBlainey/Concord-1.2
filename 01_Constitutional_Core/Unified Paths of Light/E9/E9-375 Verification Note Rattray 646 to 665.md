### Translation/OCR debt: R662
The English text reads “When a sick man is drunk, he is merely said to be unwell,” while the OCR of the Twi begins “Osikafo,” apparently “rich person.” Because this may be an OCR or translation-text issue, the ledger does not silently correct either witness. The occurrence is admitted only at the broader status-sensitive euphemism level and the discrepancy remains flagged.

### R647 material-culture note
Rattray gives a substantial note on cowries, including their continued market use, a 1913 Ejura conversion, and the expectation that colonial subsidiary coinage would displace them. This is historical monetary context and does not alter AR420.

### R654 editorial expansion
Rattray interprets R654 as evidence of an earlier “system of communism” and connects it to land tenure and clan-wide recovery of private debts. These are Rattray's historical/anthropological claims. The admitted occurrence is narrower: the proverb represents town wealth as having community-wide significance.

### R664 custom note
Rattray records a custom concerning sneezing before a chief and subsequent attribution of good or bad luck. E9 preserves this as context rather than accepting the supernatural causal claim.
