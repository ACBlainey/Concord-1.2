# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-366 — Occurrence Pattern Note: Poverty, Status Bias and Epistemic Discounting

R631, R633, R634, R638 and R643 form a remarkably consistent status-bias sequence.

- R631: a poor person's proverb does not circulate.
- R633: silk worn by the poor is treated as wasted on a tree stump.
- R634: genuine gold on a poor person is called brass.
- R638: good tobacco used by a poor person is treated as inferior residue.
- R643: decorative leaves worn by a poor person are redescribed as a sheep's halter.

The mechanism is stronger than simple material inequality.

The observer's prior model of the person's status changes how the observer evaluates:
- knowledge;
- possessions;
- evidence of value;
- quality;
- presentation.

R634 is especially clear: the source describes **genuine value being cognitively downgraded because it conflicts with the observer's status expectation**.

This provides a source-internal example of epistemic distortion generated by social status.
