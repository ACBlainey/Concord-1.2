# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-150 — Occurrence Pattern Note: Distributed Leadership, Knowledge and Collective Importance

R206 and R210 create a useful distinction inside social organisation.

### Collective constitution of importance
R206 is explicitly interpreted by Rattray as saying that a chief's importance derives from the number of subjects associated with him. Importance is therefore not simply an intrinsic property of the office-holder.

### Knowledge versus signalling role
R210 states that the hen knows dawn has arrived but still looks to the cock to announce it. Relevant knowledge and recognised communicative function are distributed across different agents.

Together these occurrences suggest that social capability can be structurally distributed:
- a leader's importance can depend on participants;
- an informed agent need not be the recognised signalling agent.

This is recorded only as an occurrence-level organisational pattern. No governance principle or root is imposed upon the corpus.
