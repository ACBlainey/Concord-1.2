## E9-147 — Mechanical Ledger Block 042: Rattray 206–210

206 / C1658 = Y — the feathers make the fowl large; Rattray records an explicit social interpretation that a chief's importance derives from the number of subjects attached to him. Apparent individual importance can therefore be constituted by the supporting collective rather than intrinsic to the office-holder alone.

207 / C1659 = H — feathers remain attached as they grow, and Rattray interprets this as subjects remaining under their chief even as their wealth or importance increases. This is clear evidence of a historical hierarchical norm, but it is not admitted as a transferable ethical prescription without further internal context.

208 / C1956 = H — once the mother hen is caught, the chickens can be gathered easily. Dependency/network leverage is visible, but the source does not supply an ethical application sufficient for admission.

209 / C385 = H — no one says good morning before the cock does. Temporal/social precedence is clear, but a transferable ethical function remains underdetermined.

210 / C1664 = Y — the hen knows dawn has arrived but nevertheless looks to the cock to make it known. Possessing knowledge and holding the socially recognised signalling role are not necessarily the same function.

### AR098 — Rattray 206 / Christaller 1658
**Minimal function:** The practical importance of a leader or office can depend substantially on the participating collective that sustains the role rather than belonging solely to the individual holder.
**Confidence:** very high because Rattray gives the explicit social interpretation.

### AR099 — Rattray 210 / Christaller 1664
**Minimal function:** Possession of relevant knowledge does not necessarily entail possession of the recognised role or authority to announce, coordinate or signal that knowledge.
**Confidence:** high.
