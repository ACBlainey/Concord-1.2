## E9-142 — Mechanical Ledger Block 041: Rattray 201–205

201 / C1644 = Y — drive away the fowl eating a friend's grain because one day it will eat yours. Harm to another's protected interest warrants intervention not only from solidarity but because tolerated harmful conduct may later reach one's own interests.

202 / C1652 = H — the fowl's eye is keen to see a single grain. Fine-grained perception is clear, but no transferable ethical function is explicit enough to admit.

203 / C1655 = H — the fowl asks whether it would have had its fill had darkness not arrived. External stopping conditions and satiation are implied, but the ethical abstraction remains underdetermined.

204 / C1648 = Y — the hen's foot does not kill her chicken. Close physical power or accidental contact within a caregiving relation is represented as bounded against lethal harm to the dependent.

205 / C1646 = Y — a white fowl kept long in a house becomes red with earth and dust. Sustained environment can materially alter outward condition or presentation.

### AR095 — Rattray 201 / Christaller 1644
**Minimal function:** Harmful conduct against another's protected interests should not be ignored merely because one's own interests are not yet affected; tolerated harm can later extend to oneself.
**Confidence:** high.

### AR096 — Rattray 204 / Christaller 1648
**Minimal function:** Power exercised in close relation to a dependent can be constrained so that ordinary contact does not become destructive harm.
**Confidence:** moderate-high.

### AR097 — Rattray 205 / Christaller 1646
**Minimal function:** Prolonged environmental exposure can alter an agent's outward condition even when its underlying identity is unchanged.
**Confidence:** high.
