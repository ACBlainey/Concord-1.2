# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-266 — Mediation Note: Rattray Commentary on Slavery

R443 opens a substantial editorial note in which Rattray describes categories of slavery and offers his own evaluation of Ashanti slavery.

This material requires explicit mediation control.

### What the source securely provides
Rattray records that:
- the proverb says a slave does not choose the master;
- slaves could be born into the condition, bought or captured in war, pledged for debt, or voluntarily enter dependence for protection;
- some slaves could succeed to a master's property;
- people in slave status could be liable to sacrifice or burial with a master.

### What must not be silently inherited
Rattray also argues that African slavery was generally unlike the severe associations later attached to the term and states that slaves were “probably quite well treated.”

That is **Rattray's 1916 interpretation**, not a mechanically established ethical fact of the proverb corpus. It is therefore retained as source mediation rather than adopted as E9's conclusion.

### Extraction consequence
R443 and R444 are classified from the concrete agency relations stated by the proverbs:
- inability to choose the master;
- inability independently to choose participation in war.

No positive or negative global evaluation of the historical institution is required to record those agency constraints.
