# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-293 — Mechanical Ledger Block 100: Rattray 496–500

496 / C1489 = Y — the proverb says that if one swears the father's oath, one should also swear the mother's. Rattray's extended note describes oath procedure as moving a dispute from private conflict into public adjudication, with investigation, open court when answered, and hierarchical appeal. The secure occurrence is that structured procedure can convert escalating private conflict into accountable public resolution.

497 / C170 = Y — no one can remove a woman's loin-cloth without her knowledge. The proverb explicitly recognises the affected person's awareness in an intimate bodily action; such an intervention cannot be treated as occurring independently of the person's knowledge.

498 / C23 = H — delayed return from bathing is attributed to dressing/decorating oneself. This is an inference about another's conduct but supplies no sufficiently secure ethical function.

499 / C25 = H — the proverb and Rattray's gloss depend on a gendered norm about conduct “unbefitting” women and predicted trouble. It is preserved as historical gender-norm evidence, not converted into a transferable ethical prescription.

500 / C27 = H — “all women are alike” is a categorical generalisation without a sufficiently specific or defensible ethical function.

### AR304 — R496/C1489
Structured adjudicative procedure can remove an escalating dispute from private confrontation and place it into public investigation and review.

### AR305 — R497/C170
An intimate action affecting another person's body cannot be treated as independent of that person's awareness.
