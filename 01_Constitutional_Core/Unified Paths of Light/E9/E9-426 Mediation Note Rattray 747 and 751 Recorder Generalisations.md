# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-426 — Mediation Note: Rattray 747 and 751 — Recorder Generalisations

Two items in this range contain unusually strong recorder-level generalisations that must not be allowed to enter the Akan ethical evidence layer.

### R747/C317
The proverb is translated: “No one wishes well for another.”

Rattray then argues that it is less selfish than it appears and offers an extended account of “primitive man,” dangerous life, limited sentiment and preference that available luck come to oneself.

E9 treatment:
- preserve the translation;
- preserve Rattray's interpretation as mediation history;
- do not treat his evolutionary/social characterisation as Akan doctrine;
- retain the item as H because the literal proverb and recorder qualification do not yield a sufficiently secure transferable mechanism.

### R751/C413
The proverb advises yielding the road to someone who claims superior speed.

Rattray then describes this as “typical” of an allegedly climate-enervated “African mind.”

E9 treatment:
- quarantine the racialised explanatory claim;
- derive the occurrence only from the proverb itself;
- do not use the commentary as evidence about African psychology, motivation, development or civilisation.

This note is important for E9's anti-pan-African and mediation controls: historical source value does not license adoption of the recorder's population-level theories.
