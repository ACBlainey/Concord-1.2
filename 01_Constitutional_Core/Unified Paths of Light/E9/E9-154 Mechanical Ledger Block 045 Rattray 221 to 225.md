## E9-154 — Mechanical Ledger Block 045: Rattray 221–225

221 / C1642 = Y — a fowl that comes early to roost does not get its owner into debt. Timely return or fulfilment of an expected obligation prevents avoidable costs from falling on the responsible party.

222 / C1650 = Z — the fowl and turkey quarrel. Conflict is stated, but no transferable ethical function is supplied.

223 / C1815 = H — the speaker kills the birds associated with announcing dawn to see what dawn will do; Rattray records a causal belief concerning birds and dawn. The item is epistemically interesting but the visible source does not establish a sufficiently clear ethical norm.

224 / C2331 = Y — Rattray explicitly presents excessive self-regard for one's wisdom sarcastically: being “too wise” can lead into supreme folly. Confidence in one's intelligence requires limits and correctability.

225 / C1568 = Y — when a host kills a fowl for a visitor, the visitor is said effectively to have eaten the fowl left at home. Hospitality creates an expectation of reciprocal future provision rather than a one-way transfer.

### AR109 — Rattray 221 / Christaller 1642
**Minimal function:** Timely fulfilment of an expected return or responsibility can prevent avoidable costs from being imposed on the party accountable for the agent or resource.
**Confidence:** moderate-high.

### AR110 — Rattray 224 / Christaller 2331
**Minimal function:** Excessive confidence in one's own wisdom can itself produce serious error; epistemic capability requires humility and correctability.
**Confidence:** very high because Rattray supplies the explicit sarcastic interpretation.

### AR111 — Rattray 225 / Christaller 1568
**Minimal function:** Hospitality or material provision can operate within an expectation of future reciprocity rather than being understood solely as unilateral loss.
**Confidence:** high.
