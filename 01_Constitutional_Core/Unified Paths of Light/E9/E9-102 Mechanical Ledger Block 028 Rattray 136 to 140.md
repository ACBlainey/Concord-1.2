## E9-102 — Mechanical Ledger Block 028: Rattray 136–140

136 / no Christaller identifier = Y — the two-headed crocodile has one shared belly yet its heads fight over which throat receives food. Rattray explicitly describes the metaphor as expressing communal ideas. The occurrence exposes conflict produced by separately claimed access to a genuinely shared outcome/resource.

137 / C3405 = H — criticism of the crocodile is voiced only after the river has been safely crossed. This plausibly concerns opportunistic criticism after dependence/risk ends, but no explanatory ethical gloss is supplied.

138 / C1739 = H — even a crab living where gold dust is found eats the food associated with poverty. Proximity to wealth does not necessarily imply access to wealth, but the ethical implication remains underdetermined.

139 / C1740 = H — a crab does not give birth to a bird. The saying indicates continuity/category inheritance, but no sufficiently explicit ethical function is supplied here.

140 / C1741 = Y — because the crab lives near the river it knows the river's language. Sustained proximity/experience is explicitly connected with contextual knowledge.
