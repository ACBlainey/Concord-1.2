# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-223 — Mechanical Ledger Block 072: Rattray 356–360

356 / C343 = H — no one sends a child on an errand and then checks whether the child is pleased. A hierarchy/obedience relation is visible, but the source does not establish a sufficiently secure transferable ethical proposition.

357 / C1577 = Y — Rattray explicitly glosses the proverb as tradition being handed down: children sing songs left to them by elders. Cultural knowledge and practice can persist through intergenerational transmission.

358 / C578 = Y — when a child cries, the child is not bound to a log. Distress-signalling is met without punitive immobilisation; the minimal occurrence supports non-destructive response to dependent distress.

359 / C587 = Y — when a child does what is not usually done, the child perceives what is not usually perceived. Departing from ordinary practice can expose an agent to consequences or knowledge not encountered under ordinary conduct.

360 / C586 = Y — Rattray explicitly says a child acting as a grown person does is punished as a grown person is punished. Taking on a higher-capability role can expose an agent to the consequences associated with that role.

### AR210 — R357/C1577
Knowledge, practices and cultural forms can be transmitted across generations and retained by later participants.

### AR211 — R358/C578
Dependent distress should not automatically be answered by destructive or punitive restraint.

### AR212 — R359/C587
Departure from ordinary practice can expose an agent to consequences or information not encountered through ordinary conduct.

### AR213 — R360/C586
Assuming the actions of a higher-responsibility role can expose an agent to consequences associated with that role.
