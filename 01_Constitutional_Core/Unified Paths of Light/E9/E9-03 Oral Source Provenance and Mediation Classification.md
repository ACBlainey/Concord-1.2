# Unified Paths of Light — E9
## E9-03 — Oral Source Provenance and Mediation Classification

## Purpose
Add the provenance controls needed when the surviving research object is an oral tradition represented through recorded or published witnesses.

## E9 provenance fields
Each admitted occurrence should preserve, where available:

1. E9 occurrence ID
2. tradition/community
3. source form
4. original-language form
5. literal translation
6. interpretive translation or gloss
7. collector/recorder
8. translator
9. collection location
10. collection date/period
11. publication source and date
12. speaker/performer/informant where published
13. performance or use context
14. variant/cognate identifier
15. normative classification
16. scope
17. minimally normalised functional proposition
18. interpretation dependence
19. historical-contact flag
20. mediation grade
21. confidence
22. quarantine/HOLD reason where applicable

## Mediation grades
**M1 — Directly attested with strong context:** original-language occurrence plus documented speaker/performance/use context.

**M2 — Attested collection:** original-language occurrence and translation/gloss preserved, but performance context limited.

**M3 — Mediated traditional witness:** credible published traditional material, but original-language/contextual chain incomplete.

**M4 — Secondary paraphrase:** scholar reports or summarises an ethical belief without an adequately recoverable occurrence.

M4 may inform background interpretation but should not normally enter the primary occurrence ledger.

## Variant control
Near-identical variants should be linked rather than multiplied as separate ethical discoveries. Materially different contextual uses may remain separate occurrences where they alter function.

## Translation control
Where literal wording and culturally interpreted meaning diverge, both should be retained. The English gloss must not silently replace the source occurrence.

## Temporal control
Collection date is not assumed to equal origin date. Claims of antiquity require independent historical evidence.
