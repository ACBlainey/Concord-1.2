# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-1289 — Oral-Medium Methodological Lessons

### Purpose

E9 was the first Unified Paths experiment to place an oral/proverbial tradition at the centre of the derivation. The experiment therefore tested not only Akan ethics but the methodology itself.

### Lessons

**1. The attested occurrence must remain the evidential unit.**  
An oral proverb can have variants, changing performance contexts and uncertain antiquity. The experiment must not reconstruct an imagined pristine original and then treat it as observed evidence.

**2. Provenance must be multidimensional.**  
Community, recorder, date, language, translation, performance/use context, variant relation, mediation and contact risk are analytically distinct.

**3. Oral-medium saturation differs from textual exhaustion.**  
Exhausting a printed witness closes that witness, not the living tradition. For distributed traditions, cross-community and source-family saturation may be more meaningful than page-level exhaustion.

**4. Proverbs produce unusually dense contextual dependence.**  
Many formulations are descriptive, ironic, strategic, prudential or socially diagnostic rather than first-order ethical rules. Zero-yield and HOLD classifications are therefore necessary evidence, not extraction failures.

**5. Operators and structural conditions can become unusually visible.**  
E9 repeatedly exposed timing, dependency, reference point, bounded inference, irreversibility, bargaining structure, network effects, resilience and local-to-system propagation. Forcing all such material into roots would distort the architecture.

**6. Functional topology survives medium change better than labels or partitions.**  
E9 froze eight substantive families rather than reproducing the seven-function X partition, yet recovered all seven X functions.

### Methodological consequence

Future oral-medium experiments should explicitly distinguish:
- witness closure;
- community coverage;
- variant coverage;
- ethical saturation;
- functional convergence.
