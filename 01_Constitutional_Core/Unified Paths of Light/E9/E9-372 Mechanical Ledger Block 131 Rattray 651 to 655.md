# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-372 — Mechanical Ledger Block 131: Rattray 651–655

651 / C2936 = Y — money is not something obtained merely by putting out one's hand and finding it. Acquisition of scarce resources requires some causal pathway rather than wish or effortless availability.

652 / C2938 = Y — money sent out returns with gain; Rattray explicitly glosses this as earning interest. Productive deployment of resources can generate additional resources rather than merely preserving the original stock.

653 / C2939 = H — “money is sharper than a sword.” The proverb clearly attributes exceptional power to money, but the mechanism and normative direction are too underdetermined for a precise transferable occurrence.

654 / C2942 = Y — gold dust in a town is said to be for the whole people. Rattray expands this into claims about communal property, land tenure and collective clan liability. The secure proverb-level occurrence is that locally held wealth can be represented as carrying community-wide claims or significance rather than purely isolated individual ownership.

655 / C2944 = Y — prudence arrives as one's gold dust is nearly exhausted. Awareness of approaching scarcity can change resource behaviour, but delayed prudence leaves less room for adaptation than earlier stewardship.

### AR424 — R651/C2936
Scarce resources are not acquired by desire alone; practical acquisition requires an effective causal pathway.

### AR425 — R652/C2938
Resources can be productively deployed so that use generates additional resources rather than merely consuming or storing them.

### AR426 — R654/C2942
Some locally held resources can carry community-wide claims or significance rather than functioning as purely isolated private holdings.

### AR427 — R655/C2944
Approaching scarcity can induce prudent resource behaviour, but prudence activated only near exhaustion leaves reduced adaptive margin.
