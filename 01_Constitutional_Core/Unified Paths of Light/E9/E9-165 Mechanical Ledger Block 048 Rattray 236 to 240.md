## E9-165 — Mechanical Ledger Block 048: Rattray 236–240

236 / C2776 = Y — when the hawk sits on her eggs, she leaves another hawk to keep watch in the sky. A necessary protective function can be delegated while the primary agent performs an incompatible caregiving task.

237 / C1610 = Y — if one sees a single parrot feather, one should not throw a stone at it because it comes from where there are many more. An apparently isolated member or trace can imply a larger associated group, so action should account for unseen collective context.

238 / C1607 = H — because the parrot is loud-voiced, no one catches it to eat it. The source clarifies the idiom as loud-mouthed/blustering, but whether this functions as deterrence, social cost, or another norm is insufficiently explicit.

239 / C1608 = Y — the parrot hides its eggs in a tree hollow because it does not want others to see them. Valuable or vulnerable dependents can be protected by limiting visibility and access.

240 / C2480 = Y — one bird in the hand is better than ten in the sky. A secured present resource can rationally outweigh a larger but unrealised or inaccessible possibility.
