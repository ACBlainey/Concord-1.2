# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-1287 — Evolutionary/Survival Hypothesis Reassessment After Akan

### Hypothesis

A second working hypothesis proposes that recurrent ethical functions may be selected culturally or developmentally because groups unable to regulate harm, deception, domination, neglect, resource misuse, unaccountability or capability failure face reduced long-run cooperation and persistence.

### E9-relevant observations

The Akan architecture independently preserves:
- harm prevention;
- reality-responsive information;
- viable agency under dependency;
- causal accountability;
- care/reciprocity;
- resource sufficiency and entitlement;
- capability development;
- continuity/adaptation.

It also explicitly preserves structural concerns involving:
- dependency and bargaining;
- power contestability;
- support networks;
- coordinated plurality;
- local-to-system propagation;
- resilience;
- robustness under stress.

These are compatible with a survival/cooperation interpretation because they regulate recurrent failure modes of multi-agent systems.

### Alternative explanations remain

The same recurrence could arise through:
- cultural diffusion;
- shared human psychology;
- convergent social learning;
- similar ecological constraints;
- methodological abstraction;
- collector/translation effects;
- combinations of these.

### Status

E9 increases the plausibility of the survival-selection hypothesis but does not uniquely identify it as the causal explanation.

A stronger test would require:
- more historically and geographically separated corpora;
- explicit comparison of ethical structures with cooperation/survival failure modes;
- non-human or synthetic multi-agent derivations;
- adversarial tests seeking stable societies lacking one or more proposed functions.
