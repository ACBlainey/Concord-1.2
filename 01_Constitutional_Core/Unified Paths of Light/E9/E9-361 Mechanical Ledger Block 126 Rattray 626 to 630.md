# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-361 — Mechanical Ledger Block 126: Rattray 626–630

626 / C1353 = Y — Rattray explains that a poor person with nothing to lose may become desperate and perform acts that a person with something at stake would hesitate to perform. Apparent boldness produced by desperation should not be confused with secure capability or unconstrained power.

627 / C1354 = H — “poverty is madness,” cross-referenced to R622. The saying records a strong association between deprivation and perceived irrationality, but its literal/general psychological claim is too broad for direct admission.

628 / C1357 = Y — “on the day of poverty” one discovers who is truly a friend. Relationships may be more reliably evidenced by behaviour under another person's adversity than by nominal affiliation during easier conditions.

629 / C1359 = Y — the poor household's only slave girl tires quickly, yet if she does not work the household does not eat. A system dependent on a single overburdened worker is vulnerable because continuity rests on capability that is both indispensable and exhaustible.

630 / C1360 = H — “the poor man does not get in a rage.” The relation between poverty and emotional restraint is stated but not sufficiently explained to support a transferable ethical occurrence.

### AR402 — R626/C1353
Risk-taking generated by desperation and absence of remaining stakes should not automatically be interpreted as secure power or unconstrained capability.

### AR403 — R628/C1357
The reliability of a relationship can be revealed by whether support persists when one party becomes vulnerable or materially disadvantaged.

### AR404 — R629/C1359
A system dependent on one indispensable but exhaustible worker is structurally vulnerable to overload and continuity failure.
