### Emerging anti-circumvention structure

AR567 adds a useful distinction: formal compliance with the literal boundary of a rule can still violate the rule's substance when a dependent derivative is used to defeat its purpose.

This should remain separate from ordinary consistency during normalisation. Later compression can test whether:
- consistency across equivalent cases;
- preservation of rule purpose;
- and resistance to derivative circumvention

are children of one parent or materially distinct structures.

### Internal-state epistemic boundary

AR565 explicitly marks a limit on knowledge of other agents: internal mental states require inference. This complements the broader evidence architecture and is especially important because it constrains motive attribution. Observable behaviour, outcomes and testimony can inform inference, but they are not direct access to another agent's thoughts.
