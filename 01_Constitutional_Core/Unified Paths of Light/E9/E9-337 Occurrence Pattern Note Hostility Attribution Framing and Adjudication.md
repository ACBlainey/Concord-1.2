# Unified Paths of Light — E9

## E9-337 — Occurrence Pattern Note: Hostility, Attribution, Framing and Adjudication

R572, R573, R576, R577 and R578 form an unusually coherent epistemic cluster around hostility.

R572: dislike expands blame beyond specific demonstrated acts.

R573: hatred produces malignant evaluative speech.

R576: a panel composed entirely of enemies cannot provide an independent route to innocence.

R577: the enemy who speaks first can shape reception of the target's later truthful account.

R578: hostile imitation distorts the target's actual performance.

These occurrences distinguish several mechanisms by which hostility can corrupt evaluation:
1. over-attribution of fault;
2. hostile commentary;
3. decision-maker conflict of interest;
4. first-framing effects;
5. distorted representation.

The cluster is stronger than a generic claim that “hatred is bad.” It identifies concrete pathways by which prior hostility can reduce epistemic and adjudicative reliability.
