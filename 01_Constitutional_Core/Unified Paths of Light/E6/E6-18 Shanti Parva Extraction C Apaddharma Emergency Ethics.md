# Unified Paths of Light — E6
## E6-18 — Śānti Parva Extraction C: Āpaddharma and Emergency Ethics

## Status
**BLIND INTERNAL EXTRACTION.**

Āpaddharma material is particularly important because it tests whether ordinary rules are treated as mechanically absolute under conditions of survival, scarcity and institutional breakdown.

| ID | Source unit | Class/scope | Behavioural proposition | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HS-041 | Āpaddharma | D,G/R | Ordinary duties may require contextual modification when literal compliance would defeat survival or the purpose of the duty. | Emergency exception, not general relativism. |
| HS-042 | Āpaddharma | D,G | Distinguish genuine necessity from convenience before invoking exceptional permission. | Functional safeguard against opportunistic exception. |
| HS-043 | Āpaddharma | P,G | Emergency departure from ordinary rules should be limited to the conditions that make departure necessary. | Proportional exception principle. |
| HS-044 | Āpaddharma | P,G | Survival can have ethical priority where loss of life would eliminate the capacity to fulfil future duties. | Context-sensitive; not unlimited self-preservation. |
| HS-045 | Āpaddharma | D,G | When duties conflict, compare the seriousness and reversibility of the harms created by available choices. | Functional synthesis; section-level audit later. |
| HS-046 | Āpaddharma | P,G | Necessity does not erase all restraint; avoid unnecessary additional injury while responding to emergency. | Strong boundary condition. |
| HS-047 | Āpaddharma | P,R | Persons entrusted with dependants have heightened obligations during scarcity or danger. | Household/ruler examples. |
| HS-048 | Āpaddharma | D,G | Rules designed for stable conditions may fail under radically changed conditions and should be interpreted by function as well as form. | Important but must not be modernised into unrestricted consequentialism. |
| HS-049 | Āpaddharma | P,G | Exceptional conduct should cease when the exceptional condition ends. | Prevents emergency normalisation. |
| HS-050 | Āpaddharma | P,G | Conduct undertaken under genuine necessity should be judged with attention to constrained alternatives. | Responsibility is contextual, not erased. |
| HS-051 | Āpaddharma | C,G | Extreme deprivation can change the feasible moral choice set and increase pressures toward otherwise prohibited conduct. | Descriptive causal proposition. |
| HS-052 | Āpaddharma | P,K/G | Those with greater capacity to preserve others during crisis carry correspondingly greater responsibility. | Capacity-sensitive emergency duty. |
| HS-053 | Āpaddharma | D,G | Conflicting rules may require preservation of the more fundamental purpose rather than literal compliance with every surface form. | Higher/lower duty reasoning. |
| HS-054 | Āpaddharma | P,G | Emergency reasoning should remain answerable to later ethical evaluation rather than becoming self-justifying. | Provisional synthesis; provenance audit required. |
| HS-055 | Āpaddharma | N,D | Hard cases can legitimately expose that ethical judgement requires interpretation rather than mechanical rule following. | Narrative/case-based pedagogy. |

## Internal note
The emergency material strongly supports a distinction between ethical constraint and rule implementation: exceptional conditions can change application without implying that all constraints disappear.
