# Unified Paths of Light — E6
## E6-58 — N3 Architectural Separation C: Recurrent Conditions

The E6 corpus repeatedly changes ethical application in response to recurrent features of the situation.

### HC1 — Vulnerability / Reduced Effective Agency
Dependency, coercion exposure, illness, social weakness or inability to protect one's own interests.

### HC2 — Entrusted or Concentrated Power
Ruler, elder, teacher, office-holder, wealth-controller or informal dominant actor.

### HC3 — Uncertainty / Incomplete Knowledge
Disputed fact, hidden identity, unreliable report, unknown consequence or limited competence.

### HC4 — Conflict / Active Threat
Aggression, retaliation, war, coercive danger or competing protective claims.

### HC5 — Scarcity / Material Dependency
Insufficient resources, debt-like dependency, food/water need, entrusted property or competing claims.

### HC6 — Developmental / Capability Difference
Learner readiness, skill, judgement, role competence or reduced ability.

### HC7 — Emergency / Survival Pressure
Materially altered conditions in which ordinary implementation may fail its underlying function.

### HC8 — Commitment / Relational Reliance
Promises, family dependency, alliance, hospitality, instruction or reciprocal expectation.

### HC9 — Status / Reputation / Group Pressure
Birth, prestige, public opinion, honour, shame, praise/blame or institutional reputation.

### HC10 — Temporal and Intergenerational Consequence
Delayed harm, resource continuity, escalation, future obligation or long-run disposition formation.

### HC11 — Institutional / Collective Coordination
Decisions affecting groups through offices, counsel, delegated agents or shared rules.

## Result
The conditions explain substantial variation that would otherwise be mistaken for contradictory principles.

Context changes application; it does not automatically erase the underlying constraint.
