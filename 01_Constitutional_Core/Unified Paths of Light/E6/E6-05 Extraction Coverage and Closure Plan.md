## Extraction order
1. Bhagavad Gītā, chapters 1–18.
2. Principal Upaniṣads in the frozen E6-02 list.

4. Mahābhārata Anuśāsana Parva.
5. Predeclared narrative sample from other Mahābhārata strata.
6. Structured Rāmāyaṇa narrative sample.

The order is administrative, not a hierarchy of authority.

## Resolution
Bhagavad Gītā: chapter/verse-level where practicable.  
Upaniṣads: section/passage-level.  
Śānti/Anuśāsana: section/thematic-unit level with stable locators.  
Large narrative strata: episode-level with explicit normative-status coding.

Do not claim line-by-line exhaustion where the extraction resolution does not support it.

## Running audit
At the end of each corpus block record:
- coverage achieved;
- provenance resolution;
- repeated proposition families;
- ambiguous narrative cases;
- translation-sensitive terms;
- removals/revisions;
- whether new functional information is still appearing.

## Closure modes
A block may close through:
1. declared corpus exhaustion at its chosen resolution;
2. predefined sample exhaustion;
3. marginal-information closure only where the sampling plan explicitly permits it.

“Predicted root found” is never a closure criterion.

## Normalisation gate
No cross-tradition comparison during extraction or normalisation.

After primary extraction:
1. provenance audit;
2. overcomplete E6-only catalogue;
3. duplicate compression;
4. parent-child compression;
5. root/operator/context separation;
6. minimum-stability attack;
7. E6 internal freeze;
8. only then score the registered X1–X7 prediction.
