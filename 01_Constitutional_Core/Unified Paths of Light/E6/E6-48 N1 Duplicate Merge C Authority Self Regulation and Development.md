# Unified Paths of Light — E6
## E6-48 — N1 Duplicate Merge C: Authority, Self-Regulation and Development

### Authority/institutions
P52 Authority is ethically evaluated by function, not possession/status alone.
P53 Entrusted power carries heightened responsibility because its errors scale outward.
P54 Select consequential agents for competence and integrity.
P55 Preserve access to truthful, unwelcome corrective counsel.
P56 Do not punish or suppress correction merely because it challenges power.
P57 Constrain and review delegated/informal power where predation or corruption is possible.
P58 Confidentiality/secrecy requires a legitimate function.
P59 Institutions need effective mechanisms of correction, not recognition of wrong alone.
P60 Exceptional authority should remain purpose-limited.
P61 Preserve institutional continuity beyond momentary preference.
P62 Institutional reputation does not automatically outweigh direct individual harm.

### Self-regulation
P63 Regulate anger before consequential action.
P64 Regulate greed, covetousness and compulsive desire.
P65 Resist pride, status intoxication and domination by praise/blame.
P66 Examine one's own motives and conduct.
P67 Cultivate patience and steadiness under gain/loss.
P68 Avoid destructive extremes.
P69 Distinguish ethical discipline from performative austerity.
P70 Recognise repeated desire/action as disposition-forming.

### Development/capability
P71 Ethical capability can develop through practice.
P72 Match instruction to readiness and capacity.
P73 Match responsibility to actual capability and conditions.
P74 Permit staged learning and recognition of ignorance.
P75 Encourage inquiry rather than passive repetition alone.
P76 Test learning through conduct/application.
P77 Preserve learner deliberation rather than destabilising it for display.
P78 Reopen conclusions when new information changes feasible action.
P79 Distinguish capability from wisdom.

## Result
Authority and development remain internally complex enough that premature fusion would lose functional distinctions.
