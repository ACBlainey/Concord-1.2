## Strong internal Śānti families
Without external comparison, the block repeatedly supports:
- authority as protective/entrusted function;
- restraint of stronger actors from predation;
- competence and integrity in consequential roles;
- truthful counsel and resistance to flattery;
- contextual/proportionate coercion;
- hearing and discrimination before judgement;
- resource extraction bounded by productive/social continuity;
- protection of vulnerable/dependent persons;
- emergency modification of ordinary rules without total suspension of restraint;
- self-control under power;
- non-injury, compassion, generosity and reduced greed;
- practice/conduct as a test of claimed ethical status;
- gradual development and learner-sensitive instruction;
- correction, acknowledgement and reform.

## Important tensions preserved
1. Non-injury versus legitimate coercive protection.
2. Truth/open counsel versus functionally justified confidentiality.
3. Ordinary duty versus emergency exception.
4. Role-specific hierarchy versus conduct/capability-based assessment.
5. Renunciant critique of possession versus ruler/householder stewardship.
6. Forgiveness versus protective judgement.

No attempt has been made to resolve these by importing an external ethical architecture.

## Provenance warning
This block is deliberately **not yet publication-grade at passage locator resolution**. Several records are thematic syntheses across the large Śānti corpus.

Before E6 normalisation, a provenance audit must:
- attach stable section/chapter locators to retained HS propositions;
- distinguish direct passage evidence from repeated-family synthesis;
- revise or remove unsupported modern formulations;
- verify emergency-ethics claims especially carefully;
- preserve translation-sensitive terminology;
- refuse a false claim of line-by-line exhaustion.

This repeats the lesson learned from E5: provenance debt is identified **before** normalisation rather than discovered after freeze.
