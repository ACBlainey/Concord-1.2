## Narrative contribution
The sample does not merely repeat didactic rules. It exposes interactions among them:
- formal authority versus legitimate authority;
- formal participation versus manipulated agency;
- truth of words versus intended belief;
- confidentiality/concealment versus exploitative deception;
- peace-seeking versus protective force;
- loyalty versus complicity;
- procedure versus substantive protection;
- immediate victory versus downstream moral cost;
- individual recognition of wrong versus institutional capacity to correct it.

## Particularly important methodological observation
The narrative material suggests that ethical function often resides in **relations among principles under conditions**, not in isolated rules.

This observation is internal to the E6 corpus and is recorded without comparison to earlier Concord or Unified Paths models.
