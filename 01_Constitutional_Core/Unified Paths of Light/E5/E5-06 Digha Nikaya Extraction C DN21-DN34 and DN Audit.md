## High-repetition material

The virtue formula recurring across early DN texts creates repeated evidence for:
- non-killing;
- non-stealing;
- truthful/non-harmful speech;
- honest livelihood;
- restraint from coercive violence.

Occurrences remain evidentially useful but must be deduplicated only during normalisation.

## Distinctive Dīgha domains

The first pass also yields:
- critique of status/lineage as ethical authority;
- epistemic humility and evidence;
- motive and attention regulation;
- consultation and institutional continuity;
- anti-coercion;
- welfare-sensitive governance;
- deprivation→crime→punishment→escalation causal chains;
- reciprocal household/social duties;
- capability-sensitive employment;
- future-oriented resource management;
- knowledge preservation after founder/authority loss.

## Tension register opened

Later analysis must retain rather than erase:
- monastic celibacy versus general sexual ethics;
- hierarchical historical social roles versus conduct-based worth;
- supernatural/cosmological framing versus separable behavioural function;
- deference to teachers versus epistemic challenge;
- institutional continuity versus correctability;
- ascetic restraint versus general-agent welfare.

## Audit warning

The E5 corpus is much larger than the Dīgha Nikāya. No normalised E5 principle may yet be frozen.

The next extraction stage is the Majjhima Nikāya.
