# Unified Paths of Light — E5
## E5-02 — Early Buddhist Corpus Boundary and Source Hierarchy

## Status

**CORPUS BOUNDARY FROZEN BEFORE EXTRACTION.**

# 1. Primary derivation corpus

E5 will derive primarily from the **Pāli Sutta Piṭaka**, with the following boundary:

### Core four Nikāyas
- Dīgha Nikāya (DN)
- Majjhima Nikāya (MN)
- Saṃyutta Nikāya (SN)
- Aṅguttara Nikāya (AN)

### Early/compact Khuddaka material admitted to the primary boundary
- Dhammapada (Dhp)
- Udāna (Ud)
- Itivuttaka (Iti)
- Sutta Nipāta (Snp)
- Theragāthā (Thag)
- Therīgāthā (Thig)

These six are treated as a predefined extension stratum rather than opportunistically added evidence.

# 2. Why this boundary

The four main Nikāyas form the large synoptic discourse corpus. The selected six Khuddaka works provide compact verse/discourse material while avoiding treating the entire heterogeneous Khuddaka collection as equally early or equally suitable for a first derivation.

The corpus is intentionally large enough to challenge extraction and compression, but bounded before results are known.

# 3. Material withheld from primary derivation

## Vinaya Piṭaka
Withheld initially.

Reason: Vinaya is ethically rich but heavily institution-specific. It will be valuable as a **post-freeze institutional holdout**, testing whether principles derived from discourses can regenerate monastic procedural ethics without having been trained on them.

## Abhidhamma Piṭaka
Withheld.

Reason: systematic doctrinal analysis should not determine the first behavioural catalogue. It may later serve as a conceptual/developmental validation layer where relevant.

## Later Khuddaka material
Withheld from primary derivation unless a later protocol explicitly opens it as validation material.

## Commentaries and post-canonical literature
Withheld until after freeze.

## Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna, Chan/Zen and later regional traditions
Strictly withheld during E5 primary derivation. These are potential later cross-developmental tests, not evidence for the initial early-Buddhist catalogue.

# 4. Translation/source hierarchy

Preferred working source:
1. stable modern translations with passage identifiers and access to Pāli;
2. alternate scholarly translations where wording materially affects an ethical proposition;
3. Pāli lexical comparison where translation ambiguity is consequential;
4. cross-language early parallels only when needed for textual robustness or later validation.

Translation differences must not be silently harmonised.

# 5. Parallel-text rule

Chinese Āgama, Sanskrit, Tibetan, Gāndhārī and other parallels are **not** used to enlarge the primary extraction merely because they support a proposition.

They may later test whether a proposition is:
- Pāli-specific;
- shared across early recensions;
- translation-sensitive;
- historically robust.

# 6. Unit of extraction

Each record should contain:

- E5 occurrence ID;
- canonical passage ID;
- source collection;
- minimal contextual passage description;
- behavioural proposition;
- normative status;
- scope: general / monastic / lay / ruler / household / interpersonal / developmental / epistemic / other;
- intention relevance;
- consequence relevance;
- uncertainty/translation note;
- provisional local cluster.

# 7. Closure

Primary extraction closes by one of the predefined M2 modes:
- corpus exhaustion;
- representative-corpus exhaustion where full extraction is technically impossible but predefined sampling is complete;
- principle saturation with documented marginal yield;
- marginal-information closure.

The closure mode must be declared from evidence, not convenience.

# 8. Corpus-integrity rule

No source may be added after seeing a desired missing principle merely to improve correspondence with another tradition.

Any later opening of withheld material must be labelled validation, not retroactive derivation.

> **The E5 primary corpus boundary is now frozen.**
