The frozen E5 Buddhist kernel was derived without consulting E3, E4, Blainey's Laws, the Decalogue results or the candidate ethical topology.

2. **All seven E5 roots have substantive representation in E4.**
3. **All seven E5 roots have substantive representation in Blainey's Laws.**
4. Differences are concentrated in **partition depth and architecture**, not obvious root contradiction.

## Particularly strong convergence
Across the three source-led derivations and the Concord kernel, recurrent functional regions include:
- protection from unjustified harm;
- truthful/reality-sensitive cognition and communication;
- protected agency against coercion/domination;
- positive concern for need/vulnerability;
- responsible resource relation;
- correction/repair/accountability;
- capability-sensitive development.

## Important asymmetries
The traditions do not package these functions identically.

E3 often preserves more social/institutional attractors.
E4 strongly separates judgement, bounded power and institutional continuity.
E5 compresses more aggressively into seven roots and shifts many social rules into operator/condition-generated applications.
The Concord explicitly adds future-facing civilisational safeguards such as anti-capture and recursive meta-governance.

## Evidential significance
This strengthens—but does not prove—the working hypothesis that recurrent ethical structures may reflect stable constraints on cooperative intelligent systems rather than merely shared wording.

The evidence is stronger than a simple thematic resemblance because:
- the anchors differ;
- the extraction paths differ;
- E5 was source-audited before normalisation;
- E5 roots were frozen before comparison;
- the number and partition of principles were not forced to match.

## Limitation introduced at E5-102

- any later E5 attractor structure is necessarily post-unblinding;
- it must not be presented as independent attractor-level replication.
