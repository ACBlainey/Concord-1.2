# Unified Paths of Light — E5
## E5-115 — Concord Residual Test: Anti-Capture, Recursive Oversight and Safe Spaces

## Question
Do the Concord's apparently distinctive safeguards survive as independent roots after X1–X7 are frozen?

## Anti-Capture
A captured institution or intelligence can violate:
- X3 by dominating effective agency;
- X5 by monopolising/control-distorting resources;
- X7 by suppressing independent development;
- X2 by controlling information;
- X6 by disabling correction.

**Finding:** anti-capture is a high-value systemic rule generated by several roots under concentrated-power and recursive-capability conditions. It need not be an eighth deep root.

## Recursive Oversight
Ordinary correction applied to a single decision is insufficient when the correcting institution can itself fail.

X6 requires preserved correctability. Under recursive governance conditions, preserving X6 requires oversight of the overseer. X2 supplies information integrity and X3 constrains domination.

**Finding:** recursive oversight is a recursive application of X6, not a new ethical direction.

## Safe Spaces / Property / Privacy
These protect bounded domains of agency and legitimate control.

X3 generates protected personal agency/boundaries; X5 generates legitimate resource/possession responsibilities; X1 constrains harmful intrusion.

**Finding:** this law is a constitutional decomposition of multiple roots into a practical protected-domain rule.

## Conclusion
The residual Concord laws remain important precisely because a constitutional kernel is not required to operate at the same compression level as a cross-cultural ethical root kernel.

> **DERIVABLE DOES NOT MEAN DISPENSABLE.**

A civilisational constitution may need explicit decompressed safeguards even when their deepest ethical justification is compositional.
