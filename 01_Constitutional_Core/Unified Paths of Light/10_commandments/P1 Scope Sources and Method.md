# Ten Commandments Comparative Project
## P1 — Scope, Sources and Method

## 1. Primary textual basis

The Decalogue appears in Exodus 20:1–17 and Deuteronomy 5:6–21. The biblical text itself identifies the covenantal material as the “ten words,” but the division into ten numbered commandments differs among later Jewish and Christian traditions.

For that reason this study compares ethical functions rather than relying upon one denominational numbering scheme.

The principal command units are:

- exclusive allegiance to God;
- prohibition of idols;
- prohibition of misuse of the divine name;
- Sabbath observance;
- honour of father and mother;
- prohibition of murder;
- prohibition of adultery;
- prohibition of theft;
- prohibition of false witness;
- prohibition of coveting.

The first group is strongly theological/covenantal; the latter group more directly regulates interpersonal and social conduct. Sabbath bridges worship, restraint, labour and social ordering.

## 3. Comparison discipline

A match is not established because two statements can be made compatible through broad interpretation.

**D — Direct:** substantially the same behavioural constraint.

**S — Strong derived:** one is a clear operational instance or immediate extension of the other.

**P — Partial/contextual:** overlap exists but significant content differs.

**N — No clear correspondence:** the rule contains substantial content not generated by the comparator without importing additional assumptions.

The project will also run the comparison in reverse. This prevents a broad Concord principle from appearing to “contain” every narrow command while hiding Concord principles absent from the Decalogue.

## 4. Key question

The central question is not whether both lists happen to contain ten items.

It is:

> **When the Decalogue is stripped only of its historical specificity—but not of its actual ethical content—does it appear to be an earlier branch-level expression of the same deeper ethical structure independently recovered by the Concord and E3, or does it contain materially different roots?**
