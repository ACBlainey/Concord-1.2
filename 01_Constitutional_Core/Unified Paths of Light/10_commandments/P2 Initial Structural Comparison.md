## Initial observation

The Decalogue does not look like ten abstract ethical primitives.

It looks much more like a compact set of **high-value behavioural constraints**, some theological and some interpersonal, which protect several deeper ethical functions.

This is immediately compatible with the emerging fractal interpretation:

**deeper principle → domain-specific rule.**

“Do not murder” is a highly compressed operational branch of Non-Harm.

“Do not bear false witness” is a highly compressed operational branch of Truth/Epistemic Integrity and Justice.

“Do not steal” operationalises agency, property boundaries and stewardship.

“Do not covet” reaches unusually far upstream by constraining the internal motivational state from which several downstream harms can emerge.

## Important non-equivalence

The first commands cannot simply be secularised into Concord rules without loss. Exclusive divine allegiance and prohibition of idolatry are covenantal/theological propositions. Functional analogies to anti-capture, truth or loyalty can be investigated, but they are not direct equivalents.

Likewise, several Concord/E3 fundamentals are not explicit standalone Decalogue commands: broad autonomy, positive care obligations, procedural justice, stewardship, anti-domination and recursive institutional oversight.

This asymmetry is potentially informative. The Decalogue may represent a compact historical/covenantal **branch-level code**, whereas E3 and Blainey's Laws are attempts at deeper substrate-neutral compression.
