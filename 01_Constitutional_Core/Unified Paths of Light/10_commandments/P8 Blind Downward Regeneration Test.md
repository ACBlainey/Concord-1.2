# Ten Commandments Comparative Project
## P8 — Downward Regeneration Test

This test reverses the upward trace.

The target is not to reproduce biblical wording. Starting from the minimal Concord-style roots and applying them to ordinary recurring civilisational domains, the question is whether the secular behavioural functions represented by the Decalogue reappear naturally.

## Domain: lethal interpersonal power

**Non-Harm** applied to irreversible bodily harm yields a strong prohibition against unjustified killing.

**Regenerated function:** protection against murder.

**Decalogue correspondence:** direct.

## Domain: testimony and adjudication

**Truth** applied where statements determine another person's treatment combines with **Non-Harm** to require accurate testimony and resistance to knowingly corrupt evidence.

**Regenerated function:** do not give false evidence against another.

**Decalogue correspondence:** direct and unusually precise.

## Domain: possession and transfer

**Autonomy + Stewardship** applied to controlled resources requires legitimate consent for transfer; **Non-Harm** constrains dispossession.

**Regenerated function:** do not take another's protected resources without legitimate authority or consent.

**Decalogue correspondence:** strong/direct.

## Domain: intimate commitments

**Autonomy + Non-Harm** require consent and boundaries. Once voluntary commitments exist, **Truth/Responsibility** generates fidelity to those commitments and protection against deceptive betrayal.

**Regenerated function:** respect intimate boundaries and commitments; do not violate them through betrayal.

**Decalogue correspondence:** strong functional regeneration of the secular core of adultery prohibition.

## Domain: acquisition desire

Applying **Autonomy + Stewardship + Non-Harm** recursively asks not only whether taking is wrong but what motivational states systematically push agents toward violating another's protected domain. E3's Self-Regulation branch appears here.

**Regenerated function:** cultivate restraint toward acquisitive impulses directed at another's protected goods/relationships.

**Decalogue correspondence:** strong, although regulating desire itself requires a deeper theory of internal ethical responsibility.

## Domain: intergenerational dependency

**Non-Harm** plus positive care/responsibility applied across changing dependency relationships generates duties between generations. E3's Care and Trust branches make this much clearer than the minimal roots alone.

**Regenerated function:** recognise obligations within parent/child and intergenerational relationships.

**Decalogue correspondence:** partial/strong; “honour” is a particular cultural formulation rather than the only possible generated rule.

## Domain: continuous labour and resource use

**Non-Harm + Stewardship**, expanded through Care and Self-Regulation, generate limits against relentless extraction of persons, dependants, animals and resources. Protected rest becomes a plausible implementation.

**Regenerated function:** impose periodic boundaries on labour/extraction and protect rest.

**Decalogue correspondence:** partial/strong with Sabbath's social function; worship and sacred seventh-day specification are not regenerated.

## Domain: ultimate allegiance and worship

The substrate-neutral roots do not independently generate exclusive allegiance to one deity, prohibition of images as idols, or sanctification of a particular divine name.

**Regenerated function:** none equivalent without adding theological premises.

**Decalogue correspondence:** intentionally fails.

## Result

The secular interpersonal core of the Decalogue is substantially regenerable from deeper roots once those roots are applied to the relevant domains.

The regeneration is strongest for:
- murder;
- false witness;
- theft;
- relational fidelity.

It is strong but more interpretive for:
- coveting;
- intergenerational duty;
- Sabbath's social/rest function.

It fails, as it should, to regenerate specifically theological covenantal content.

## Resolution finding

The test strengthens the classification of the Decalogue as predominantly **L3: concrete behavioural constraints**, with some L2 motivational/domain content and irreducible covenantal theology.

E3 occupies the intermediate **L1 functional-attractor layer** and is especially useful for explaining how a very compressed root expands into the Decalogue's domain rules.

This is consistent with, but does not prove, the proposed fractal/generative ethical architecture.
