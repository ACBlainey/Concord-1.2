## Negative and tension evidence

Numbers contains several especially important counterweights to easy convergence claims:

- the gender-asymmetric suspected-adultery procedure;
- severe Sabbath sanction;
- lethal authority-conflict narratives;
- conquest material;
- punitive violence in Numbers 25;
- the Midian war in Numbers 31;
- gender/status asymmetry in vow law;
- inheritance continuity achieved partly through restriction of marriage choice.

These are preserved as data rather than forced into a universal ethical synthesis.

## Methodological observation

Numbers contains an unexpectedly clear example of **recursive legal development**.

Numbers 27 records a petition revealing an inheritance gap and a resulting general rule. Numbers 36 then identifies a secondary consequence of that reform and introduces another adjustment.

At source level the pattern is:

**existing rule → edge case → petition → review → corrected rule → downstream consequence → further correction.**

This may later be relevant to theories of recursive oversight or adaptive law, but those comparisons remain quarantined until E4 freeze.
