## Corpus note

E4 follows the Jewish Tanakh form of Daniel: twelve chapters, without the Greek additions. Daniel combines court narrative and apocalyptic vision. Dream interpretation, miraculous rescue and predictions are theological/literary material and are not converted into empirical rules. Imperial decrees are analysed as institutional acts without assuming the narrator endorses every response.

## Methodological observation

Daniel independently reinforces a source-level institutional issue already visible within Esther: **uncorrectable law is itself a risk multiplier**.

The recurring mechanism is:

**status-sensitive ruler → adviser manipulation → formally valid decree → protected/irrevocable rule → discovered injustice → decision-maker unable to reverse it cleanly.**

Daniel also supplies a distinctive alternative to immediate coercion in chapter 1:

**conscientious disagreement → bounded trial → observable result → revised practical judgement.**

Both structures are retained as E4-internal findings only.
