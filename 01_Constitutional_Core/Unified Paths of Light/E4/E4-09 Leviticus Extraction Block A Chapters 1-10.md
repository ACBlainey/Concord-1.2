# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-09 — Leviticus Extraction Block A: Chapters 1–10

## Status
**PRIMARY EXTRACTION — no cross-anchor comparison.**

## Source-form note

Leviticus 1–10 is dominated by sacrificial, priestly and purity procedure. Under the frozen E4 protocol, prescription alone is not sufficient for substrate-neutral ethical extraction. Technical cultic rules are therefore retained as covered source material but not converted into ethics unless a separable behavioural structure is sufficiently clear.

## Extracted source occurrences

### E4-LEV-001 — Leviticus 1–5
**Source form:** sacrificial / purification legislation  
**Normative status:** ritual and reparative procedure  
**Proposition:** **MOSTLY CONTEXT-BOUND.** Sacrificial mechanisms are not generalised. Several embedded responsibility distinctions are extracted separately below.

### E4-LEV-002 — Leviticus 4:2, 13, 22, 27
**Source form:** wrongdoing legislation  
**Normative status:** explicit differentiated condition  
**Proposition:** Wrongdoing committed unintentionally remains capable of requiring acknowledgement and corrective response.  
**Provisional cluster:** unintended wrongdoing / responsibility.

### E4-LEV-003 — Leviticus 4:13–21
**Source form:** collective wrongdoing law  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** A community may bear responsibility for wrongful conduct even where the failure was initially unrecognised.  
**Provisional cluster:** collective responsibility.

### E4-LEV-004 — Leviticus 5:1
**Source form:** testimony obligation  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** A person possessing relevant evidence may bear responsibility for failing to disclose it when formally called to testify.  
**Provisional cluster:** testimony / positive evidential duty.

### E4-LEV-005 — Leviticus 5:2–5
**Source form:** impurity/guilt legislation  
**Normative status:** mixed ritual and responsibility structure  
**Proposition:** Becoming aware of a previously unrecognised breach can create a duty to acknowledge it rather than rely on prior ignorance indefinitely.  
**Provisional cluster:** knowledge / acknowledgement.

### E4-LEV-006 — Leviticus 5:20–26 [6:1–7 in many Christian numberings]
**Source form:** fraud/property law with ritual remedy  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Fraud, wrongful retention, robbery, extortion and false denial of entrusted property require restitution to the injured party in addition to any religious remedy.  
**Provisional cluster:** property integrity / restitution.

### E4-LEV-007 — Leviticus 5:23–24
**Source form:** restitution law  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Material repair should restore the principal loss and may require an additional compensatory amount.  
**Provisional cluster:** restitution / compensation.

### E4-LEV-008 — Leviticus 6–7
**Source form:** sacrificial administration  
**Normative status:** predominantly cultic/technical  
**Proposition:** **NO ADDITIONAL GENERAL EXTRACTION.**

### E4-LEV-009 — Leviticus 8–9
**Source form:** priestly installation / ritual implementation  
**Normative status:** cultic/technical  
**Proposition:** **NO GENERAL ETHICAL EXTRACTION.**

### E4-LEV-010 — Leviticus 10:1–3
**Source form:** priestly transgression narrative  
**Normative status:** theological sanction  
**Proposition:** **QUARANTINED.** The death of Nadab and Abihu is inseparable from sacred-office and cultic context and is not converted into a general rule.

### E4-LEV-011 — Leviticus 10:8–11
**Source form:** priestly capacity rule  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Persons exercising specialised judgement or teaching responsibility should avoid impairment that compromises their capacity to distinguish and instruct.  
**Notes:** The immediate rule concerns priestly alcohol use; the generalisation is retained narrowly around functional impairment.  
**Provisional cluster:** role capacity / impaired judgement.

### E4-LEV-012 — Leviticus 10:16–20
**Source form:** Moses/Aaron procedural dispute  
**Normative status:** explanation accepted after inquiry  
**Proposition:** Apparent procedural non-compliance should be investigated and the responsible party heard before a judgement is maintained.  
**Provisional cluster:** hearing / correctable judgement.

## Block A observation

The low extraction density is itself important. E4 does not attempt to manufacture ethical propositions from ritual detail merely to maintain a constant yield across books.

The strongest separable material in this block concerns:

- responsibility despite unintentional wrongdoing;
- later acknowledgement after ignorance ends;
- positive duties concerning testimony;
- restitution for fraud and wrongful possession;
- functional capacity in entrusted roles;
- willingness to revise judgement after hearing an explanation.
