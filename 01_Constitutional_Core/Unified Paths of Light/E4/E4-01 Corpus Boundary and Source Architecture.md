# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-01 — Corpus Boundary and Source Architecture

## 1. Experimental population

E4 examines a bounded Jewish scriptural anchor:

> **Tanakh minus the Book of Psalms.**

The corpus is treated according to the Jewish Tanakh structure rather than rearranged into a Christian Old Testament ordering.

The three principal divisions are:

### Torah
Genesis  
Exodus  
Leviticus  
Numbers  
Deuteronomy

### Nevi'im — Prophets
Joshua  
Judges  
Samuel  
Kings  
Isaiah  
Jeremiah  
Ezekiel  
The Twelve

### Ketuvim — Writings
Proverbs  
Job  
Song of Songs  
Ruth  
Lamentations  
Ecclesiastes  
Esther  
Daniel  
Ezra–Nehemiah  
Chronicles

**Psalms is excluded from derivation and reserved as a holdout corpus.**

Where Jewish canonical convention treats books such as Samuel, Kings, The Twelve, Ezra–Nehemiah or Chronicles differently from modern printed subdivisions, source locators may retain familiar book/chapter references while the experiment preserves their canonical grouping.

## 2. Why Psalms is withheld

The original Unified Paths work already extracted ethics from the Psalms.

Including Psalms in E4 derivation would therefore weaken independence by allowing previously studied material into the Jewish anchor.

Instead Psalms provides an unusually valuable internal canonical holdout:

**derive from non-Psalm Tanakh → freeze → test against Psalms.**

No Psalm-derived principle catalogue may be used to guide E4 extraction or normalisation before freeze.

## 3. Why the whole remaining Tanakh is preferable to arbitrary equal-volume sampling

The Tanakh is finite and canonically bounded.

Although substantially larger and more heterogeneous than Psalms, it is sufficiently defined to permit systematic traversal without inventing a word-count equivalence.

E4 therefore uses **corpus exhaustion** as its intended primary closure mode rather than sampling an arbitrary quantity equal to the Psalm corpus.

## 4. Source hierarchy variables

E4 preserves but does not conflate:

- canonical division;
- approximate chronology where responsibly available;
- genre;
- interpretive dependence where demonstrable;
- ethical abstraction level assigned only after extraction.

The Torah's foundational canonical status is recorded as a property of the tradition. It is not converted into an automatic numerical weighting of ethical validity.

## 5. Genre architecture

E4 contains markedly different source forms.

### Legal / covenantal material
May contain dense sequences of behavioural and ritual rules.

### Narrative
Ethical content may require a complete episode rather than an isolated verse.

### Prophetic material
May combine ethical criticism, historical claims, theological material, poetry and prediction.

### Wisdom material
May contain compact sayings, dialogues or extended arguments.

### Poetry and lament
May express behavioural principles indirectly and require contextual caution.

### Genealogical / administrative / historical records
May contain little or no extractable behavioural ethical content.

The method must therefore standardise the extraction criterion rather than source length.

## 6. Ethical Source Unit rule

For E4:

> **An Ethical Source Unit is the smallest contextually sufficient passage, teaching, saying, narrative element or rule from which a distinct behavioural ethical proposition can responsibly be extracted.**

An ESU may consist of one verse, several verses, a legal cluster, a proverb, a speech segment or a narrative episode.

Verse numbering is a locator, not an assumption that one verse equals one ethical unit.

## 7. Inclusion criterion

Include material where a behavioural principle can be separated responsibly from its immediate theological, ritual, historical or narrative form.

Examples of potentially includable domains include:

- harm and protection;
- truth and testimony;
- justice and adjudication;
- property and exchange;
- treatment of vulnerable persons;
- responsibility and entrusted power;
- family and relational conduct;
- resource use;
- labour;
- conflict;
- care;
- restraint;
- social inclusion/exclusion;
- governance and authority.

This list is illustrative, not exhaustive and not a target catalogue.

## 8. Exclusion / quarantine criterion

Material should not be converted into substrate-neutral ethics where doing so would erase its actual function.

Potential examples include:

- purely ritual prescription;
- sacrificial procedure;
- genealogical information;
- purely theological assertion;
- cultic specification;
- historical description without a defensible behavioural proposition;
- divine-command content whose behavioural generalisation cannot be separated without distortion.

Excluded material remains part of source coverage; it is not treated as absent.

## 9. Translation and textual issues

E4 should use a documented modern scholarly translation as the working English text and consult alternative translations or Hebrew lexical information where wording materially affects ethical extraction.

Translation disagreement must be recorded rather than silently resolved toward a preferred ethical interpretation.

The experiment should distinguish:

**textual ambiguity → translation ambiguity → ethical interpretation ambiguity.**

## 10. External validation boundary

Post-biblical Jewish material is excluded from primary derivation.

After the E4 catalogue is frozen, a deliberately diverse sample may be drawn from later Jewish ethical/legal/interpretive traditions to test whether the biblical catalogue:

- recovers the principle;
- requires nuance;
- combines existing principles;
- misses a genuine candidate addition;
- or encounters context-bound material.

This is validation, not retroactive expansion of the derivation corpus.

## 11. Closure

The intended primary closure criterion is:

> **corpus exhaustion of the predefined Tanakh-minus-Psalms derivation corpus.**

If practical or source-access limitations prevent complete traversal, that limitation must be declared and the closure status changed rather than silently described as exhaustive.

> **Corpus boundary frozen before extraction.**
