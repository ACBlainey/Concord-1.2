## E4-44 — Lamentations Extraction and Audit

## Status
**PRIMARY EXTRACTION — no cross-anchor comparison. Psalms remain withheld.**

## Source-form note

Lamentations is catastrophe poetry. It preserves grief, theological interpretation, siege memory, accusations, shame and appeals for justice. Descriptions of atrocity are not prescriptions. Claims that catastrophe is divine punishment remain theological interpretations rather than empirical causal laws. Imprecatory material is not converted into a general permission for revenge.

## Methodological observation

Lamentations adds a perspective that rule-oriented extraction can easily miss: **civilisational harm is multidimensional**.

Catastrophe damages life and property, but also food access, caregiving, dignity, legal process, social trust, cultural practices, civic gathering, memory and the ability of vulnerable people to make unconstrained choices.

This is descriptive evidence about harm topology, not yet a comparison with later Concord concepts.
