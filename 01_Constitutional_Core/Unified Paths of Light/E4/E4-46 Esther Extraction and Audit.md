## Source-form note

Esther is court narrative shaped by imperial power, minority vulnerability, concealment, patronage, decrees and lethal conflict. Actions by protagonists are not automatically normative. The Persian court's coercive gender order, Haman's exterminatory policy and the later killing of enemies are retained as tension evidence rather than universalised. The Hebrew Esther text's distinctive absence of explicit divine speech also makes the narrative particularly useful for analysing human institutional mechanisms without importing causal claims not stated in the text.

## Methodological observation

Esther provides one of E4's clearest institutional demonstrations that **lawfulness and ethical legitimacy are separable**. A formally issued, authenticated and widely distributed decree can remain catastrophically wrong.

It also exposes a second structural problem:

**harmful decision → administrative propagation → originator removed → harmful rule persists → indirect remediation required.**

The source therefore raises corrigibility as an institutional property without requiring comparison to any later Concord framework.
