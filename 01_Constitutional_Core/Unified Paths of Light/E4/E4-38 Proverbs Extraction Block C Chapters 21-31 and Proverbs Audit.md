## Negative and tension evidence

Proverbs contains gendered relational archetypes, corporal-discipline sayings, prosperity-like generalisations and status assumptions. These have not been silently universalised. Wisdom statements are treated as tendencies unless the text clearly presents a categorical behavioural norm.

## Methodological observation

Proverbs materially changes the **resolution** of E4 without yet changing its comparison target.

Legal texts frequently provide rules. Prophets frequently expose institutional failure. Proverbs repeatedly asks how an individual develops the judgement required to use rules and institutions well.

A source-derived sequence appears:

**instruction → attention → practice → external counsel → correction → improved judgement → more reliable action.**

Its inverse also appears:

**self-certainty → rejected correction → impulsive action/speech → repeated error → degraded judgement or trust.**

This developmental layer must be preserved during later compression rather than being merged automatically into generic knowledge or truth principles.
