# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-55 — Provisional Normalised Catalogue A: Life, Truth, Justice and Agency

## Status

**PROVISIONAL NORMALISATION — NOT FROZEN.**

Derived only from E4's 1,565 non-Psalm Tanakh source-occurrence records.

# A. Life, harm and conflict

### P-E4-001 — Protect Life Against Unjustified Killing
Human life receives strong protection against intentional, unjustified destruction.

### P-E4-002 — Distinguish Deliberate Harm, Accident and Negligence
Culpability should track relevant differences in intention, foreseeability and responsibility rather than treating all harmful outcomes identically.

### P-E4-003 — Use Proportionate Response
Response to wrongdoing or threat should not exceed what is justified by the harm, culpability and protective need.

### P-E4-004 — Permit Necessary Protection Without Converting Defence Into Unlimited Retaliation
Protection against active threat can be legitimate while remaining bounded against revenge, plunder or unnecessary extension of harm.

### P-E4-005 — Do Not Transfer Guilt Automatically Across Persons or Generations
Responsibility should attach to relevant agents rather than being inherited merely through family or group membership.

### P-E4-006 — Protect Noncombatants, Captives and Persons No Longer Posing the Original Threat
Conflict status does not erase all constraints on treatment.

### P-E4-007 — Prefer De-Escalation Where Protection Can Be Achieved Without Escalation
Inquiry, mediation, restraint and negotiated resolution can prevent avoidable expansion of conflict.

### P-E4-008 — Repair Harm Where Repair Is Possible
Stopping wrongdoing does not necessarily complete the obligation; restitution, restoration or material repair may remain due.

### P-E4-009 — Do Not Infer Guilt From Suffering
Adverse outcomes are not reliable proof that the sufferer committed wrongdoing.

# B. Truth, knowledge and evidence

### P-E4-010 — Communicate Truthfully
Do not knowingly distort material information through false statement or deceptive representation.

### P-E4-011 — Do Not Give False Testimony or False Accusation
Claims capable of imposing serious consequences on another person require particular protection against fabrication.

### P-E4-012 — Verify Before Consequential Action
Where a claim will justify sanction, conflict or major intervention, relevant evidence should be checked before action.

### P-E4-013 — Hear More Than One Side of a Contested Claim
Initial testimony or first appearance should remain open to challenge by relevant counterevidence and opposing accounts.

### P-E4-014 — Attribute Claims, Actions and Responsibility Accurately
Do not assign an action, motive, statement or consequence to the wrong person or group.

### P-E4-015 — Preserve Independent Corrective Information
Institutions should not suppress, purchase or structurally exclude information merely because it challenges a preferred conclusion.

### P-E4-016 — Do Not Manufacture Reassurance
Information authorities should not conceal serious conditions through comforting but unreliable claims.

### P-E4-017 — Match Confidence to Available Knowledge
Uncertainty, complexity and incomplete causal access should constrain certainty.

### P-E4-018 — Correct Models When Surviving Evidence Contradicts Them
Tradition, consensus or prior theory should not immunise a claim against material counterexample.

### P-E4-019 — Distinguish Technical Capability, Information and Wisdom
Ability to know, build or control something does not itself establish sound judgement about how it should be used.

### P-E4-020 — Preserve Provenance and Durable Records
Consequential claims, commitments, transfers and institutional history benefit from records capable of later verification.

### P-E4-021 — Make Governing Information Intelligible to Those Expected to Follow It
Rules are not fully operational merely because authorities possess them; affected persons require accessible explanation.

### P-E4-022 — Do Not Defend Authority Through Falsehood or Partiality
A valued institution, leader or cause does not become entitled to fabricated evidence or distorted reasoning.

### P-E4-023 — Interpret Distress Speech in Context
Speech produced under extreme grief, fear or suffering should not automatically be interpreted as detached ordinary testimony.

# C. Justice, adjudication and accountability

### P-E4-024 — Judge Impartially
Status, wealth, kinship, group membership or power should not determine the merits of a case.

### P-E4-025 — Reject Bribery and Purchased Judgement
Material inducement must not buy adjudicative or authoritative decisions.

### P-E4-026 — Provide a Meaningful Hearing
Persons exposed to serious accusation or sanction should have a practical opportunity to state and answer the case.

### P-E4-027 — State the Charge or Disputed Claim Clearly
A person cannot meaningfully answer an accusation that remains undefined or continuously shifting.

### P-E4-028 — Give Reasons Relevant to the Decision
Consequential judgement should connect evidence, rule and outcome rather than relying solely on superior power.

### P-E4-029 — Maintain Access to Adjudication
A formal right to appeal or be heard is weak if the relevant authority is practically inaccessible.

### P-E4-030 — Use Competent and Trustworthy Decision-Makers
Entrusted adjudicative roles require relevant knowledge and reliability, not title alone.

### P-E4-031 — Clarify Jurisdiction and Role Boundaries
Authority in one domain does not automatically create unlimited authority in another.

### P-E4-032 — Permit Correction of Erroneous Decisions
Rules and institutions should retain a viable route for correcting discovered error, manipulation or injustice.

### P-E4-033 — Do Not Let Procedure Launder Substantive Wrong
Formal regularity does not make an intrinsically harmful objective ethically legitimate.

### P-E4-034 — Apply Accountability to Officeholders and Insiders
Kinship, institutional loyalty or elite status should not shield wrongdoing from review.

### P-E4-035 — Preserve Dissent and Internal Challenge
Corrective criticism should remain possible within institutions, including criticism directed upward.

### P-E4-036 — Distinguish Accountability From Guaranteed Material Outcome
Justice does not require pretending that consequences always arrive immediately or visibly.

# D. Agency, consent, conscience and relational boundaries

### P-E4-037 — Respect Meaningful Refusal
Power, hierarchy or relationship does not automatically erase another person's capacity to refuse.

### P-E4-038 — Do Not Manufacture Consent Through Coercion
Compliance produced by overwhelming threat or institutional compulsion should not be treated as equivalent to unconstrained agreement.

### P-E4-039 — Respect Conscientious Boundaries
Where possible, serious conscientious disagreement should be addressed without forcing outward conformity through disproportionate punishment.

### P-E4-040 — Use Negotiation or Bounded Trial Where It Can Resolve a Practical Disagreement
A testable compromise may preserve agency while producing evidence about disputed consequences.

### P-E4-041 — Preserve Agency While Providing Protection
Vulnerability can justify assistance and safeguards without automatically transferring all decision-making to the protector.

### P-E4-042 — Treat Commitments as Voluntary and Consequential
Commitments gain ethical force through meaningful undertaking and should not be made or dissolved casually.

### P-E4-043 — Respect Intimate and Relational Boundaries
Sexual or intimate access is not created merely by desire, status or opportunity.

### P-E4-044 — Do Not Force Intimacy Before Reciprocal Desire
Intimate relationship should not be manufactured through pressure where mutual readiness is absent.

### P-E4-045 — Preserve Legitimate Privacy Under Vulnerability
Concealment or control of personal information can be protective where disclosure exposes a person to unjustified danger.

### P-E4-046 — Do Not Convert Persons Into Resources of Authority
Institutional power should not reduce persons to labour, sexual, political or symbolic assets without meaningful regard for their agency.
