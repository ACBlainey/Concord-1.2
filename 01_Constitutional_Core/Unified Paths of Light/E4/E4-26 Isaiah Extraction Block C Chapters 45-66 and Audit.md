## Isaiah-internal recurrent structures

Without comparison to other anchors, Isaiah repeatedly supplies evidence concerning:

- justice over empty ritual performance;
- positive protection of vulnerable persons;
- corruption, bribery and office abuse;
- oppressive accumulation and displacement;
- limits of superficial judgement;
- manipulation of moral language;
- epistemic humility;
- unjust legislation and procedural capture;
- refuge from oppression;
- public-purpose leadership;
- pressure for agreeable information;
- independent truth-telling;
- propaganda and fear manipulation;
- claimed authority tested against evidence;
- restraint toward vulnerable persons;
- inclusion beyond inherited status;
- positive aid and liberation;
- repair of damaged shared structures;
- enjoyment of one's labour without expropriation.

## Negative and tension evidence

Isaiah also contains severe judgement, destruction and vengeance imagery, geopolitical theology, gendered humiliation, religious polemic and interpretively contested suffering material. These remain explicitly quarantined.

## Methodological observation

Isaiah strongly reinforces the need to distinguish **institutional appearance from institutional ethical function**.

The text repeatedly portrays systems that retain recognisable forms—worship, law, rulers, judges, advisers, wealth, defence—while their function is corrupted through exploitation, falsehood, bribery, coercion or suppression of corrective information.

This observation is not yet mapped to any later Concord architecture.
