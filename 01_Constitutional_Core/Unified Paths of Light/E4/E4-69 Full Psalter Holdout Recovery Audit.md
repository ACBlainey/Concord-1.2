# 3. Principle-level coverage

The holdout provides support or recognisable behavioural expression for a large majority of J001–J034.

Strongly recurring examples include:

- **J001** life/protection;
- **J004** de-escalation/peace;
- **J006** adverse outcome does not prove guilt/worth;
- **J007–J012** truth, attribution, verification, corrective information, epistemic calibration and correction;
- **J014–J015** memory and intelligible instruction;
- **J016–J017** impartial judgement and hearing;
- **J025–J027** aid, vulnerability and anti-exploitation;
- **J030** accumulation is not welfare;
- **J031** bounded authority;
- **J033–J034** resilience, development and capability-sensitive responsibility.

Some frozen principles are sparse or represented mainly through compound rather than direct Psalm expression, especially:
- detailed culpability distinctions;
- explicit jurisdiction;
- property structure;
- entrusted-resource stewardship;
- formal procedural reason-giving;
- explicit consent/refusal.

That asymmetry is expected from Psalm genre and is itself informative.

# 4. Genre effect

The holdout confirms that **genre materially shapes ethical resolution**.

Psalms strongly recover:
- vulnerability;
- truth/deceit;
- oppression;
- correction;
- moral memory;
- peace;
- aid;
- limits of wealth/status;
- uncertainty under suffering.

They recover less directly:
- legal procedure;
- transactional rules;
- property transfer;
- administrative custody;
- institutional role design.

This supports the methodological distinction between:
- absence from a genre;
- absence from a tradition;
- absence from an ethical architecture.

# 6. What the holdout does not establish

The result does not establish that:
- Psalms and the rest of the Tanakh are independent cultural samples;
- every Psalm is ethical instruction;
- the frozen 34 are the only possible compression;
- the 34 are universal natural laws;
- theological and ritual content is reducible to the E4 catalogue.

It establishes a narrower but meaningful result:

> A 34-principle catalogue derived and frozen from the non-Psalm Tanakh recovers almost all behaviourally comparable ethical propositions identified in a subsequently opened Psalm holdout, while also preserving visible tension material.
