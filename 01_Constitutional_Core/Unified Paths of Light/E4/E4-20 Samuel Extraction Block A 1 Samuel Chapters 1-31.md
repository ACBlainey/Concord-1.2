# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-20 — Samuel Extraction Block A: 1 Samuel Chapters 1–31

## Status
**PRIMARY EXTRACTION — no cross-anchor comparison.**

## Source-form note

Samuel is one book in the Jewish canonical structure but is conventionally divided into 1 and 2 Samuel in chapter referencing. This block covers 1 Samuel.

The text is especially valuable for authority transition: priestly failure, prophetic leadership, demand for monarchy, royal constraint, institutional corruption and the deterioration of Saul's rule.

## Extracted source occurrences

### E4-SAM-001 — 1 Samuel 1:9–18
**Source form:** Hannah/Eli interaction  
**Normative status:** accusation corrected after explanation  
**Proposition:** Apparent misconduct should not be treated as established merely from superficial observation; hearing the person concerned can correct a false judgement.  
**Provisional cluster:** hearing / evidential humility.

### E4-SAM-002 — 1 Samuel 1:21–28
**Source form:** vow fulfilment narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit fulfilment  
**Proposition:** A voluntarily undertaken commitment can create a continuing obligation to follow through once its stated condition is met.  
**Provisional cluster:** promise integrity.

### E4-SAM-003 — 1 Samuel 2:12–17
**Source form:** priestly corruption narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit condemnation  
**Proposition:** Officials who control access to a shared sacred/public system abuse office when they appropriate resources beyond their authorised entitlement.  
**Provisional cluster:** office abuse / resource extraction.

### E4-SAM-004 — 1 Samuel 2:22–25
**Source form:** abuse by priestly officials  
**Normative status:** explicit negative evaluation

### E4-SAM-005 — 1 Samuel 2:27–36; 3:11–14

## Internal observations

1 Samuel contributes unusually rich evidence concerning the ethics of office:

- corruption by officials;
- supervisory failure;
- structural risks of monarchy;
- constitutional recording of office;
- public accountability;
- rash executive rules;
- community constraint on disproportionate punishment;
- status insecurity and abuse;
- concealed harmful assignments;
- internal challenge to rulers;
- refusal of manifestly wrongful orders;
- mediation and leader correctability.

The text repeatedly distinguishes **holding authority** from **using authority legitimately**.

This remains an internal source observation only. No Concord or Ethical Topology comparison has been made.
