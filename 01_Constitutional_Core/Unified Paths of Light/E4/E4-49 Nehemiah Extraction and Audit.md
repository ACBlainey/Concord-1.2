## Status

## Source-form note

Nehemiah is first-person restoration narrative with administrative lists, conflict, labour organisation, economic reform and religious boundary enforcement. The narrator's approval of his own actions is not treated as independent validation. Violence, ethnic/religious exclusion and coercive reforms are preserved as tension evidence.

## Methodological observation

Nehemiah provides a dense example of restoration under simultaneous **external threat and internal exploitation**. It resists a simplistic model in which external danger excuses internal injustice.

A recurring source-level sequence is:

**condition report → direct inspection → distributed repair → threat adaptation → internal grievance → investigation → restitution → institutional maintenance.**

The book also supplies an important extraction control: narrator confidence in reform does not remove the need to evaluate coercive methods separately.
