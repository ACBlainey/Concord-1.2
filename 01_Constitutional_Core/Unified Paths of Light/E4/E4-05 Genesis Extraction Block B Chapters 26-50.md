### Recurrent internal themes

Without consulting other anchors, Genesis repeatedly supplies evidence around:

- protection of life and restraint of violence;
- proportionality and limits on retaliation;
- accountability and recognition of one's own wrongdoing;
- truth, deception and evidential integrity;
- knowledge/intent as relevant to culpability;
- restitution and repair;
- peaceful settlement and boundary-making;
- agreement and commitment integrity;
- property/resource transfer;
- vulnerable-person and dependant welfare;
- entrusted responsibility;
- intimate/relational boundaries;
- hospitality;
- reconciliation and non-retaliation;
- foresight and resource stewardship;
- concentration of power under scarcity;
- self-regulation.

These are **Genesis-internal provisional clusters**, not E4 final principles.

## Methodological observation

Genesis demonstrates why E4 cannot use a simple verse-count extraction method. Some of its strongest ethical evidence emerges from complete narrative arcs in which:

**action → consequence → recognition/rebuke → repair**

jointly establishes the normative structure.

This observation should be retained for later methodology review but does not alter the frozen E4 protocol during extraction.
