# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-06 — Exodus Extraction Block A: Chapters 1–15

## Status
**PRIMARY EXTRACTION — no cross-anchor comparison.**

## Method note
Exodus begins with political oppression, forced labour, reproductive violence, resistance, liberation and conflict. Narrative action is not automatically normative. Explicit evaluation, protected conduct, condemnatory structure and consequence-linked material receive priority.

## Extracted source occurrences

### E4-EXO-001 — Exodus 1:8–14
**Source form:** political oppression narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit oppressive policy  
**Proposition:** A governing power can convert demographic fear into systematic exploitation and coercive labour against a population.  
**Provisional cluster:** oppressive power / forced labour.

### E4-EXO-002 — Exodus 1:15–22
**Source form:** state killing order  
**Normative status:** explicit harmful command resisted by protagonists  
**Proposition:** Political authority does not make an order to kill innocents ethically legitimate.  
**Provisional cluster:** limits of authority / protection of life.

### E4-EXO-003 — Exodus 1:17–21
**Source form:** resistance narrative  
**Normative status:** positively evaluated refusal  
**Proposition:** Refusal to execute a wrongful command can be morally preferable to obedience to authority.  
**Provisional cluster:** conscientious resistance.

### E4-EXO-004 — Exodus 2:1–10
**Source form:** infant rescue narrative  
**Normative status:** protective conduct  
**Proposition:** Vulnerable life may warrant protection even where such protection conflicts with prevailing state policy.  
**Provisional cluster:** protection of vulnerable life.

### E4-EXO-005 — Exodus 2:11–15
**Source form:** Moses intervenes in violence  
**Normative status:** mixed / ethically ambiguous  
**Proposition:** Recognition of oppression can motivate intervention, but retaliatory killing introduces a separate ethical problem.  
**Notes:** **AMBIGUITY FLAG.** Do not treat Moses' killing as normative merely because the victim was abusing another person.  
**Provisional cluster:** intervention / proportionality.

### E4-EXO-006 — Exodus 2:16–20
**Source form:** intervention at well  
**Normative status:** protective action  
**Proposition:** Intervention may be justified to protect persons being wrongfully driven from fair access to a shared resource.  
**Provisional cluster:** protection / access to resources.

### E4-EXO-007 — Exodus 2:23–25
**Source form:** oppression recognition narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit attention to suffering  
**Proposition:** Persistent suffering under coercive conditions remains morally significant even when embedded in an established political order.  
**Provisional cluster:** recognition of oppression.

### E4-EXO-008 — Exodus 3:7–10
**Source form:** liberation commission  
**Normative status:** explicit response to oppression  
**Proposition:** Severe exploitation and suffering can generate an obligation for liberating intervention.  
**Provisional cluster:** liberation / protection.

### E4-EXO-009 — Exodus 3:16–22
**Source form:** liberation/resource narrative  
**Normative status:** reparative but context-specific  
**Proposition:** **CAUTION.** The departure includes transfer of material goods after prolonged exploitation; retain as possible reparative structure without prematurely defining a general rule.  
**Provisional cluster:** exploitation / material redress.

### E4-EXO-010 — Exodus 4:10–16
**Source form:** delegation dialogue  
**Normative status:** explicit accommodation/delegation  
**Proposition:** A person's limitation in one capability need not exclude them from a task where complementary assistance can be provided.  
**Provisional cluster:** capability accommodation / delegation.

### E4-EXO-011 — Exodus 5:1–18
**Source form:** labour-policy narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit escalation of coercion  
**Proposition:** Authority acts abusively when it increases required output while withholding necessary means and punishing workers for predictable failure.  
**Provisional cluster:** labour exploitation / abusive administration.

### E4-EXO-012 — Exodus 5:14–16
**Source form:** punishment of supervisors  
**Normative status:** consequence-linked grievance  
**Proposition:** Punishing intermediaries for outcomes caused by impossible conditions imposed from above misallocates responsibility.  
**Provisional cluster:** fair attribution of responsibility.

### E4-EXO-013 — Exodus 5:19–23
**Source form:** complaint/accountability narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit grievance  
**Proposition:** Leadership intervention should remain answerable to the real consequences experienced by the people it purports to help.  
**Provisional cluster:** leadership accountability.

### E4-EXO-014 — Exodus 6:5–8
**Source form:** liberation commitment  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Sustained coercive labour and domination are treated as conditions from which a population may legitimately seek release.  
**Provisional cluster:** freedom from domination.

### E4-EXO-015 — Exodus 7–11
**Source form:** plague/conflict narrative  
**Normative status:** theological conflict with large-scale harm  
**Proposition:** **QUARANTINED FOR LATER ANALYSIS.** These chapters contain escalating coercion, collective harm and disputed questions of responsibility, but their theological architecture prevents confident substrate-neutral extraction at this stage.

### E4-EXO-016 — Exodus 8–10, repeated negotiations
**Source form:** ruler negotiation / reversal  
**Normative status:** repeated commitment and retraction  
**Proposition:** Repeatedly making concessions under pressure and withdrawing them when pressure recedes undermines the reliability of negotiation.  
**Provisional cluster:** commitment integrity / negotiation.

### E4-EXO-017 — Exodus 10:7
**Source form:** counsel to ruler  
**Normative status:** explicit internal warning  
**Proposition:** Leaders should remain capable of receiving internal warning when their policy is producing escalating collective damage.  
**Provisional cluster:** correctability of authority.

### E4-EXO-018 — Exodus 12:43–49
**Source form:** ritual/legal boundary  
**Normative status:** mixed covenantal regulation  
**Proposition:** **MOSTLY CONTEXT-BOUND.** The passage establishes membership conditions for a ritual practice. One narrower structural observation is retained: persons admitted to the same covenantal practice are subjected to the same governing rule.  
**Provisional cluster:** equal rule within admitted status.

### E4-EXO-019 — Exodus 12:49
**Source form:** legal formulation  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** A shared legal framework may apply the same rule to resident outsider and native participant within its defined jurisdiction.  
**Provisional cluster:** equal application of law.

### E4-EXO-020 — Exodus 13:17–18
**Source form:** route-selection narrative  
**Normative status:** protective strategy  
**Proposition:** Leadership may need to account for the developmental readiness and foreseeable vulnerability of those being led rather than choose the shortest path.  
**Notes:** Strongly narrative/theological; retain cautiously.  
**Provisional cluster:** capability-sensitive leadership.

### E4-EXO-021 — Exodus 14:10–14
**Source form:** crisis narrative  
**Normative status:** leadership response  
**Proposition:** Severe collective fear can require stabilising leadership rather than contempt for those experiencing it.  
**Provisional cluster:** crisis leadership.

### E4-EXO-022 — Exodus 14–15
**Source form:** liberation/conflict narrative  
**Normative status:** theological deliverance  
**Proposition:** **NO ADDITIONAL GENERAL EXTRACTION.** The liberation from pursuing coercive power is ethically relevant, but detailed military/theological events are not converted into general rules here.

## Block A observations
The opening Exodus material adds unusually strong evidence concerning **authority itself**: forced labour, illegitimate commands, conscientious resistance, correctability of rulers, attribution of responsibility and release from domination.

This remains an Exodus-internal observation only.
