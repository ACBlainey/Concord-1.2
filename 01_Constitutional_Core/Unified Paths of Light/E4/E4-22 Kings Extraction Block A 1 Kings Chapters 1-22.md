# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-22 — Kings Extraction Block A: 1 Kings Chapters 1–22

## Status
**PRIMARY EXTRACTION — no cross-anchor comparison.**

## Source-form note

Kings evaluates monarchy across succession, administration, wealth, justice, religious policy, prophetic criticism and institutional decline. The text's own theological evaluations are recorded but are not automatically converted into substrate-neutral principles.

## Extracted source occurrences

### E4-KNG-001 — 1 Kings 1
**Source form:** contested succession  
**Normative status:** political transition narrative  
**Proposition:** Ambiguous succession arrangements can create competing claims and destabilising pre-emptive attempts to seize authority.  
**Provisional cluster:** succession clarity / power transition.

### E4-KNG-002 — 1 Kings 1:11–40
**Source form:** intervention in succession crisis  
**Normative status:** political/accountability action  
**Proposition:** Existing commitments concerning succession can be invoked and verified before an attempted power transfer becomes irreversible.  
**Provisional cluster:** commitment accountability / succession.

### E4-KNG-003 — 1 Kings 2
**Source form:** consolidation and executions  
**Normative status:** political violence / mixed retrospective grievances  
**Proposition:** **MAJOR AMBIGUITY / QUARANTINE.** Succession, justice, vengeance and political consolidation are intertwined and cannot be reduced to a clean general rule.

### E4-KNG-004 — 1 Kings 3:5–15
**Source form:** Solomon's request  
**Normative status:** leadership ideal  
**Proposition:** A ruler can prioritise discernment for judging the population over personal wealth, longevity or victory.  
**Provisional cluster:** competence / public-purpose leadership.

### E4-KNG-005 — 1 Kings 3:16–28
**Source form:** disputed maternity adjudication  
**Normative status:** judicial narrative  
**Proposition:** Where testimony alone does not resolve a dispute, a decision-maker may seek additional behavioural evidence that discriminates between competing claims.  
**Notes:** The threatened division of the child is narrative-specific and is not generalised as an acceptable evidential technique.  
**Provisional cluster:** evidential reasoning / adjudication.

### E4-KNG-006 — 1 Kings 4
**Source form:** administration/provision  
**Normative status:** descriptive  
**Proposition:** **NO GENERAL EXTRACTION from administrative scale alone.**

### E4-KNG-007 — 1 Kings 5–7
**Source form:** construction/resource mobilisation  
**Normative status:** administrative with labour extraction  
**Proposition:** **PARTLY QUARANTINED.** Large public/sacred projects depend on organised labour and resources; description alone does not establish ethical legitimacy of the extraction.

### E4-KNG-008 — 1 Kings 8
**Source form:** temple dedication/prayer  
**Normative status:** theological with justice themes  
**Proposition:** **MOSTLY THEOLOGICAL.** Separable concern for fair judgement and outsiders is retained only where explicit below.

### E4-KNG-009 — 1 Kings 8:31–32
**Source form:** oath/adjudication prayer  
**Normative status:** ethical ideal in theological form  
**Proposition:** Adjudication should distinguish wrongdoing from innocence rather than punish without differentiation.  
**Provisional cluster:** differentiated judgement.

### E4-KNG-010 — 1 Kings 8:41–43
**Source form:** outsider inclusion in worship  
**Normative status:** theological  
**Proposition:** A resident or visiting outsider is not represented as categorically outside access merely because of origin.  
**Provisional cluster:** outsider access; retained cautiously.

### E4-KNG-011 — 1 Kings 9
**Source form:** covenantal warning / construction aftermath  
**Normative status:** theological/administrative  
**Proposition:** **NO DISTINCT GENERAL EXTRACTION.**

### E4-KNG-012 — 1 Kings 10:14–29
**Source form:** royal wealth and military accumulation  
**Normative status:** descriptive with possible tension against prior law  
**Proposition:** **TENSION FLAG.** Exceptional royal prosperity includes accumulation of wealth, horses and military resources; later comparison may test this against previously extracted constraints on kings, but no comparison is performed now.

### E4-KNG-013 — 1 Kings 11
**Source form:** Solomon's decline  
**Normative status:** theological/political  
**Proposition:** **MOSTLY QUARANTINED.** Religious evaluation dominates; institutional effects of concentrated royal household and alliances are not inferred beyond the text.

### E4-KNG-014 — 1 Kings 12:1–15
**Source form:** Rehoboam and labour grievance  
**Normative status:** political consultation narrative  
**Proposition:** A new ruler's refusal to reduce burdens, combined with preference for status-affirming advice over experienced counsel, can fracture political legitimacy.  
**Provisional cluster:** burdens / counsel / legitimacy.

### E4-KNG-015 — 1 Kings 12:6–11
**Source form:** competing counsel  
**Normative status:** explicit advice contrast  
**Proposition:** Advice that equates authority with intensified domination can be institutionally destructive even when it appeals to a ruler's desire to appear strong.  
**Provisional cluster:** domination / leadership judgement.

### E4-KNG-016 — 1 Kings 12:16–24
**Source form:** secession and attempted coercive recovery  
**Normative status:** political/conflict narrative  
**Proposition:** Excessive burden can contribute to secession, and coercive attempts to restore lost authority risk escalating institutional failure into civil war.  
**Provisional cluster:** overreach / fragmentation / escalation.

### E4-KNG-017 — 1 Kings 13
**Source form:** prophetic obedience/deception narrative  
**Normative status:** theological and ambiguous  
**Proposition:** **QUARANTINED.** Conflicting claimed authority and deception make the narrative unsuitable for simple generalisation.

### E4-KNG-018 — 1 Kings 14–16
**Source form:** dynastic succession/coups  
**Normative status:** political/theological  
**Proposition:** **MOSTLY QUARANTINED.** Recurrent coups and dynastic violence are retained as institutional instability evidence without deriving a preferred regime.

### E4-KNG-019 — 1 Kings 16:8–22
**Source form:** rapid coups and factional succession  
**Normative status:** political instability narrative  
**Proposition:** When authority is acquired through recurrent violent replacement rather than stable transfer, institutional continuity can collapse into factional competition.  
**Provisional cluster:** succession instability / violent capture.

### E4-KNG-020 — 1 Kings 17
**Source form:** Elijah/widow narrative  
**Normative status:** theological with care structure  
**Proposition:** Severe scarcity does not eliminate the ethical significance of provision to a vulnerable household, though the miraculous context prevents economic generalisation.  
**Provisional cluster:** care under scarcity.

### E4-KNG-021 — 1 Kings 18
**Source form:** Carmel confrontation  
**Normative status:** theological/religious conflict  
**Proposition:** **MAJOR QUARANTINE.** Religious contest and killing of prophets are not converted into general epistemic or coercive rules.

### E4-KNG-022 — 1 Kings 19:1–18
**Source form:** Elijah under threat/exhaustion  
**Normative status:** theological narrative  
**Proposition:** **CAUTIOUS EXTRACTION.** A severely exhausted and threatened agent may require rest, food and recovery before further responsibility is imposed.  
**Provisional cluster:** capacity restoration.

### E4-KNG-023 — 1 Kings 20
**Source form:** war narratives  
**Normative status:** military/theological  
**Proposition:** **MOSTLY QUARANTINED.**

### E4-KNG-024 — 1 Kings 21:1–16
**Source form:** Naboth's vineyard  
**Normative status:** severe abuse-of-power narrative  
**Proposition:** Political power can corrupt property transfer when refusal of a proposed exchange is bypassed through fabricated accusation, manipulated procedure and lethal coercion.  
**Provisional cluster:** property consent / false evidence / state abuse.

### E4-KNG-025 — 1 Kings 21:5–14
**Source form:** engineered judicial killing  
**Normative status:** explicit conspiracy  
**Proposition:** Formal legal procedure can be weaponised through false witnesses and compliant officials to give wrongful state violence an appearance of legitimacy.  
**Provisional cluster:** procedural capture / false evidence.

### E4-KNG-026 — 1 Kings 21:17–29
**Source form:** prophetic confrontation of king  
**Normative status:** accountability narrative  
**Proposition:** Acquisition produced by wrongful state violence remains morally attributable to the ruler who benefits from and accepts the result.  
**Provisional cluster:** beneficiary responsibility / power accountability.

### E4-KNG-027 — 1 Kings 22:1–28
**Source form:** competing prophetic counsel  
**Normative status:** theological/epistemic conflict  
**Proposition:** A ruler's preference for agreeable advice can marginalise dissenting information precisely because it challenges the desired course of action.  
**Provisional cluster:** motivated information selection / dissent.

### E4-KNG-028 — 1 Kings 22:5–8
**Source form:** counsel selection  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Numerical abundance of supportive advisers does not itself establish the reliability of their conclusion, especially where incentives favour agreement with power.  
**Provisional cluster:** consensus / incentive distortion.

### E4-KNG-029 — 1 Kings 22:13–18
**Source form:** pressure on dissenting adviser  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** An information-bearing role loses integrity if the speaker is expected to align their report with the preferred consensus rather than communicate what they believe to be true.  
**Provisional cluster:** independence of advice / truth.

### E4-KNG-030 — 1 Kings 22:26–28
**Source form:** punishment of dissenting counsel  
**Normative status:** coercive response  
**Proposition:** Punishing unwelcome advice can suppress corrective information without establishing that the advice was false.  
**Provisional cluster:** dissent protection / epistemic governance.
