## Kings-internal recurrent structures

Without external comparison, Kings repeatedly supplies evidence concerning:

- succession instability;
- rule-bound and public-purpose leadership;
- burdens imposed by authority;
- concentrated extraction;
- institutional incentives;
- justice and equity;
- false evidence and captured procedure;
- independent advice and suppression of dissent;
- property consent;
- corruption through private benefit;
- repair of institutional resource systems;
- individual rather than inherited punishment;
- information manipulation under conflict;
- institutional memory;
- detection of drift against governing records;
- reform after rediscovery of neglected standards.

## Negative and tension evidence

Kings also preserves conquest, executions, coups, religious coercion, mass killing and theological causal interpretations. These remain in the evidence base and are not made to disappear beneath the institutional material.

## Methodological observation

The Josiah narrative produces a particularly interesting source-level control sequence:

**governing record neglected → institutional divergence → record rediscovered → discrepancy recognised → inquiry → public disclosure → reform.**

No later Concord/system-state terminology is applied at this stage.
