# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-62 — Adversarial Merge Test of the 37-Principle Core

## Status

**FINAL PRE-FREEZE PRESSURE TEST.**

No Psalm material or external ethical framework is admitted.

# 1. Purpose

The previous pass produced 37 principles judged irreducible at the working resolution.

This audit now deliberately attempts to break that result by seeking deeper parents.

The question is not:

> Can two principles be described with one broad word?

It is:

> Can one principle be removed while the remaining system regenerates its behavioural constraint without adding a new premise?

# 2. Candidate deep merges tested

## Test 1 — Life protection into generic non-harm

**Attempt:** Replace protection of life with a broad prohibition on harm.

**Failure:** A generic harm rule requires a theory of harm severity and trade-off. Explicit protection of life carries information about a particularly high-stakes protected interest.

**Decision:** keep life protection at this resolution.

## Test 2 — Culpability distinction into proportionality

**Attempt:** Treat intention/accident/negligence solely as inputs to proportional response.

**Failure:** The catalogue would no longer independently require agents to distinguish causal and mental-state responsibility before response.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 3 — De-escalation into proportionality

**Attempt:** Define the less escalatory route as inherently more proportionate.

**Failure:** This imports a preference not contained in proportionality itself where immediate burdens are otherwise equal.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 4 — Repair into non-harm

**Attempt:** Define repair as merely ending continuing harm.

**Failure:** Restitution for completed harm can remain due after active harm has ceased.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 5 — Truth and accurate attribution

**Attempt:** Treat accurate attribution as ordinary truthful representation.

**Failure:** A statement can contain individually true facts while misleadingly assigning responsibility through association or group transfer. Attribution remains distinct.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 6 — Verification into truth

**Attempt:** Collapse epistemic verification into honesty.

**Failure:** Honest belief can be false.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 7 — corrective information into verification

**Attempt:** Assume verification automatically protects dissenting evidence.

**Failure:** A system may value verification in principle while structurally suppressing evidence before it enters the process.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 8 — uncertainty calibration into verification

**Attempt:** Verified evidence should determine confidence automatically.

**Failure:** Evidence can be genuine but incomplete; calibration adds a rule about inference and action under uncertainty.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 9 — correctability into verification

**Attempt:** New evidence should simply trigger verification.

**Failure:** Nothing in verification alone requires revision after error is established.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 10 — capability/wisdom distinction into uncertainty

**Attempt:** Treat technical capability as merely another uncertain indicator.

**Failure:** The source distinction is categorical: competence in one dimension does not establish normative judgement in another.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 11 — records/memory into corrective information

**Attempt:** Treat preservation as a way to protect correction.

**Failure:** Records also support continuity, commitments, transfers and succession independent of active dissent.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 12 — intelligibility into truth

**Attempt:** Truthful rules are sufficient.

**Failure:** Unintelligible truth cannot reliably guide affected agents.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 13 — impartiality into accurate attribution

**Attempt:** Accurate case facts should generate impartiality.

**Failure:** A decision-maker can know the facts and still favour wealth, kin or status.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 14 — hearing into verification

**Attempt:** A hearing is simply one verification technique.

**Failure:** Hearing also protects the standing of the accused/affected person as a participant in consequential judgement.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 15 — reason-giving into hearing

**Attempt:** A meaningful hearing implies reasons.

**Failure:** An authority can hear fully and decide without disclosing why.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 16 — competence/trustworthiness into bounded authority

**Attempt:** Properly bounded roles solve competence.

**Failure:** A perfectly bounded office can still be occupied by an incapable or unreliable agent.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 17 — jurisdiction into bounded authority

**Attempt:** Merge both into one authority-limitation rule.

**Result:** **MERGE ACCEPTED.**

The ethically material content of jurisdictional limits is a specification of bounded authority: holding one office does not expand authority beyond its legitimate domain.

**New parent:**  
**Authority Must Be Entrusted, Bounded and Jurisdictionally Limited.**

**Reduction:** 2 → 1.

## Test 18 — corrigibility into correctability

**Attempt:** Merge personal/model correctability with institutional corrigibility.

**Result:** **MERGE ACCEPTED WITH SCOPE EXPANSION.**

Both express the same deeper rule across agent and system scales:

**Preserve the Capacity to Correct Credible Error or Harm.**

The source evidence supports this recursively at individual, epistemic, decisional and institutional levels.

**Reduction:** 2 → 1.

## Test 19 — formal validity vs ethical legitimacy into justice

**Attempt:** Assume justice itself blocks harmful formalism.

**Failure:** “Justice” would become an undefined bridge premise. The source repeatedly demonstrates technically valid systems producing harm.

**Decision:** keep explicit meta-constraint.

## Test 20 — consent/refusal into bounded authority

**Attempt:** If authority is bounded, refusal follows.

**Failure:** Agency is a protected interest independent of office design and applies within intimate and relational contexts too.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 21 — conscience into consent

**Attempt:** Conscience is simply refusal.

**Failure:** Conscience specifies why some demanded conduct retains normative significance even where the authority otherwise has legitimate scope.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 22 — aid-preserving agency into consent + aid

**Attempt:** derive from positive aid and consent.

**Result:** **MERGE ACCEPTED AS COMPOUND.**

Once meaningful consent/refusal and positive aid are independently retained, the anti-paternalistic application can be regenerated.

**Reduction:** 1 independent position removed.**

## Test 23 — commitment integrity into consent

**Attempt:** voluntary consent creates obligation.

**Failure:** Consent explains entry but not continued fidelity, promise performance or honest dissolution.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 24 — privacy into consent

**Attempt:** disclosure is simply a consent question.

**Failure:** Privacy also protects against surveillance, exposure and information acquisition without a discrete disclosure transaction.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 25 — positive aid into life protection/non-harm

**Attempt:** severe unmet need is a form of harm.

**Failure:** This imports a positive-duty bridge. Non-infliction alone does not generate assistance.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 26 — dependant protection into positive aid

**Attempt:** dependants are simply high-need recipients.

**Failure:** Dependency changes responsibility because effective agency/capability is reduced and another agent may hold custodial power.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 27 — non-exploitation into dignity

**Attempt:** exploitation is degrading.

**Failure:** Exploitation can occur without subjective humiliation; the core concerns asymmetric extraction.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 28 — property into stewardship

**Attempt:** both regulate resources.

**Failure:** Ownership and entrustment are different relations.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 29 — realised benefit into stewardship

**Attempt:** good stewardship should maximise benefit.

**Failure:** The principle also applies to personally controlled accumulation where no trust relation exists.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 30 — consultation into hearing

**Attempt:** consultation is collective hearing.

**Failure:** adjudicative hearing protects a party under judgement; consultation gathers distributed knowledge and participation before collective action.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 31 — institutional maintenance into stewardship

**Attempt:** institutions are resources to steward.

**Failure:** This requires redefining stewardship so broadly that its entrusted-resource content disappears.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 32 — resilience into future consequence

**Attempt:** foresight automatically generates preparation.

**Failure:** Awareness of future risk does not itself impose a positive duty to create robustness.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 33 — dignity into consent

**Attempt:** consensual treatment is dignified treatment.

**Failure:** consent and degradation are separable.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 34 — developmental judgement into competence

**Attempt:** competent agents have developed judgement.

**Failure:** competence is a selection property; developmental cultivation is a process and obligation concerning how judgement is formed.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 35 — future consequence into stewardship

**Attempt:** stewardship includes future effects.

**Failure:** future-consequence reasoning applies to actions beyond entrusted resources.

**Decision:** keep.

## Test 36 — capacity-sensitive obligation into proportionality

**Attempt:** proportionality should account for capacity.

**Failure:** proportionality constrains response to harm/threat; capacity-sensitive obligation governs what can reasonably be demanded and how responsibility scales with capability.

**Decision:** keep.

# 4. Interpretation

The important result is not the number 34.

The important result is the **change in compression regime**.

From 108 to 65, many removals were straightforward duplication or domain implementation.

From 65 to 37, compression required explicit derivability tests.

From 37 to 34, almost every attempted merge failed because it erased a distinct:
- protected interest;
- epistemic operation;
- agency relation;
- positive duty;
- institutional property;
- temporal responsibility;
- or resource relation.

This is evidence that E4 is approaching a stable ethical resolution rather than merely stopping at an arbitrary list length.
