# Reconstructed catalogue

## Harm, protection and responsibility

**E4-C01 — Protect Life Against Unjustified Killing**  
Life is protected against intentional unjustified destruction.

**E4-C02 — Distinguish Deliberate Harm, Accident and Negligence**  
Culpability should track intention, foreseeability and relevant responsibility.

**E4-C03 — Use Proportionate Response**  
Response should remain proportionate to harm, culpability and protective need.

**E4-C04 — Bound Necessary Defence Against Unlimited Retaliation**  
Protection against active threat does not create unrestricted permission for revenge, plunder or unnecessary extension of harm.

**E4-C05 — Do Not Transfer Responsibility Without Cause**  
Guilt should not be assigned automatically to families, groups or generations because of another agent's conduct.

**E4-C06 — Protect Captives and Persons No Longer Posing the Original Threat**  
Cessation or absence of active threat changes permissible treatment.

**E4-C07 — Prefer De-Escalation Where Equivalent Protection Is Available**  
Avoid unnecessary escalation when protection can be achieved by a less harmful route.

**E4-C08 — Repair Harm Where Repair Is Possible**  
Ending wrongdoing may leave restitution, restoration or other repair obligations.

**E4-C09 — Do Not Infer Moral Worth or Guilt From Adverse Outcome**  
Suffering, poverty, illness, defeat or loss are not reliable proof of guilt or inferior moral worth.

## Truth, knowledge and epistemic integrity

**E4-C10 — Represent Material Facts Honestly**  
Do not knowingly cause others to act on materially false representation.

**E4-C11 — Attribute Consequential Claims and Responsibility Accurately**  
Do not attach accusations, actions, motives or responsibility to the wrong target.

**E4-C12 — Verify Consequential Claims Through Relevant Evidence and Challenge**  
Claims supporting serious action should face evidence checking and material counterevidence.

**E4-C13 — Protect Corrective Information Against Suppression, Distortion and Incentive Capture**  
Information that challenges preferred conclusions should remain capable of reaching decision-makers.

**E4-C14 — Match Confidence and Action to Available Knowledge**  
Uncertainty should constrain confidence and the scale or irreversibility of action without requiring paralysis.

**E4-C15 — Remain Correctable When Credible Error Is Identified**  
Persons, models and decisions should remain open to revision when evidence materially undermines them.

**E4-C16 — Distinguish Capability From Wisdom**  
Technical power, information or skill does not itself establish sound judgement about use.

**E4-C17 — Preserve Reliable Records and Memory Needed for Continuity, Verification and Correction**  
Important commitments, transfers, events and governing knowledge should remain recoverable.

**E4-C18 — Make Governing Information Intelligible to Those Expected to Follow It**  
Rules should be accessible and understandable to affected participants.

**E4-C19 — Interpret Distress Speech in Context**  
Communication under extreme grief, fear or suffering should be interpreted with attention to its condition of production.

## Justice and institutional accountability

**E4-C20 — Judge Impartially**  
Status, wealth, kinship, group membership and power should not decide the merits of a case.

**E4-C21 — Reject Bribery and Purchased Judgement**  
Material inducement should not buy authoritative decisions.

**E4-C22 — Provide a Meaningful and Practically Accessible Hearing**  
Persons facing serious accusation or sanction should have a real opportunity to answer the case.

**E4-C23 — State the Charge or Disputed Claim Clearly**  
Consequential accusation should be sufficiently defined to permit response.

**E4-C24 — Give Reasons Relevant to Consequential Decisions**  
Judgement should connect relevant evidence, rule and outcome rather than resting solely on superior power.

**E4-C25 — Entrust Roles to Competent and Trustworthy Agents**  
Consequential roles require relevant capability and reliability.

**E4-C26 — Clarify Jurisdiction and Role Boundaries**  
Authority in one role does not automatically create authority in every domain.

**E4-C27 — Preserve Corrigibility of Consequential Decisions and Institutions**  
Systems should retain viable means to revise discovered error, manipulation or harm.

**E4-C28 — Do Not Confuse Formal or Administrative Validity With Ethical Legitimacy**  
Legal form, procedural regularity or administrative efficiency cannot by themselves justify substantive harm.

**E4-C29 — Do Not Shield Wrongdoing Through Office, Kinship or Group Loyalty**  
Institutional or relational loyalty should not block warranted accountability.

**E4-C30 — Do Not Assume Accountability Is Always Immediate or Visibly Material**  
Delayed or absent visible consequence does not establish innocence or erase ethical evaluation.

## Agency and relational boundaries

**E4-C31 — Respect Meaningful Consent and Refusal**  
Do not manufacture consent through coercion or treat hierarchy as eliminating meaningful refusal.

**E4-C32 — Respect Serious Conscientious Boundaries**  
Deep normative disagreement should not automatically be overridden through disproportionate punishment or forced conformity.

**E4-C33 — Preserve Agency While Providing Protection or Aid**  
Assistance should preserve effective decision-making where possible rather than unnecessarily replacing it.

**E4-C34 — Treat Commitments as Voluntary and Consequential**  
Commitments should be meaningfully undertaken and then treated as carrying real obligations.

**E4-C35 — Respect Intimate Agency and Reciprocal Relational Boundaries**  
Status, desire or opportunity does not create entitlement to intimate access.

**E4-C36 — Preserve Legitimate Privacy Under Vulnerability**  
Control of personal information can be protective where disclosure creates unjustified danger.

**E4-C37 — Do Not Convert Persons Into Resources of Authority**  
Institutional power should not reduce persons to labour, sexual, political or symbolic assets without regard for their agency.

## Care, vulnerability and positive obligation

**E4-C38 — Provide Materially Meaningful Aid Under Severe Need Where Capacity Exists**  
Severe vulnerability can generate positive duties, not only prohibitions on direct harm.

**E4-C39 — Protect Dependants and Persons With Reduced Effective Agency**  
Reduced capability can create heightened protective responsibility.

**E4-C40 — Do Not Exploit Vulnerability or Shift Disproportionate Burdens Onto Low-Power Persons**  
Power asymmetry must not become an extraction opportunity.

**E4-C41 — Use Available Access or Influence to Represent Persons Unable to Secure a Hearing**  
Greater access can create responsibility to advocate for persons effectively excluded from decision channels.

## Property, labour and stewardship

**E4-C42 — Respect Legitimate Property and Possession**  
Power alone does not create entitlement to another person's assets.

**E4-C43 — Do Not Exploit Debt or Scarcity**  
Material vulnerability should not be used to create disproportionate dependency or dispossession.

**E4-C44 — Pay and Protect Workers Fairly**  
Labour should not be extracted while denying due compensation or basic treatment.

**E4-C45 — Steward Entrusted Resources**  
Control of another person's or a community's assets creates duties of custody, purpose and accountability.

**E4-C46 — Make Entrusted Responsibility and Transfers Traceable**  
Named custody, measurement and records should permit later accounting where stakes justify it.

**E4-C47 — Use Shared Resources for Their Legitimate Purpose**  
Institutional resources should not be privately redirected through patronage or conflict of interest.

**E4-C48 — Evaluate Resources by Realised Benefit, Not Accumulation Alone**  
Possession and accumulation are not identical to welfare, security or useful purpose.

## Power, institutions and continuity

**E4-C49 — Treat Authority as Entrusted and Bounded**  
Office creates responsibility within a role, not ownership of the governed or unlimited discretion.

**E4-C50 — Consult Before Consequential Collective Decisions**  
Relevant counsel should inform major collective decisions.

**E4-C51 — Prevent Institutional Capture Through Patronage, Kinship and Private Networks**  
Private relationships should not override legitimate institutional purpose.

**E4-C52 — Distribute Roles Where Complexity Requires It**  
Complex systems can improve resilience and accountability through differentiated responsibility.

**E4-C53 — Maintain and Repair Institutional Function, Not Merely Institutional Form**  
An institution requires continuing operation, provisioning, repair and review.

**E4-C54 — Adapt Institutional Form When Conditions Change While Preserving Legitimate Function**  
Institutional purpose may require changes in roles or procedures.

**E4-C55 — Prepare Succession Through Transfer of Authority, Knowledge, Resources and Support**  
Continuity requires more than naming a successor.

**E4-C56 — Build Proportionate Resilience Against Foreseeable Failure**  
Preparation, maintenance and appropriate redundancy should reduce avoidable fragility.

## Dignity, social standing and group relations

**E4-C57 — Respect Dignity Across Status Differences**  
Poverty, labour status, outsider identity, defeat or social rank do not erase morally relevant standing.

**E4-C58 — Do Not Infer Individual Threat, Guilt or Inferiority Solely From Group Identity**  
Group membership alone is insufficient evidence for individual culpability or danger.

**E4-C59 — Permit Conduct and Participation to Carry Evidential Weight in Assessing Outsider Standing**  
Outsider identity need not exhaust evaluation of a person's relationship to a community.

## Development, self-regulation and future consequence

**E4-C60 — Regulate Reactive Emotion Before Consequential Action**  
Anger and similar reactive states should not determine action where delay can reduce error or disproportionate harm.

**E4-C61 — Cultivate Judgement Through Instruction, Practice, Counsel and Correction**  
Reliable judgement develops through learning and feedback.

**E4-C62 — Do Not Treat Age, Status or Popularity as Proof of Wisdom**  
Hierarchy and consensus are not sufficient evidence of sound judgement.

**E4-C63 — Preserve Individual Responsibility Under Group Pressure**  
Participation in a group does not erase personal responsibility for harmful conduct.

**E4-C64 — Consider Foreseeable Effects on Future Agents and Future System Capacity**  
Present action should account for foreseeable downstream effects on successors and system viability.

**E4-C65 — Match Burden to Capacity and Responsibility to Capability**  
Agents should not be assigned obligations beyond relevant capacity, while greater capability, authority or access can increase reasonable responsibility.
