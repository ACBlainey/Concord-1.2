### E4-LEV-038 — Leviticus 22:28
**Source form:** animal-use restriction  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Human use of animals may be subject to limits that prevent taking a parent animal and its offspring together on the same day.  
**Notes:** The underlying rationale is not made explicit and should not be invented.  
**Provisional cluster:** bounded animal use.

### E4-LEV-039 — Leviticus 23
**Source form:** festival/rest calendar  
**Normative status:** covenantal/ritual with work limits  
**Proposition:** Collective life may reserve recurring periods in which ordinary productive labour is suspended.

### E4-LEV-040 — Leviticus 24:10–16
**Source form:** blasphemy case  
**Normative status:** theological offence and severe sanction  
**Proposition:** **QUARANTINED.** The offence and punishment are inseparable from covenantal theology and are not converted into substrate-neutral ethical law.

**Source form:** injury/homicide law  
**Normative status:** explicit

### E4-LEV-042 — Leviticus 24:22

### E4-LEV-044 — Leviticus 25:8–17
**Source form:** Jubilee/property law  
**Normative status:** explicit

**Provisional cluster:** economic reset / fair valuation.

### E4-LEV-045 — Leviticus 25:14,17
**Source form:** commercial command  
**Normative status:** explicit

**Provisional cluster:** non-exploitative exchange.

### E4-LEV-046 — Leviticus 25:23
**Source form:** land-tenure law  
**Normative status:** theological property limitation  
**Proposition:** Possession of land is represented as limited rather than absolute ownership unconstrained by higher obligations.  
**Notes:** The theological ownership claim remains context; the limited-tenure structure is retained cautiously.  
**Provisional cluster:** bounded property.

### E4-LEV-047 — Leviticus 25:25–34
**Source form:** kinship/property restoration law  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Severe economic loss need not permanently extinguish a household's connection to essential productive property; mechanisms of redemption/restoration may intervene.  
**Provisional cluster:** restoration / economic vulnerability.

### E4-LEV-048 — Leviticus 25:35–38
**Source form:** poverty/lending law  
**Normative status:** explicit positive duty  
**Proposition:** A community member falling into economic insecurity should be supported rather than converted into an opportunity for interest-based extraction.  
**Provisional cluster:** support under poverty / non-exploitative finance.

### E4-LEV-049 — Leviticus 25:39–43
**Source form:** debt-servitude regulation  
**Normative status:** explicit within historical institution  
**Proposition:** Economic vulnerability should not license treatment of a dependent community member as an unrestricted slave, and authority over them must not be ruthless.  
**Provisional cluster:** limits on domination / vulnerable debtor.

### E4-LEV-050 — Leviticus 25:44–46
**Source form:** foreign-slavery law  
**Normative status:** explicit historical institution  
**Proposition:** **ETHICAL TENSION / QUARANTINE.** The text permits materially different treatment of foreign slaves from Israelite dependants. This asymmetry must be preserved and must not be normalised away by the surrounding anti-oppression material.

### E4-LEV-051 — Leviticus 25:47–55
**Source form:** debt/servitude redemption law  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Economic dependency can remain subject to redemption, valuation and release rather than becoming necessarily permanent.  
**Provisional cluster:** redemption / bounded dependency.

### E4-LEV-052 — Leviticus 26
**Source form:** covenant blessings/curses  
**Normative status:** theological/covenantal sanction  
**Proposition:** **NO GENERAL ETHICAL EXTRACTION.** Consequence structure is recorded as theological covenant material rather than empirical social law.

## Leviticus-internal recurrent structures

Without external comparison, the book supplies recurring evidence concerning:

- responsibility for intentional and unintentional wrongdoing;
- acknowledgement after later discovery;
- positive testimony obligations;
- restitution and compensation;
- fair hearing and correctable judgement;
- intimate and kinship boundaries;
- provision for poor persons and outsiders;
- protection against wage withholding and exploitation;
- protection where vulnerability can be exploited;
- impartial adjudication;
- harmful speech and protective intervention;
- restraint of revenge;
- accurate weights and measures;
- equal legal treatment in defined matters;
- periodic restraint of labour and land extraction;
- limits on economic exploitation;
- restoration/redemption mechanisms;
- bounded dependency.

## Negative and tension evidence

E4 must preserve features that resist clean universalisation.

Leviticus contains substantial ritual and purity material that yields no defensible substrate-neutral principle under the current method.

It also contains explicit social asymmetries, especially in Leviticus 25's different treatment of Israelite and foreign slaves. This is not a methodological nuisance. It is evidence.

A comparative method seeking only convergence could easily suppress such material. E4 instead retains it as a future test of:

- historical contextuality;
- limits of ethical generalisation;
- internal ethical tension;
- later ethical development;
- possible compression/decompression across textual strata.

None of those explanations is selected during primary extraction.

## Methodological observation

Leviticus demonstrates that **source seniority and ethical universalisability are not equivalent**.

A canonically central legal text can contain both highly generalisable ethical propositions and strongly context-bound or ethically asymmetric rules.

This is relevant to M3, but the observation remains descriptive until the complete E4 catalogue is frozen.
