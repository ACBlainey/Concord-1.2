## Jeremiah-internal recurrent structures

Without comparison to any external anchor, Jeremiah repeatedly supplies evidence concerning:

- substantive justice over institutional legitimacy claims;
- protection of outsiders and vulnerable persons;
- ruler responsibility for justice and labour exploitation;
- suppression of critics;
- false reassurance;
- corruption of specialist interpretation;
- provenance of authoritative claims;
- empirical accountability of predictions;
- false attribution of messages;
- public hearing and precedent in defence of dissent;
- durable records and resilient preservation;
- censorship and attempted record destruction;
- threat-intelligence verification;
- consultation integrity;
- reform that survives the removal of immediate pressure;
- individual rather than inherited responsibility.

## Negative and tension evidence

Jeremiah also contains severe judgement and vengeance imagery, national-destruction oracles, theological explanations of catastrophe, retaliatory wishes, conflict-specific submission counsel and coercive political contexts. These remain explicitly quarantined.

## Methodological observation

Jeremiah makes **provenance** unusually explicit.

The text repeatedly asks, in its own theological terms:

- Who actually authorised this message?
- Did the claimed source communicate it?
- Has another message been copied or falsely attributed?
- Does a prediction occur?
- Is dissent being answered or merely suppressed?
- Can a governing record survive attempted destruction?

At source level this forms a striking sequence:

**claim → source attribution → provenance challenge → prediction/test → institutional reception → attempted suppression → record persistence.**

This observation is retained independently. No E3, Psalm, Concord or Ethical Topology terminology has been imported.
