# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-04 — Genesis Extraction Block A: Chapters 1–25

## Status

**PRIMARY EXTRACTION — no cross-anchor comparison.**

## Method

Genesis 1–25 was treated as narrative scripture. Character actions were not automatically converted into ethical rules. Priority was given to explicit prescriptions, explicit evaluations, explicit condemnations, consequence-linked evaluations and propositions sufficiently supported by narrative structure.

The propositions below remain source-level extractions. They are not yet the frozen E4 ethical catalogue.

## Extracted source occurrences

### E4-GEN-001 — Genesis 1:26–30
**Source form:** creation narrative / human commission  
**Normative status:** explicit commission  
**Proposition:** Human power over living systems carries an assigned relationship of governance/use rather than mere accidental possession.  
**Notes:** The passage supports a responsibility-bearing human relation to the non-human world, but the exact ethical content of “rule/dominion” should not be over-normalised at this stage.  
**Provisional cluster:** human responsibility toward living systems.

### E4-GEN-002 — Genesis 1:27
**Source form:** creation narrative  
**Normative status:** status attribution  
**Proposition:** Humanity is assigned a distinctive common status that is not restricted to a particular family, class or nation.  
**Notes:** Later dignity interpretations are quarantined; the extraction records only the common status explicitly assigned to humankind.  
**Provisional cluster:** common human standing.

### E4-GEN-003 — Genesis 2:15
**Source form:** creation narrative / assigned task  
**Normative status:** explicit commission  
**Proposition:** A human recipient of an environment may bear duties to cultivate and preserve it.  
**Provisional cluster:** custodial responsibility.

### E4-GEN-004 — Genesis 2:16–17
**Source form:** prohibition  
**Normative status:** explicit rule  
**Proposition:** Granted freedom of use may coexist with defined boundaries and consequences.  
**Notes:** The particular prohibition is theological/contextual; the behavioural structure is retained cautiously.  
**Provisional cluster:** bounded agency.

### E4-GEN-005 — Genesis 2:18–24
**Source form:** relational origin narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit evaluative/narrative formulation  
**Proposition:** Human social existence is relational; durable intimate bonds can create obligations transcending prior household attachment.  
**Provisional cluster:** relational commitment.

### E4-GEN-006 — Genesis 3:1–13
**Source form:** transgression narrative  
**Normative status:** consequence-linked evaluation  
**Proposition:** Concealment, blame displacement and refusal to own one's action aggravate the breakdown produced by transgression.  
**Notes:** Extracted from the accountability structure rather than treating every narrative detail as prescription.  
**Provisional cluster:** responsibility/accountability.

### E4-GEN-007 — Genesis 4:3–8
**Source form:** Cain and Abel narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit warning followed by condemned harm  
**Proposition:** Destructive impulse should be governed before it becomes violence against another person.  
**Provisional cluster:** self-restraint / prevention of interpersonal harm.

### E4-GEN-008 — Genesis 4:9–12
**Source form:** accountability narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit condemnation  
**Proposition:** A person may be held accountable for grave harm done to another and cannot evade responsibility merely by denying relational obligation.  
**Provisional cluster:** responsibility for harm.

### E4-GEN-009 — Genesis 4:10
**Source form:** condemnation  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Unjust killing constitutes a grave wrong demanding accountability.  
**Provisional cluster:** protection of life.

### E4-GEN-010 — Genesis 4:15
**Source form:** protective limitation  
**Normative status:** explicit protective act  
**Proposition:** Accountability for serious wrongdoing does not automatically license unrestricted retaliatory violence.  
**Notes:** Cain remains protected from indiscriminate killing despite his offence.  
**Provisional cluster:** limits on retaliation.

### E4-GEN-011 — Genesis 6:5–13
**Source form:** corruption/flood narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit negative evaluation  
**Proposition:** Pervasive violence and corruption are presented as destructive conditions capable of rendering an entire social order intolerable.  
**Provisional cluster:** violence/corruption as systemic failure.

### E4-GEN-012 — Genesis 8:21–22
**Source form:** post-catastrophe commitment  
**Normative status:** explicit restraint  
**Proposition:** Recognition of persistent human moral weakness need not justify repeated total destruction; restraint can follow judgement.  
**Notes:** Strongly theological setting; retained as a behavioural structure with caution.  
**Provisional cluster:** restraint after wrongdoing.

### E4-GEN-013 — Genesis 9:1–6
**Source form:** post-flood rules  
**Normative status:** explicit prohibition/accountability  
**Proposition:** Human life receives special protection, and intentional bloodshed attracts serious accountability.  
**Provisional cluster:** protection of life.

### E4-GEN-014 — Genesis 9:4
**Source form:** consumption boundary  
**Normative status:** explicit prohibition  
**Proposition:** Permission to use living creatures for food is not represented as wholly unrestricted.  
**Notes:** Exact blood prohibition remains covenantal/legal and should not yet be generalised beyond the text.  
**Provisional cluster:** bounded use of living resources.

### E4-GEN-015 — Genesis 9:8–17
**Source form:** covenant narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit commitment  
**Proposition:** A powerful party can bind itself by durable commitments protecting others from recurrence of catastrophic harm.  
**Provisional cluster:** commitment / restraint of power.

### E4-GEN-016 — Genesis 11:1–9
**Source form:** Babel narrative  
**Normative status:** consequence-linked, interpretively ambiguous  
**Proposition:** Large-scale human coordination and concentration can become ethically problematic when directed toward self-aggrandising centralisation.  
**Notes:** **AMBIGUITY FLAG.** The text's precise ethical target is disputed; retain for later review rather than normalising confidently.  
**Provisional cluster:** concentration / collective ambition.

### E4-GEN-017 — Genesis 12:10–20
**Source form:** Abram/Sarai in Egypt narrative  
**Normative status:** consequence-linked / external rebuke  
**Proposition:** Deception that exposes another person to serious risk can generate responsibility even when motivated by self-protection.  
**Provisional cluster:** deceptive risk transfer.

### E4-GEN-018 — Genesis 13:5–12
**Source form:** Abram/Lot dispute-resolution narrative  
**Normative status:** constructive action  
**Proposition:** Resource conflict may be de-escalated through separation, negotiated choice and willingness by a stronger claimant to forgo first advantage.  
**Provisional cluster:** peaceful dispute resolution.

### E4-GEN-019 — Genesis 14:21–24
**Source form:** post-conflict exchange  
**Normative status:** explicit refusal  
**Proposition:** Refusing material gain may protect independence where accepting it could create compromised obligation or misleading claims of patronage.  
**Provisional cluster:** independence / integrity in material relations.

### E4-GEN-020 — Genesis 16:1–6
**Source form:** household conflict narrative  
**Normative status:** ethically ambiguous / harm-bearing  
**Proposition:** Treating a dependent person instrumentally within another party's reproductive or household objectives can create severe conflict and vulnerability.  
**Notes:** **AMBIGUITY FLAG.** Narrative description is not equivalent to explicit condemnation; retain as possible consequence-linked evidence only.  
**Provisional cluster:** vulnerable/dependent persons.

### E4-GEN-021 — Genesis 16:7–13
**Source form:** Hagar narrative  
**Normative status:** recognition/protection within theological narrative  
**Proposition:** The suffering and interests of a socially vulnerable person remain morally visible even when that person lacks household power.  
**Provisional cluster:** vulnerable-person recognition.

### E4-GEN-022 — Genesis 18:1–8
**Source form:** hospitality narrative  
**Normative status:** exemplary narrative action  
**Proposition:** Hospitality may entail proactive provision for strangers rather than minimal tolerance of their presence.  
**Provisional cluster:** hospitality.

### E4-GEN-023 — Genesis 18:16–33
**Source form:** Abrahamic intercession / justice dialogue  
**Normative status:** explicit moral argument  
**Proposition:** Collective judgement should distinguish culpable from non-culpable persons; the innocent should not simply be swept into punishment for the guilty.  
**Provisional cluster:** discriminating justice.

### E4-GEN-024 — Genesis 18:23–25
**Source form:** justice dialogue  
**Normative status:** explicit normative appeal  
**Proposition:** Legitimate judgement is expected to be just rather than arbitrary, including when exercised by overwhelming authority.  
**Provisional cluster:** justice constrains power.

### E4-GEN-025 — Genesis 19:1–11
**Source form:** Sodom narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit threatened violence / protective response  
**Proposition:** Collective coercive sexual violence against strangers is a grave form of wrongdoing, and guests may require active protection from a hostile group.  
**Notes:** Lot's proposed treatment of his daughters is not treated as normative.  
**Provisional cluster:** protection from coercive violence / hospitality.

### E4-GEN-026 — Genesis 19:30–38
**Source form:** Lot/daughters narrative  
**Normative status:** ethically problematic narrative action  
**Proposition:** Intoxication and deception can remove meaningful capacity for informed sexual participation.  
**Notes:** **CAUTION.** The narrative does not formulate a general law; retain as candidate evidence of compromised agency pending later corroboration.  
**Provisional cluster:** impaired agency / intimate conduct.

### E4-GEN-027 — Genesis 20:1–18
**Source form:** Abraham/Sarah/Abimelech narrative  
**Normative status:** explicit defence, warning and restitution  
**Proposition:** Moral responsibility depends partly upon knowledge and intent; an agent acting without relevant knowledge is distinguished from deliberate wrongdoing.  
**Provisional cluster:** culpability / knowledge.

### E4-GEN-028 — Genesis 20:3–7
**Source form:** divine warning dialogue  
**Normative status:** explicit  
**Proposition:** Integrity of intention and innocence of knowledge are relevant to evaluating culpability, but correction is required once the truth is known.  
**Provisional cluster:** culpability / corrective duty.

### E4-GEN-029 — Genesis 20:14–16
**Source form:** restitution narrative  
**Normative status:** reparative action  
**Proposition:** Where wrongful circumstances have endangered another party, material restitution and public vindication may contribute to repair.  
**Provisional cluster:** restitution / reputation repair.

### E4-GEN-030 — Genesis 21:8–21
**Source form:** Hagar/Ishmael expulsion narrative  
**Normative status:** mixed / protective intervention  
**Proposition:** Dependants affected by household decisions retain morally relevant welfare interests even when excluded from the dominant household.  
**Notes:** Narrative contains competing interests and divine instruction; extraction is limited to the explicit protection/provision of the vulnerable parties.  
**Provisional cluster:** dependant welfare.

### E4-GEN-031 — Genesis 21:22–32
**Source form:** Abraham/Abimelech agreement  
**Normative status:** explicit dispute settlement  
**Proposition:** Disputes over resources can be stabilised through truthful complaint, negotiated recognition and witnessed agreement.  
**Provisional cluster:** negotiated settlement / resource claims.

### E4-GEN-032 — Genesis 22:1–19
**Source form:** binding of Isaac  
**Normative status:** theological test narrative  
**Proposition:** **NO SUBSTRATE-NEUTRAL EXTRACTION AT THIS STAGE.**  
**Notes:** The episode is ethically consequential but inseparable from its theological narrative without importing a contested interpretation. It is therefore quarantined rather than converted into a behavioural principle.

### E4-GEN-033 — Genesis 23:3–20
**Source form:** burial-property negotiation  
**Normative status:** negotiated transaction  
**Proposition:** Durable transfer of property may require explicit terms, recognised consent, public witnessing and completed payment.  
**Provisional cluster:** legitimate property transfer.

### E4-GEN-034 — Genesis 24:1–58
**Source form:** marriage-arrangement narrative  
**Normative status:** negotiated family process  
**Proposition:** Major relational arrangements involve commitments among affected parties and should not be inferred solely from private intention.  
**Notes:** Cultural structure differs markedly from modern individual consent models; do not over-normalise.

### E4-GEN-035 — Genesis 24:57–58
**Source form:** marriage-arrangement dialogue  
**Normative status:** explicit consultation  
**Proposition:** The person whose life is directly affected by a major relational transition is explicitly asked whether she will proceed.  
**Notes:** Retain the narrow textual fact; do not inflate it into a complete modern consent doctrine.  
**Provisional cluster:** affected-person consultation.

## Block A observations — not final principles

Genesis 1–25 already produces recurrent evidence concerning:

- protection of life;
- accountability for harm;
- limits on retaliation;
- bounded exercise of power;
- responsibility toward dependants and vulnerable persons;
- justice that distinguishes culpability;
- knowledge and intent in culpability;
- restitution;
- peaceful dispute settlement;
- legitimate property transfer;
- hospitality;
- relational commitment;
- human responsibility toward living systems.

Several episodes remain intentionally ambiguous or quarantined.

No comparison has been made with E3, Psalms, the Decalogue analysis or the Concord Ethical Kernel.
