## Source-form note

Ecclesiastes (Qohelet) is reflective wisdom literature. It deliberately tests simple relationships between wisdom, labour, wealth, justice, pleasure and outcomes. Descriptive pessimism is not automatically normative, and theological conclusions are not converted into empirical guarantees.

## Methodological observation

Ecclesiastes reinforces a recurring E4 distinction between **agency and control**. Wisdom, labour, skill, wealth and planning matter, but none grants complete control over outcomes. This constrains moral systems that infer worth directly from success or guilt directly from failure.

It also contributes a practical uncertainty ethic: act without pretending to know everything, diversify where outcomes are uncertain, preserve correctability, and distinguish possession from realised welfare.
