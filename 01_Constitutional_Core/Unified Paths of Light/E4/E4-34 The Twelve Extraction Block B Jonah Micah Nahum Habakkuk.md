## E4-34 — The Twelve Extraction Block B: Jonah, Micah, Nahum and Habakkuk

## Status
**PRIMARY EXTRACTION — no cross-anchor comparison.**

# Jonah

### E4-TWL-041 — Jonah 1:1–3
**Source form:** refusal of commission  
**Normative status:** theological narrative  
**Proposition:** **NO GENERAL EXTRACTION from prophetic disobedience alone.**

### E4-TWL-042 — Jonah 1:4–16
**Source form:** shipboard crisis  
**Normative status:** narrative with collective-risk decisions  
**Proposition:** In shared danger, participants seek causal information, question the implicated person and hesitate before imposing irreversible lethal harm.  
**Provisional cluster:** crisis inquiry / restraint.

### E4-TWL-043 — Jonah 1:11–14
**Source form:** proposed sacrifice under danger  
**Normative status:** explicit reluctance  
**Proposition:** Even under severe collective risk, actors may recognise the moral danger of causing an innocent or insufficiently adjudicated person's death.  
**Provisional cluster:** lethal restraint / uncertainty.

### E4-TWL-044 — Jonah 2
**Source form:** prayer  
**Normative status:** theological/poetic  
**Proposition:** **NO GENERAL ETHICAL EXTRACTION.**

### E4-TWL-045 — Jonah 3:1–10
**Source form:** warning and behavioural reform  
**Normative status:** theological with explicit social response  
**Proposition:** A threatened community can respond to warning by changing harmful conduct, including violence, rather than treating prior behaviour as irreversible.  
**Provisional cluster:** collective correctability / violence reduction.

### E4-TWL-046 — Jonah 3:10
**Source form:** changed response after reform  
**Normative status:** theological  
**Proposition:** A threatened sanction can be withdrawn when the behaviour motivating it genuinely changes.  
**Provisional cluster:** conditional sanction / reform.

### E4-TWL-047 — Jonah 4:1–11
**Source form:** dispute over mercy  
**Normative status:** theological narrative  
**Proposition:** Desire for punishment can persist even after an opponent reforms, creating tension between retribution and mercy.  
**Provisional cluster:** mercy / retribution / correctability.

### E4-TWL-048 — Jonah 4:10–11
**Source form:** closing moral question  
**Normative status:** explicit theological comparison  
**Proposition:** Concern for a large population can include persons whose understanding is limited rather than treating ignorance as grounds for indifference to their destruction.  
**Provisional cluster:** limited knowledge / humane concern.

## Block B observation

This block strongly separates **correctability** from **impunity**. Jonah treats changed conduct as relevant to sanction, while Micah and Habakkuk repeatedly deny that power, wealth, institutional status or large achievement can erase the means by which those advantages were obtained.
