# Unified Paths of Light — E4
## E4-54 — Blind Normalisation Protocol and Merge Rules

## Status

**NORMALISATION PHASE OPEN.**

Primary extraction is closed at **1,565 source-occurrence records**. Psalms remain withheld. E3, the Psalm synthesis, Decalogue comparison, Blainey's Laws and Ethical Topology remain quarantined.

# 1. Objective

The purpose of normalisation is to compress repeated and context-specific source occurrences into the **lowest stable set of behaviourally distinct E4 principles** without forcing a predetermined count or importing another framework's categories.

The operative rule is:

> **Preserve evidence. Compress repetition. Retain distinction.**

# 2. Unit of normalisation

A normalised principle is not:
- a verse;
- a theme word;
- a theological doctrine;
- a historical event;
- a frequency count.

It is a behaviourally meaningful ethical proposition capable of accounting for multiple source occurrences without erasing a material difference in duty, protected interest, agency, procedure, culpability or failure mode.

# 3. Merge test

Two candidate propositions may merge when all of the following hold:

1. their behavioural requirement or prohibition is substantially the same;
2. their protected interest is substantially the same;
3. their difference is primarily context, role, genre or implementation;
4. neither proposition adds a distinct threshold, duty, right, failure mode or accountability relation;
5. the merged formulation can still explain the source evidence without becoming vague.

They remain separate where merging would erase a meaningful distinction.

# 4. Parent/implementation control

A domain-specific expression may be an implementation of a broader principle.

Example structure:

**broader principle → domain expression**

rather than:

**two unrelated principles.**

But parent-child relation alone does not justify deletion. A child remains separate if its behavioural content cannot be reliably regenerated from the parent without adding an independent premise.

# 5. Compound-rule control

Some source rules may express intersections among simpler principles.

A candidate is marked **compound** where its behaviour can be explained only by simultaneous application of two or more more-basic constraints.

Compound status is recorded during normalisation; it does not automatically remove the rule. Removal occurs only if the component principles reproduce the ethically relevant content without loss.

# 6. Frequency control

Repeated occurrence demonstrates emphasis and cross-context recurrence.

It does **not** demonstrate multiple principles.

Conversely, rarity does not justify deletion where a source occurrence contains a distinct behavioural constraint.

# 7. Tension control

Negative and ethically contested material is not converted into positive principles simply because it occurs in canonical narrative.

Tension records remain:
- counterexamples;
- boundary cases;
- failure modes;
- evidence of internal disagreement or historical limitation.

# 8. Genre control

Legal rule, wisdom maxim, prophetic critique, narrative case, lament, lyric and administrative procedure may converge on the same principle while providing different evidence.

Cross-genre recurrence strengthens source breadth but does not multiply principles.

# 9. Speaker-status control

Character speech remains distinguished from narrator framing and explicit normative instruction.

Job is the strongest control case: a morally plausible statement does not become E4 doctrine merely because a confident speaker states it.

# 10. Abstraction ceiling

Normalisation must not rise so high that distinct ethical constraints disappear into labels such as:
- goodness;
- justice;
- responsibility;
- flourishing.

A candidate principle must remain sufficiently precise to constrain behaviour.

# 11. Abstraction floor

Normalisation must not remain so concrete that every application becomes a separate principle.

Examples such as:
- honest weights;
- truthful testimony;
- accurate reports;
- non-deceptive counsel

may share a parent principle while retaining substructure where the protected function differs.

# 12. Blindness rule

During normalisation no candidate may be added, deleted, renamed, split or merged **because it resembles or fails to resemble**:
- a Psalm principle;
- an E3 Q principle;
- an E3 attractor;
- a Blainey Law;
- a Decalogue command;
- a predicted Ethical Topology node.

The E4 evidence decides the catalogue.

# 13. Working classification

Candidates will be provisionally grouped only to make deduplication manageable:

A. Life, harm and conflict  
B. Truth, knowledge and evidence  
C. Justice, adjudication and accountability  
D. Agency, consent, conscience and relational boundaries  
E. Care, vulnerability and positive obligation  
F. Property, labour, exchange and resources  
G. Leadership, power and institutional integrity  
H. Social relations, inclusion and cohesion  
I. Self-regulation, development and resilience  
J. Stewardship, continuity and future consequence

These are **working bins, not attractors and not frozen principles**. A principle may move between bins.

# 14. Stability test

A catalogue reaches provisional stability when another merge would cause at least one of:

- loss of behavioural specificity;
- loss of a distinct protected interest;
- loss of a distinct agency relationship;
- loss of a distinct procedural requirement;
- loss of a distinct culpability distinction;
- inability to regenerate a recurrent domain application;
- collapse of a documented tension into a positive rule.

Only after stability testing will E4 IDs be frozen.

# 15. Immediate workflow

The 1,565 occurrence records will now be compressed in staged passes:

1. construct provisional candidates;
2. merge obvious recurrence;
3. separate false friends;
4. identify compounds and implementations;
5. test edge cases;
6. perform minimum-kernel pressure test;
7. freeze the stable catalogue.

Psalms remain closed throughout.

> **Blind E4 normalisation begins here.**
