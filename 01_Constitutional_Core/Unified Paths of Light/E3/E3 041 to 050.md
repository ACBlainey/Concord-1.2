# E3 Unit 041 — Qur'an 3:186
## Patient Restraint Under Injury and Insult

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 3:186 anticipates material and personal trials and offensive speech from opponents, then commends patience and disciplined restraint.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Expect adversity without allowing it to determine moral conduct.**
- **Exercise restraint under verbal provocation.**
- **Endurance can be an active ethical discipline rather than passive surrender.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 5:11–12; Romans 12:17–21 | Endure insult without retaliating in kind. | A | Do not reproduce hostility when provoked. |
| Judaism — Proverbs 19:11 | Patience and overlooking offence are praised. | A/B | Restrain reaction to insult. |
| Buddhism — Dhammapada 3–5 | Hatred is not ended by hatred. | A | Break cycles of reactive hostility. |
| Hinduism — Bhagavad Gita 2:14–15 | Endurance through changing pleasure and pain is praised. | B | Maintain disciplined conduct under adversity. |
| Sikhism — patience and overcoming anger | Hostility should not govern one's conduct. | B | Endure without surrendering to anger. |
| Confucianism — self-mastery | The cultivated person governs emotional reaction through principle. | B | Restrain impulsive response. |
| African Traditional | Patience and reconciliation recur in many communal traditions. | B/C | Endure provocation without escalating communal conflict. |

### Conclusion
The unit reinforces the restraint-under-hostility cluster while explicitly including verbal injury and social adversity.

# E3 Unit 042 — Qur'an 3:188
## Ethics of Credit, Praise, and False Reputation

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 3:188 condemns delight in wrongful conduct while seeking praise for things one has not actually done.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Do not claim credit for work or virtue you did not perform.**
- **Do not construct a false moral reputation.**
- **Praise should correspond to genuine conduct rather than manipulation of appearances.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 6:1–5; 23:5 | Performing or presenting righteousness for reputation is criticised. | A/B | Do not make false moral appearance the purpose of conduct. |
| Judaism — prohibitions on deception; Pirkei Avot | Misleading others and honour-seeking are ethically suspect. | B | Do not obtain esteem through deception. |
| Buddhism — Dhammapada 73–74 | Desire for recognition and status among others is criticised. | A/B | Do not seek undeserved honour. |
| Hinduism — Bhagavad Gita 16:4; 17:18 | Pride and austerity performed for honour are morally inferior. | A/B | Virtue should not be staged for status. |
| Sikhism — rejection of haumai | Ego-driven reputation is a spiritual and ethical distortion. | B | Do not make self-exaltation the aim of conduct. |
| Confucianism — Analects 4.14 | Concern should be with deserving recognition, not merely obtaining it. | A/B | Become worthy rather than manipulate reputation. |
| African Traditional | Reputation ordinarily depends on recognised conduct within community. | B/C | Social esteem should correspond to actual contribution. |

### Conclusion
The source contributes an anti-fraud principle concerning reputation: moral credit itself can be wrongfully appropriated.

# E3 Unit 043 — Qur'an 4:2
## Protection of Orphan Property

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:2 commands restoration of orphan property, prohibits substituting inferior property for valuable property, and forbids consuming orphan wealth by mixing it into one's own.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Return property to its rightful vulnerable owner.**
- **Do not exploit guardianship for enrichment.**
- **Do not substitute inferior assets when entrusted with another's property.**
- **Vulnerability increases fiduciary responsibility rather than permission to exploit.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — James 1:27; Mark 12:40 | Orphans are protected and exploitation of vulnerable households is condemned. | A/B | Do not exploit vulnerable dependants. |
| Judaism — Exodus 22:22; property law | Orphans receive explicit protection against affliction. | A/B | Vulnerability demands protection. |
| Buddhism — Second Precept | Taking what is not given is prohibited. | B | Do not appropriate another's property. |
| Hinduism — asteya and guardianship duties | Wrongful appropriation is prohibited. | B | Custody does not create ownership. |
| Sikhism — honest earning and seva | Exploitative acquisition conflicts with honest livelihood. | B | Do not enrich yourself through another's vulnerability. |
| Confucianism — righteousness over profit | Gain is subordinate to rightful conduct. | B | Do not prefer advantage to fiduciary duty. |
| African Traditional | Kinship guardianship commonly entails obligations to preserve dependent property. | B/C | Guardians hold resources for dependants, not for personal seizure. |

### Conclusion
This is a direct fiduciary ethic: control over vulnerable persons' assets creates heightened responsibility.

# E3 Unit 044 — Qur'an 4:3
## Justice as a Constraint on Plural Marriage

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:3 permits plural marriage within a specific social/legal framework but explicitly conditions it on the ability to deal justly, directing a single marriage where injustice is feared.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Permission does not remove the duty of justice.**
- **Do not assume responsibilities toward multiple dependants when you reasonably fear you cannot treat them fairly.**
- **Self-limitation is required where expanded freedom creates foreseeable injustice.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — marital fidelity traditions | The dominant Christian marriage model differs, but obligations of faithful treatment constrain marital freedom. | C | Relationship freedom remains ethically bounded. |
| Judaism — biblical plural marriage and later restrictions | Historical plural marriage existed alongside duties toward wives; later Jewish law changed practice. | B | Multiple family obligations remain subject to duties of provision and justice. |
| Buddhism — sexual misconduct precept | Sexual relationships are subject to ethical restraint. | C | Desire does not override relational duties. |
| Hinduism — diverse historical marriage rules | Marriage forms vary; dharma imposes household obligations. | C | Household authority carries duties. |
| Sikhism — monogamous marital norm | The institutional form differs materially. | D/C | No direct equivalent to the specific permission; fairness remains a general relational ethic. |
| Confucianism — historically differentiated household structures | Family hierarchy carried role obligations but is not equivalent to the Qur'anic rule. | C | Expanded household authority entails responsibilities. |
| African Traditional | Polygynous systems exist in some traditions and not others; duties vary by society. | C | Where plural households exist, fairness and provision are locally regulated. |

### Conclusion
The specific marriage rule is not universal. The general ethical mechanism is significant: **freedom is narrowed when its exercise creates a foreseeable inability to act justly**.

# E3 Unit 045 — Qur'an 4:4
## Respect for Women's Property and Voluntary Transfer

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:4 requires giving women their marriage gifts and permits another use only where the woman voluntarily remits part of it.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Recognise a woman's independent property claim.**
- **Transfer what is owed directly to the rightful person.**
- **Voluntary consent distinguishes a gift from appropriation.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — general property and non-theft ethics | Wrongful appropriation is prohibited, though historical marital-property systems varied. | B/C | Do not take another person's property without right. |
| Judaism — ketubah and marital-property law | Marriage creates enforceable economic claims for women. | A/B | Marital status does not erase recognised economic rights. |
| Buddhism — Second Precept | Do not take what has not been given. | A/B | Voluntary giving differs from appropriation. |
| Hinduism — stridhana traditions | Women can possess recognised forms of separate property. | A/B | Certain property belongs to the woman herself. |
| Sikhism — equality and rejection of exploitative custom | Women possess equal spiritual dignity; exploitative material practices are rejected. | B | Gender does not justify wrongful appropriation. |
| Confucianism — historical property rules vary | No sufficiently close universal canonical equivalent. | C/D | General fairness exists, but direct equivalence is weak. |
| African Traditional | Women's property rights vary greatly across societies. | C | No single pan-African rule should be asserted. |

### Conclusion
The source provides a concrete property-and-consent rule. Cross-traditional equivalence is uneven and is preserved as such.

# E3 Unit 046 — Qur'an 4:5–6
## Stewardship for Persons Not Yet Ready to Manage Property

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:5–6 instructs guardians to maintain vulnerable dependants from property under management, speak kindly to them, test orphans for maturity, return property when they are capable, avoid consuming it wastefully before they mature, and use witnesses when transferring it.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Guardianship is stewardship, not ownership.**
- **Support dependants from resources held for their benefit.**
- **Capacity should be assessed rather than permanently presumed absent.**
- **Return control when sufficient maturity/capacity is demonstrated.**
- **Do not consume another's assets before they can reclaim them.**
- **Use transparent transfer procedures and witnesses.**
- **Treat dependent persons with dignity while safeguarding their interests.**

### Comparative Analysis — Stewardship and Capacity

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — stewardship teachings; protection of vulnerable people | Entrusted resources must be handled faithfully. | B | Custody creates responsibility, not entitlement. |
| Judaism — guardianship/property traditions | Guardians owe duties concerning property entrusted for another. | A/B | Preserve and return another's assets responsibly. |
| Buddhism — non-stealing and compassionate care | Do not appropriate another's property; protect vulnerable persons. | B | Vulnerability does not license exploitation. |
| Hinduism — guardianship and property law traditions | Custodial responsibility is recognised in legal traditions. | B | Manage dependent property for the owner's benefit. |
| Sikhism — honest dealing and service | Entrusted resources should be handled honestly. | B | Power over another's resources creates duties. |
| Confucianism — benevolent guardianship and role responsibility | Authority over dependants carries duties aimed at their development. | B | Care should develop rather than indefinitely suppress agency. |
| African Traditional | Age-grade and kinship guardianship often transition responsibility with maturity. | B/C | Guardianship should serve eventual capable participation. |

### Discussion
This unit is institutionally rich. Particularly important is its developmental structure: protection during incapacity is paired with **testing for maturity and returning property when capability is reached**. Protection is therefore not automatically permanent control.

### Conclusion
The unit contributes a strong stewardship principle and an early form of capacity-sensitive restoration of agency.

# E3 Unit 047 — Qur'an 4:7
## Recognised Inheritance Claims for Women and Men

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:7 states that both men and women have assigned shares in property left by parents and close relatives.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Do not erase a person's property claim solely because of sex.**
- **Inheritance should operate through recognised rules rather than unrestricted seizure by stronger parties.**
- **Women possess legally cognisable economic interests.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity / biblical tradition | Biblical inheritance rules historically differ by sex and circumstance; later Christian legal systems vary. | C | No direct universal scriptural equivalent. |
| Judaism — Numbers 27:1–11 | Daughters can inherit where there are no sons, establishing recognised female inheritance claims within a different allocation system. | A/B | Women can possess recognised inheritance rights. |
| Buddhism | Canonical monastic/property contexts do not supply a close universal inheritance rule. | D | No clear direct equivalent identified. |
| Hinduism | Historical inheritance rules vary by school and period; female property rights exist but differ. | C | Women may possess recognised property claims, but structures vary. |
| Sikhism | Equality ideals support women's standing, while civil inheritance rules depend on historical/legal context. | B/C | Sex should not erase human dignity or legitimate claims. |
| Confucianism | Traditional patrilineal inheritance structures do not provide a close equivalent. | D/C | Direct equivalence is weak. |
| African Traditional | Inheritance systems vary widely, including patrilineal, matrilineal, and mixed arrangements. | D/C | No pan-African equivalent can responsibly be asserted. |

### Conclusion
This unit intentionally produces weak convergence. It is retained because the Qur'anic anchor explicitly creates female inheritance claims; E3 does not broaden other traditions until they appear equivalent.

# E3 Unit 048 — Qur'an 4:8–10
## Humane Distribution and Severe Protection of Orphan Wealth

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:8–10 encourages kindness toward relatives, orphans, and needy people present at estate division, asks decision-makers to imagine vulnerability affecting their own descendants, and strongly condemns unjust consumption of orphan property.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Formal entitlement does not exhaust generosity.**
- **Speak kindly to people who are economically excluded from a distribution.**
- **Use role reversal to understand vulnerability.**
- **Protect dependent property from predatory guardians.**
- **Consider how you would want your own vulnerable descendants treated.**

### Comparative Analysis — Role Reversal and Vulnerability

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 7:12 | Treat others as you would wish to be treated. | A | Apply reciprocal standards. |
| Judaism — Hillel, Shabbat 31a | Do not do to another what is hateful to you. | A | Use reciprocity to constrain conduct. |
| Buddhism — reciprocity/non-harm formulations | Recognise that others fear suffering as oneself does. | A/B | Understand another's vulnerability through one's own. |
| Hinduism — Mahabharata reciprocity formulations | Do not impose what would be harmful to yourself. | A/B | Apply moral role reversal. |
| Sikhism — equality and shared dignity | Other persons' welfare is not morally inferior to one's own. | B | Extend equal concern beyond self and family. |
| Confucianism — Analects 15.24 | Do not impose on others what you do not desire yourself. | A | Reciprocity is a practical ethical test. |
| African Traditional — Ubuntu | One's humanity is relationally connected to the humanity of others. | B | Treat others' vulnerability as socially significant. |

### Conclusion
The passage joins fiduciary protection with a striking perspective-taking heuristic: imagine leaving one's own children vulnerable and act accordingly.

# E3 Unit 049 — Qur'an 4:19
## Consent, Non-Coercion, and Kind Treatment in Marriage

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:19 prohibits inheriting women against their will, prohibits coercive pressure intended to recover property previously given, and instructs living with wives in recognised kindness even where dislike exists.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **A person is not inheritable property.**
- **Do not coerce intimate partners for financial gain.**
- **Do not use relationship power to recover gifts through pressure.**
- **Treat a partner decently even when affection is strained.**
- **Immediate dislike does not justify abusive conduct.**

### Comparative Analysis — Non-Coercion in Intimate Relations

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Ephesians 5:25–33; 1 Corinthians 7 | Marriage carries duties of care and reciprocal obligation. | B | Partnership does not justify deliberate mistreatment. |
| Judaism — marital consent/property traditions | Jewish law developed protections and obligations within marriage, though historical structures differ. | B | Marriage is governed by duties rather than unrestricted possession. |
| Buddhism — Sigalovada Sutta | A husband owes respect and honour to his wife. | A/B | Treat a spouse with respect rather than domination. |
| Hinduism — household dharma and non-harm | Family authority remains ethically constrained. | B | Intimate relationships do not suspend duties of proper conduct. |
| Sikhism — marital partnership and equality | Marriage is framed as shared spiritual life. | B | A spouse is a partner, not property. |
| Confucianism — reciprocal family obligations | Historical hierarchy differs, but roles carry duties of humane treatment. | C | Relational authority entails responsibility. |
| African Traditional | Marriage and bridewealth systems vary greatly; coercive and consensual structures cannot be generalised. | C/D | No single pan-African equivalent should be asserted. |

### Conclusion
The Qur'anic source directly limits property-like treatment and financial coercion of women. Cross-traditional analogues exist but are historically uneven.

# E3 Unit 050 — Qur'an 4:29–30
## Property by Mutual Consent and Prohibition of Destructive Harm

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:29–30 prohibits consuming one another's wealth unjustly, distinguishes legitimate trade conducted by mutual consent, and prohibits destructive killing/harm.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Do not acquire another person's wealth through wrongful means.**
- **Mutual consent is a core condition of legitimate exchange.**
- **Economic interaction should distinguish voluntary trade from exploitation.**
- **Do not engage in wrongful destructive harm against one another.**

### Comparative Analysis — Mutual Consent in Exchange

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — prohibitions on theft, fraud, and extortion | Property transfer should not depend on wrongful taking. | B | Do not enrich yourself through coercion or fraud. |
| Judaism — Leviticus 19:11–13; commercial law | Theft, deception, and economic oppression are prohibited. | A/B | Exchange must respect property and honest dealing. |
| Buddhism — Second Precept | Do not take what has not been given. | A/B | Legitimate possession depends on voluntary transfer. |
| Hinduism — asteya | Taking what is not rightfully given is prohibited. | A/B | Do not appropriate another's property. |
| Sikhism — kirat karo | Wealth should be earned honestly. | B | Economic gain should arise through legitimate activity. |
| Confucianism — righteousness over profit | Profit is not legitimate when detached from right conduct. | B | Gain remains constrained by justice. |
| African Traditional | Exchange systems generally depend on recognised reciprocal obligations, though forms vary. | B/C | Legitimate transfer is socially recognised rather than predatory. |

### Discussion
The explicit pairing of **mutual consent** with legitimate trade gives this unit a clear transactional principle. It also links economic non-exploitation to protection from destructive harm, keeping property ethics within a broader non-harm framework.

### Conclusion
Honest exchange and prohibition of wrongful appropriation are highly convergent. The Qur'anic formulation makes mutual consent particularly explicit.

| Cluster | Units |
|---|---|
| Restraint under insult and adversity | 041 |
| False credit and reputation manipulation | 042 |
| Fiduciary protection of orphan property | 043, 046, 048 |
| Justice limiting otherwise permitted freedom | 044 |
| Women's property/economic claims | 045, 047 |
| Developmental stewardship and restoration of control | 046 |
| Reciprocity and perspective-taking | 048 |
| Non-coercion in marriage | 049 |
| Consent and honest economic exchange | 050 |

The data continues to produce uneven convergence where appropriate. Units 044, 045, 047, and 049 are especially sensitive to historically different marriage, gender, and property systems; equivalence has therefore not been forced.

A notable emergent pattern is the repeated conversion of **power into stewardship obligations**. Guardianship over orphan property (043, 046, 048), marital/legal power (044, 049), and economic power (050) are all constrained by duties toward the person exposed to that power.

Unit 046 is particularly significant because guardianship is developmental rather than automatically permanent: protect property while capacity is immature, assess maturity, then return control. This is recorded as an anchor-derived finding without importing downstream Concord categories.
