# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Near-Universal Evidence Matrix 002

**Principles:** Q024, Q025, Q026, Q031, Q033.

---

## Q024 — Protect Property of Vulnerable or Incapacitated Persons

Protection of vulnerable persons is extremely widespread. The fiduciary requirement to protect **their property** is narrower.

Jewish and biblical material provides direct orphan-property and guardian-duty routes; Islamic evidence is the anchor. Other traditions may protect dependants without framing their assets as a separately protected legal interest.

**Decision:** **Common / Near-universal at exact formulation.**

## Q025 — Fair Inheritance and Transfer

Inheritance exists across societies, but ethical rules differ substantially over heirs, gender, lineage, primogeniture, communal ownership and testamentary freedom.

General fairness or property respect is insufficient evidence for equivalent inheritance ethics.

**Decision:** **Common.** This should not be pushed upward merely because every tradition has inheritance customs.

## Q026 — No Exploitative Finance

Condemnation of greed and exploitation is widespread. Specific financial rules concerning interest, debt, lending and unjust gain differ considerably.

Biblical/Jewish and Islamic traditions contain explicit lending rules; Buddhist, Hindu, Sikh, Confucian and African traditions can supply anti-exploitation ethics, but those are not automatically equivalent to the same finance rule.

**Decision:** **Common / Near-universal at functional anti-exploitation level; Common at narrower financial mechanism level.**

## Q031 — Non-Transactional Aid

Generosity is widespread. Giving without expectation of return, recognition, reciprocal advantage or status gain is a stronger formulation.

Several traditions explicitly value selfless giving, including Buddhist *dana*, Hindu traditions of giving, Sikh *seva*, Christian charity and Qur'anic giving. Confucian and African reciprocity traditions may support aid while conceptualising reciprocity differently.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at functional level.**

## Q033 — Structured Social Provision

Care for the vulnerable is close to universal. A structured mechanism that allocates resources to defined categories is not.

Religious charity institutions, communal relief and state/lineage systems vary greatly.

**Decision:** **Common.** Universal concern should not be confused with universal institutionalisation.

---

## Matrix 002 Finding

Property and economic ethics are particularly sensitive to **institutional form**.

Every civilisation must distribute property, inheritance and aid somehow. The existence of a social mechanism is not itself evidence that the mechanism instantiates the same ethical rule.
