# E3 Unit 221 — Qur'an 6:108
## Do Not Provoke Reciprocal Desecration

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:108 prohibits insulting what other communities hold sacred when doing so predictably provokes reciprocal insult.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Speech should be evaluated partly by foreseeable reciprocal effects.
- Disagreement does not require deliberate desecration.
- Provocation can create escalating cycles between groups.
- Restraint can protect one's own community as well as another's dignity.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — 1 Peter 3:15–16 | Conviction should be expressed with gentleness and respect. | A/B | Disagree without gratuitous insult. |
| Judaism | Respectful treatment of outsiders' property and peace can provide functional parallels. | B/C | Avoid needless provocation. |
| Buddhism — Right Speech | Harsh, divisive speech is constrained. | A | Do not use speech to generate hostility. |
| Hinduism | Pluralist traditions often support respectful coexistence. | B | Difference need not become desecration. |
| Sikhism | Respect for other faiths is strongly present historically. | B | Preserve conviction without degrading others. |
| Confucianism — propriety | Speech should preserve workable social relations. | B | Restrain inflammatory expression. |
| African Traditional | Inter-sacred relations vary widely. | C | Reciprocal respect can reduce intergroup escalation. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: duplicate of Unit 083; merge.**

# E3 Unit 222 — Qur'an 6:141
## Consume Without Waste

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:141 permits enjoyment of produce while requiring its due and prohibiting waste or excess.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Legitimate consumption and stewardship can coexist.
- Production can carry distributive obligations.
- Waste is ethically relevant even where possession is legitimate.
- Ownership does not imply moral indifference to resource loss.

| Hinduism — moderation | Disciplined use of resources is valued. | B | Avoid excess. |
| Sikhism | Honest earning and sharing discourage wasteful self-indulgence. | B | Use resources responsibly. |
| Confucianism — frugality | Frugality is a recognised virtue. | A/B | Avoid wasteful excess. |
| African Traditional | Stewardship norms vary by ecology and community. | B/C | Resource use should preserve communal continuity. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: duplicate of Unit 084; merge.**

# E3 Unit 223 — Qur'an 7:33
## Do Not Claim Moral Authority Without Knowledge

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 7:33 groups wrongful aggression with saying about God what one does not know.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Do not present unsupported claims as authoritative moral knowledge.
- Epistemic uncertainty should constrain prescription.
- Moral authority can be abused through confident fabrication.
- Claims with high normative consequences require adequate grounds.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — James 3:1 | Teachers face heightened responsibility. | B | Normative authority carries epistemic responsibility. |
| Judaism — Deuteronomy 18:20 | False claims of divine authority are seriously constrained. | A | Do not falsely claim sacred authority. |
| Buddhism — Kalama Sutta | Claims should not be accepted merely through asserted authority. | B | Test claims before granting authority. |
| Hinduism — pramana | Knowledge claims require valid grounds. | B | Normative assertion should track knowledge. |
| Sikhism | Truthful speech constrains false spiritual authority. | B | Do not fabricate moral certainty. |
| Confucianism — Analects 2:17 | Knowing what one knows and does not know is knowledge. | A | Acknowledge epistemic limits. |
| African Traditional | Sacred authority is tradition-specific. | C | Claims to authority should follow recognised knowledge. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a strong epistemic-humility and authority principle.

# E3 Unit 224 — Qur'an 7:56

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 7:56 prohibits causing corruption on earth after it has been set right.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Functioning order has preservation value.
- Change should account for damage to systems that already work.
- Reform and disruption are not automatically synonymous.
- Avoid degrading social or ecological conditions without sufficient reason.

| Confucianism — social harmony | Good order should be cultivated rather than casually destabilised. | A/B | Preserve legitimate functioning relationships. |

**Audit disposition: duplicate of Unit 088; merge.**

# E3 Unit 225 — Qur'an 8:1
## Disputed Collective Resources Require Rule-Governed Allocation

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 8:1 responds to dispute over war gains by removing them from unilateral claimant control and coupling the issue with reconciliation and obedience to a governing rule.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Contested collective resources should not simply go to the strongest claimant.
- Distribution disputes require legitimate rules.
- Material conflict can damage group cohesion.
- Allocation mechanisms should precede or constrain private appropriation.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Acts 6:1–6 | Distribution disputes are answered with structured administration. | A/B | Shared resources need legitimate allocation procedures. |
| Judaism — Numbers 31 / communal allocation traditions | Collective gains can be distributed by rule rather than seizure. | B | Common resources require recognised allocation. |
| Buddhism — sangha property rules | Communal property is governed by institutional rules. | A/B | Shared assets are not private spoils. |
| Hinduism | Royal/community allocation traditions provide partial parallels. | C | Collective resources require rule-governed distribution. |
| Sikhism | Communal resources are administered for shared purposes. | B | Common assets should serve legitimate communal ends. |
| Confucianism | Public resources should be administered through proper authority. | B | Allocation should not be captured by private appetite. |
| African Traditional | Common-resource institutions vary widely. | B/C | Collective assets require recognised distributive norms. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** The transferable principle is institutional allocation of contested common resources.

# E3 Unit 226 — Qur'an 8:20–22
## Listen Responsively Rather Than Perform Agreement

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 8:20–22 criticises people who say they hear while not actually listening or responding.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Nominal consultation is not genuine consultation.
- Listening requires responsiveness to relevant information.
- Institutions can simulate participation while ignoring input.
- Performative agreement undermines trust and learning.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — James 1:22 | Hearing without doing is explicitly criticised. | A | Listening should affect conduct. |
| Judaism | Study is directed toward action. | B | Hearing ethical instruction should not be performative. |
| Buddhism | Learning requires practice, not mere recitation. | B | Convert hearing into disciplined response. |
| Hinduism | Hearing and reflection precede realised practice. | B | Information should be genuinely processed. |
| Sikhism | Truth is to be lived, not merely heard. | B | Listening should change action. |
| Confucianism | Learning requires reflection and practice. | A/B | Genuine learning is responsive. |
| African Traditional | Deliberative listening norms vary. | C | Consultation without responsiveness is hollow. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** It strengthens the project's distinction between procedural appearance and functional reality.

Qur'an 8:27 prohibits knowingly betraying trusts.

- Access is not ownership.

# E3 Unit 229 — Qur'an 8:58
## End Agreements Openly Rather Than Betray Them Covertly

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 8:58 requires that where treaty betrayal is genuinely feared, the agreement be returned or terminated openly so the parties stand on clear terms.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Suspicion does not license secret betrayal.
- Agreements can be ended, but termination should be communicated.
- Procedural transparency matters even under distrust.
- Reciprocity should be restored openly before hostile action replaces agreement.

# E3 Unit 230 — Qur'an 8:61
## Reciprocate Credible Peace Openings

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 8:61 directs acceptance when an opponent genuinely inclines toward peace.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Conflict should not perpetuate itself after a credible peace opportunity appears.
- Past hostility does not make reconciliation permanently unavailable.
- Peace offers deserve evaluation rather than automatic rejection.
- Security concerns and willingness to de-escalate must be held together.
