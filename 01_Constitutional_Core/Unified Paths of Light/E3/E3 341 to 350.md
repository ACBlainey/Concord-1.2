# E3 Unit 341 — Qur'an 5:42
## Adjudication Should Remain Fair Even When One Could Decline the Case

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:42 permits declining a dispute but requires justice if one chooses to adjudicate it.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Voluntary jurisdiction does not permit biased judgement.
- Once authority is exercised, procedural fairness attaches to it.
- Discretion over whether to act is different from discretion over what counts as justice.
- Institutions should not accept cases merely to favour one side.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Impartial judgement is strongly required. | B | If judging, judge fairly. |
| Judaism — Deuteronomy 1:16–17 | Judges are commanded to hear and judge fairly. | A | Jurisdiction entails impartiality. |
| Buddhism — sangha procedure | Once communal adjudication occurs, recognised procedure matters. | B | Decision authority requires fair process. |
| Hinduism — judicial dharma | Judges/rulers are bound by standards of justice. | B | Authority does not permit arbitrary outcome. |
| Sikhism | Justice is a core constraint on authority. | B | Exercise judgement without favour. |
| Confucianism | Proper office entails righteous decision. | B | Role discretion remains bounded by duty. |
| African Traditional | Adjudicative systems vary. | C | Accepted jurisdiction creates duties of fair hearing. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 075.** The jurisdiction/fairness distinction is worth preserving in synthesis notes.

# E3 Unit 342 — Qur'an 5:89
## Broken Commitments Can Require Repair Rather Than Mere Condemnation

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:89 regulates deliberate oaths and provides a structured form of expiation when they are broken.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Commitments matter, but systems also need pathways after breach.
- Accountability can include concrete repair rather than permanent exclusion.
- The seriousness of an obligation can be reflected through restitution.
- Ethical governance should distinguish careless speech from deliberate commitment.
- Corrective mechanisms can restore participation after failure.

### Comparative Analysis

| Judaism | Vow law and restitution provide strong parallels. | A | Commitments carry consequences and repair procedures. |
| Buddhism | Confession and restoration procedures exist in monastic discipline. | B | Failure can be acknowledged and corrected. |
| Hinduism | Vow and expiation traditions provide parallels. | B | Breach can require corrective action. |
| Sikhism | Truthful living supports accountability and correction. | B | Repair inconsistency rather than normalising it. |
| Confucianism | Trustworthiness requires correction after failure. | B | Restore reliability through conduct. |
| African Traditional | Oath/reparation practices vary. | C | Breach may create obligations of repair. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 078 and commitment-repair cluster.**

# E3 Unit 343 — Qur'an 5:100
## Popularity or Abundance Does Not Establish Moral Quality

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:100 states that bad and good are not equal even when the abundance of the bad is impressive.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Quantity is not quality.
- Majority prevalence is not sufficient evidence of moral legitimacy.
- Popularity can create cognitive pressure without changing ethical substance.
- Systems should preserve independent standards against crowd effects.
- Scale should be evaluated rather than worshipped.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 7:13–14 | Popularity is not treated as proof of rightness. | A/B | Majority adoption does not establish moral truth. |

| Buddhism | Truth is not determined solely by tradition, report, or numbers. | A/B | Evaluate claims independently of popularity. |
| Hinduism | Valid knowledge is not reducible to popularity. | B | Quantity does not determine truth. |
| Sikhism | Truthful living is not validated by status or mass approval. | B | Preserve principle against social pressure. |
| Confucianism | The exemplary person can maintain righteousness against profit/status pressure. | B | Social prevalence does not settle rightness. |
| African Traditional | Communal consensus can carry strong authority in some traditions, limiting direct equivalence. | C | Consensus and ethical quality are analytically distinct. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 080.** This is important to anti-majoritarian epistemic safeguards.

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:152 combines protection of orphan property, full measure and weight, obligations calibrated to capacity, just speech even concerning relatives, and fulfilment of commitments.

- Justice is cross-domain: property, commerce, speech, and promises.

- Obligation should not exceed actual capacity.
- Vulnerability increases duties of stewardship.
- Reliable standards make complex social cooperation possible.

# E3 Unit 346 — Qur'an 8:58
## End Agreements Transparently Before Treating the Other Party as Unbound

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 8:58 instructs that where serious betrayal is feared, an agreement should be returned or terminated openly so both parties know where they stand.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Suspicion does not justify covert breach.
- If a treaty or agreement must end, termination should be communicated.
- Parties should not be held to obligations that the other side has secretly abandoned.
- Transparency reduces ambush and informational asymmetry.
- Rule-governed exit is part of trustworthy cooperation.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Promise integrity strongly converges, though treaty procedure is less explicit. | B | Do not secretly abandon commitments. |
| Judaism | Covenant and treaty obligations strongly support transparent dealing. | B | End commitments through recognised process. |
| Buddhism | Truthful speech and non-deception provide functional convergence. | B | Do not exploit hidden informational asymmetry. |
| Hinduism — statecraft traditions | Treaty law includes conditions of alliance and rupture. | B | Agreement changes should be explicit. |
| Sikhism | Truthful dealing constrains treacherous breach. | B | Do not conceal abandonment of commitment. |
| Confucianism — xin | Trustworthiness requires clarity in commitments. | A/B | Make changed obligations known. |
| African Traditional | Treaty/covenant procedures vary. | C | Legitimate exit should be recognisable to both parties. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 093.** This is a strong procedural-transparency principle.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Retaliatory symmetry is not always the best response.
- Constructive action can interrupt escalation.

- Generosity and reconciliation can transform relational dynamics.
