# E3 Unit 321 — Qur'an 90:12–17
## Liberation, Feeding Under Scarcity, Patience, and Compassion

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 90:12–17 associates the difficult ethical path with freeing a person from bondage, feeding during famine an orphan relative or severely poor person, and mutual encouragement toward patience and compassion.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Freedom from coercive bondage is a positive good.
- Aid becomes especially important during systemic scarcity.
- Kinship can intensify care obligations without exhausting them.
- Compassion should be socially reinforced, not left solely to private disposition.
- Communities can mutually encourage resilience and mercy.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Liberation, feeding the hungry, and mercy strongly converge. | A/B | Free, feed, and sustain vulnerable people. |
| Judaism | Redemption from bondage, poor relief, and kin obligations strongly converge. | A | Freedom and subsistence are serious duties. |
| Buddhism | Compassion and generosity strongly converge; bondage is inconsistent with non-harm. | B | Reduce suffering and support need. |
| Hinduism | Dana, compassion, and liberation themes provide functional parallels. | B | Aid the vulnerable, especially under hardship. |
| Sikhism | Seva, equality, and feeding through langar strongly converge. | A | Institutionalise compassionate support. |
| Confucianism | Humane governance and care for vulnerable persons converge. | B | Social order requires compassionate provision. |
| African Traditional | Mutual aid under scarcity is widespread but diverse. | B | Community solidarity is tested under hardship. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a high-density positive-duty source linking liberty, subsistence, resilience, and compassion.

# E3 Unit 322 — Qur'an 93:9–10
## Do Not Oppress the Orphan or Repel the Petitioner

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 93:9–10 prohibits harsh treatment of an orphan and rebuking or repelling a person who asks.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Vulnerability should not invite domination.
- People seeking assistance deserve dignified treatment even when aid cannot be granted.
- Power asymmetry increases duties of restraint.
- Social-support systems should protect applicants from humiliation.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — James 1:27 | Orphans receive explicit protective concern. | A | Protect vulnerable dependants. |
| Judaism — Exodus 22:22 | Affliction of orphans is explicitly prohibited. | A | Do not exploit vulnerable persons. |
| Buddhism | Compassion and generous conduct provide strong convergence. | A/B | Respond to need without contempt. |
| Hinduism | Dana and compassion support dignified aid. | B | Do not humiliate the needy. |
| Sikhism | Seva and equality strongly converge. | A/B | Assistance should preserve dignity. |
| Confucianism | Benevolence toward vulnerable persons provides functional convergence. | B | Treat petitioners humanely. |
| African Traditional | Kin/orphan support norms vary but are often strong. | B | Vulnerability creates protective duties. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** The dignity of the petitioner is distinct from the quantity of aid provided.

# E3 Unit 324 — Qur'an 104:1–3
## Slander and Wealth Accumulation Can Reinforce False Superiority

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 104:1–3 condemns habitual mockery and slander alongside obsessive accumulation and counting of wealth accompanied by an illusion of security or permanence.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Reputational aggression and material status can reinforce one another.
- Wealth can create false confidence in immunity from consequence.
- Counting resources is not itself wrong, but identity built around accumulation can distort conduct.
- Social systems should be alert to status power being converted into humiliation of others.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Luke 12:15–21 / James 3 | Hoarded wealth and destructive speech are both criticised. | A/B | Wealth does not legitimise contempt. |
| Judaism | Harmful speech and greed are both strongly constrained. | A/B | Material success does not excuse slander. |
| Buddhism | Attachment, conceit, and harmful speech strongly converge. | A | Do not turn possession into ego and harm. |
| Hinduism | Greed, pride, and harmful speech are recurrent vices. | A/B | Wealth should not feed arrogance. |
| Sikhism | Greed and ego are major vices; slander is condemned. | A | Resist wealth-based superiority. |
| Confucianism | Profit should remain subordinate to righteousness; contempt violates propriety. | B | Status must not displace humane conduct. |
| African Traditional | Wealth/status norms vary. | C | Accumulation should not license humiliation. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge across anti-slander, anti-greed, and humility clusters while preserving this combined source.**

# E3 Unit 326 — Qur'an 107:4–7
## Public Virtue Is Hollow When Paired With Showmanship and Withholding Small Help

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 107:4–7 criticises performative religious conduct undertaken for display while ordinary acts of assistance are withheld.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Public signalling is not a substitute for practical beneficence.
- Small forms of assistance matter because civilisation depends on routine cooperation.
- Visible virtue can conceal low-cost selfishness.
- Evaluation should compare symbolic claims with mundane behaviour.
- Institutions should avoid rewarding appearance so strongly that substance becomes secondary.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 6:1–4 | Public performance of righteousness for praise is explicitly criticised. | A | Do good rather than merely display goodness. |
| Judaism | Prophetic ethics criticise ritual detached from justice and care. | A/B | Formal observance cannot replace practical duty. |
| Buddhism | Intention matters; giving for status is ethically inferior to less attached generosity. | A/B | Motive and conduct matter beyond display. |
| Hinduism — Bhagavad Gita 17:18–22 | Practice and giving for honour or display are treated as inferior. | A | Do not turn virtue into status theatre. |
| Sikhism | Empty ritual without truthful living and service is strongly criticised. | A | Practice must become conduct. |
| Confucianism | Ritual without genuine humaneness is deficient. | A/B | Form should embody substance. |
| African Traditional | Ritual/social-display relations vary greatly. | C | Public status should not substitute for actual reciprocity. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a strong anti-performativity and implementation-integrity source.

# E3 Unit 327 — Qur'an 109:1–6
## Deep Religious Disagreement Need Not Be Resolved by Forced Conformity

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 109 presents a clear separation between incompatible worship commitments and concludes with each side retaining its religion.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Some deep disagreements may remain unresolved.
- Coexistence does not require doctrinal merger.
- Clear boundaries can sometimes preserve peace better than coerced consensus.
- Tolerance can mean allowing persistent difference rather than pretending difference does not exist.
- The passage should be read alongside, rather than as a replacement for, the Qur'an's other non-compulsion and intergroup ethics passages.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Historical and textual traditions on religious exclusivity/toleration are mixed. | C | Some modern interpretations support coexistence without doctrinal agreement. |
| Judaism | Community boundaries coexist historically with frameworks for non-Jewish communities. | B/C | Difference need not require doctrinal assimilation. |
| Buddhism | Plural religious coexistence has substantial functional compatibility. | B | Do not require identical belief for peaceful coexistence. |
| Hinduism | Multiple-path traditions provide strong functional convergence in some schools. | A/B | Difference can persist without forced merger. |
| Sikhism | Respect for other religious paths provides strong convergence. | A/B | Preserve conviction while recognising others' freedom. |
| Confucianism | Less centred on exclusive religious profession; coexistence is functionally compatible. | B | Social harmony need not require identical metaphysics. |
| African Traditional | Religious pluralism varies greatly. | C | Multiple traditions can coexist without full doctrinal convergence. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain cautiously.** Its functional relevance is peaceful persistence of disagreement, not a claim that all doctrines are equivalent.

# E3 Unit 329 — Qur'an 6:52
## Do Not Exclude Low-Status Participants to Appease Elites

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:52 prohibits driving away sincere people of lower apparent status and states that one party is not responsible for the other's account in a way that would justify exclusion.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Inclusion should not be traded for elite approval.
- Social status is not reliable evidence of moral worth.
- Leaders should resist pressure to remove inconvenient low-power participants.
- Accountability should not be transferred merely to rationalise exclusion.
- Community design should protect against prestige capture.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — James 2:1–7 | Status-based preference in assembly is explicitly condemned. | A | Do not exclude poor people to favour elites. |
| Judaism | Impartial justice and dignity traditions provide strong parallels. | A/B | Status should not determine fair inclusion. |
| Buddhism — anti-caste teachings | Spiritual standing is not determined by social rank. | A | Do not make class a condition of access. |
| Hinduism | Hierarchical and egalitarian strands coexist, limiting simple equivalence. | C | Some traditions challenge inherited status exclusion. |
| Sikhism — caste rejection | Strong direct functional convergence. | A | Shared community should cross status divisions. |
| Confucianism | Moral merit can outrank wealth, though hierarchy remains. | B | Wealth is not equivalent to virtue. |
| African Traditional | Status structures vary widely. | C | No universal analogue asserted. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge with Units 219, 259, and 318.** This was an identified early omission and strengthens the anti-status-capture cluster.

# E3 Unit 330 — Qur'an 7:33
## Prohibit Aggression and Unsupported Claims to Moral Authority

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 7:33 groups prohibitions including indecency, wrongdoing, unjust aggression, and speaking about divine authority without knowledge.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Aggression is not legitimised merely by power or desire.
- Moral authority should not be claimed without knowledge.
- Epistemic misconduct can become a governance harm when unsupported claims are imposed on others.
- Behavioural non-harm and epistemic restraint belong within the same ethical architecture.
- Rule systems should distinguish genuine authority from invented certainty.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Mark 7:7–8 / non-harm teachings | Invented religious commands and wrongful conduct are both criticised. | A/B | Do not impose unsupported moral authority. |
| Judaism — Deuteronomy 4:2 | Adding invented commands is constrained. | A | Normative authority has limits. |
| Buddhism | False claims and harmful action are both ethically constrained. | B | Do not fabricate authority or aggress. |
| Hinduism | Epistemic traditions distinguish valid authority from unsupported assertion. | B | Normative claims require grounds. |
| Sikhism | False religiosity and oppressive conduct are criticised. | B | Do not convert unsupported claims into domination. |
| Confucianism | Correct naming and disciplined knowledge provide functional parallels. | B | Authority claims should correspond to reality. |
| African Traditional | Normative authority is highly tradition-specific. | C | Claims of binding authority require recognised legitimacy. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge partly with Unit 246.** This was another identified early omission and reinforces the connection between epistemic restraint and anti-oppression.

# Interim Findings: Audit Units 321–330

The audit reaches the end-region of the Qur'anic canon while also recovering known early omissions. Units 321–327 add liberation, dignified aid, collective ethical reinforcement, anti-performativity, and coexistence across unresolved disagreement. Units 328–330 deliberately return to previously identified omissions, demonstrating why the canonical audit was necessary before freezing the source corpus.

The audit is **not yet declared complete**. Remaining known omissions and a final verse-by-verse completeness check must be resolved before the source index is frozen and deduplication begins.
