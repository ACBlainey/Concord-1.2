# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Paper IV: Principle Normalisation and Compression Method

**Anchor Text:** Qur'an  
**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey  
**Date:** 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

The extraction and canonical-audit phase of E3 closed at Unit 420 because continued source-level extraction was increasingly producing propositions that would be merged during normalisation.

Paper IV now changes the unit of analysis.

The preceding files preserved **source occurrences**. Paper IV derives **principle identities**.

The task is not to shorten the audit mechanically. It is to determine which extracted propositions express the same functional ethic, which remain genuinely distinct, which are subordinate implementations of broader principles, and which require exclusion.

The output of this phase will be the independently derived **Qur'an-anchored normalised ethical catalogue**.

It must be completed before comparison with the Psalm-derived catalogue.

---

## 2. Three Analytical Layers

E3 now formally distinguishes:

### Layer A — Source Occurrence

A Qur'anic passage capable of supporting an ethical extraction.

A single principle may have many Layer-A sources.

### Layer B — Extracted Proposition

The functional behavioural lesson extracted from a source occurrence.

Different wording or context can produce several propositions that are semantically close.

### Layer C — Normalised Principle

A proposition that remains after semantic overlap has been resolved.

Layer C is the level used for the final cross-anchor comparison.

Therefore:

> **Source frequency is evidence of emphasis; it is not evidence of principle multiplicity.**

---

## 3. Compression Rules

Two propositions should normally be merged where they prescribe substantially the same behaviour under substantially the same ethical logic.

They should remain separate where merging would destroy an operationally important distinction.

Examples:

- accurate weights, honest measures, and symmetric commercial standards can normally be merged;
- forgiveness and proportionate redress should not be collapsed into a single rule if doing so would erase the victim's retained agency;
- generosity and protection against exploitative finance may both concern resources but perform different ethical functions;
- truthfulness and verification are related but distinct: one governs the speaker; the other governs the receiver or decision-maker;
- privacy and freedom from spying overlap but are not necessarily identical to protection of reputation.

Compression must therefore seek the **lowest stable number of principles that preserves meaningful behavioural distinctions**.

---

## 4. Preservation Before Compression

No raw evidence is deleted.

Each normalised principle must retain:

- its supporting E3 unit numbers;
- its principal Qur'anic source locations;
- important contextual variants;
- evidence of recurrence;
- any material qualification;
- and its cross-traditional comparison history.

A merged principle therefore becomes an index over evidence rather than a replacement for it.

This preserves auditability.

---

## 5. Compound Units

Some source units contain several separable ethics.

During normalisation, a compound source may support more than one final principle.

This does not create new source occurrences. It simply prevents an artificially broad source unit from forcing distinct behaviours into one principle.

Conversely, several units may collapse into one final principle.

The mapping is therefore many-to-many where necessary.

---

## 6. Exclusion Rule

A source or proposition is excluded where it cannot safely be generalised into ordinary behaviour.

The binding example is former **Unit 126 — Qur'an 18:71–82**, formally removed by Unit 420 because its exceptional hidden-knowledge structure could wrongly be converted into permission for otherwise harmful acts.

Excluded material remains visible in the audit trail.

---

## 7. Principle Record Format

Each normalised principle will contain:

1. **Normalised Principle**
2. **Operational Meaning**
3. **Primary Qur'anic Sources**
4. **Supporting E3 Units**
5. **Merged Proposition Families**
6. **Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes**
7. **Cross-Traditional Prevalence**
8. **Normalisation Decision**

The cross-traditional field is inherited from the source-level comparisons but is reassessed at principle level. Repetition of the same comparison does not increase the number of traditions supporting a principle.

---

## 8. Cross-Traditional Prevalence

The original Unified Paths work classified principles by breadth of support across eight traditions.

E3 will preserve that logic.

At normalised-principle level:

- **Universal:** functional equivalent in 8/8 traditions;
- **Near-universal:** 7/8;
- **Common:** 5–6/8;
- **Limited / tradition-weighted:** fewer than 5/8.

Grades A–D from the extraction stage remain evidence about strength of equivalence.

A tradition should not be counted merely because a vague analogy can be constructed. Where the source record only established a weak or uncertain C/D relation, the normalisation stage should preserve that uncertainty.

---

## 9. Source Frequency

Each principle should also preserve a separate indicator of Qur'anic recurrence.

This is not a moral ranking.

Its purpose is descriptive: to distinguish principles supported by a single selected source from principles recurring across many source contexts.

The final catalogue should therefore permit two independent questions:

> **How widely does this principle recur within the Qur'anic anchor?**

and

> **How widely does this principle recur across the eight traditions?**

These variables must never be conflated.

---

## 10. Independence From the Psalm Result

The Psalm-derived catalogue must not be used as the template for deciding how many Qur'anic principles should exist or how they should be named.

Normalisation is performed from the E3 corpus itself.

Only after the Qur'an-derived catalogue is frozen should the two anchor-derived catalogues be aligned.

This protects the central experimental value of E3.

If the two catalogues converge independently, that convergence is evidence.

If they differ, the difference is also evidence.

Neither outcome should be engineered.

---

## 11. Numbering

Normalised principles will receive a new identifier:

> **E3-N001, E3-N002, E3-N003...**

This prevents confusion with the 420 raw source-occurrence units.

The numbering does not imply rank or importance.

---

## 12. Stopping Rule for Normalisation

Normalisation is complete when:

1. every retained source proposition maps to at least one normalised principle;
2. every normalised principle has traceable source support;
3. no two principles remain functionally equivalent without a documented reason for keeping them separate;
4. compound principles have been split where merger would erase operational distinctions;
5. excluded cases are explicitly recorded;
6. the catalogue can be reconstructed from the audit trail.

Only then is the Qur'an-derived catalogue frozen for cross-anchor comparison.

# Transition Statement

Paper IV begins the conversion of the E3 evidence layer into its principle layer.

The objective is not maximum compression.

The objective is **loss-minimising compression**: remove duplication while preserving every distinction that materially changes ethical behaviour, governance, rights, duties, or institutional design.

> **Preserve the evidence. Compress the repetition. Retain the distinction.**
