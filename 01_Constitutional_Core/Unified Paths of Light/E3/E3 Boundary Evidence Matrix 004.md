# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Universal / Near-Universal Boundary Evidence Matrix 004

**Principles:** Q048, Q050, Q052, Q054, Q055.

---

## Q048 — Reciprocal Relational Rights and Responsibilities

Reciprocity is deeply represented across the comparison traditions, but the final E3 formulation joins reciprocity with an explicit rights-and-responsibilities structure. Biblical, Jewish, Confucian and African relational ethics strongly support reciprocal obligation; Buddhist, Hindu and Sikh traditions likewise contain mutual duties, compassion and restraint.

The potential overclaim lies in translating all reciprocal-duty traditions into modern rights language.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at the exact formulation; the parent principle of reciprocal relational obligation is a strong Universal candidate.**

## Q050 — Competence and Trustworthiness in Entrusted Roles

Trustworthiness in office or responsibility is broadly supported. Confucian role ethics is especially strong; biblical/Jewish traditions repeatedly demand just and trustworthy leadership; Sikh, Hindu and Buddhist traditions contain role/duty and ethical-leadership material. African political and communitarian traditions provide strong functional support where specific communities or documented philosophies are identified.

Competence is the narrower component: moral trustworthiness does not itself prove a merit/competence requirement.

**Decision:** **Near-universal as the compound principle; trustworthy office is broader than explicit competence selection.**

## Q052 — Rule-Governed Resolution of Disagreement

Most traditions possess mechanisms or ideals for resolving disputes, but their forms differ substantially: adjudication, mediation, communal reconciliation, moral cultivation, arbitration or authoritative interpretation.

A generic commitment to justice cannot by itself establish a rule-governed dispute procedure.

**Decision:** **Near-universal / Common boundary. Strong evidence exists for dispute resolution, but not yet for a sufficiently comparable rule-governed form in all traditions.**

## Q054 — Collective Responsibility to Resist Corruption

Opposition to wrongdoing is widespread. Collective responsibility is more demanding than individual refusal: it requires some duty to protect the social order against corruption.

Biblical prophetic ethics, Jewish communal/legal duties, Sikh resistance to injustice, Confucian remonstrance and African communitarian responsibility provide strong routes. Buddhist and Hindu evidence should be tied to explicit social-duty sources rather than inferred from individual virtue alone.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at functional level.**

## Q055 — Do Not Cooperate in Wrongdoing

This principle has unusually strong functional reach. Traditions frequently warn against corrupt company, complicity, assisting wrongdoing, following immoral counsel or participating in harmful conduct.

The Psalm anchor independently recovered the same association/complicity architecture.

**Decision:** **Strong Universal candidate, although the precise legal concept of complicity should not be projected onto every tradition.**

## Matrix 004 Finding

This tranche separates **role ethics** from **institutional procedure**.

Trustworthy conduct in a role is extremely widespread. Specific mechanisms for selecting competent officials or resolving disputes by formal rules are more anchor- and institution-dependent.
