# E3 Unit 271 — Qur'an 24:22
## Do Not Withdraw Social Support Solely From Personal Resentment

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 24:22 tells people of means not to swear off assistance to relatives, needy people, and emigrants after personal injury, and commends pardon and overlooking.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Personal resentment should not automatically terminate legitimate support.
- Resource power should not become an instrument of private revenge.
- Forgiveness can include restoration of practical assistance.
- Social welfare should be protected from impulsive retaliatory withdrawal.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Luke 6:27–35 | Beneficence may extend toward those who have caused injury. | A | Do not make aid contingent on affection alone. |
| Judaism — Proverbs 25:21 | Aid may be given even to an enemy. | A | Need can retain moral force despite hostility. |
| Buddhism — metta / dana | Generosity and goodwill are not restricted to liked persons. | A/B | Do not let resentment govern generosity. |
| Hinduism — dana / kshama | Giving and forgiveness provide strong functional convergence. | B | Personal injury need not cancel beneficence. |
| Sikhism — seva | Service should not be restricted by ego or grievance. | B | Continue rightful aid despite resentment. |
| Confucianism | Benevolence and role duties can survive interpersonal strain. | B | Do not abandon legitimate duties from anger. |
| African Traditional | Kinship support and reconciliation vary widely. | B/C | Feud should not automatically destroy support obligations. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a useful anti-retaliatory-resource principle.

# E3 Unit 272 — Qur'an 24:27–29
## Obtain Permission Before Entering Private Space

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 24:27–29 instructs people not to enter inhabited homes other than their own without permission and greeting, while distinguishing uninhabited places intended for public use.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Physical access requires consent where privacy is reasonably expected.
- Ownership or social familiarity does not erase another person's boundary.
- Private and public spaces can legitimately carry different access rules.
- Consent should precede intrusion rather than be presumed after entry.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Respect for household boundaries provides functional convergence, though no close canonical rule is equally explicit. | B/C | Do not intrude into private space without permission. |
| Judaism | Privacy protections in rabbinic law provide strong parallels. | A/B | Respect household privacy. |
| Buddhism — monastic discipline | Entry and interpersonal boundaries are regulated. | B | Access should follow recognised permission. |
| Hinduism | Household and guest norms provide partial parallels. | B/C | Private space carries access expectations. |
| Sikhism | No close canonical analogue identified. | C | Respect another household's boundaries. |
| Confucianism — propriety | Proper entry and relational boundaries are important. | B | Access should follow appropriate social procedure. |
| African Traditional | Household access norms vary substantially. | C | Private spaces commonly have recognised boundaries. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a strong privacy-and-consent source.

# E3 Unit 273 — Qur'an 24:30–31
## Regulate the Gaze and Preserve Intimate Privacy

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 24:30–31 directs men and women to restrain their gaze and preserve sexual modesty, with additional historically situated dress and disclosure provisions.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Sexual ethics includes attention and observation, not only physical acts.
- People can bear duties not to objectify or intrude visually.
- Intimate privacy deserves protection.
- The transferable core should be distinguished from culture-specific implementation rules.

### Comparative Analysis

|---|---|---:|---|

| Judaism — modesty traditions | Visual and bodily modesty are strongly regulated. | A/B | Preserve intimate boundaries. |
| Buddhism | Sexual restraint includes disciplining sensory desire. | B | Govern attention as part of sexual ethics. |
| Hinduism | Brahmacharya and sensory restraint provide strong parallels. | B | Discipline sexual attention. |
| Sikhism | Control of lust is a major ethical theme. | B | Do not let desire govern perception and conduct. |
| Confucianism | Sexual propriety exists but differs in framing. | C | Intimate relations require restraint. |
| African Traditional | Modesty norms vary widely. | C | Intimate privacy is culturally regulated. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain at functional level.** Avoid treating historically specific dress rules as universal equivalents.

# E3 Unit 274 — Qur'an 24:33
## Do Not Coerce Vulnerable People Into Sexual Exploitation

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 24:33 explicitly prohibits compelling slave women into prostitution for material gain.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Sexual consent cannot be overridden for another person's profit.
- Dependency and ownership-like power intensify duties against exploitation.
- Economic incentives do not justify sexual coercion.
- Institutions should protect persons whose material dependence makes refusal costly.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Sexual exploitation and treating persons as instruments conflict with core neighbour-love ethics. | B | Do not profit from coerced sexuality. |
| Judaism | Sexual coercion is constrained, though historical legal contexts differ. | B | Power does not erase sexual protections. |
| Buddhism — sexual misconduct precept | Sexual exploitation conflicts strongly with ethical restraint. | A/B | Do not exploit sexually vulnerable persons. |
| Hinduism | Non-harm and sexual ethics provide functional convergence. | B | Do not coerce sexuality for gain. |
| Sikhism | Human equality and control of lust strongly oppose exploitation. | B | Do not commodify vulnerable persons sexually. |
| Confucianism | Family/sexual propriety gives partial convergence but not a close equivalent. | C | Power should not license sexual abuse. |
| African Traditional | Historical practices vary too widely for a uniform claim. | C | No universal equivalent asserted. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is an explicit anti-coercion and anti-exploitation principle.

# E3 Unit 275 — Qur'an 24:58–59
## Privacy Boundaries Should Adapt to Context and Maturity

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 24:58–59 establishes permission rules for household members at specified private times and changes expectations as children mature.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Privacy is contextual rather than binary.
- Household familiarity does not eliminate all privacy.
- Access rules can vary by time and developmental stage.
- Maturing persons should learn stronger consent and boundary norms.
- Good systems can graduate permissions as agency develops.

### Comparative Analysis

|---|---|---:|---|

| Buddhism — monastic boundary rules | Access and privacy can vary by status/context. | B/C | Boundaries can be context-sensitive. |
| Hinduism | Household-stage and propriety rules offer partial parallels. | C | Maturity changes responsibilities. |
| Sikhism | No close canonical analogue identified. | C/D | Respect household privacy. |
| Confucianism | Age and role alter expectations of propriety. | B | Boundaries can develop with maturity. |
| African Traditional | Age-graded social rules are widespread but diverse. | B/C | Permissions can change with developmental stage. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This has particular relevance to graduated agency and developmental governance.

# E3 Unit 278 — Qur'an 25:68–72
## Integrated Integrity: Life, Sexual Conduct, Truth, and Non-Participation in Falsehood

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 25:68–72 groups prohibitions on wrongful killing and illicit sexual conduct with avoidance of false testimony and dignified disengagement from degrading activity.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Integrity spans bodily, relational, and informational conduct.
- Truthfulness is not separable from broader non-harm.
- Refusal to participate in falsehood is an ethical action.
- Character is cross-domain consistency rather than isolated rule compliance.

# E3 Unit 280 — Qur'an 28:26
## Select Agents for Both Capability and Trustworthiness

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 28:26 recommends hiring a person on the combined grounds of strength/capability and trustworthiness.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Competence without integrity is insufficient.
- Integrity without relevant capacity may also be insufficient.
- Selection systems should evaluate both ability and trust.
- Critical roles should not be assigned solely through familiarity, prestige, or preference.
