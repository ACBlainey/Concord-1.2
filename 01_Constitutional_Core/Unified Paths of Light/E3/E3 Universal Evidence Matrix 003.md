## Q064 — Control Anger Before It Controls Action

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Anger restraint is directly biblical. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Scriptural/rabbinic anger restraint is identifiable. | SQ-A/B |
| Buddhism | Anger/hatred control is canonical and central. | SQ-A |
| Hinduism | Anger is repeatedly treated as destructive; self-control is foundational. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | *Krodh* (anger) is one of the central vices; Sikh sources explicitly contrast it with self-control/patience. | SQ-A |
| Confucian | Self-mastery and control of reactive conduct are strongly represented; exact anger passage should be fixed. | SQ-B |
| African Traditional | Hunhu/Ubuntu scholarship explicitly identifies *kuzvibata* (self-control) versus reckless behaviour; exact anger-specific provenance is weaker. | SQ-B/C |

**Audit decision:** **Strong cross-traditional self-control principle; exact anger-specific 8/8 remains provisional.**

## Q077 — Good Association Supports Ethical Conduct

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Good/bad company as a moral influence has direct biblical support. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Wisdom literature strongly addresses companionship and moral influence. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | Spiritual friendship and avoidance of unwholesome association have canonical support. | SQ-A |
| Hinduism | *Satsanga* and association-with-the-good traditions provide strong functional support. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | *Sangat*—ethical/spiritual community—is a central Sikh institution and practice. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Friendship, exemplary persons and learning through association are central Analects themes. | SQ-A |
| African Traditional | Communal formation is strongly documented in Hunhu/Ubuntu; Shona teaching that a child is raised by the clan/village directly supports social formation, though not every association is thereby ethically good. | SQ-B |

**Audit decision:** **Strong 8-family functional support.** This is one of the better candidates for secure Universal status once citations are normalised.

## Q083 — Distinguish Accident From Deliberate Harm

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Intent and culpability distinctions exist in biblical and later Christian ethics, but exact source must be specified. | SQ-B |
| Judaism | Strong legal distinctions between intentional and unintentional wrongdoing are directly identifiable in Torah law. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | Intention (*cetana*) is fundamental to moral evaluation, providing strong functional support. | SQ-A |
| Hinduism | Intention, knowledge and agency affect moral responsibility, but exact source mapping is required. | SQ-B |
| Sikhism | General moral responsibility is clear; a specific accident-versus-intent rule has not yet been adequately demonstrated. | SQ-C |
| Confucian | Intention, character and circumstances matter to moral judgement, but no precise equivalent has yet been fixed. | SQ-C |
| African Traditional | Restorative African justice evaluates wrongs relationally and contextually, but current evidence does not establish a general accident-versus-deliberate distinction across the category. | SQ-C/D |

**Audit decision:** **Do not certify Universal.** Q083 should be treated as **unconfirmed Near-universal/Common pending direct evidence**, despite its preliminary Universal label.

## Q088 — Do Not Covet Another's Advantages Into Injustice

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Direct anti-covetousness command. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Direct Torah anti-covetousness command. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | Non-greed/non-covetousness is foundational. | SQ-A |
| Hinduism | Non-possessiveness/contentment and restraint of greed strongly support the principle. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | Greed (*lobh*) is one of the central vices; contentment is a recognised virtue. | SQ-A |
| Confucian | Restraint of profit-seeking and cultivation of righteousness over gain are strong functional parallels. | SQ-A/B |
| African Traditional | African ethics literature documents social obligations, human welfare and relational restraint; Ubuntu moral theory treats theft/deception for marginal gain as wrong, but a direct envy/covetousness source should be added. | SQ-B/C |

**Audit decision:** **Strong candidate; African exact provenance remains to be tightened.**

## Q089 — Institutional Trust Is a Shared Resource

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Truth, fidelity and trustworthy office are well supported, but “institutional trust as a shared resource” is a derived systems claim. | SQ-B/C |
| Judaism | Covenant, truthful judgement and trustworthy institutions support the components; systems-level formulation is derivative. | SQ-B/C |
| Buddhism | Truthful conduct and ethical community support trust components; explicit institutional-resource framing is weak. | SQ-C |
| Hinduism | Dharma and trustworthy role conduct support components; systems-level formulation is derivative. | SQ-C |
| Sikhism | Truthful living, honest livelihood and trustworthy community support components. | SQ-B/C |
| Confucian | Trustworthiness (*xin*) is directly central to social and political order; this tradition gives unusually strong support to the systems-level principle. | SQ-A/B |
| African Traditional | Ubuntu/African moral philosophy strongly treats relationship, trust and communal welfare as socially constitutive; the exact institutional-resource formulation remains modern/derivative. | SQ-B |

**Audit decision:** **Do not count Q089 as direct 8/8 textual universality.** It is better classified as a **cross-traditional systems synthesis built from widely recurrent trust components**.
