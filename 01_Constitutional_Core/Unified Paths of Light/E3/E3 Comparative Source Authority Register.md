# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Comparative Source Authority Register

**Status:** Working authority register for publication-grade source remediation.

---

## 1. Purpose

Functional comparison and documentary quality are separate questions. This register defines what kinds of evidence may support the final seven-tradition comparison.

A principle is not upgraded merely because a plausible parallel can be remembered. The comparison must point to an identifiable source or a defensible scholarly representation of a tradition.

## 2. Evidence Hierarchy

**Tier A — Primary / foundational text:** identifiable passage in a recognised canonical or foundational source.

**Tier B — Authoritative tradition source:** recognised commentary, legal/ethical corpus, named philosophical text, or scholarly edition representing the tradition.

**Tier C — Scholarly synthesis:** reliable academic discussion of a documented ethical tradition where no single canon supplies the appropriate unit.

**Tier D — Generic assertion:** unsourced claims such as “African tradition values X” or “Eastern religions teach Y.” Tier D cannot establish secure prevalence.

## 3. Tradition Register

### Christianity / Biblical Tradition
Preferred evidence: identifiable biblical passages. Use wider Christian ethical sources only where the comparison principle is not adequately represented in the biblical corpus.

### Judaism
Preferred evidence: Tanakh, Mishnah/Pirkei Avot, Talmud and identifiable Jewish legal/ethical texts. **Sefaria** is an appropriate searchable textual repository; passage identity, not merely repository name, must be recorded.

### Buddhism
Preferred evidence: identifiable early Buddhist discourses and recognised canonical collections, with **SuttaCentral** preferred for searchable canonical location where available. Later Buddhist traditions may be used when clearly labelled rather than projected onto “Buddhism” generically.

### Hinduism
Preferred evidence: identifiable foundational texts rather than generic “Hindu teaching.” **Bhagavad Gita / Gita Supersite (IIT Kanpur)** is an approved high-value source for principles it actually supports. For example, Gita 16.2 directly groups non-injury, truthfulness, absence of anger, compassion, non-covetousness and gentleness; 13.8 includes humility, non-injury, forbearance and self-control. These passages can support several ethical clusters without pretending to establish every operational child rule.

### Sikhism
Preferred evidence: identifiable passages from the **Guru Granth Sahib** and other clearly identified authoritative Sikh sources. Generic references to *seva*, truthful living or humility must be replaced with a passage or an authoritative scholarly/tradition source before receiving SQ-A/B.

### Chinese / Confucian
Preferred evidence: **Analects**, **Mencius**, and other identified classical Confucian texts. The **Chinese Text Project** is an approved textual repository. Later commentary may be used if clearly identified as later commentary rather than attributed directly to Confucius.

### African Traditional Religions / African Ethical Traditions
There is no single pan-African canon. Evidence must identify the relevant people, language, community, philosophical tradition, customary institution, proverb corpus, oral tradition or scholarly ethnographic/philosophical source.

Approved forms include:
- specifically identified Akan ethical concepts and maxims;
- specifically identified Ubuntu/Hunhu philosophical material;
- named Yoruba, Shona or other community traditions;
- reliable African philosophy/ethics scholarship that states its evidentiary scope.

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy article **African Ethics** is an approved orientation/synthesis source because it identifies the social, duty-based, common-good and humanistic features of documented African ethical thought and gives specific Akan examples. It must not be used to imply that every African society holds every proposition.

## 4. No-Fabrication Rule

If an exact source cannot be verified, the row remains SQ-C or SQ-D.

A weak citation is not repaired from memory.

## 5. Compound-Principle Rule

A source proving a parent ethic does not automatically prove an operational child.

Examples:
- truthfulness does not prove formal evidentiary procedure;
- generosity does not prove structured welfare;
- hospitality does not prove a general right of refusal;
- justice does not prove modern due process;
- non-injury does not prove a particular proportionality doctrine.

## 6. Publication Rule

The final prevalence matrix should record, at minimum:

**Q ID | Tradition | Functional Grade | Source-Quality Grade | Exact Source | Functional Note | Final Presence Decision**

Only functional A/B with SQ-A/B should normally count as **secure presence**.
