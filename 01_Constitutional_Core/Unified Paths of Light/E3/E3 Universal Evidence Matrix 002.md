# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Universal Evidence Matrix 002

**Principles:** Q015 Forgiveness and Reconciliation; Q016 Justice Without Partiality; Q032 Positive Duty Toward Severe Need; Q063 Patience Under Difficulty.

---

## Q015 — Forgiveness and Reconciliation

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Forgiveness and reconciliation are directly and repeatedly biblical. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Forgiveness, repentance and reconciliation have strong scriptural/rabbinic provenance. | SQ-A/B |
| Buddhism | Non-hatred, compassion and relinquishment of hostility provide strong functional equivalence; interpersonal forgiveness is not always framed identically. | SQ-A/B |
| Hinduism | Forgiveness/non-retaliation are identifiable virtues; exact textual location should be fixed. | SQ-B |
| Sikhism | Forgiveness and overcoming hostility are recognised Sikh virtues. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Harmony, reciprocity and restoration of proper relations support reconciliation; “forgiveness” requires careful translation into the Confucian ethical vocabulary. | SQ-B |
| African Traditional | Ubuntu restorative-justice scholarship explicitly describes restoration of victims, reintegration of offenders and restoration of social harmony as goals of justice-making. | SQ-A/B |

**Audit decision:** **Reconciliation/restorative settlement has strong seven-family evidence.** The compound “forgiveness and reconciliation” should allow traditions to instantiate either through functionally equivalent non-hostility/restoration rather than require identical theology of forgiveness.

## Q016 — Justice Without Partiality

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Biblical impartial-judgement material is direct. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Torah/Tanakh contains direct impartial-justice rules. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | Equanimity and non-favouritism support the functional ethic; legal impartiality should be sourced carefully. | SQ-B |
| Hinduism | Dharma and just-rule traditions provide support; exact impartiality passage required. | SQ-B |
| Sikhism | Justice and equality are recognised Sikh ethical virtues; modern scholarly synthesis treats justice as a general virtue grounded in truthful living. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Just/virtuous government is strongly textual, though role ethics complicates modern “impartiality” language. | SQ-B |
| African Traditional | Ubuntu justice scholarship explicitly centres justice, human dignity, participation and restoration; this is identifiable Southern African philosophical provenance rather than a pan-African canon. | SQ-A/B |

**Audit decision:** **Strong justice convergence; exact “without partiality” wording needs tradition-specific functional care.**

## Q032 — Positive Duty Toward Severe Need

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Direct care for poor, hungry, stranger and vulnerable persons. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Direct charity, gleaning, poor-relief and stranger-protection duties. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | Generosity and compassion toward suffering persons are canonical. | SQ-A/B |
| Hinduism | *Dana* and duties of aid/hospitality provide strong support. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | *Seva*, sharing and welfare of all provide direct ethical support. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Benevolence and humane government support positive care duties, though mechanisms differ. | SQ-B |
| African Traditional | African ethics scholarship describes generosity, compassion, hospitality and obligation to kin/neighbours as social goods; Ubuntu restorative literature documents mutual support/caring obligations. | SQ-A/B |

**Audit decision:** **Very strong functional universality candidate.** Differences concern recipient scope and institutional mechanism, not the existence of positive aid duties.

## Q063 — Patience Under Difficulty

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Patience/endurance in adversity is directly biblical. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Patience/endurance themes have identifiable scriptural provenance. | SQ-A/B |
| Buddhism | Patience/forbearance (*khanti/kshanti*) is a canonical virtue. | SQ-A |
| Hinduism | Forbearance/endurance is an identifiable virtue across foundational ethical traditions. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | Patience is explicitly identified as a Sikh virtue in both educational and scholarly summaries grounded in Guru Granth Sahib ethics. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Perseverance, self-mastery and endurance are strongly represented, though final publication should cite the exact passage chosen. | SQ-B |
| African Traditional | Ubuntu/Hunhu sources strongly support self-control and endurance within relational obligations, but “patience under difficulty” needs a more direct named proverb/community source for an A/B-grade exact match. | SQ-B/C |

**Audit decision:** **Strong candidate but African exact-match provenance remains the weakest link.**

---

## Matrix 002 Finding

The strongest result in this tranche is Q032: positive aid to persons in serious need survives the provenance test especially well.

Q015 and Q016 also appear robust, but their English labels combine concepts that traditions may realise through different ethical vocabularies. Functional equivalence should therefore be retained without pretending semantic identity.
