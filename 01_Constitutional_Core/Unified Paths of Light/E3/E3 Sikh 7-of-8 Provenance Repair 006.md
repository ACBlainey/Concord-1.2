## Q051 — Authority Is a Trust, Not Ownership

Sikh institutional history and political theology strongly constrain authority through service, collective responsibility and the subordination of ego. These are important functional analogues.

But the frozen principle makes a specific constitutional distinction: authority is held **in trust** rather than possessed as ownership.

Unless an authoritative Sikh source directly establishes that trusteeship relationship, service-oriented leadership should not be rewritten as the same proposition.

**Decision:** retain Sikh **P**.

## Q075 — Preserve Social Bonds Against Needless Severance

Sikh community ethics strongly values fellowship, sangat, solidarity and preservation of communal relationship.

The frozen proposition is narrower: it prohibits **needless severance** of social bonds. Valuing community does not necessarily establish a rule against severance, especially where separation may sometimes be ethically warranted.

**Decision:** retain Sikh **P**.
