# E3 Unit 081 — Qur'an 5:101–102
## Do Not Manufacture Burdens Through Needless Inquiry

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:101–102 warns against questions that, once answered, would create unnecessary difficulty, recalling earlier communities whose excessive questioning became a source of trouble.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Do not create needless burdens merely because additional rules can be demanded.**
- **More information or regulation is not automatically beneficial.**
- **Inquiry should be proportionate to its practical value and foreseeable consequences.**
- **Avoid converting uncertainty into unnecessary constraint where no genuine problem requires resolution.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Mark 2:27; Matthew 23:4 | Rules are criticised when they become burdens detached from human good. | A/B | Do not turn moral systems into needless burdens. |
| Judaism — rabbinic attention to avoiding unnecessary prohibitions, alongside detailed law | Direct equivalence is mixed because inquiry and legal specification are also strongly valued. | C | Regulation should remain connected to legitimate purpose. |
| Buddhism — Middle Way | Avoid unhelpful extremes and speculative questions irrelevant to liberation. | B | Do not multiply burdens without practical ethical benefit. |
| Hinduism — pragmatic distinctions among duties | No close universal equivalent identified. | C | Duty should remain proportionate to context. |
| Sikhism — rejection of empty ritualism | Formal burdens without ethical substance are criticised. | B | Do not confuse unnecessary formalism with moral value. |
| Confucianism — practical learning and proper measure | Inquiry and ritual should serve cultivation rather than sterile complexity. | B/C | Means should remain connected to their human purpose. |
| African Traditional | No single cross-traditional equivalent can be responsibly asserted. | D/C | Rules are generally embedded in practical community contexts. |

### Conclusion
This source contributes a restraint principle aimed not at ignorance but at **unnecessary complexity that creates avoidable burdens**.

# E3 Unit 082 — Qur'an 5:106–108
## Witnesses, Evidence, and Deterrence of False Testimony

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:106–108 establishes procedures for testimony concerning bequests, including witnesses, solemn attestation, and a corrective mechanism where dishonesty is discovered.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Important transfers should be supported by evidence.**
- **Witnesses carry duties of truthfulness.**
- **Procedures should anticipate the possibility of false testimony.**
- **Systems should contain mechanisms for correcting compromised evidence.**
- **Accountability can deter evidentiary fraud.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity / biblical tradition — Matthew 18:16; 2 Corinthians 13:1 | Multiple witnesses are used to establish serious matters. | A/B | Consequential claims should have evidentiary support. |
| Judaism — Deuteronomy 19:15–21 | Multiple witnesses and sanctions for false testimony are explicit. | A | Evidence systems must protect against false witness. |
| Buddhism — truthful speech | Deliberate false testimony violates truthfulness. | B | Do not deceive through formal testimony. |
| Hinduism — dharmashastra evidentiary traditions | Witnesses and truthful testimony form part of adjudication. | A/B | Legal evidence requires truthful witnesses. |
| Sikhism — truthful living | False testimony conflicts with truthfulness and justice. | B | Do not corrupt proceedings through falsehood. |
| Confucianism — trustworthiness and adjudicative responsibility | Formal evidence traditions vary, but honesty in public roles is expected. | B | Public testimony should be reliable. |
| African Traditional | Witnesses and oath procedures occur widely in customary adjudication, though forms vary. | B | Serious claims require recognised evidentiary processes. |

### Conclusion
The passage strengthens the institutional strand: trustworthy outcomes require not only virtuous witnesses but procedures designed for the possibility that witnesses may fail.

# E3 Unit 083 — Qur'an 6:108
## Do Not Provoke Reciprocal Desecration Through Abusive Speech

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:108 prohibits insulting what others worship because doing so may provoke them, through hostility and ignorance, to insult what the believers hold sacred.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Do not needlessly insult another community's sacred commitments.**
- **Speech should be evaluated partly by foreseeable reciprocal consequences.**
- **Restraint can prevent escalation without requiring agreement.**
- **Respectful disagreement is different from deliberate provocation.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — 1 Peter 3:15–16 | Defence of belief is to be conducted with gentleness and respect. | A/B | Disagree without needless contempt. |
| Judaism — speech ethics | Harmful and provocative speech is constrained, though no exact universal analogue is asserted. | B | Speech remains ethically accountable during disagreement. |
| Buddhism — Right Speech | Harsh, divisive, and harmful speech should be avoided. | A | Do not use speech to inflame hostility. |
| Hinduism — Bhagavad Gita 17:15 | Disciplined speech should be truthful, non-agitating, and beneficial. | A | Truth does not require gratuitous provocation. |
| Sikhism — truthful speech without slander | Religious conviction does not justify malicious speech. | B | Speak truth without contempt. |
| Confucianism — li and harmony | Proper speech avoids needless social degradation. | B | Disagreement should remain civil. |
| African Traditional | Sacred objects and traditions vary greatly; reciprocal respect is context-specific. | C | Avoid insults likely to trigger communal escalation. |

### Conclusion
The verse supplies a clear de-escalation rule: refrain from an otherwise available form of expression when its foreseeable function is reciprocal hostility rather than constructive disagreement.

# E3 Unit 084 — Qur'an 6:141
## Use Resources Without Waste

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:141 discusses agricultural produce, giving what is due at harvest, and explicitly prohibits wasteful excess.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Use productive resources without waste.**
- **Material abundance does not justify careless consumption.**
- **Production carries social obligations as well as private benefit.**
- **Stewardship applies at the point where resources are generated and consumed.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Buddhism — moderation and non-attachment | Consumption should avoid excess and heedlessness. | B | Use material goods with restraint. |
| Hinduism — moderation and reverence for natural order | Wasteful excess conflicts with disciplined use. | B | Consume responsibly. |
| Sikhism — honest production, sharing, simplicity | Resources should support livelihood and others rather than egoic excess. | B | Use and share resources responsibly. |
| Confucianism — frugality | Frugality is a recognised virtue. | A/B | Avoid wasteful extravagance. |
| African Traditional | Stewardship norms regarding land, livestock, and harvest are widespread but highly local. | B/C | Resource use carries obligations to community and continuity. |

### Conclusion
Anti-waste ethics show broad functional convergence and connect personal consumption to wider stewardship.

# E3 Unit 085 — Qur'an 6:151
## Core Prohibitions: Life, Family Duty, Sexual Harm, and Infanticide

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:151 gathers several major commands: good treatment of parents, prohibition of killing children because of poverty, avoidance of shameful acts, and prohibition of killing a person except under recognised right.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Treat parents with care.**
- **Do not kill children because economic circumstances make them burdensome.**
- **Do not normalise destructive sexual or indecent conduct.**
- **Do not wrongfully take human life.**

# E3 Unit 086 — Qur'an 6:152
## Orphan Property, Fair Measure, Proportionate Obligation, and Just Speech

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:152 commands protection of orphan property until maturity, full measure and weight with justice, recognises that obligation is bounded by capacity, and requires justice in speech even concerning close relatives.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Protect vulnerable property until its owner is capable of managing it.**
- **Measure and trade honestly.**
- **Do not impose obligations beyond reasonable capacity.**
- **Speak and testify justly even where kinship creates pressure.**
- **Standards should be both fair and realistically performable.**

### Comparative Analysis — Honest Measure

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity / biblical tradition — Leviticus 19:35–36 | Honest weights and measures are explicitly required. | A | Do not manipulate measurement for gain. |
| Judaism — Leviticus 19:35–36; Deuteronomy 25:13–16 | False weights are prohibited. | A | Commercial measurement must be honest. |
| Buddhism — Right Livelihood | Deceptive trade conflicts with right livelihood. | B | Do not profit through commercial deception. |
| Hinduism — commercial dharma | Fraudulent measures are condemned in legal traditions. | A/B | Exchange requires honest measurement. |
| Sikhism — honest earning | Wealth should be earned without deception. | B | Trade truthfully. |
| Confucianism — xin | Trustworthiness is necessary for social exchange. | B | Economic relations require reliable standards. |
| African Traditional | Market fairness and recognised measures vary locally but deceptive exchange is broadly socially disruptive. | B/C | Do not manipulate exchange against another party. |

### Discussion
This unit combines fiduciary, commercial, evidentiary, and proportionality principles. Particularly notable is the explicit pairing of obligation with capacity: ethical systems should demand what can reasonably be borne rather than treating impossible performance as the standard.

### Conclusion
The verse reinforces multiple established clusters while adding **capacity-bounded obligation** as a clear principle.

# E3 Unit 087 — Qur'an 7:31
## Moderation in Consumption

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 7:31 permits eating and drinking while prohibiting excess.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Material enjoyment is not inherently wrong.**
- **Consumption should remain within reasonable bounds.**
- **Avoid excess even in legitimate goods.**
- **The ethical problem can lie in quantity and loss of restraint rather than the object itself.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Philippians 4:5; temperance traditions | Self-control and moderation are praised. | B | Legitimate appetite should remain governed. |
| Judaism — moderation traditions | Enjoyment is permitted but excess is constrained. | B | Use permitted goods without destructive excess. |
| Buddhism — Middle Way | Both indulgence and severe self-mortification are rejected. | A/B | Avoid extremes of consumption. |
| Hinduism — Bhagavad Gita 6:16–17 | Excess and deprivation in eating/sleeping are both rejected for disciplined life. | A | Moderation supports stable agency. |
| Sikhism — restraint from greed | Appetite should not dominate the person. | B | Consume without becoming governed by desire. |
| Confucianism — doctrine of the mean / ritual moderation | Proper measure is central to cultivated conduct. | A/B | Avoid excess and deficiency. |
| African Traditional | Feasting and restraint are context-dependent; no single rule is universal. | C | Consumption remains embedded in social obligations and limits. |

### Conclusion
Moderation is highly convergent and differs from blanket renunciation: legitimate enjoyment is retained while excess is constrained.

# E3 Unit 090 — Qur'an 7:199
## Forgiveness, Positive Conduct, and Disengagement from Provocation

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 7:199 instructs taking the path of pardon, enjoining recognised good, and turning away from the ignorant.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Make allowance for ordinary human fault where appropriate.**
- **Actively encourage good conduct rather than focusing only on prohibition.**
- **Not every provocation requires engagement.**
- **Disengagement can be preferable to escalation with people acting from ignorance.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 5:39–44; Proverbs tradition | Forgiveness and non-retaliatory responses are encouraged. | A/B | Do not answer every offence through escalation. |
| Judaism — Proverbs 19:11; Leviticus 19:18 | Overlooking offence and rejecting vengeance are praised. | A/B | Restrain retaliation. |
| Buddhism — Dhammapada 3–5 | Hatred is not ended through hatred. | A | Break hostile feedback loops. |
| Hinduism — forgiveness and self-control | Forbearance is a recognised virtue. | B | Do not let provocation govern action. |
| Sikhism — overcoming anger and enmity | Anger should not dominate conduct. | B | Respond without hatred. |
| Confucianism — measured response and self-mastery | The cultivated person avoids being captured by petty provocation. | B | Preserve dignity through restraint. |
| African Traditional | Reconciliation and strategic disengagement vary by tradition. | B/C | Avoid escalating minor conflict into enduring feud. |

### Discussion
This unit combines three strategies: pardon past fault, promote positive conduct, and refuse unnecessary engagement with provocation. It therefore treats non-escalation as an active form of ethical judgement rather than mere passivity.

### Conclusion
Forgiveness and restraint again show broad convergence, while the explicit option to disengage adds a practical conflict-management heuristic.

This group broadens the corpus in several directions.

Unit 082 continues the institutional trend by designing evidentiary procedure around the possibility of dishonesty rather than assuming ideal participants.

Units 084, 086, 087, and 089 independently reinforce **material proportionality and stewardship**: avoid waste, measure honestly, keep demands within capacity, and restrain excess.

Unit 083 is also significant for inter-community ethics. It does not require agreement with another religion's beliefs; instead it constrains deliberate insult because of predictable reciprocal escalation.
