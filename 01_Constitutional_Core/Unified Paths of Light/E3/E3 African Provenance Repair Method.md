# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## African Provenance Repair Method

African Traditional Religions and African ethical traditions do not form a single textual canon. The E3 audit must therefore avoid manufacturing a pseudo-scripture by combining unrelated traditions without provenance.

## Acceptable evidence forms

### A — Named primary/traditional evidence
- proverb or maxim attributed to a named people/tradition;
- named customary institution;
- documented oral teaching;
- traditional legal or reconciliation practice with identifiable provenance.

### B — High-quality scholarly reconstruction
- scholarship explicitly describing the ethics of a named tradition;
- comparative African philosophy that identifies its evidential scope;
- authoritative reference work that distinguishes local evidence from broader inference.

### C — Broad synthesis
Useful for discovery and corroboration but not sufficient by itself for an 8/8 secure promotion when the proposition is narrow.

## Promotion rule

For an African P→S upgrade:
1. identify the people/tradition or evidential scope;
2. identify the source or scholarly authority;
3. state the behavioural proposition actually supported;
4. compare it to the frozen E3-Q formulation;
5. reject promotion if only the parent ethic is demonstrated.

## Scope discipline

Evidence from Akan ethics may securely establish that an Akan ethical tradition contains a principle. It must not be rewritten as “all African traditions teach this.”

For E3's eight-family prevalence model, a secure African-family cell therefore means:

> a sufficiently well-attested ethical analogue within a clearly identified African traditional ethical source, used as evidence of family-level recurrence with its scope explicitly disclosed.

It does not mean universal distribution across the African continent.
