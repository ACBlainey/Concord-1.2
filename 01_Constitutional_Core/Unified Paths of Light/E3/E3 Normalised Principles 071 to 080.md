## Method Note

This tranche normalises capacity, moderation, burden, social resilience, moral independence, and rule-design principles. Several are particularly relevant to the Concord's later systems work, but they remain derived here from the Qur'anic E3 corpus rather than imported from Concord doctrine.

---

# E3-N071 — Obligation Should Track Capacity

## Normalised Principle

**Do not impose duties or material burdens beyond a person's genuine capacity where the purpose of the duty can be preserved by proportionate adjustment.**

### Operational Meaning

A rule that ignores capability can transform responsibility into impossibility. Ethical systems should calibrate burden while preserving legitimate obligations.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:233; 6:152; 64:16; 65:7.

### Supporting E3 Units

**021, 086, 179, 181, 308, 310, 390**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- capacity-aware duty;
- maintenance according to means;
- proportionate obligation;
- no impossible burden.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Capacity adjustment should not become a pretext for avoiding duties one is actually able to fulfil.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong functional equivalents occur across many traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N072 — Do Not Manufacture Unnecessary Burdens

## Normalised Principle

**Do not create prohibitions, inquiries, or procedural burdens merely because greater restriction appears more morally serious.**

### Operational Meaning

Over-constraint can damage agency and institutional legitimacy. Rules require purpose, evidence, and proportionality.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 5:87–88; 5:101–102; 16:116.

### Supporting E3 Units

**081, 217, 218, 246, 413**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- no needless inquiry;
- no invented prohibition;
- no arbitrary severity;
- burden minimisation;
- constraint requires justification.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Necessary safety rules remain legitimate. The principle rejects burden without adequate ethical function.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong functional parallels occur in several traditions, while traditions also differ significantly in rule density.

**Preliminary classification: Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N073 — Avoid Self-Destructive Extremes

## Normalised Principle

**Do not pursue otherwise legitimate aims in ways that predictably destroy the agent, community, or resources required to sustain those aims.**

### Operational Meaning

Ethics must remain survivable. Sacrifice can be meaningful, but systems that routinely consume their own capacity cannot preserve their values.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:195; 17:29; related moderation passages.

### Supporting E3 Units

**011, 115, 250**, with resource-balance support **141, 277**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- avoid self-destruction;
- do not give or spend into ruin;
- sustainable obligation;
- preserve future capacity.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This is not a ban on risk or sacrifice. It constrains needless or structurally unsustainable destruction.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong moderation and self-preservation analogues occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N074 — Moral Quality Is Not Determined by Quantity or Popularity

## Normalised Principle

**Do not infer ethical value merely from abundance, popularity, wealth, numerical dominance, or social prestige.**

### Operational Meaning

Quantity can provide evidence about prevalence but not moral correctness. Ethical evaluation requires independent criteria.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 5:100; 18:28; related status passages.

### Supporting E3 Units

**080, 125, 259**, with humility/status support **122, 168**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- quantity is not quality;
- popularity is not correctness;
- wealth is not virtue;
- status is not moral proof.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Consensus and frequency can still be evidence in appropriate domains. They are not self-validating moral standards.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong functional parallels occur in most traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N075 — Preserve Independent Moral Agency Under Group Pressure

## Normalised Principle

**Do not surrender moral judgement merely because family, leaders, peers, or a dominant group demand conformity.**

### Operational Meaning

Social coordination is valuable, but group membership must not erase individual responsibility.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 31:15; 4:135; related 5:8.

### Supporting E3 Units

**066, 071, 148, 215, 284**, with family-agency support **282**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- conscience against family pressure;
- justice against self/kin interest;
- independent responsibility;
- resistance to factional conformity.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Independent agency does not mean immunity from evidence, law, or legitimate shared obligations.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong parallels occur widely, though traditions differ in balancing individual and communal authority.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal / Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N076 — Distinguish Peaceful Difference From Active Threat

## Normalised Principle

**Evaluate outsiders and dissenting groups by conduct rather than treating difference itself as evidence of hostility.**

### Operational Meaning

Identity-based threat assumptions create unnecessary conflict. Ethical security requires discrimination between peaceful difference and active aggression.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 60:8–9; 3:113–115.

### Supporting E3 Units

**036, 175, 176, 305, 386, 387**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- peaceful out-groups;
- do not erase moral differences within another group;
- conduct-based threat assessment;
- no collective guilt by identity.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The principle does not deny real threats. It requires evidence of harmful conduct rather than identity alone.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong functional parallels exist, though historical practice varies substantially.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal / Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N077 — Safe Passage and Protection for a Requesting Opponent

## Normalised Principle

**Where an opponent seeks temporary protection, hearing, or safe passage in good faith, preserve a protected route rather than exploiting vulnerability created by the request itself.**

### Operational Meaning

Conflict rules should permit exits, communication, and protected contact. Without such mechanisms, parties have fewer pathways away from violence.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 9:6.

### Supporting E3 Units

**096, 232**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- asylum;
- safe conduct;
- protected hearing;
- non-exploitation of surrender/contact;
- conflict exit route.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Protection can remain security-sensitive. The principle does not require ignoring credible evidence of immediate abuse of safe passage.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Safe-conduct and hospitality analogues occur in several traditions but are not uniformly expressed.

**Preliminary classification: Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.** It performs a distinctive de-escalatory function.

# E3-N078 — Honour Reliable Counterparties

## Normalised Principle

**Do not betray or arbitrarily disadvantage parties who continue to honour legitimate agreements merely because they belong to an opposing or different group.**

### Operational Meaning

Trustworthy conduct should matter more than group categorisation. Reliability deserves reciprocal reliability.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 9:4.

### Supporting E3 Units

**095, 231**, with agreement-integrity support **093, 109, 245**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- honour agreements with reliable counterparties;
- conduct over identity;
- reciprocal treaty fidelity;
- no opportunistic betrayal.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This is a specific application of N006, retained provisionally because it links covenant integrity to out-group ethics.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong treaty and covenant parallels occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain provisionally; candidate for nesting under N006 during final consolidation.**

# E3-N079 — Future-Consequence Awareness

## Normalised Principle

**Evaluate present conduct partly by its reasonably foreseeable future consequences rather than by immediate gain alone.**

### Operational Meaning

Ethical judgement should include temporal externalities. Short-term benefit can create long-term harm to trust, resources, relationships, or institutional stability.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 59:18; related 16:94.

### Supporting E3 Units

**174, 419**, with sustainability links **084, 115, 141, 170**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- consider what is sent ahead;
- long-horizon responsibility;
- future consequences;
- systemic trust effects;
- sustainability.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Foreseeability is bounded. Agents are not responsible for every remote or unknowable consequence.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Long-horizon prudence and consequence awareness occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N080 — Resilience Under Gain and Loss

## Normalised Principle

**Do not allow either loss or success to destabilise judgement into despair, arrogance, or reckless conduct.**

### Operational Meaning

Stable agency requires emotional and institutional resilience across changing conditions. Both deprivation and abundance can distort behaviour.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 57:23; related patience and anti-arrogance passages.

### Supporting E3 Units

**300**, with support **122, 168, 205, 256**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- resilience under loss;
- humility under gain;
- emotional equilibrium;
- avoid destabilisation by fortune.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The principle does not prohibit grief, joy, ambition, or celebration. It constrains loss of ethical self-command.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equanimity/moderation parallels occur throughout the comparison traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**
