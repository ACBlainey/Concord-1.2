# E3 Unit 231 — Qur'an 9:4
## Do Not Punish Reliable Partners for Others' Breaches

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 9:4 exempts treaty partners who have honoured their commitments and not assisted hostile action, requiring fulfilment of their agreements to term.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Responsibility should be individualised or party-specific where evidence allows.
- Do not generalise one party's betrayal to all members of a broader category.
- Reliable partners should receive the benefit of their reliability.
- Collective conflict does not erase distinctions in conduct.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Romans 12:17–18 | Treatment should respond to actual conduct rather than indiscriminate hostility. | B | Do not generalise guilt. |
| Judaism — Deuteronomy 24:16 | Responsibility should not automatically transfer between persons. | A/B | Attribute responsibility specifically. |
| Buddhism | Karma is individualised around intentional action. | B | Judge conduct rather than category alone. |
| Hinduism | Individual duty/action matters despite group identity. | B | Distinguish actors by conduct. |
| Sikhism | Justice should not become indiscriminate vengeance. | B | Protect those who have kept faith. |
| Confucianism | Accurate role and conduct assessment matters to justice. | B | Distinguish reliable from unreliable actors. |
| African Traditional | Collective responsibility varies substantially. | C | Where possible, responsibility should track actual conduct. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: duplicate of Unit 095; merge.**

# E3 Unit 232 — Qur'an 9:6
## Grant Protection and Safe Passage to a Requesting Opponent

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 9:6 requires protection for an opponent who seeks it and safe delivery to a place of security.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Enemy status does not erase every individual protection.
- A request for refuge can change the immediate moral relationship.
- Safe conduct must be honoured once granted.
- Conflict categories should contain humanitarian exceptions.

# E3 Unit 233 — Qur'an 9:34–35
## Do Not Hoard Wealth While Withholding Social Contribution

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 9:34–35 condemns accumulation of gold and silver when wealth is withheld from its proper beneficial use.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Accumulation is ethically relevant when resources are removed from legitimate social circulation.
- Wealth can create obligations beyond private possession.
- Hoarding differs from prudent saving when it becomes systematic refusal of responsibility.
- Economic power should not become insulated from moral claims.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — James 5:1–5 | Hoarded wealth amid injustice is sharply criticised. | A | Wealth should not be accumulated through social neglect. |
| Judaism — tzedakah / sabbatical ethics | Wealth carries distributive obligations. | A/B | Possession does not cancel duties to others. |
| Buddhism — non-attachment / dana | Hoarding reinforces attachment; generosity is cultivated. | A/B | Do not make accumulation an end in itself. |
| Hinduism — dana | Wealth is subject to duties of giving. | B | Circulate resources toward legitimate need. |
| Sikhism — vand chhakna | Earnings are to be shared. | A/B | Do not isolate wealth from community. |
| Confucianism — righteousness over profit | Wealth pursuit is subordinate to rightness. | B | Profit does not override social duty. |
| African Traditional | Redistribution and reciprocal claims vary but are often significant. | B | Accumulation exists within communal obligations. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge with stewardship cluster.** It adds explicit anti-hoarding language.

# E3 Unit 234 — Qur'an 9:60
## Social Support Can Be Structured by Defined Beneficiary Classes

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 9:60 specifies categories for alms distribution, including poor and needy persons and other defined purposes.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Aid can be institutional rather than purely spontaneous.
- Distribution systems can define eligible categories in advance.
- Administration itself can be recognised as part of delivery.
- Social support should not depend solely on donor preference or personal visibility.

# E3 Unit 237 — Qur'an 10:99
## Coercion Cannot Manufacture Conviction

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 10:99 rhetorically rejects compelling people until they become believers.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Inward conviction is not equivalent to forced outward compliance.
- Coercive power has functional limits in matters of belief.
- A system can command speech or behaviour without thereby producing genuine assent.
- Policy should distinguish controllable compliance from internal conviction.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Voluntary-faith themes provide functional support, despite complex coercive histories. | B/C | Genuine belief differs from compelled profession. |
| Judaism | Direct equivalence is limited and context-specific. | C | Inward conviction is not reducible to external enforcement. |
| Buddhism | Voluntary undertaking of practice gives partial convergence. | B | Forced assent is not genuine understanding. |
| Hinduism | Pluralist strands support limits on compelled uniformity. | B | Belief cannot simply be imposed. |
| Sikhism | Defence of religious conscience provides strong functional convergence. | A/B | Protect conscience from coercion. |
| Confucianism | No close doctrinal-belief analogue. | C | Moral cultivation cannot be reduced to forced words. |
| African Traditional | No uniform analogue. | D | No clear general equivalence asserted. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: duplicate of Unit 100 and related to Unit 024; merge while retaining source frequency.**

# E3 Unit 238 — Qur'an 11:85
## Commercial Fraud Is a Form of Wider Corruption

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 11:85 commands full measure and weight with justice, prohibits depriving people of what is theirs, and links commercial wrongdoing to corruption.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Fraud is not merely a private transaction problem.
- Systematic cheating can degrade the wider social order.
- Do not exploit control over measurement.
- Commercial trust is public infrastructure.

# E3 Unit 239 — Qur'an 11:116
## Communities Need People Who Resist Corruption

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 11:116 laments the scarcity of people in earlier communities who possessed enough enduring virtue to restrain corruption.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Stable communities require actors willing to resist degrading norms.
- Private virtue alone may be insufficient when corruption becomes systemic.
- Ethical minorities can have disproportionate protective value.
- Institutions should preserve channels for principled dissent and correction.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 5:13–16 | Communities are called to exert preserving moral influence. | B | Ethical actors can protect wider social conditions. |
| Judaism — prophetic tradition | Prophetic critique resists systemic injustice. | A/B | Societies need voices that oppose corruption. |
| Buddhism — sangha / righteous counsel | Ethical communities can preserve standards against wider disorder. | B | Maintain institutions of moral correction. |
| Hinduism — dharma protection | Right order requires actors who preserve and restore dharma. | B | Resist systemic degradation. |
| Sikhism — sant-sipahi ideal | Ethical commitment includes resistance to injustice. | A/B | Virtue may require active opposition to wrong. |
| Confucianism — remonstrance | Officials may have duties to correct rulers and disorder. | A | Institutions need principled correction. |
| African Traditional | Councils/elders may restrain abuse in particular systems. | B/C | Community requires mechanisms against corruption. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** It introduces the civilisational function of principled dissent.

# E3 Unit 240 — Qur'an 12:55
## Public Responsibility Should Be Matched to Competence and Trustworthiness

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 12:55 depicts Joseph offering himself for responsibility over stores on the basis that he is a knowledgeable guardian.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Public office should be connected to relevant competence.
- Stewardship roles require both technical knowledge and trustworthiness.
- Self-nomination need not be vanity where it transparently communicates relevant capability.
- Critical-resource management should not be assigned solely by status or favour.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Acts 6:3 | Administrators are selected for character and capability. | A/B | Match responsibility to competence and trust. |
| Judaism — Exodus 18:21 | Officials should combine capability with trustworthy character. | A | Select capable, trustworthy administrators. |
| Buddhism | Monastic offices use competence and disciplinary qualification. | B | Roles should be assigned to qualified persons. |
| Hinduism — statecraft traditions | Officials are expected to possess relevant qualities and skills. | B | Governance requires competence and integrity. |
| Sikhism | Service leadership values character and capability. | B | Entrust responsibility to reliable persons. |
| Confucianism — merit and virtue in office | Governance strongly emphasises cultivated competence and moral character. | A | Office should follow merit and virtue. |
| African Traditional | Selection criteria vary widely, often combining lineage with demonstrated capability. | C | Critical roles should consider competence and trust. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a strong merit-plus-integrity governance principle.
