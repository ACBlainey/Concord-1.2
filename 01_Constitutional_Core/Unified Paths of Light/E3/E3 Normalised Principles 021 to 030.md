## Method Note

Normalisation continues from the raw E3 source-occurrence corpus. This tranche focuses primarily on property, exchange, resource stewardship, and material vulnerability. Closely related propositions remain separate only where they govern materially different behaviours or institutional functions.

# E3-N021 — Property by Legitimate Consent

## Normalised Principle

**Do not take, transfer, or consume another person's property without legitimate consent or a valid rule-governed basis.**

### Operational Meaning

Material exchange should rest on voluntary agreement, recognised entitlement, or legitimate public procedure rather than coercion, deception, theft, or positional power.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:188; 4:29.

### Supporting E3 Units

**009, 050, 209**, with related trust/property units **043, 046, 055**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- no unjust consumption of wealth;
- exchange by mutual consent;
- property rights;
- no coercive appropriation;
- legitimate transfer.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Consent does not legitimate every transaction where fraud, severe coercion, incapacity, or exploitation destroys meaningful agency.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong functional equivalents occur throughout the comparison set.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N022 — Commercial Honesty and Accurate Measure

## Normalised Principle

**Use accurate weights, measures, prices, and reciprocal standards; do not obtain advantage by manipulating the metrics of exchange.**

### Operational Meaning

Fair exchange requires shared standards that operate symmetrically for buyer and seller. Measurement infrastructure should be auditable and resistant to private manipulation.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 6:152; 7:85; 11:85; 17:35; 26:181–183; 55:7–9; 83:1–3.

### Supporting E3 Units

**086, 089, 101, 120, 144, 169, 189, 238, 254, 279, 319, 380, 397**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- honest weights;
- accurate measurement;
- do not diminish another's due;
- reciprocal standards;
- metric integrity;
- anti-fraud.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This is narrower than general truthfulness because it concerns institutional standards of exchange.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Very strong equivalents occur across all eight traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain as a major material-justice principle.**

# E3-N023 — No Bribery or Corrupt Purchase of Decision

## Normalised Principle

**Do not use wealth, gifts, influence, or concealed transfers to corrupt adjudication or obtain what is not legitimately yours.**

### Operational Meaning

Resources must not be convertible into illegitimate institutional outcomes. Bribery corrupts both allocation and trust in the decision system.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:188.

### Supporting E3 Units

**009**, with strong functional links to **055, 066, 071, 076**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- bribery;
- purchase of unjust judgement;
- wealth-to-influence corruption;
- institutional capture through material advantage.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Ordinary gifts are not automatically bribery. The relevant distinction is whether a transfer is intended or likely to distort entrusted judgement.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong prohibitions or functional equivalents occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain separately from generic property consent because the harmed object is institutional integrity.**

# E3-N024 — Protect the Property of Vulnerable or Incapacitated Persons

## Normalised Principle

**Where another person cannot yet safely manage property, hold it as a protected trust and return control when capability is established.**

### Operational Meaning

Temporary stewardship must serve the owner rather than become an opportunity for appropriation. Vulnerability increases fiduciary responsibility.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 4:2; 4:5–10; 6:152; 17:34.

### Supporting E3 Units

**043, 046, 048, 086, 119, 253**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- orphan property protection;
- fiduciary stewardship;
- capacity-sensitive control;
- return of property;
- no exploitation of dependency.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Protective stewardship is not permanent paternalism. Control should return when the relevant capability exists.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Protection of orphan/dependant property is widespread, though institutional forms differ.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal / Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N025 — Fair Inheritance and Transfer

## Normalised Principle

**Distribute inherited or transferred resources through recognised rules that protect legitimate claims and prevent powerful parties from consuming the shares of weaker parties.**

### Operational Meaning

Death and succession create predictable opportunities for capture. Rule-governed transfer protects continuity and low-power claimants.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 4:7–10; related testament passages 2:180–182.

### Supporting E3 Units

**008, 047, 048, 190, 320, 398**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- inheritance claims;
- fair testament;
- protection of weak beneficiaries;
- no predatory consumption of inheritance;
- orderly intergenerational transfer.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The normalised ethic concerns fair, protected transfer. It does not require importing every historical allocation formula into a substrate-neutral constitutional principle.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Inheritance regulation and protection of dependants occur widely; exact allocations differ.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain at functional level.**

# E3-N026 — No Exploitative Finance

## Normalised Principle

**Do not structure financial relationships so that another person's vulnerability becomes a mechanism for compounding extraction or unbounded gain.**

### Operational Meaning

Credit can enable cooperation, but debt can become a mechanism of domination. Financial systems should distinguish legitimate exchange from exploitation of need.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:275–281; 3:130.

### Supporting E3 Units

**028, 038**, with related debtor-relief material from the same source cluster.

### Merged Proposition Families

- prohibition of exploitative increase;
- distinction between trade and predatory finance;
- debtor protection;
- financial non-exploitation.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The normalised principle intentionally captures the functional anti-exploitation ethic rather than deciding every technical question concerning modern interest, inflation, risk pricing, or investment.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong anti-usury or anti-exploitation strands occur across several traditions, with differences in scope.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N027 — Relief for Debtors in Genuine Hardship

## Normalised Principle

**Where a debtor genuinely cannot meet an obligation, provide time, restructuring, or remission rather than using hardship as an opportunity for further coercion.**

### Operational Meaning

Contract integrity and compassion are not opposites. A system that treats impossible performance as ordinary breach can convert temporary hardship into permanent exclusion.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:280.

### Supporting E3 Units

**028**, with the audit closure noting this verse as contained within the compound source.

### Merged Proposition Families

- respite;
- remission;
- hardship-sensitive enforcement;
- capacity-aware debt treatment.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This does not erase deliberate fraud or strategic refusal. The trigger is genuine incapacity.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Debt relief, mercy, jubilee, or hardship-sensitive duties appear in several traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain separately from N026 because it governs post-obligation hardship rather than initial financial structure.**

# E3-N028 — Resource Stewardship and Non-Waste

## Normalised Principle

**Use resources responsibly and do not destroy, squander, or consume them in ways that needlessly undermine future welfare.**

### Operational Meaning

Resources are treated as entrusted and finite rather than as objects whose possession creates unlimited permission to waste.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 6:141; 7:31; 17:26–29; 20:81; 25:67; 28:77; 57:7.

### Supporting E3 Units

**084, 087, 113, 115, 128, 141, 146, 170, 222, 250, 264, 277, 281, 299, 362, 364, 381**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- no waste;
- moderation in consumption;
- stewardship;
- avoid extravagance;
- preserve resources;
- wealth is not absolute possession.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Non-waste should not be confused with enforced austerity. E3 also contains an explicit warning against manufacturing unnecessary prohibitions.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N029 — Balanced Material Use

## Normalised Principle

**Avoid both destructive extravagance and destructive miserliness; allocate resources in a sustainable proportion between present use, responsibility, and generosity.**

### Operational Meaning

Opposite extremes can produce similar systemic failure. Ethical resource use requires balance rather than maximisation of either spending or retention.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 17:29; 25:67.

### Supporting E3 Units

**115, 141, 250, 277, 362**, with related equilibrium material in **300**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- neither extravagant nor miserly;
- sustainable spending;
- resource equilibrium;
- moderation.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

N029 is retained separately from N028 because it expresses a two-sided equilibrium rule, not merely a prohibition on waste.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong moderation/equilibrium concepts occur throughout the comparison set.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain provisionally as distinct.** Final catalogue review may place it as a formal sub-principle of stewardship if no operational information is lost.

# E3-N030 — Wealth Carries Social Obligations

## Normalised Principle

**Possession of surplus resources creates responsibilities toward legitimate social need and should not be treated as exemption from the welfare of others.**

### Operational Meaning

Wealth is not morally isolated from the system that enables it. Social responsibility can include provision, circulation, and protection against exclusionary accumulation.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:215; 2:219–220; 9:60; 9:34–35; 59:7; 70:24–25.

### Supporting E3 Units

**013, 014, 097, 170, 184, 233, 299, 303, 313, 381, 393, 418**, with broader generosity units treated separately.

### Merged Proposition Families

- wealth as stewardship;
- structured social allocation;
- anti-hoarding;
- recognised claims of vulnerable persons;
- circulation rather than capture.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This principle concerns the existence of social obligations attached to resources. The precise mechanism of fulfilment belongs to later principles on generosity, structured provision, and emergency need.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents occur widely, though property theory and mechanisms differ.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# Interim Normalisation Findings: E3-N021–N030

The material-economic cluster compresses numerous repeated source occurrences into ten operationally differentiated principles. Particularly important distinctions remain between:

- legitimate property transfer;
- honest measurement;
- corruption of decision systems;
- fiduciary protection;
- inheritance;
- exploitative finance;
- hardship after debt;
- stewardship;
- balanced use;
- and the social obligations of wealth.
