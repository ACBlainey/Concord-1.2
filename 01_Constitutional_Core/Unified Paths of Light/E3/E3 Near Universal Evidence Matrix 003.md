# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Near-Universal Evidence Matrix 003

**Principles:** Q035, Q037, Q039, Q041, Q043.

---

## Q035 — Human Dignity Beyond Status

Traditions widely criticise arrogance, misuse of rank and mistreatment of vulnerable people. Yet they differ over hierarchy, caste, class, office, gender and inherited social roles.

A broad principle that moral worth is not reducible to wealth/status has strong support; a modern doctrine of equal social status would be anachronistic in several cases.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at the functional anti-status formulation.**

## Q037 — Privacy and Freedom From Intrusive Surveillance

Privacy is strongly protected in some traditions through rules governing entry, gossip, exposure of faults, household boundaries or spying. It is not equally explicit as a general right across all traditions.

The Psalm anchor did not recover a comparable independent principle.

**Decision:** **Common / Near-universal.** Do not infer privacy merely from general non-harm.

## Q039 — Inclusion Against Elite Capture

Care for poor or low-status people and criticism of arrogant elites are widespread. Requiring institutions to preserve participation against elite capture is a systems-level extension.

**Decision:** **Common.** The parent anti-status ethic is broader; the institutional anti-capture mechanism is not demonstrated as near-universal.

## Q041 — Coexistence Without Forced Sameness

Tolerance, coexistence and restraint toward outsiders appear in many traditions, but some traditions also contain exclusivist or assimilationist strands. E3's formulation specifically protects continued difference rather than merely temporary toleration.

**Decision:** **Common / Near-universal.**

## Q043 — Family Respect With Preserved Moral Agency

Filial respect and family duty are extremely widespread. The boundary that family authority cannot override independent moral agency is more variable.

Confucian filial ethics, for example, strongly values family obligation while also containing traditions of remonstrance rather than simple obedience. Other traditions likewise impose limits on parental or kin authority, but the exact balance differs.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at functional level.**

## Matrix 003 Finding

This tranche highlights the difference between **protecting a person** and **protecting the person's agency**.

Many traditions strongly protect family, community and vulnerable members. E3 frequently adds a second-order constraint: the protective institution must not consume the agency of the person it protects.

That distinction should remain visible in the final synthesis.
