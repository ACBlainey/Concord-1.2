## Method Note

Normalisation continues from the E3 source-occurrence corpus. Principle boundaries are preserved where collapsing them would materially alter behaviour, rights, duties, or institutional design.

# E3-N011 — Sanctity and Protection of Life

## Normalised Principle

**Do not intentionally take innocent life, and structure social responsibility around the protection and preservation of life.**

### Operational Meaning

Human life establishes a high threshold against intentional destruction. The ethic is not only prohibitory: saving and protecting life are positively valued.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 5:32; 6:151; 17:33.

### Supporting E3 Units

**060, 073, 085, 118, 251, 252**, with protection-of-oppressed material later separated under its own principle.

### Merged Proposition Families

- prohibition of wrongful killing;
- sanctity of life;
- saving life;
- protection of dependants from lethal harm;
- life as a constraint on retaliation.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This principle does not by itself settle every question of defensive force, punishment, or war. Those are governed by separate proportionality and conflict principles.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Protection of life has strong functional equivalents throughout the comparison set.

**Preliminary classification: Universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain as foundational.**

# E3-N012 — Non-Aggression and Proportionality

## Normalised Principle

**Do not initiate wrongful aggression, and where force or redress is legitimate, do not exceed the harm or necessity that justifies it.**

### Operational Meaning

The principle constrains both initiation and response. Injury does not create unlimited permission.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:190–194; 16:126; 17:33; 42:40–43.

### Supporting E3 Units

**007, 010, 111, 118, 130, 159, 248, 252, 291, 373**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- no transgression in conflict;
- proportionate retaliation;
- limits on redress;
- restraint after injury;
- defensive rather than expansionary force.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Proportionality does not require retaliation. Forgiveness and reconciliation remain separate positive options.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents exist widely, though traditions differ on permissible force.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N013 — Legitimate Self-Defence and Protection Against Oppression

## Normalised Principle

**Persons and communities may defend against serious aggression and may bear positive duties to protect people who cannot escape oppression themselves.**

### Operational Meaning

Non-aggression does not require passive submission to severe harm. Protection should remain tied to defence and liberation rather than domination.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 4:75; 22:39–40; 42:41; related 49:9.

### Supporting E3 Units

**129, 159, 211, 265, 291, 375, 410**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- defensive force;
- protection of persecuted people;
- defence of worship/community space;
- victim agency;
- positive duty against oppression.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This principle is bounded by N012. Protective purpose does not erase proportionality or protection of innocents.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

The protective ethic is widespread; views on coercive force vary.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal depending equivalence threshold.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain separately from non-aggression.**

# E3-N014 — Peace and De-Escalation

## Normalised Principle

**Prefer credible opportunities to reduce conflict, and do not allow provocation alone to determine your response.**

### Operational Meaning

Peace is treated as an active option requiring reciprocal recognition, disciplined speech, and sometimes disengagement.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 8:61; 25:63; 41:34; 73:10.

### Supporting E3 Units

**090, 094, 123, 140, 157, 187, 230, 257, 276, 289, 361, 370, 395**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- accept genuine peace openings;
- answer provocation without mirroring it;
- use better speech;
- disengage with dignity;
- transform hostile cycles.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Peace does not mean ignoring active aggression. N013 preserves defensive agency.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Very strong equivalents occur across the comparison set.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N015 — Forgiveness and Reconciliation

## Normalised Principle

**Where safety and justice permit, leave open the possibility of forgiveness, reform, and restored relationship after wrongdoing.**

### Operational Meaning

Wrongdoing need not permanently fix the identity of the wrongdoer or the relationship. Forgiveness is ethically valuable when freely chosen, not coerced from victims.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 3:134; 4:149; 12:92; 42:37, 40–43.

### Supporting E3 Units

**004, 039, 068, 072, 090, 105, 111, 142, 156, 157, 159, 161, 208, 241, 276, 289, 291, 371, 373**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- forgiveness after injury;
- anger restraint;
- moral recovery;
- reconciliation;
- answering harm with better conduct.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Forgiveness is not compulsory erasure of accountability. It must remain compatible with victim agency and future safety.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents occur across all eight traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N016 — Justice Without Partiality

## Normalised Principle

**Apply justice impartially despite wealth, kinship, status, hostility, group identity, or self-interest.**

### Operational Meaning

Justice is corrupted when the identity of the parties changes the standard.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 4:58; 4:135; 5:8; 5:42; related 49:9.

### Supporting E3 Units

**055, 066, 071, 075, 076, 215, 294, 375**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- judge fairly;
- justice against self-interest;
- justice toward disliked people;
- no status partiality;
- impartial conflict adjudication.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Impartiality does not require identical treatment where relevant circumstances differ. It requires relevantly similar cases to be governed by the same ethical standard.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents are present across the comparison set.

**Preliminary classification: Universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain as foundational.**

# E3-N017 — Procedural Fairness

## Normalised Principle

**Use transparent, evidence-sensitive, and reviewable procedures where decisions materially affect another person's rights, property, family, liberty, or reputation.**

### Operational Meaning

Good outcomes cannot reliably compensate for arbitrary procedure. Fair process distributes power, records reasons, and constrains unilateral abuse.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:282–283; 4:35; 5:106–108; 24:6–9; 65:1–2.

### Supporting E3 Units

**029, 030, 052, 082, 180, 268**, with adjudication support in **075, 076**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- documentation;
- witnesses;
- arbitration;
- defined procedures in high-conflict situations;
- reliable evidence;
- procedural constraints on unilateral power.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

N017 governs process. N016 governs impartial substantive standard. Both are required for robust justice.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Formal mechanisms differ, but functional procedural safeguards occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal / Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N018 — Accountability With Possibility of Reform

## Normalised Principle

**Wrongdoing should have consequences, but accountability systems should preserve a credible path for acknowledgement, repair, and reform where future safety permits.**

### Operational Meaning

A stable ethical system must avoid both impunity and permanent moral exclusion.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 5:38–39; 25:68–71; 39:53.

### Supporting E3 Units

**074, 142, 156**, with related correction and repentance material in **012, 068**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- accountability after wrongdoing;
- reform changes moral status;
- correction rather than permanent exclusion;
- moral recovery remains possible.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Reform does not automatically cancel restitution, safeguarding, or consequences already required by justice.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Reform/repentance concepts occur widely though institutional implications differ.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N019 — Do Not Shield Wrongdoing Through Loyalty or Office

## Normalised Principle

**Do not use loyalty, advocacy, office, or institutional procedure to knowingly protect deliberate wrongdoing from legitimate accountability.**

### Operational Meaning

Roles intended to serve justice can be captured and converted into mechanisms for insider protection.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 4:105–107; related impartiality passages.

### Supporting E3 Units

**212, 411**, with functional links to **066, 076, 160, 292**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- anti-capture advocacy;
- loyalty has limits;
- office must not become concealment;
- insider status does not erase accountability.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This does not prohibit defence, representation, or due process for accused persons. It prohibits knowingly corrupting those functions to conceal deliberate wrongdoing.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong functional equivalents occur in many traditions, though this exact institutional formulation is less explicit in some.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.** It is sufficiently operationally distinct from generic impartial justice to justify separate treatment.

# E3-N020 — Conflict Intervention Should Restore Just Peace

## Normalised Principle

**When intervention in conflict is justified, distinguish aggressor from victim, restrain ongoing aggression, and return to fair reconciliation when the aggression ends.**

### Operational Meaning

Neutrality can perpetuate injustice when one party is actively aggressing. Yet intervention should not become permanent punishment or partisan domination.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 49:9–10.

### Supporting E3 Units

**164, 294, 375**, with related peace and defensive-force units **094, 129, 211, 265**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- impartial conflict intervention;
- restraint of aggressor;
- restoration of peace;
- return to fairness after aggression ceases;
- community responsibility for internal conflict.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This is more specific than N013 because it governs third-party or communal intervention between conflicting parties.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Mediation and peace restoration are widespread; the duty to act coercively against an aggressor is less uniformly explicit.

**Preliminary classification: Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain provisionally as distinct.** It may later sit beneath a broader conflict-governance principle, but merging it now would erase a meaningful institutional rule.

# Interim Normalisation Findings: E3-N011–N020

The second tranche establishes a coherent justice-and-conflict architecture. Several principles are closely related but have deliberately not been collapsed because they govern different actors or stages:

- life protection;
- limits on force;
- defensive/protective agency;
- peace and de-escalation;
- forgiveness;
- impartial justice;
- fair procedure;
- reform;
- anti-capture;
- third-party conflict intervention.

This illustrates the intended meaning of **loss-minimising compression**.
