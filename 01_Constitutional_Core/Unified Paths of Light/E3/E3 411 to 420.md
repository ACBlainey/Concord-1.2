## Method Note

This tranche continues the completeness audit while explicitly testing whether previously selected passages should survive final inclusion. Inclusion requires a separable, generalisable behavioural ethic; narrative material whose apparent lesson depends on exceptional hidden knowledge must not be converted into a general permission.

# E3 Unit 411 — Qur'an 4:105–107
## Advocacy Should Not Be Used to Shield Deliberate Wrongdoing

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:105–107 warns against arguing on behalf of people who betray trust and against becoming an advocate for deliberate wrongdoing.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Loyalty does not justify distortion of justice.
- Representation has ethical limits.
- Advocates should not knowingly convert procedure into concealment of wrongdoing.
- Institutions need safeguards against factional protection of insiders.
- Due process and truthful advocacy should be distinguished from corrupt shielding.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Partiality and complicity in wrongdoing are strongly constrained. | B | Loyalty does not excuse deliberate injustice. |
| Judaism | Judicial law prohibits partiality and false advocacy. | A | Do not distort justice for insiders. |
| Buddhism | Wrong livelihood and false speech oppose knowingly assisting harmful deception. | B | Do not enable wrongdoing through one's role. |
| Hinduism | Dharma constrains loyalty when it conflicts with justice. | B | Role duty is bounded by right conduct. |
| Sikhism | Justice should not be surrendered to group loyalty. | B | Do not protect wrongdoing because it is one's own side. |
| Confucianism | Loyalty includes remonstrance rather than blind compliance. | A/B | Proper loyalty can require correction. |
| African Traditional | Advocacy and kinship duties vary. | C | Insider loyalty should not wholly defeat communal justice. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 212 and anti-capture advocacy cluster.**

# E3 Unit 413 — Qur'an 5:87–88
## Do Not Manufacture Prohibitions Against Legitimate Goods

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:87–88 warns against prohibiting good things made lawful and against transgression, while commending lawful and good consumption.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Over-constraint can itself be a governance failure.
- Rule-makers should not manufacture burdens without adequate basis.
- Ethical systems require safeguards against both permissiveness and unnecessary prohibition.
- Legitimate goods should not be suppressed merely to display severity.
- Constraint needs justification.

### Comparative Analysis

|---|---|---:|---|

| Judaism | Rabbinic safeguards coexist with warnings around adding to law; the relationship is complex. | B | Constraint should remain lawfully grounded. |
| Buddhism | The Middle Way rejects unnecessary extremes of self-mortification. | A/B | More restriction is not always better. |
| Hinduism | Asceticism is valued in some contexts but harmful/extreme austerity is also criticised. | B | Discipline should not become destructive excess. |
| Sikhism | Empty asceticism and ritual restriction are strongly criticised. | A/B | Ethical life is not maximised by arbitrary denial. |
| Confucianism | Propriety seeks appropriate measure rather than maximal restriction. | B | Rules should fit legitimate purpose. |
| African Traditional | Prohibition systems vary greatly. | C | No uniform analogue asserted. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 217.** This remains especially relevant to Roadway Equilibrium's over-constraint boundary.

# E3 Unit 420 — Qur'an 18:71–82
## Audit Exclusion: Exceptional Hidden-Knowledge Narrative Is Not a General Behavioural Rule

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 18:71–82 narrates actions by al-Khidr that appear harmful or unlawful to Moses until later explanations disclose exceptional knowledge unavailable to ordinary observers.

### Extracted Ethical Core
For audit purposes, **no general permission to imitate these acts is extractable**. The narrative may support epistemic humility about incomplete knowledge, but its exceptional structure makes it unsafe as a direct behavioural source.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Narratives involving exceptional divine knowledge do not automatically create ordinary permissions. | C | Exceptional narrative is not general law. |
| Judaism | Exceptional prophetic narratives likewise require interpretive limits. | C | Narrative context constrains generalisation. |
| Buddhism | Skillful-means narratives can be context-sensitive and should not be naively universalised. | C | Exceptional cases require caution. |
| Hinduism | Exceptional epic/divine actions are not automatically ordinary behavioural rules. | C | Narrative role matters. |
| Sikhism | No close functional equivalent required for this exclusion decision. | D | No general behavioural rule inferred. |
| Confucianism | Ordinary governance should rely on accessible reasons rather than claims of hidden knowledge. | C | Public rules require publicly usable justification. |
| African Traditional | Exceptional sacred narratives vary too widely for a general comparison. | D | No uniform analogue asserted. |

### Discussion

Earlier E3 Unit 126 treated this passage narrowly as a warning about apparent harm and hidden information. The canonical audit confirms the methodological concern already identified before that unit was created: the narrative depends on extraordinary knowledge unavailable to ordinary agents. Converting it into a behavioural principle risks licensing harmful action on the claim that unseen benefits will later justify it.

The epistemic lesson — human observers can lack relevant information — is independently supported elsewhere in the corpus without this danger.

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: REMOVE Unit 126 from the final ethical source corpus.** Retain Qur'an 18:71–82 only in the methodological audit record as an example of **Preservation Before Compression and exclusion discipline**: not every morally significant sacred narrative should become a generalisable ethical rule.

# Interim Findings: Audit Units 411–420

This tranche contains an important methodological correction. Most units confirm existing clusters, while Unit 419 adds a useful systems-level refinement concerning trust-network destabilisation. Unit 420 formally resolves the outstanding concern about **E3 Unit 126**: it should not survive into the final ethical catalogue.

The audit should continue until the remaining coverage and exclusion checks are exhausted; only then should the source index be frozen and normalisation begin.
