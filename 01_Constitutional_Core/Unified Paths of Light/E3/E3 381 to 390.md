# E3 Unit 381 — Qur'an 57:7
## Possession Can Be Understood as Stewardship Rather Than Absolute Ownership

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 57:7 describes people as successors or trustees in relation to wealth and calls for giving from what has been entrusted.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Control of resources need not imply unlimited moral ownership.
- Present holders inherit systems and assets they did not wholly create.
- Stewardship creates obligations to future and surrounding persons.
- Wealth can be understood as temporary custody within a longer chain.
- Property rights and resource responsibilities can coexist.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — stewardship traditions | Human possession is commonly framed as stewardship under higher ownership. | A | Hold resources responsibly rather than absolutely. |
| Judaism | Land and wealth are subject to covenantal constraints and social duties. | A/B | Ownership remains ethically bounded. |
| Buddhism | Non-attachment weakens absolute possessive identity. | B | Use possessions without treating them as ultimate self. |
| Hinduism | Non-attachment and duty constrain possession. | B | Resources should serve legitimate duties. |
| Sikhism | Honest earning and sharing strongly converge. | B | Wealth carries social responsibility. |
| Confucianism | Property/profit is subordinate to righteousness and social duty. | B | Possession does not cancel obligation. |
| African Traditional | Communal tenure and stewardship forms vary widely. | B | Resource holding can entail intergenerational/community duties. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 170 and stewardship cluster.**

# E3 Unit 382 — Qur'an 58:8–10
## Secret Coordination Becomes Unethical When Its Purpose Is Harm

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 58:8–10 distinguishes harmful secret consultation associated with wrongdoing and hostility from consultation directed toward righteousness and restraint.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Privacy is not inherently unethical; purpose matters.
- Hidden coordination increases risk when used to evade accountability for harm.
- Institutions should distinguish legitimate confidentiality from conspiratorial abuse.
- Process opacity becomes dangerous when combined with harmful objectives.
- Governance needs both protected deliberation and anti-capture safeguards.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — John 3:20 / conspiracy warnings | Concealment for wrongdoing is morally suspect. | B | Privacy must not become cover for harm. |
| Judaism | Secret plotting against others is strongly condemned. | B | Confidentiality does not license conspiracy. |
| Buddhism | Intention determines much ethical quality; harmful collusion conflicts with right conduct. | B | Judge hidden coordination by purpose and effects. |
| Hinduism | Harmful conspiracy conflicts with dharmic conduct. | B | Secrecy does not erase responsibility. |
| Sikhism | Truthful living opposes covert harmful manipulation. | B | Do not coordinate secretly for wrongdoing. |
| Confucianism | Factional intrigue is a recurrent governance concern. | A/B | Hidden faction should not capture public purpose. |
| African Traditional | Secret institutions vary widely in role and legitimacy. | C | Secrecy itself is insufficient; harmful use is the concern. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 171.** The legitimate-confidentiality versus harmful-conspiracy distinction should be preserved.

# E3 Unit 383 — Qur'an 58:11
## Make Space for Others and Recognise Knowledge Without Converting It Into Domination

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 58:11 instructs people to make room in assemblies and associates elevated standing with faith and knowledge.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Shared spaces should not be monopolised.
- Participation sometimes requires incumbents to make room.
- Knowledge deserves recognition but should remain integrated with ethical character.
- Inclusion can be implemented through mundane procedural behaviour.
- Status should not block access to collective spaces.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|

| Hinduism | Respect for learning is strong, while social access traditions vary. | C | Knowledge may merit respect without unlimited dominance. |
| Sikhism — langar/sangat equality | Shared spaces deliberately flatten status distinctions. | A | Make room across social rank. |
| Confucianism | Learning deserves honour but office entails duty. | B | Knowledge should serve social order. |
| African Traditional | Assembly and elder-status norms vary. | C | Participation requires recognised access rules. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 172 and participation/access cluster.**

# E3 Unit 384 — Qur'an 59:9
## Scarcity Does Not Eliminate the Possibility of Generosity

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 59:9 praises people who give preference to others even while themselves in need and who resist possessive greed.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Generosity is most informative when resources are constrained.
- Scarcity need not erase concern for others.
- Hoarding impulses can be consciously regulated.
- Mutual aid can increase resilience where central abundance is absent.
- Ethical systems should recognise exceptional altruism without necessarily demanding universal self-sacrifice.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Mark 12:41–44 | Giving from scarcity is strongly praised. | A | Generosity is not limited to surplus. |
| Judaism | Charity remains important even among people of modest means, though duties are bounded. | A/B | Scarcity does not eliminate all responsibility. |
| Buddhism — dana | Generosity is central and not restricted to the wealthy. | A | Give despite attachment and scarcity. |
| Hinduism — dana | Giving is a major virtue across circumstances. | A/B | Share resources rather than hoard them. |
| Sikhism — vand chhakna | Sharing is a core practice. | A | Divide resources with others. |
| Confucianism | Benevolence can require sacrifice for others. | B | Need does not erase humane concern. |
| African Traditional | Mutual-aid systems often operate precisely under scarcity, though diverse. | A/B | Reciprocity can sustain communities under constraint. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 173 and generosity-under-scarcity cluster.**

# E3 Unit 387 — Qur'an 60:9

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 60:9 distinguishes those who actively fight, expel, or assist expulsion from peaceful outsiders.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Threat assessment should be behaviour-based.
- Identity categories are too coarse for legitimate security decisions.
- Active persecution changes obligations in ways mere difference does not.
- Systems should distinguish adversary, competitor, dissenter, and peaceful neighbour.
- Overbroad enemy classification creates avoidable injustice.

| Sikhism | Resistance is directed toward oppression rather than identity alone. | A/B | Oppose persecution, not mere difference. |
| Confucianism | Governance should classify conduct accurately. | B | Security response should fit actual behaviour. |

# E3 Unit 388 — Qur'an 61:2–3

- Public commitment creates accountability.
- Institutional legitimacy erodes when declared principles and actual behaviour diverge.
- Ethical language should not become reputational camouflage.
- Systems need mechanisms that compare promises with performance.
- Integrity requires alignment between stated rule and enacted conduct.

# E3 Unit 389 — Qur'an 65:6
## Separation Does Not Justify Housing-Based Harassment

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 65:6 requires housing according to means during a defined separation context and prohibits harassment intended to make living conditions intolerable.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Relationship breakdown does not erase basic material duties.
- Control over housing can become a coercive weapon.
- Exit processes should not be designed to punish vulnerability.
- Dependence on another for shelter increases the other's duty not to exploit that leverage.
- Procedural justice includes material conditions during transition.

### Comparative Analysis

| Hinduism | Family support duties provide partial parallels. | B/C | Relationship change does not erase all obligation. |
| Sikhism | Family responsibility and dignity provide functional convergence. | B | Preserve basic welfare during conflict. |
| Confucianism | Family duties continue to constrain treatment amid conflict. | B | Role breakdown does not justify cruelty. |
| African Traditional | Separation/property systems vary widely. | C | Vulnerability should not be exploited through shelter control. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge with separation-fairness cluster.** This passage adds a specific anti-coercion mechanism: housing leverage.

# E3 Unit 390 — Qur'an 65:7
## Material Obligations Should Be Proportionate to Actual Capacity

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 65:7 directs those with abundance to spend from abundance and those with constrained means to spend from what they have been given, stating that obligation is not imposed beyond available capacity.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Equal obligation need not mean identical numerical burden.
- Capacity is relevant to fair allocation of duties.
- Progressive responsibility can coexist with universal obligation.
- Systems should avoid demanding impossible performance.
- Resource-aware duties can preserve both accountability and realism.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — 2 Corinthians 8:12 | Giving is assessed according to what a person has. | A | Duty should reflect capacity. |
| Judaism | Charity duties are structured partly by means and circumstance. | A/B | Obligation can scale with resources. |
| Buddhism | Giving is voluntary and context-sensitive rather than numerically uniform. | B | Capacity affects feasible duty. |
| Hinduism | Duty varies with role and circumstance. | B | Obligation should reflect actual situation. |
| Sikhism | Sharing is integrated with honest earning and household responsibility. | B | Contribute from available means. |
| Confucianism | Appropriate action is sensitive to role and circumstance. | B | Fair duty is context-aware. |
| African Traditional | Contribution systems vary. | B/C | Communal duties often reflect means or role. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge with capacity-aware obligation cluster.** This has direct relevance to proportional burden design.

# Interim Findings: Audit Units 381–390

The audit is now deep into recurrence confirmation. Units 389–390 nevertheless sharpen two practically important ideas: **dependency must not be weaponised during institutional or relational exit**, and **obligations should scale to real capacity**. These are candidates for explicit treatment during final normalisation even if merged into broader principles.
