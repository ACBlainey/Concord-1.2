## Q029 — Wealth Carries Social Obligations

### Sikh row

**Exact source:** Sri Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1245.  
**Passage:** the Guru Nanak line conventionally translated to the effect that one who earns by labour and gives some from what is in hand recognises the path.

**Functional assessment:** Directly supports the proposition that legitimately earned resources are not exclusively self-regarding and should be shared.

**Source-quality decision:** **SQ-A** — identifiable primary scripture and exact Ang.

**Functional grade:** **A/B**.

**Repair decision:** Sikh row is eligible for **S — Secure**.

### African row

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry *African Ethics*, while explicitly warning against treating Akan ethics as a microcosm of all African ethics, documents a duty-oriented and common-good structure in African moral thought. Its Akan material states that social morality includes mutual helpfulness, collective responsibility, cooperation, interdependence and reciprocal obligations; it also treats human welfare and the common good as central.

**Decision:** existing African **S** remains supportable when labelled as **Akan/African-ethics scholarly synthesis**, not as a fictitious pan-African canon.

### Consequence

Q029 presently had seven secure cells with Sikh P as the sole non-secure cell. This repair supplies a defensible exact Sikh source for the frozen principle.

**Candidate matrix result after controlled update: 8/8 Universal.**

## Q032 — Positive Duty Toward Severe Need

### Sikh row

SGGS Ang 1245 establishes a positive sharing duty. Sikh practice also develops this through *vand chhako*, *seva* and communal provision.

The exact Ang proves positive sharing, but Q032 is specifically **duty toward severe need**. The passage does not by itself specify severity.

**Decision:** retain Sikh **P** pending a direct primary-text source connecting positive aid to a person in serious need.

### African row

The *African Ethics* synthesis provides substantially stronger evidence. It records the Akan maxim translated as **“A human being needs help”** and explains its normative force: a person deserves and therefore ought to be helped in appropriate circumstances. It further discusses distress and unfortunate circumstances as calling for sympathy, compassion and assistance.

**Decision:** African **S** remains secure with scoped Akan provenance.

### Consequence

**No upgrade yet. Q032 remains 7/8 secure.**

## Q035 — Human Dignity Beyond Status

### Sikh row

Primary-scripture evidence is materially stronger than the earlier concept-level citation.

Relevant SGGS evidence includes:
- Ang 349: instruction not to treat social class/status as determinative;
- Ang 747: the teaching is described as shared across the four caste categories;
- Ang 1106: the socially low are elevated rather than treated as morally inferior.

Scholarly analysis of the Guru Granth Sahib also identifies equality of human beings and opposition to caste hierarchy as a core social implication.

**Source-quality decision:** **SQ-A** for the exact SGGS passages.

**Functional grade:** **A/B** for dignity/moral standing beyond inherited status.

**Repair decision:** Sikh row is eligible for **S — Secure**.

### African row

The scoped Akan evidence is unusually direct: the maxim **“The human being is more beautiful than gold”** is interpreted as requiring recognition of human worth and respect by virtue of humanity rather than wealth/status.

**Decision:** African **S** remains secure.

### Consequence

Q035 had seven secure cells with Sikh P as the sole non-secure cell.

**Candidate matrix result after controlled update: 8/8 Universal.**
