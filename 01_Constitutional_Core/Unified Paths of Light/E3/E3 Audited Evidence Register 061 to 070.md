# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Audited Comparative Evidence Register — Q061–Q070

**Status:** Working row-level register.

---

## Q061 — Responsibility for Advocacy and Influence

Traditions recognise responsibilities attached to teaching, counsel, speech and leadership. The E3 principle generalises this to responsibility for consequential advocacy.

**Current result:** **Common / Near-universal.**

## Q062 — Reciprocal Courtesy

Reciprocity is extremely widespread, including Confucian reciprocal formulations and comparable regard-for-others rules elsewhere.

**Current result:** **Near-universal exact; Universal reciprocal-regard parent is plausible.**

## Q063 — Patience Under Difficulty

Patience, endurance and forbearance recur across the comparison families. Bhagavad Gita 13.8 supplies direct forbearance evidence.

**Current result:** **Strong high-tier candidate; exact 8/8 remains provenance-dependent.**

## Q064 — Control Anger Before It Controls Action

Anger restraint is directly represented in Buddhist and Hindu sources and strongly paralleled elsewhere.

**Current result:** **Strong high-tier candidate; likely Near-universal secure, possible Universal after exact provenance.**

## Q065 — Obligation Should Track Capacity

Many traditions recognise inability, mitigation, age, circumstance or proportional duty. A general capacity-calibrated obligation rule is more explicit in some than others.

**Current result:** **Common / Near-universal.**

## Q066 — Do Not Manufacture Unnecessary Burdens

Compassion, moderation and opposition to needless hardship recur, but the second-order rule against creating avoidable normative burdens is more specific.

**Current result:** **Common.**

## Q067 — Avoid Self-Destructive Extremes

Moderation and self-control are exceptionally widespread. Traditions differ over asceticism and the exact meaning of excess.

**Current result:** **Strong Near-universal; plausible Universal functional moderation parent.**

## Q068 — Moral Quality Is Not Determined by Quantity or Popularity

Resistance to crowd judgement, status and superficial numerical success appears widely, but not always as an explicit meta-rule.

**Current result:** **Near-universal functional.**

## Q069 — Preserve Independent Moral Agency Under Group Pressure

Traditions contain warnings against corrupt crowds, conformity, bad association and status pressure. E3 generalises these into preservation of independent moral judgement.

**Current result:** **Near-universal functional.**

## Q070 — Distinguish Peaceful Difference From Active Threat

Many traditions distinguish wrongdoers/enemies from peaceful outsiders in some contexts, but the E3 classification rule is unusually operational.

**Current result:** **Common.**

---

## Register 007 Finding

Q061–Q070 expose a transition from ordinary virtues to **meta-ethical safeguards**:

- responsibility for influence;
- capacity-calibrated obligation;
- anti-overconstraint;
- resistance to popularity;
- independent agency under group pressure;
- classification of difference before coercive response.

These principles regulate how ethical systems themselves are applied.

> **End of Audited Comparative Evidence Register Q061–Q070**
