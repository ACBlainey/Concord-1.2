# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## African Provenance Repair 001 — Q001, Q015, Q031

## Q001 — Truthful Communication

The existing African P reflects a real but insufficiently localised truth/honesty claim.

Akan ethical scholarship treats truthfulness/honesty as a virtue within character ethics, while the wider scholarly reconstruction of African ethics treats good character and trustworthiness as socially necessary.

**Assessment:** strong functional support, but the present record still lacks a sufficiently precise named maxim/textual location for the exact communication formulation.

**Decision:** retain African **P** pending tighter primary/traditional provenance.

## Q015 — Forgiveness and Reconciliation

African evidence is substantially stronger at the reconciliation level because multiple named customary traditions contain institutionalised reconciliation/restoration practices. However, the E3 cell must not convert restorative settlement automatically into interpersonal forgiveness.

**Assessment:** reconciliation is strongly supported; the compound “forgiveness and reconciliation” remains only partly demonstrated by the current scoped evidence.

**Decision:** retain African **P**.

## Q031 — Non-Transactional Aid

Akan duty/common-good ethics securely supports aid, mutuality and helping those in need. But mutual aid and reciprocal obligation are not the same as aid given without expectation of return.

Indeed, reciprocity may cut against using those sources as direct evidence for the **non-transactional** qualifier.

**Decision:** retain African **P**.

## Result

No promotion is made in this tranche.

This is an important negative result: African provenance repair must not turn communal obligation, reconciliation or honesty into a stronger proposition than the identified tradition actually supplies.
