# E3-N031 — Generosity Without Humiliation

## Normalised Principle

**Give material help in ways that preserve the recipient's dignity and do not convert assistance into humiliation, control, or reputational leverage.**

### Operational Meaning

Aid can itself become harmful if it is followed by reminders, degradation, manipulation, or public status extraction.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:261–264; 2:267; 2:268–274; 93:9–10.

### Supporting E3 Units

**025, 026, 027, 114, 188, 194, 317, 322, 396, 402**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- give without injury;
- discreet giving;
- kind refusal;
- non-humiliating aid;
- assistance without demanded gratitude.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This is not merely generosity. It constrains the *manner* in which help is delivered.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong functional equivalents occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N032 — Non-Transactional Aid

## Normalised Principle

**Where assistance is given to relieve genuine need, do not automatically manufacture a debt of gratitude, obedience, praise, or repayment.**

### Operational Meaning

Vulnerability should not be converted into domination. Some forms of assistance are ethically strongest when they do not create a new dependency relationship.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 76:8–9.

### Supporting E3 Units

**188, 317, 396**, with related anti-performative material **025, 054, 199, 406**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- aid without return;
- no demand for gratitude;
- no reputational extraction;
- recipient agency.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Reciprocal exchange remains legitimate in other contexts. This principle governs aid whose purpose is relief rather than trade.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong parallels exist in Christianity, Buddhism, Hinduism, Sikhism, and several charity traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N033 — Positive Duty Toward Severe Need

## Normalised Principle

**Do not treat extreme vulnerability as somebody else's problem; provide practical assistance where basic welfare, freedom, or survival is at serious risk.**

### Operational Meaning

Non-harm includes positive duties under severe need. Ethical adequacy is partly tested by whether vulnerable people receive practical support.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 74:42–44; 89:17–20; 90:12–16; 107:1–3.

### Supporting E3 Units

**183, 190, 191, 194, 198, 312, 316, 320, 321, 325, 398, 399, 405**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- feed the hungry;
- protect orphans;
- liberation from bondage;
- practical help;
- failure by omission;
- emergency provision.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This principle is strongest under severe vulnerability. It does not imply that every unmet preference generates an unlimited claim on others.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents occur across all or nearly all comparison traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain as foundational positive-duty principle.**

# E3-N034 — Structured Social Provision

## Normalised Principle

**Build reliable mechanisms for directing resources toward recognised categories of need rather than relying exclusively on spontaneous individual charity.**

### Operational Meaning

Persistent vulnerability requires institutional as well as personal response. Provision can be structured, predictable, and accountable.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 9:60; 70:24–25; related 59:7.

### Supporting E3 Units

**097, 184, 303, 313, 393**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- defined social-support allocation;
- recognised claims on wealth;
- distribution beyond ad hoc charity;
- resource circulation.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This principle does not specify a single tax or welfare architecture. It extracts the functional idea that recurrent need can justify structured provision.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Institutional forms vary substantially, but organised support exists widely.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N035 — Mutual Aid Under Scarcity

## Normalised Principle

**Scarcity does not eliminate all responsibility to others; communities should preserve reciprocal support and, where possible, generosity even under constraint.**

### Operational Meaning

Ethical systems should remain functional when resources are scarce. Mutual aid increases resilience and prevents scarcity from collapsing immediately into pure competition.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 59:9; 90:12–17.

### Supporting E3 Units

**173, 191, 192, 304, 321, 384, 399, 400**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- prefer others despite need;
- share under scarcity;
- emergency mutual aid;
- patience and compassion under pressure.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The principle does not require universal self-destruction for others. Capacity and sustainability remain relevant.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong mutual-aid analogues occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N036 — Human Dignity Beyond Status

## Normalised Principle

**Do not treat wealth, ancestry, social rank, group identity, or institutional prestige as proof of greater inherent human worth.**

### Operational Meaning

Status may describe role or circumstance but should not become a licence for degradation or exclusion.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 17:70; 49:13; related 18:28 and 6:52.

### Supporting E3 Units

**080, 124, 125, 167, 219, 258, 259, 297, 318, 329, 378, 414**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- broad human dignity;
- anti-status exclusion;
- ancestry does not determine worth;
- wealth does not establish superiority;
- low-status persons remain legitimate participants.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Equal dignity does not imply identical role, expertise, or responsibility. Functional differentiation must not become ontological hierarchy.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong but differently formulated equivalents occur across most traditions; some traditions contain internal hierarchical tensions.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N037 — No Ridicule, Defamation, or Degrading Labels

## Normalised Principle

**Do not create social dominance by mocking, defaming, humiliating, or fixing people into degrading labels.**

### Operational Meaning

Everyday speech can create durable status injury even without formal coercion. Dignity therefore requires speech-level safeguards.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 49:11; 68:10–13; 104:1.

### Supporting E3 Units

**165, 182, 197, 295, 311, 324, 376, 391, 404**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- no ridicule;
- no degrading nicknames;
- no slander;
- no status humiliation;
- no contemptuous labelling.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Legitimate criticism of conduct remains permissible. The target is degradation of persons rather than evidence-based critique.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N038 — Privacy and Freedom From Intrusive Surveillance

## Normalised Principle

**Respect legitimate private space, information, and boundaries; suspicion alone does not justify intrusion or surveillance.**

### Operational Meaning

Privacy protects agency, dignity, and trust. Information acquisition can itself be harmful even where no physical property is taken.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 24:27–29; 24:58–59; 49:12.

### Supporting E3 Units

**136, 139, 166, 272, 275, 296, 377**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- permission before entry;
- household privacy;
- no spying;
- maturity-sensitive privacy;
- limits on suspicion-driven intrusion.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Privacy is not absolute where strong evidence and legitimate safeguarding duties justify proportionate investigation. The principle rejects arbitrary intrusion.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Privacy concepts differ in form but functional equivalents are widespread.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N039 — Protection of Reputation

## Normalised Principle

**Do not damage another person's reputation through unsupported accusation, malicious gossip, or irresponsible amplification of scandal.**

### Operational Meaning

Reputation is socially consequential. Information systems should therefore constrain both fabrication and reckless transmission.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 24:4; 24:11–16; 49:12.

### Supporting E3 Units

**133, 134, 153, 166, 267, 269, 287, 296, 377**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- serious accusations require evidence;
- no backbiting;
- no scandal amplification;
- no false attribution;
- reputational non-harm.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Protection of reputation must not become suppression of truthful reporting about genuine wrongdoing. Evidence-based accountability remains legitimate.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong speech/reputation protections occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain separately from privacy because truthful public accountability and private intrusion raise different questions.**

# E3-N040 — Inclusion Against Elite Capture

## Normalised Principle

**Do not exclude sincere or legitimate low-status participants merely to gain approval, comfort, or support from higher-status actors.**

### Operational Meaning

Participation systems are vulnerable to capture when leaders optimise for prestige. Inclusion rules should track conduct and legitimate qualification rather than social embarrassment.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 6:52; 18:28; 80:1–10.

### Supporting E3 Units

**125, 219, 259, 318, 329, 414**, with related assembly-access material **172, 302, 383**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- do not drive away low-status participants;
- do not court elites by neglecting vulnerable seekers;
- make room in shared spaces;
- anti-status capture.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This does not prohibit legitimate competence requirements. It prohibits status-based exclusion unrelated to the actual purpose of participation.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong parallels occur in several traditions, especially anti-caste, anti-partiality, and hospitality ethics.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# Interim Normalisation Findings: E3-N031–N040

The social-support and dignity cluster shows a recurring Qur'anic distinction between merely possessing a benevolent intention and constructing conditions under which vulnerable persons retain agency and dignity.

The resulting principles distinguish:

- generosity;
- non-transactional aid;
- positive duty under severe need;
- structured provision;
- mutual aid under scarcity;
- dignity;
- protection from humiliation;
- privacy;
- reputation;
- and anti-status inclusion.
