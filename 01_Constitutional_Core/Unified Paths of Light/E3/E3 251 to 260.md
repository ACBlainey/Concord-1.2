# E3 Unit 251 — Qur'an 17:31
## Economic Fear Does Not Justify Killing Dependants

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 17:31 prohibits killing children from fear of poverty.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Vulnerable dependants must not bear the ultimate cost of economic anxiety.
- Anticipated scarcity does not justify lethal harm.
- Fear of future burden is not equivalent to actual necessity.
- Ethical systems should protect low-power persons when resources appear threatened.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity / biblical tradition | Protection of children and prohibition of murder provide direct functional convergence. | A/B | Do not kill dependants because they impose costs. |
| Judaism | Murder is prohibited; children are not disposable economic burdens. | A/B | Economic pressure does not erase protection of life. |
| Buddhism — First Precept | Taking life is prohibited. | A | Scarcity does not justify killing. |
| Hinduism — ahimsa | Non-harm strongly converges. | A | Protect vulnerable life. |
| Sikhism | Protection of life and vulnerable persons strongly converges. | B | Do not sacrifice dependants to material fear. |
| Confucianism | Family duties strongly oppose abandonment of children. | B | Dependency creates responsibility. |
| African Traditional | Child-protection norms vary historically and culturally. | C | Economic strain should not erase duties to dependants. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 085 while preserving independent source occurrence.**

# E3 Unit 252 — Qur'an 17:33
## Protect Life and Restrain Retaliation After Wrongful Killing

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 17:33 prohibits wrongful killing and, where a killing has occurred, warns the victim's representative not to exceed limits in retaliation.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Human life receives strong protection.
- Serious victimisation does not justify unlimited revenge.
- Justice mechanisms should constrain retaliatory escalation.
- Victim rights and proportionality can coexist.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — murder prohibition / non-retaliation teachings | Life protection is direct; retaliation is further restrained. | A | Protect life and limit vengeance. |
| Judaism — murder prohibition / lex talionis | Both life protection and proportionality are explicit. | A | Justice must not become excessive revenge. |
| Buddhism — First Precept | Killing is prohibited; retaliation conflicts with non-harm. | A | Do not answer killing with uncontrolled killing. |
| Hinduism — ahimsa / legal proportionality | Strong functional convergence. | A/B | Protect life and constrain punishment. |
| Sikhism | Defensive force is bounded by justice. | B | Do not turn justice into vengeance. |
| Confucianism | Punishment should be ordered rather than personally vindictive. | B | Channel response through legitimate restraint. |
| African Traditional | Compensation/restorative mechanisms vary widely. | B/C | Prevent feud escalation after lethal harm. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge with life-and-proportionality cluster.**

# E3 Unit 253 — Qur'an 17:34
## Protect Orphan Property and Fulfil Commitments

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 17:34 prohibits approaching orphan property except for its benefit until maturity and commands fulfilment of commitments.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Guardianship is stewardship, not ownership.
- Vulnerability increases fiduciary responsibility.
- Property controlled for another should be managed for that person's benefit.
- Commitments create accountability.

| Sikhism | Honest dealing and care for vulnerable persons converge. | B | Do not convert stewardship into appropriation. |
| Confucianism | Trustworthiness and guardianship duties support convergence. | B | Roles carrying trust create obligations. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Units 043/046/069 while preserving this compound source occurrence.**

# E3 Unit 255 — Qur'an 17:36
## Do Not Assert or Pursue What You Do Not Know

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 17:36 warns against following or asserting matters without knowledge and links perception and judgement to accountability.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Claims should be proportionate to evidence.
- Do not convert uncertainty into confident assertion.
- Information acquisition and interpretation carry responsibility.
- Epistemic discipline is an ethical obligation when beliefs affect action.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Proverbs 18:13 / James 1:19 | Judgement before adequate hearing is criticised. | A/B | Know before concluding. |
| Judaism — Deuteronomy 19:15 | Serious claims require adequate evidence. | A/B | Evidence should precede judgement. |
| Buddhism — Kalama Sutta | Claims should be examined rather than accepted uncritically. | A/B | Do not assent without adequate grounds. |
| Hinduism — pramana traditions | Valid knowledge requires legitimate means of knowing. | A/B | Match belief to evidence. |
| Sikhism | Truthfulness and discernment provide functional convergence. | B | Do not present ignorance as knowledge. |
| Confucianism — Analects 2:17 | Knowing the limits of one's knowledge is itself knowledge. | A | Admit what you do not know. |
| African Traditional | Epistemic authority is tradition-specific. | C | Claims should follow recognised grounds of knowledge. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is one of the clearest Qur'anic epistemic-responsibility principles.

# E3 Unit 256 — Qur'an 17:37

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 17:37 prohibits walking the earth arrogantly and reminds the person of the limits of human physical power.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Do not inflate status beyond actual capacity.
- Human limitation should constrain arrogance.
- Social presence need not become domination.
- Humility is partly accurate self-assessment.

### Comparative Analysis

| Hinduism — Bhagavad Gita 13:7–8 | Humility and absence of pretence are praised. | A | Cultivate humility. |
| Sikhism — rejection of haumai | Ego is a major obstacle. | A | Resist self-exaltation. |
| Confucianism — modesty | The cultivated person avoids boastfulness. | A/B | Let conduct rather than arrogance establish worth. |
| African Traditional | Humility norms vary but boastfulness is often socially regulated. | B | Do not elevate self above community without cause. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge with humility cluster.**

# E3 Unit 257 — Qur'an 17:53
## Use the Best Available Speech to Prevent Discord

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 17:53 directs people to say what is best because destructive forces can exploit hostile speech to create conflict.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Speech choices can alter conflict trajectories.
- Where several truthful formulations are available, choose the one least likely to create needless hostility.
- Communication should be designed for constructive effect, not merely speaker satisfaction.
- Small verbal escalations can become larger social fractures.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Ephesians 4:29 | Speech should build up rather than corrupt. | A | Choose constructive speech. |
| Judaism — Proverbs 15:1 | A gentle answer can turn away wrath. | A | Use speech to de-escalate. |
| Buddhism — Right Speech | Speech should be truthful, timely, beneficial, and non-divisive. | A | Communicate in ways that reduce harm. |
| Hinduism — Bhagavad Gita 17:15 | Speech should be truthful, beneficial, and non-agitating. | A | Speak truth constructively. |
| Sikhism | Truthful speech is disciplined against ego and hostility. | B | Speak without needlessly inflaming. |
| Confucianism | Proper speech supports harmony and role integrity. | B | Communication should preserve workable relations. |
| African Traditional | Proverbs and mediation traditions often value careful speech, with local variation. | B | Speech can preserve or fracture community. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** It strengthens the project's information-and-conflict architecture.

# E3 Unit 258 — Qur'an 17:70
## Recognise Broad Human Dignity

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 17:70 states that the children of Adam have been honoured and granted capacities and provisions.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Human dignity is broader than membership in a particular faction.
- Baseline worth should not depend solely on status, wealth, ethnicity, or local group.
- Institutions should distinguish universal human standing from role-specific privileges.
- Shared humanity can provide a floor beneath social difference.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Genesis 1:27 | Humanity bears a shared divinely grounded dignity. | A | Human worth precedes social rank. |
| Judaism — Genesis 1:27 | Shared creation grounds broad human significance. | A | Humanity has baseline dignity. |
| Buddhism | Universal sentient concern provides functional but differently grounded convergence. | B | Moral concern should not depend on rank. |
| Hinduism | Universal spiritual identity appears strongly in some traditions. | B | Deep worth can transcend social distinction. |
| Sikhism — human equality | Equal spiritual worth is strongly emphasised. | A | Reject status-based human inferiority. |
| Confucianism | Shared moral potential provides partial convergence. | B | Human beings possess cultivable moral standing. |
| African Traditional | Personhood and dignity are often relationally grounded, with major variation. | B/C | Social status should not erase fundamental personhood. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a significant dignity source for later comparison with substrate-neutral Concord ethics, without presuming equivalence.

# E3 Unit 259 — Qur'an 18:28
## Do Not Abandon Sincere Low-Status People for Elite Attraction

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 18:28 instructs perseverance with sincere companions and warns against turning away from them in pursuit of worldly display.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Do not redesign community solely to attract wealth or prestige.
- Status incentives can corrupt inclusion.
- Sincere low-status participants should not be discarded for elite approval.
- Institutional attention is itself a distributable resource susceptible to capture.

# E3 Unit 260 — Qur'an 18:66–70
## Learning Requires Epistemic Humility and Agreed Process

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 18:66–70 depicts Moses asking to learn from one with knowledge he lacks and agreeing to conditions for the learning process.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Expertise can be domain-specific; high status in one domain does not imply complete knowledge.
- Learning begins with recognition of one's epistemic limits.
- Teacher–learner relationships can legitimately establish process rules.
- Questions are valuable, but timing and context can matter to understanding.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Disciple-teacher relationships and humility provide functional parallels. | B | Learning requires willingness to receive instruction. |
| Judaism | Rabbinic learning strongly values questioning within disciplined study. | B | Learning combines humility and inquiry. |
| Buddhism — teacher/student traditions | Guidance requires receptivity and disciplined practice. | B | Recognise limits and learn through process. |
| Hinduism — guru-shishya traditions | Student humility and structured instruction are prominent. | A/B | Expertise should be approached with disciplined openness. |
| Sikhism | Guru-centred learning strongly emphasises humility. | A/B | Ego obstructs learning. |
| Confucianism — Analects | The learner should recognise ignorance and learn from others. | A | Epistemic humility enables learning. |
| African Traditional | Apprenticeship/elder teaching structures vary widely. | B/C | Knowledge transmission often requires recognised process. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain cautiously.** The narrative supports a generalisable epistemic-humility principle without requiring the later events of the story to be universalised.
