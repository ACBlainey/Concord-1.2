# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey

---

## 1. Introduction

Paper I established E3 as a directional mirror of the original *Unified Paths of Light / Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity* experiment.

This paper defines the operational framework required before detailed extraction begins.

The methodological problem is different from simply compiling well-known Islamic moral teachings. A selective list assembled from familiar verses would introduce curator bias and weaken comparison with the original Psalms study.

E3 therefore requires a reproducible answer to four questions:

1. What constitutes the Qur'anic anchor corpus?
2. What qualifies as an ethical source unit?
3. Which seven traditions form the comparison set?
4. How will equivalent teachings be identified and graded?

---

## 2. Anchor Corpus: The Qur'an

The anchor corpus is the complete Qur'an.

The extraction process is not restricted to a predetermined list of famous ethical passages. All surahs are within scope, and passages are considered according to whether they contain extractable behavioural ethical content.

Every extracted source unit will retain the surah name, surah number, verse number or verse range, and sequential E3 source-unit number.

The sequential E3 number is an organisational device only. The canonical Qur'anic reference remains authoritative for locating the source.

---

## 3. Translation and Source Integrity

Translation creates unavoidable interpretive risk.

The project therefore distinguishes between the Qur'anic reference, the wording of the translation consulted, and the project's own reduced ethical core.

The reduced ethical core must never be presented as though it were the Qur'anic text itself.

Where translation differences materially affect ethical interpretation, the issue should be noted rather than silently resolved.

For the detailed comparison corpus, quotations should remain limited to the amount required to establish the relevant teaching, with precise references retained so readers can consult complete translations independently.

---

## 4. Ethical Inclusion Criteria

A Qur'anic passage qualifies for extraction where it contains a reasonably identifiable prescription, prohibition, virtue, vice, duty, responsibility, or behavioural judgement with ethical significance.

| Category | Examples of Functional Content |
|----------|--------------------------------|
| **Non-harm** | restraints upon violence, cruelty, oppression, or abuse |
| **Justice** | impartiality, fair judgement, proportionality, protection from wrongdoing |
| **Truth** | honesty, truthful testimony, avoidance of deception or slander |
| **Property** | theft, fraud, exploitation, inheritance, fair exchange |
| **Care** | duties toward poor, orphaned, dependent, displaced, or vulnerable persons |
| **Family / interpersonal** | duties between relatives, spouses, parents, children, neighbours, and community members |
| **Promises / trust** | covenants, contracts, entrusted property, keeping commitments |
| **Character** | humility, patience, gratitude, courage, restraint, generosity |
| **Conflict ethics** | limits on retaliation, reconciliation, treatment of opponents, restraint in conflict |
| **Governance / civic ethics** | consultation, authority, public responsibility, judgement |
| **Knowledge / reasoning** | responsible judgement, avoidance of unsupported accusation, reflection where behaviourally relevant |

This list is descriptive rather than exhaustive.

---

## 5. Exclusion and Separation Rules

The original project excluded ritual, cosmological, and narrative material when no separable behavioural ethic was present. E3 retains that rule.

Normally excluded as stand-alone ethical units are descriptions of creation or cosmology; eschatological description without a behavioural prescription; historical narrative without a generalisable ethical lesson; statements whose content is exclusively theological; and ritual detail whose only function is ritual performance.

A passage containing both theological or ritual material and behavioural ethical instruction may still qualify. Only the separable ethical content is normalised for comparison.

This is a methodological abstraction for cross-traditional analysis; it is not a claim that the ethical, theological, legal, and ritual dimensions of the Qur'an are intrinsically separable within Islam.

---

## 6. Extraction Procedure

Each candidate source unit passes through the following sequence.

### Step 1 — Identify the Source Unit

Locate a verse or short contiguous passage containing an ethical teaching.

### Step 2 — Preserve Context

Record enough surrounding context to avoid converting a context-specific statement into a misleading universal rule.

### Step 3 — Extract Distinct Principles

A single source unit may contain several ethical teachings. Each distinct principle is identified separately.

### Step 4 — Reduce to Functional Essence

Express each principle in plain English without requiring acceptance of a theological premise.

### Step 5 — Determine Actionability

Give priority to teachings that prescribe, prohibit, encourage, or discourage behaviour.

### Step 6 — Compare Across the Seven Traditions

Search for the closest defensible ethical equivalent in each comparison tradition.

### Step 7 — Grade the Equivalent

Classify it as direct textual, strong functional, inferred/corollary, or no clear equivalent.

### Step 8 — Preserve Difference

Where the comparison fails, record the absence rather than forcing equivalence.

---

## 8. Comparison Source Principle

Where possible, comparison evidence should come from foundational, canonical, or historically authoritative ethical sources associated with each tradition.

The project should prefer identifiable textual evidence over broad summaries.

Where a tradition is internally diverse or principally oral, the comparison must state the source tradition or provenance as precisely as practicable.

A generic assertion such as "African religion teaches..." is weaker evidence than an identified proverb, community tradition, or documented ethical formulation.

Similarly, a secondary summary should not be presented as though it were a quotation from a canonical source.

---

## 9. Functional Equivalence Test

Two teachings qualify as ethical equivalents when their **behavioural instruction or practical moral function** substantially overlaps, even if their theological rationale differs.

The judgement asks:

1. Are they addressing substantially the same kind of conduct?
2. Do they encourage or prohibit substantially the same behaviour?
3. Would following them produce substantially similar interpersonal or social effects?
4. Is the equivalence explicit, strongly functional, or merely inferential?

| Grade | Test |
|-------|------|
| **A — Direct textual equivalent** | Both sources explicitly state substantially the same ethical rule |
| **B — Strong functional equivalent** | Different formulation, same practical behavioural instruction |
| **C — Inferred / corollary equivalent** | Similarity depends on a defensible implication |
| **D — No clear equivalent identified** | Available evidence is insufficient for a responsible equivalence claim |

These grades measure the **strength of the comparison**, not the ethical importance of the teaching.

## 11. File Structure

The original detailed Psalms corpus was stored in groups of fifty psalms, producing very large Markdown files.

E3 will use smaller working files.

The default division is:

> **10 Qur'anic ethical source units per file**

Example filenames are **E3 001 to 010.md**, **E3 011 to 020.md**, and **E3 021 to 030.md**.

This division is based on sequential extraction units rather than Qur'anic verse numbers.

If a source unit spans several adjacent verses, it remains one unit.

The smaller files improve GitHub handling, reviewability, correction, provenance tracking, incremental development, and future machine analysis.

---

## 12. Corpus-Freezing Procedure

Before final synthesis, E3 will maintain a master source index containing every accepted Qur'anic ethical source unit.

The purpose is to prevent later comparison results from silently changing the extraction population.

Candidate passages may be added, removed, split, or merged during the initial extraction review, but such changes should occur **before the corpus is treated as frozen for synthesis**.

Once frozen:

> **Cross-traditional convergence must not determine whether a Qur'anic ethical teaching remains in the dataset.**

A teaching with few parallels is data, not an error.

---

## 13. Avoiding Duplicate Inflation

The Qur'an may express closely related ethical principles in multiple locations.

Every relevant source may be preserved, but repeated teachings must not automatically be counted as separate distinct ethical principles during final synthesis.

E3 therefore distinguishes:

- **source frequency** — how often a principle appears in the Qur'anic extraction;
- **principle identity** — whether repeated sources instantiate the same normalised ethic;
- **cross-traditional prevalence** — how many comparison traditions contain an equivalent.

This prevents a frequently repeated principle in the anchor corpus from being mistaken for a more universally shared principle across traditions.

---

## 14. Comparison with the Original Psalms Result

The original Psalm-derived synthesis will not be used to decide the E3 ethical categories during extraction.

Only after the Qur'an-anchored synthesis has been generated should the two outputs be directly compared.

That later comparison can examine principles independently recovered by both anchor methods; principles emphasised more strongly by one anchor; principles unique to one extraction; differences caused by source genre, theology, law, historical context, or textual structure; and the degree to which both routes converge upon the existing Concord Ethical Kernel.

This creates a form of **anchor-robustness test** for the original comparative method.

---

## 15. Summary

E3 uses the complete Qur'an as its anchor corpus and preserves the seven non-Islamic comparison traditions of the original study.

Ethical source units are selected on Qur'anic grounds before comparative synthesis. Each is reduced to its functional behavioural core and compared against identifiable teachings from the other traditions.

Equivalence is graded rather than assumed, non-convergence is preserved, duplicate principles are controlled during synthesis, and the original Psalm-derived result is withheld as a derivational template.

The resulting corpus should therefore be capable of answering the central E3 question:

> **When the same comparative method begins from the Qur'an, what ethical structure emerges?**
