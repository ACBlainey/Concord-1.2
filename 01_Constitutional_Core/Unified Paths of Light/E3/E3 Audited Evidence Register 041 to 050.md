# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Audited Comparative Evidence Register — Q041–Q050

**Status:** Working row-level register.

---

## Q041 — Coexistence Without Forced Sameness

Confucian Analects 13.23 supplies unusually direct evidence for harmony without uniformity. Other traditions support plural coexistence to varying degrees, but not always as a generalised principle.

**Current result:** **Common / Near-universal.**

## Q042 — Respectful Disagreement

Respectful speech is widespread. A positive norm for sustaining disagreement without coercive homogenisation is more specific.

**Current result:** **Near-universal / Common.**

## Q043 — Family Respect With Preserved Moral Agency

Family respect and filial duties are exceptionally broad. E3 adds the constraint that respect does not extinguish independent moral judgement.

**Current result:** **Near-universal functional.**

## Q044 — Child and Dependant Welfare

Protection, nourishment, education and care of children/dependants recur very widely across the traditions.

**Current result:** **Strong Universal candidate at functional level.**

## Q045 — Consent and Non-Coercion in Intimate Relationships

Anti-coercion and protections against exploitation exist widely, but historical relationship norms differ substantially and should not be translated uncritically into a fully modern consent doctrine.

**Current result:** **Common / Near-universal functional anti-coercion.**

## Q046 — Sexual Non-Exploitation and Bodily Boundaries

Sexual restraint and prohibitions on misconduct are widespread. Buddhist sexual-misconduct precepts and many other traditions supply strong parent evidence. Exact bodily-boundary formulations differ.

**Current result:** **Near-universal functional anti-exploitation.**

## Q047 — Fairness in Relationship Dissolution

Traditions frequently regulate separation, divorce or dissolution, but rules and protected interests differ substantially.

**Current result:** **Common.**

## Q048 — Reciprocal Relational Rights and Responsibilities

Reciprocal obligations within relationships are strongly recurrent. Confucian reciprocity and African duty-oriented ethics provide useful anchors, while “rights” terminology may be anachronistic for some sources.

**Current result:** **Near-universal exact; reciprocal relational obligation may be stronger.**

## Q049 — Consultation in Shared Affairs

Consultation, counsel and deliberation recur widely, but not all traditions make shared consultation a general governance rule.

**Current result:** **Near-universal / Common boundary.**

## Q050 — Competence and Trustworthiness in Entrusted Roles

Trustworthiness is extremely broad. Explicit competence requirements for office/entrusted roles are less uniformly stated.

**Current result:** **Near-universal compound; parent trustworthiness stronger.**

---

## Register 005 Finding

Q041–Q050 show a consistent **agency-preservation layer**:

- coexist without forced sameness;
- disagree respectfully;
- preserve moral agency within family;
- protect dependants;
- prohibit coercion;
- constrain sexual exploitation;
- regulate dissolution;
- preserve reciprocity;
- consult affected participants;
- and require trustworthy competence in entrusted roles.

This is not reducible to non-harm. It concerns whether persons remain **participants in decisions that structure their lives**.

> **End of Audited Comparative Evidence Register Q041–Q050**
