# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Audited Comparative Evidence Register — Q081–Q089

**Status:** Working row-level register; completes first traversal of E3-Q001–E3-Q089.

---

## Q081 — Do Not Exploit Addiction or Impaired Judgement

Sobriety and restraint around intoxicants are strongly represented in several traditions, including the Buddhist precept against intoxicants leading to heedlessness. E3-Q081 is a second-order rule: it prohibits exploiting another person's reduced agency.

**Current result:** **Common.** Do not infer exploitation protection merely from self-directed sobriety rules.

## Q082 — Repair Harm Where Possible

Restitution, repentance, restoration, reconciliation and restorative justice recur broadly.

**Current result:** **Strong Near-universal; possible Universal broad restorative parent.**

## Q083 — Distinguish Accident From Deliberate Harm

Many traditions distinguish intention, negligence and deliberate wrongdoing, but documentary strength is uneven across the seven comparison families.

**Current result:** **Common / Near-universal.** Earlier provisional Universal treatment should not survive without direct culpability/intention evidence.

## Q084 — Do Not Passively Legitimate Degrading Wrongdoing

Non-complicity and resistance to wrongdoing are widespread. The exact duty not to normalise degrading conduct through passive participation is a more specific second-order rule.

**Current result:** **Near-universal / Common.**

## Q085 — Hospitality Has Boundaries

Hospitality is widely valued. Traditions differ on the conditions under which hospitality may be refused, withdrawn or constrained to prevent harm.

**Current result:** **Common / Near-universal.** Parent hospitality is much stronger than the boundary rule.

## Q086 — Right of Refusal Must Be Respected

Consent, non-coercion and voluntary participation have important analogues, but a general right of refusal is not uniformly explicit in the comparison traditions.

**Current result:** **Common.**

## Q087 — Developmental Agency Should Expand With Capability

Traditions frequently distinguish children, learners, novices, dependants and mature agents, but E3's general rule of progressively expanding agency with demonstrated capability is a systems abstraction.

**Current result:** **Common.**

## Q088 — Do Not Covet Another's Advantages Into Injustice

Non-covetousness, non-envy and restraint against wrongful acquisition are exceptionally broad. Biblical prohibition, Bhagavad Gita 16.2 and comparable traditions provide strong anchors.

**Current result:** **Strong Universal candidate.**

## Q089 — Institutional Trust Is a Shared Resource

Confucian Analects 12.7 gives unusually direct political evidence for the indispensability of public trust. Other traditions strongly support trustworthiness, faithful authority and social confidence, but fewer formulate institutional trust itself as the protected object.

**Current result:** **Cross-traditional systems synthesis; not presently secure as direct 8/8 textual universality.**

## First-Traversal Closure Finding

The full frozen Qur'an-derived catalogue, E3-Q001–E3-Q089, has now been carried through a source-quality/prevalence review.

This traversal does **not** constitute the final numerical prevalence result. It establishes the decisions and cautions needed to construct that result without inflating broad parent ethics into operational-child matches.

Three recurrent layers are visible:

1. **Deep ethical attractors** — truth, life protection, justice, care, generosity, restraint, trust, non-complicity.
2. **Widely recurring elaborations** — forgiveness, patience, anger control, moderation, restorative repair, constrained authority, reciprocity and resilience.
3. **Operational / systems safeguards** — verification, procedure, privacy, consent mechanics, capacity scaling, anti-overconstraint, threat classification, safe passage, public-purpose allocation, impaired-agency protection, refusal and developmental agency.
