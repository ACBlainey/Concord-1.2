# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Universal / Near-Universal Boundary Evidence Matrix 003

**Principles:** Q034, Q036, Q038, Q042, Q046.

---

## Q034 — Mutual Aid Under Scarcity

Positive aid duties are broadly recurrent. The scarcity condition is a stronger test: giving when resources are constrained is explicitly valorised in some traditions but not necessarily isolated in every tradition.

African communitarian ethics strongly supports mutual responsibility and need-based duty, including the idea that concern for others is not merely optional.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at exact scarcity formulation; strong universal parent cluster of mutual aid.**

## Q036 — No Ridicule, Defamation, or Degrading Labels

False accusation, slander and harmful speech have broad support across canonical traditions. Ridicule and degrading labels are more specific subtypes. Some traditions provide direct speech ethics, while others ground the result in dignity, propriety or non-harm.

**Decision:** **Near-universal as the compound principle; anti-defamation/malicious-speech parent is a strong Universal candidate.**

## Q038 — Protection of Reputation

Truthfulness, false-witness prohibitions, malicious-speech restrictions and honour/dignity norms provide broad support. However, “reputation” can be culturally structured very differently, and legitimate criticism must remain distinguishable from wrongful reputational harm.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at functional level.**

## Q042 — Respectful Disagreement

Non-harmful speech, truthfulness, reciprocity and propriety support respectful disagreement across traditions. Confucian sources are especially strong on propriety and relational respect; Buddhist right speech provides a clear behavioural route. The existence of respectful speech ethics does not automatically prove a general norm of pluralistic disagreement.

**Decision:** **Near-universal, not secure Universal at the exact pluralistic formulation.**

## Q046 — Sexual Non-Exploitation and Bodily Boundaries

Most traditions regulate sexual conduct and condemn at least some forms of coercive or exploitative sexuality. But the modern normalised formulation “bodily boundaries” cannot be projected backwards as if every tradition articulated contemporary consent doctrine.

The correct comparison is functional: prevention of sexual exploitation, coercion and wrongful sexual conduct.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at functional anti-exploitation level; do not claim textual universality of the modern bodily-boundary formulation.**

## Matrix 003 Finding

This tranche reinforces the need for **functional equivalence without historical anachronism**.

The project should neither:

- demand modern terminology from ancient sources,

nor:

- translate every ancient restriction into a modern right that it did not actually formulate.

The proper question remains whether the source independently constrains the same class of harmful conduct.
