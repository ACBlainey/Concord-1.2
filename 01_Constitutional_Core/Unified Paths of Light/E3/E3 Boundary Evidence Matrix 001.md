# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Universal / Near-Universal Boundary Evidence Matrix 001

**Principles:** Q004, Q005, Q007, Q014, Q018.

---

## Purpose

These principles were not frozen as certain universals. Their catalogue labels already recognised a Universal/Near-universal boundary. The audit therefore asks which side of that boundary the evidence presently supports.

## Q004 — Reliable Testimony and Evidence

| Tradition | Audit finding |
|---|---|
| Christianity | Strong direct tradition of truthful testimony and prohibition of false witness. |
| Judaism | Strong direct legal/textual evidence concerning witnesses and false testimony. |
| Buddhism | Truthful speech is strong; formal evidentiary procedure is a narrower claim and should not be inferred automatically. |
| Hinduism | Truth and witness duties exist in legal/ethical traditions, but exact source must be fixed. |
| Sikhism | Truthful living is strong; formal testimony/evidence equivalence requires more precise sourcing. |
| Confucian | Trustworthiness and sincerity are strong; formal evidentiary procedure is not equally direct. |
| African traditions | Truthfulness can be sourced specifically—for example Bukusu sage-philosophy material explicitly condemns falsehood—but formal evidence/testimony procedure is not established across the category. |

**Decision:** **Near-universal at the exact formulation; universal only at the broader truthful-speech parent level.**

## Q005 — Accurate Attribution of Responsibility

Christian, Jewish, Buddhist and several South Asian traditions possess strong responsibility/intention structures. Confucian ethics clearly evaluates character and conduct, while African relational ethics strongly addresses responsibility to others. The exact rule against transferring blame to an innocent person is nevertheless more specific than generic accountability.

**Decision:** **Near-universal / strong common. Do not elevate to secure Universal without direct attribution evidence for every tradition.**

## Q007 — Entrusted Responsibility

Promise, trust and role-duty structures are widespread. Confucian ethics is especially strong on role responsibility and trustworthiness; African communitarian ethics explicitly treats obligations and responsibilities to others as central. Yet “entrustment” as a distinct fiduciary category is not identically formulated everywhere.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at functional level.**

## Q014 — Peace and De-Escalation

Non-retaliation, harmony, compassion, reconciliation and restraint recur very broadly. African philosophical documentation includes explicit work on Yoruba conceptions of peace and extensive Ubuntu reconciliation/restorative-justice literature. Traditions differ, however, over legitimate force and the scope of pacific restraint.

**Decision:** **Near-universal with exceptionally broad support; do not collapse differences over force into an artificial 8/8 direct match.**

## Q018 — Accountability With Possibility of Reform

Repentance, forgiveness, rehabilitation, moral cultivation and restoration are widespread but differently structured. Ubuntu restorative-justice scholarship gives particularly strong African evidence for reintegration and restoration; Buddhist, Confucian and Hindu traditions often frame change through cultivation rather than juridical rehabilitation.

**Decision:** **Near-universal at functional level; universal parent cluster of moral corrigibility is plausible but the compound formulation remains too specific for secure 8/8.**

---

## Matrix 001 Finding

The boundary audit confirms a recurring rule:

> **A universal parent ethic must not automatically make every operational child principle universal.**

Truthfulness does not by itself prove formal evidence procedure. Accountability does not automatically prove precise blame attribution. Peace does not erase different rules concerning justified force.
