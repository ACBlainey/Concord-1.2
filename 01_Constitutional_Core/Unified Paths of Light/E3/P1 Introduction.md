# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3

## Qur'anic Comparative Ethical Extraction

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey  
**Date:** 2026  
**Paper:** I of V

---

## 1. Introduction

The original *Unified Paths of Light / Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity* project examined ethical convergence across eight of the world's large and enduring religious and philosophical traditions.

Its systematic extraction phase began with the 150 Psalms. Ethical principles were identified in the Psalter, reduced to non-theological plain-English behavioural statements, and compared with functional equivalents in Judaism, Buddhism, Hinduism, Islam, Sikhism, Confucianism, and African Traditional Religions.

E3 repeats that comparative process while changing the anchor corpus.

The **Qur'an** now becomes the source from which the initial ethical principles are independently extracted. Christianity and the biblical tradition move to the comparative side of the analysis alongside the remaining traditions.

The purpose is not to demonstrate that the Qur'an agrees with conclusions already reached. It is to determine what happens when the same method begins from a different sacred text.

---

## 2. Central Hypothesis

The broader hypothesis remains that of the parent project:

> **Religions that have sustained large populations over long historical periods contain core ethical principles that function as durable social heuristics for cooperation, stability, responsibility, and civilizational resilience.**

E3 introduces a second, more specific test:

> **If ethical convergence is genuinely cross-traditional rather than an artefact of the original anchor corpus, changing the anchor from the Psalms to the Qur'an should independently recover a substantially overlapping ethical structure while also revealing genuine differences in emphasis.**

The degree of convergence is not predetermined.

---

## 3. Methodological Approach

The project again strips ethical teachings to their **functional essence**.

A Qur'anic passage may contain theological claims, historical references, ritual requirements, legal context, moral instruction, and behavioural prescriptions simultaneously. E3 does not require these elements to be denied or reinterpreted. It separates the behavioural ethical content for comparative purposes.

| Layer | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|
| **Theological** | Recorded where necessary for context but not evaluated as a truth claim |
| **Narrative / historical** | Not treated as an ethical principle by itself |
| **Ritual** | Normally excluded unless a distinct behavioural ethic is embedded within it |
| **Legal / social** | Included where it expresses a generalisable ethical duty or prohibition |
| **Functional ethical core** | Extracted into plain-English behavioural form |

For example, a passage requiring honesty in testimony may arise within a specifically Islamic theological or legal setting. Its functional ethical core can nevertheless be expressed as:

> **Give truthful testimony and do not distort evidence because of personal interest or hostility.**

That behavioural core can then be compared with teachings concerning truthful witness in the other traditions without requiring theological equivalence.

---

## 4. Ethical Equivalence Through Diverse Expression

The methodological rule of the original project is retained:

> **Exact verbal equivalence is neither expected nor required.**

Different traditions may express a similar behavioural principle as a direct command, prohibition, wisdom teaching, narrative example, virtue, legal duty, warning about consequences, or description of exemplary character.

The comparison asks:

1. What behaviour is prescribed, prohibited, encouraged, or discouraged?
2. What underlying ethical principle does that behaviour instantiate?
3. Does another tradition contain a **functionally equivalent** teaching?

E3 is a map of ethical equivalence, not a search for identical wording.

---

## 5. Evidence Strength

To improve auditability while preserving the original method, E3 records the strength of each claimed cross-traditional equivalent.

| Classification | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| **Direct textual equivalent** | The comparison source explicitly prescribes or prohibits substantially the same behaviour |
| **Strong functional equivalent** | Different wording or framing expresses substantially the same behavioural principle |
| **Inferred / corollary equivalent** | The relationship is reasonable but depends upon an implication rather than an explicit matching instruction |
| **No clear equivalent identified** | The available comparison material does not justify claiming equivalence |

An inferred equivalent must not be presented as though it were direct textual evidence.

## 7. Independence Safeguards

Because the original Psalm-derived ethical catalogue already exists, E3 faces a methodological risk that the first project did not: **prior-result contamination**.

### 7.1 Qur'an-First Extraction

A passage is selected because it contains ethical content in the Qur'an, not because it matches a principle found in the Psalms.

### 7.2 Source List Before Comparative Judgement

The Qur'anic ethical source list is established before the final cross-comparative synthesis.

### 7.3 Preserve Non-Convergence

If an extracted Qur'anic principle has no convincing equivalent in another tradition, this is recorded rather than repaired through increasingly remote analogy.

### 7.4 Preserve Distinctive Emphases

Principles particularly prominent or distinctive within the Qur'an remain visible even if they do not reach universal or majority status.

### 7.5 No Retrofitting to Blainey's Laws

Blainey's Laws and the current Concord Ethical Kernel are downstream comparison targets. They are not extraction categories imposed upon the Qur'an.

---

## 8. Unit of Analysis

The unit of analysis is the **Qur'anic ethical source unit**.

A source unit may be one verse containing a complete ethical instruction or a short sequence of adjacent verses forming one inseparable ethical argument.

This avoids both extremes:

> **Do not artificially fragment a coherent teaching merely to maintain verse-by-verse numbering.**

> **Do not combine unrelated teachings merely because they occur in the same surah.**

Each source unit receives a sequential E3 extraction number while retaining its exact Qur'anic surah-and-verse reference.

---

## 9. Comparison Traditions

To preserve comparability with the original experiment, the eight-tradition frame remains constant while Islam changes from comparison tradition to anchor tradition.

The seven comparison traditions are:

1. Christianity / biblical tradition
2. Judaism
3. Buddhism
4. Hinduism
5. Sikhism
6. Chinese / Confucian tradition
7. African Traditional Religions

The purpose is methodological symmetry.

---

## 10. Ultimate Aim

E3 has two outputs.

The first is an independently derived **Qur'an-anchored comparative ethical catalogue**.

The second is a later comparison between that catalogue and the Psalm-anchored catalogue produced by the original project.

Three broad outcomes are possible: substantial convergence; partial convergence with different emphases; or significant divergence. All three are informative.

The strongest form of the project's hypothesis would receive support if substantially the same behavioural ethical core emerges despite changing the anchor corpus. Divergence would instead identify where the original result depended upon its starting text or where traditions encode materially different ethical priorities.

---

## 11. Summary of Key Concepts

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Anchor inversion** | Qur'an replaces Psalms as the initial extraction corpus |
| **Qur'an-first extraction** | Ethical teachings are identified before comparison with the previous result |
| **Functional ethical core** | The actionable behavioural content of a teaching |
| **Ethical equivalence** | Similar behavioural principles expressed through different cultural or textual forms |
| **Evidence strength** | Direct, strong functional, inferred/corollary, or no clear equivalent |
| **Source unit** | One verse or a short inseparable group of verses containing an ethical teaching |
| **Independent derivation** | The Qur'an-anchored ethical structure is allowed to emerge without being fitted to the Psalm-derived result |

---

## 12. Conclusion

E3 repeats the central comparative experiment of *Unified Paths of Light* from a new direction.

The Qur'an is treated as the anchor corpus. Its ethical teachings will be extracted according to the same functional principles used in the original project and then compared against the other seven traditions.

The central methodological requirement is independence. The existence of the earlier Psalm-derived catalogue makes it possible to perform a stronger test, but only if that catalogue is prevented from determining what E3 extracts.

The project therefore begins with the Qur'an, preserves genuine differences, grades the strength of claimed equivalents, and postpones comparison with the original final ethical core until the Qur'an-anchored synthesis has been completed.

> **The purpose is not to reproduce the first answer. It is to repeat the method and discover whether the answer reproduces itself.**
