# E3 Unit 331 — Qur'an 2:11–12
## Do Not Present Corruption as Reform

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 2:11–12 describes people who are told not to cause corruption yet claim that they are reformers.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Beneficial labels do not establish beneficial effects.
- Reform claims should be tested against actual consequences.
- Actors can misclassify destructive behaviour as improvement.
- Systems need independent feedback rather than relying solely on self-description.
- Good intentions or claimed intentions do not remove outcome accountability.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 7:16–20 | Claims are tested by their fruits. | A | Judge reform by consequences and conduct. |
| Judaism — prophetic critique | Public claims of righteousness can be tested against justice and conduct. | B | Labels do not override effects. |
| Buddhism | Intention matters but conduct and consequences remain morally relevant. | B | Examine whether action actually reduces harm. |
| Hinduism | Dharma is assessed through right action rather than assertion alone. | B | Claimed righteousness requires substantive alignment. |
| Sikhism | Truthful living outranks empty profession. | A/B | Test declared virtue against lived conduct. |
| Confucianism — rectification of names | Names and actual functions should correspond. | A | Do not call corruption reform. |
| African Traditional | Accountability norms vary. | C | Claimed benefit should be tested against communal effects. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is an important anti-self-certification and outcome-verification principle.

# E3 Unit 332 — Qur'an 2:27
## Covenant-Breaking, Severed Bonds, and Corruption Can Form a Single Failure Pattern

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 2:27 links breaking a covenant after commitment, severing relationships that ought to be maintained, and spreading corruption.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Institutional breakdown can arise from interacting failures rather than isolated acts.
- Commitment integrity and social continuity are connected.
- Severing legitimate cooperative bonds can amplify wider disorder.
- Systems should monitor patterns of breach, fragmentation, and destructive consequence together.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Covenant fidelity and reconciliation strongly converge. | B | Broken commitments can fracture community. |
| Judaism | Covenant integrity is foundational. | A | Preserve legitimate commitments and bonds. |
| Buddhism | Right conduct and non-divisive speech oppose fragmentation. | B | Do not generate destructive division. |
| Hinduism | Dharma includes fidelity to legitimate duties and relations. | B | Preserve sustaining obligations. |
| Sikhism | Truthful conduct and communal solidarity provide functional convergence. | B | Betrayal and division undermine community. |
| Confucianism | Trust and relational continuity are central to social order. | A/B | Broken trust damages the relational system. |
| African Traditional | Kinship/covenant structures vary. | B/C | Social bonds can carry stabilising obligations. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with covenant and anti-corruption clusters, preserving this compound source occurrence.**

# E3 Unit 333 — Qur'an 2:42
## Do Not Mix Truth With Falsehood or Conceal What You Know

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 2:42 prohibits clothing truth with falsehood and knowingly concealing truth.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Deception can operate by mixture rather than outright fabrication.
- Selective omission can be morally equivalent to misleading assertion.
- Possessing relevant knowledge can create disclosure duties in appropriate contexts.
- Information integrity requires attention to framing as well as literal truth.
- Technically true statements can still be used deceptively.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Ephesians 4:25 | Falsehood is rejected in favour of truthful speech. | A | Communicate without deceptive distortion. |
| Judaism — Exodus 23:7 | False matters are to be avoided. | A | Do not manipulate truth into deception. |
| Buddhism — Right Speech | False and misleading speech is constrained. | A | Speak truthfully and non-deceptively. |
| Hinduism — satya | Truthfulness is a central discipline. | A | Preserve truth in speech. |
| Sikhism — truthful living | Truth must be embodied rather than manipulated. | A | Do not conceal or distort truth for advantage. |
| Confucianism — xin / rectification of names | Trust depends on correspondence between words and reality. | A/B | Preserve informational integrity. |
| African Traditional | Truth norms vary by tradition. | B/C | Deliberate deception damages trust. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is stronger than a simple prohibition on lying because it includes concealment and deceptive mixture.

# E3 Unit 335 — Qur'an 3:134

- Anger need not determine action.
- Emotional restraint can prevent escalation.
- Forgiveness can be a deliberate exercise of power.
- Generosity and anger regulation can coexist as aspects of stable character.
- Ethical agency includes the interval between emotion and response.

# E3 Unit 336 — Qur'an 4:29
## Exchange Requires Mutual Consent and Must Not Become Destructive Appropriation

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:29 prohibits consuming one another's property wrongfully while permitting trade conducted by mutual consent, alongside a prohibition on destructive harm.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Legitimate exchange requires consent.
- Formal transaction does not legitimise wrongful appropriation.
- Economic and bodily non-harm can sit within one protective framework.
- Market systems should distinguish voluntary exchange from coercive extraction.
- Consent should be substantive enough to separate trade from taking.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Theft is prohibited and fair dealing strongly supported. | A/B | Do not appropriate another's property without legitimate agreement. |
| Judaism | Property and commercial law strongly protect legitimate ownership and exchange. | A | Exchange should be lawful and non-fraudulent. |
| Buddhism — Second Precept | Taking what is not given is prohibited. | A | Consent distinguishes receipt from appropriation. |
| Hinduism — asteya | Non-stealing strongly converges. | A | Do not take without legitimate permission. |
| Sikhism — honest earning | Exploitative acquisition conflicts with honest livelihood. | A/B | Gain should arise through legitimate exchange. |
| Confucianism | Profit is subordinate to righteousness and trustworthy dealing. | B | Commerce should preserve fairness. |
| African Traditional | Property/exchange systems vary. | C | Legitimate transfer generally depends on recognised consent/rule. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 050 while preserving mutual-consent language as a major economic principle.**

# E3 Unit 337 — Qur'an 4:36
## Social Responsibility Extends Beyond Kin to Neighbours, Travellers, and Dependants

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:36 groups duties toward parents and relatives with orphans, poor people, near and distant neighbours, companions, travellers, and persons under one's authority.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Ethical concern should extend beyond immediate family.
- Proximity creates duties but should not monopolise them.
- Travellers and socially disconnected people remain within the moral field.
- Power over dependants increases responsibility.
- Civilisation requires overlapping circles of care rather than a single loyalty boundary.

# E3 Unit 340 — Qur'an 5:32
## Human Life Has Exceptional Moral Weight

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:32, in a passage explicitly framed through a rule prescribed to the Children of Israel, compares wrongful killing of one person to killing humanity and saving one life to saving humanity.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Individual life should not be treated as morally negligible.
- Saving life is a positive ethical achievement.
- Severe violence requires extraordinary justification.
- The value of a person is not reducible to numerical insignificance.
- Source context should be preserved rather than erased in universalisation.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Sanctity-of-life and rescue duties strongly converge. | A/B | Protect human life. |
| Judaism — Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5 and biblical context | Very close textual/interpretive parallel exists in Jewish tradition. | A | One life carries profound moral weight. |
| Buddhism — First Precept | Killing is prohibited. | A | Preserve life and avoid killing. |
| Hinduism — ahimsa | Non-harm strongly protects life. | A | Avoid destructive violence. |
| Sikhism | Human dignity and protection of the vulnerable strongly converge. | A/B | Protect life against wrongful harm. |
| Confucianism | Humane concern opposes arbitrary killing. | B | Human life deserves serious protection. |
| African Traditional | Life-protection norms are widespread but diverse. | B | Wrongful killing gravely damages person and community. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge with Unit 073.** Preserve the passage's stated Israelite legal context in the final source notes.
