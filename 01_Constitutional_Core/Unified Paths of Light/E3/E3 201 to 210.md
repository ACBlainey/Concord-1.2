# E3 Unit 201 — Qur'an 2:11–12
## Do Not Cause Corruption While Claiming to Reform

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 2:11–12 describes people told not to spread corruption who instead claim to be reformers.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Do not disguise destructive conduct as reform merely by naming it reform.**
- **Evaluate interventions by their effects as well as their stated intentions.**
- **Self-description is not sufficient evidence that an action is beneficial.**
- **Reform language can itself become a shield against accountability.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 7:16–20 | Conduct is evaluated by its fruits rather than claims alone. | A/B | Judge reform partly by outcomes. |
| Judaism — prophetic critique | Claimed righteousness does not excuse harmful conduct. | B | Moral labels cannot substitute for justice. |
| Buddhism | Intention matters, but consequences and wholesome/unwholesome effects also matter. | B | Do not mistake self-justification for beneficial action. |
| Hinduism — dharmic evaluation of action | Claimed purpose does not erase harmful action. | B | Assess conduct beyond rhetoric. |
| Sikhism — truthful living | Outward claims should correspond to reality. | B | Do not call harmful conduct righteous. |
| Confucianism — rectification of names | Names should correspond to actual roles and conduct. | A/B | Do not let labels conceal reality. |
| African Traditional | No single textual equivalent; leaders' claims are often judged through communal consequences. | C | Claimed reform should be tested against actual effects. |

### Conclusion
This audit addition is important because it introduces an explicit **anti-false-reform** principle absent from the initial sequence.

# E3 Unit 202 — Qur'an 2:27

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 2:27 condemns breaking covenant after commitment, severing what should remain connected, and causing corruption.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Reliable commitments sustain social order.**
- **Do not destroy relationships or connections whose maintenance carries legitimate value.**
- **Repeated breach and fragmentation can become system-level corruption.**
- **Individual acts can damage the connective structure of a community.**

| Hinduism — relational dharma | Relationships carry continuing duties. | B | Do not abandon rightful obligations casually. |
| Sikhism — sangat and truthful living | Community depends on reliable relationships. | B | Maintain trustworthy bonds. |
| Confucianism — relational ethics | Social order is constituted through durable relationships. | A/B | Relationships generate obligations. |
| African Traditional | Kinship and communal continuity are highly significant in many traditions. | A/B | Do not casually sever socially sustaining bonds. |

### Conclusion
This source anticipates Unit 107 and should remain as independent frequency evidence.

# E3 Unit 203 — Qur'an 2:42
## Do Not Mix Truth With Falsehood or Conceal What You Know

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 2:42 prohibits clothing truth with falsehood and knowingly concealing truth.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Do not create deception by mixing accurate information with false information.**
- **Selective truth can be deceptive when crucial known facts are intentionally concealed.**
- **Information integrity concerns composition and omission as well as outright fabrication.**
- **Knowledge can create disclosure responsibilities in relevant contexts.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Ephesians 4:25 | Falsehood is to be replaced by truthful speech. | A | Communicate truthfully. |
| Judaism — Exodus 23:7 | Keep far from falsehood. | A | Do not engineer deceptive representation. |
| Buddhism — Right Speech | False speech is prohibited. | A | Do not intentionally mislead. |
| Hinduism — satya | Truthfulness is foundational. | A | Align communication with truth. |
| Sikhism — truthful living | Truth should govern speech and conduct. | A | Do not manipulate truth. |
| Confucianism — xin / rectification of names | Words should reliably correspond to reality. | A/B | Preserve semantic and factual integrity. |
| African Traditional | Truth norms vary in expression but deception undermines trust. | B | Do not knowingly distort shared understanding. |

### Conclusion
This is more precise than a generic prohibition on lying because it captures **half-truth and strategic omission**.

# E3 Unit 204 — Qur'an 2:148
## Compete in Doing Good

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 2:148 acknowledges differing orientations and directs people to race or compete toward good works.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Difference need not be resolved before constructive action can occur.**
- **Competition can be redirected from domination toward beneficial conduct.**
- **Plural communities can compare themselves through contribution rather than hostility.**
- **Status incentives can be attached to doing good rather than defeating an out-group.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Hebrews 10:24 | People should spur one another toward love and good works. | A | Use social influence to increase beneficial action. |
| Judaism — mitzvah traditions | Active performance of good deeds is central. | B | Direct effort toward constructive action. |
| Buddhism — wholesome action | Practitioners are encouraged to cultivate skilful conduct. | B | Channel effort toward beneficial action. |
| Hinduism — karma yoga | Action should be directed toward right duty. | B | Compete with oneself and others in constructive conduct rather than domination. |
| Sikhism — seva | Service gives social energy a beneficial direction. | B | Seek distinction through service rather than hierarchy. |
| Confucianism — emulation of virtue | Good examples can stimulate cultivation. | A/B | Let competition reinforce virtue. |
| African Traditional | Prestige through generosity/service exists in particular traditions but cannot be generalised universally. | C | Social incentives can reward contribution. |

### Conclusion
The source introduces a useful institutional insight: competition itself need not be eliminated if its reward function is redirected toward good.

# E3 Unit 209 — Qur'an 4:29

### Extracted Ethical Core
- **Legitimate exchange requires more than transfer; it requires valid consent.**
- **Do not obtain another's property through wrongful means.**
- **Economic interaction should preserve the agency of both parties.**
- **Formal appearance of exchange does not cure coercion or fraud.**

### Comparative Analysis

| Confucianism — trustworthiness | Exchange requires reliable voluntary relationships. | B | Legitimate commerce depends on trust. |

### Conclusion
Audit result: preserve the consent sub-principle in Unit 050's coding rather than create an inflated duplicate count.
