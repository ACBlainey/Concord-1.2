## Method Note

# E3 Unit 211 — Qur'an 4:75
## Act for People Subjected to Oppression

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:75 appeals on behalf of oppressed men, women, and children who seek deliverance from an unjust social environment.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Vulnerability can create positive duties, not merely duties of non-harm.
- Protection should include people unable to secure their own safety.
- Children and other low-power persons deserve particular attention.
- Indifference to severe oppression can itself be an ethical failure.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Luke 4:18; Matthew 25 | Protection and care of oppressed or vulnerable people are strongly valued. | A/B | Assist people under severe oppression. |
| Judaism — Exodus liberation tradition; Proverbs 31:8–9 | Speak and act for those unable to defend themselves. | A | Defend vulnerable people. |
| Buddhism — compassion | Suffering generates duties of compassionate response, though coercive rescue is more complex. | B | Do not remain indifferent to severe suffering. |
| Hinduism — protection within dharma | Protection of vulnerable people is a recognised duty. | B | Strength carries protective responsibility. |
| Sikhism — defence of the oppressed | Protection against tyranny is a strong historical ethic. | A | Use capacity to protect those facing oppression. |
| Confucianism — humane government | Rulers should protect people from predation and disorder. | B | Authority should secure vulnerable people. |
| African Traditional | Protective duties are often embedded in kinship/community structures, with wide variation. | B | Community strength should protect vulnerable members. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** It adds a positive protective-duty formulation beyond the corpus's many non-harm rules.

# E3 Unit 212 — Qur'an 4:105–107
## Do Not Use Advocacy to Shield Deliberate Wrongdoing

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:105–107 warns against becoming an advocate for people who betray trust or wrong themselves and others.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Loyalty does not justify knowingly defending wrongdoing.
- Advocacy carries ethical responsibility for what it enables.
- Group membership should not override evidence and justice.
- Representation should not become concealment of deliberate abuse.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — 1 Timothy 5:21 | Judgement should avoid partiality. | B | Do not let allegiance corrupt judgement. |
| Judaism — Exodus 23:1–3 | Do not join false causes or distort justice through partiality. | A | Do not advocate falsehood. |
| Buddhism | Right Speech and non-harm constrain knowingly deceptive advocacy. | B | Do not use speech to enable wrongdoing. |
| Hinduism | Dharma constrains loyalty when loyalty conflicts with justice. | B | Allegiance does not excuse injustice. |
| Sikhism | Truth and justice outrank corrupt factional loyalty. | B | Do not defend wrong merely because the wrongdoer is one's own. |
| Confucianism | Loyalty is bounded by remonstrance and righteousness. | A/B | True loyalty can require correction. |
| African Traditional | Kin loyalty is often strong but commonly constrained by adjudicative norms. | C | Group solidarity should not erase accountability. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This is a useful anti-capture rule for advocates, representatives, and institutions.

# E3 Unit 213 — Qur'an 4:112
## Do Not Transfer Your Own Guilt to an Innocent Person

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:112 condemns committing an offence and then placing its blame on an innocent person.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Responsibility should attach to the actual actor.
- Do not evade accountability by manufacturing another person's guilt.
- False accusation compounds the original wrong.
- Institutions should guard against scapegoating.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity / biblical tradition — false-witness prohibition | False accusation is prohibited. | A | Do not assign guilt falsely. |
| Judaism — Deuteronomy 19:16–19 | Malicious false witnesses are directly addressed. | A | Protect the innocent from transferred blame. |
| Buddhism — Right Speech | Deliberate falsehood causing harm is prohibited. | A | Do not save oneself through harmful lies. |
| Hinduism — satya | Truthfulness prohibits false attribution. | A/B | Responsibility should track fact. |
| Sikhism — truthful living | False blame conflicts with truth. | A/B | Do not scapegoat. |
| Confucianism — rectification and trustworthiness | Names and responsibility should correspond to reality. | B | Attribute conduct accurately. |
| African Traditional | False accusation is serious in many customary systems, though procedures vary. | B | Do not transfer guilt to the innocent. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain/merge with Units 153 and 186 during synthesis.** It adds the specific mechanism of scapegoating.

# E3 Unit 214 — Qur'an 4:148
## Victims May Speak About Wrong Done to Them

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 4:148 generally disfavors public expression of harmful speech while making an exception for a person who has been wronged.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Privacy and civility rules should not silence victims.
- Public criticism can be justified when it names experienced wrongdoing.
- Protection of reputation should not become protection of abuse.
- Ethical speech rules require context-sensitive exceptions.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Matthew 18:15–17 | Wrongdoing may be named and escalated through structured process. | B | Civility does not require silence about harm. |
| Judaism — harmful-speech law includes protective-purpose exceptions | Speech can be permitted to prevent harm under conditions. | A/B | Reputation rules should not conceal danger. |
| Buddhism | Right Speech permits truthful, beneficial speech even when difficult. | B | Necessary truth can outweigh comfort. |
| Hinduism | Truth and non-harm require contextual balancing. | B | Speaking of wrongdoing can be justified for protection. |
| Sikhism | Truthful resistance to injustice is strongly valued. | B | Do not silence truthful reports of oppression. |
| Confucianism — remonstrance | Necessary criticism can be a duty. | B | Harmony should not suppress corrective truth. |
| African Traditional | Public accusation and mediation procedures vary widely. | C | Victims require legitimate channels to name harm. |

### Discussion
This passage was included in Unit 068 (4:148–149). The audit confirms the victim-voice exception as a distinct coding feature.

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: cross-reference Unit 068; do not double-count.**

# E3 Unit 215 — Qur'an 5:8
## Hatred Must Not Distort Justice

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:8 commands steadfast justice and explicitly warns that hatred of a people must not lead to injustice.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Justice applies to disliked groups as well as allies.
- Hostility is a predictable source of adjudicative bias.
- Fairness is most meaningful when emotionally costly.
- Institutions should anticipate bias produced by conflict.

| Buddhism — universal metta | Ill will should not govern treatment. | A | Do not act from hatred. |
| Hinduism — equanimity | Ethical discipline resists partiality from attachment/aversion. | B | Apply principle despite dislike. |
| Sikhism — equality and justice | Enmity should not govern justice. | B | Judge beyond faction. |
| Confucianism — righteousness | Right conduct should not be subordinated to private feeling. | B | Principle outranks resentment. |
| African Traditional | Impartiality norms vary by institution and community. | C | Conflict should not automatically determine judgement. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: duplicate of Unit 071; merge.**

# E3 Unit 216 — Qur'an 5:48

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:48 recognises differentiated communities and directs them to compete in good works rather than making difference itself the centre of conflict.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Normative diversity need not prevent constructive coexistence.
- Communities can channel rivalry into beneficial contribution.
- Difference should not automatically become hostility.
- Shared good action can coexist with distinct identities.

| Confucianism — harmony without sameness | Harmony does not require uniformity. | A | Cooperate without sameness. |

# E3 Unit 217 — Qur'an 5:87–88
## Do Not Manufacture Prohibitions Against Legitimate Goods

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:87–88 warns believers not to forbid good things made lawful and not to transgress.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Ethical systems can fail through excessive prohibition as well as permissiveness.
- Do not manufacture burdens without adequate justification.
- Legitimate enjoyment is compatible with moral discipline.
- Restraint should be proportionate rather than ascetic by default.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — Colossians 2:20–23 | Self-made prohibitions and severe ascetic rules are questioned. | A/B | Do not confuse unnecessary deprivation with virtue. |
| Judaism | Law distinguishes prohibited from permitted rather than treating all pleasure as suspect. | B | Do not invent prohibitions casually. |
| Buddhism | Middle Way rejects both indulgence and extreme self-mortification. | A | Avoid both excess and needless deprivation. |
| Hinduism — moderation traditions | Disciplined enjoyment can coexist with duty. | B | Restraint need not become blanket denial. |
| Sikhism | Householder life rejects compulsory world-renouncing asceticism. | A/B | Ethical life can include legitimate ordinary goods. |
| Confucianism | Properly regulated enjoyment is compatible with ritual order. | B | Moderation differs from prohibition. |
| African Traditional | No single analogue can be responsibly generalised. | C | Legitimate goods are culturally regulated rather than universally rejected. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** This provides an important counterweight against over-constraint.

# E3 Unit 218 — Qur'an 5:101–102
## Do Not Create Needless Burdens Through Excessive Questioning

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 5:101–102 warns against inquiries that can generate burdensome disclosures or rules.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- More information and more regulation are not automatically better.
- Inquiry should be proportionate to legitimate need.
- Curiosity can impose avoidable costs.
- Systems should consider the burden created by answering or formalising every possible question.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity | Some New Testament passages resist needless rule multiplication, though no exact parallel is strong. | B/C | Avoid burdens without clear benefit. |
| Judaism | Extensive inquiry is generally valued, making direct equivalence contextual rather than simple. | C | Questions should still serve legitimate interpretation. |
| Buddhism — refusal of unprofitable speculative questions | Some questions are set aside when they do not aid liberation. | A/B | Inquiry should have useful purpose. |
| Hinduism | Philosophical inquiry is strongly valued; no close general prohibition. | C | Discern productive from unproductive inquiry. |
| Sikhism | No clear direct equivalent identified. | C/D | Avoid empty disputation. |
| Confucianism | Learning is valued, but practical moral cultivation disciplines inquiry. | B/C | Knowledge-seeking should serve good judgement. |
| African Traditional | No uniform analogue. | D | No clear cross-traditional equivalent asserted. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: duplicate of Unit 081; merge.**

# E3 Unit 219 — Qur'an 6:52
## Do Not Exclude People Because They Lack Status

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:52 warns against dismissing sincere but socially vulnerable followers in order to accommodate status pressures from others.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Do not make wealth or prestige a condition of moral inclusion.
- Low-status participants should not be sacrificed to attract elite approval.
- Institutions should resist status capture.
- Social visibility is not a reliable measure of human worth.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — James 2:1–7 | Preferential seating and treatment of the rich are explicitly criticised. | A | Do not privilege wealth in communal inclusion. |
| Judaism | Human dignity and charity traditions constrain contempt for the poor. | B | Poverty does not erase dignity. |
| Buddhism — anti-caste inclusion | Birth/status is not decisive for spiritual worth. | A | Do not exclude by inherited status. |
| Hinduism | Evidence is mixed because egalitarian and hierarchical traditions coexist. | C | Some strands reject status as measure of ultimate worth. |
| Sikhism — rejection of caste / langar | Status exclusion is directly opposed. | A | Shared community should cross rank. |
| Confucianism | Moral cultivation can outrank inherited nobility, though hierarchy remains significant. | B | Worth is not reducible to wealth. |
| African Traditional | Status structures vary substantially. | C | No uniform cross-cultural rule asserted. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: retain.** It is a direct anti-elite-capture and inclusion principle.

# E3 Unit 220 — Qur'an 6:68
## Withdraw From Degrading Discourse Rather Than Legitimate It

### Qur'anic Source
Qur'an 6:68 instructs disengagement from gatherings engaged in degrading discourse about sacred matters until the subject changes.

### Extracted Ethical Core
- Presence can confer social legitimacy on harmful discourse.
- Withdrawal is a non-coercive response to unacceptable speech.
- Boundaries can be enforced without suppressing all speech by force.
- Re-engagement can remain possible after the harmful conduct stops.

### Comparative Analysis

| Tradition / Text | Equivalent | Grade | Reduced Core |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Christianity — 2 Timothy 2:23 | Foolish and destructive controversies may be avoided. | B | Disengage from corrosive discourse. |
| Judaism — Psalm 1:1 | Avoid participation in corrupting company. | A/B | Do not legitimise harmful conduct through participation. |
| Buddhism | Avoidance of unwholesome association is a recurring discipline. | B | Withdraw from contexts that reinforce harmful conduct. |
| Hinduism — satsanga contrast | Association influences character. | B | Choose environments that support right conduct. |
| Sikhism — sangat | Company affects moral formation. | B | Do not remain in corrosive association unnecessarily. |
| Confucianism | Companionship is selected partly for its formative effects. | B | Association carries moral consequences. |
| African Traditional | No single analogue asserted. | C | Participation can signal communal endorsement. |

### Conclusion
**Audit disposition: merge conceptually with Unit 067 while preserving this separate source occurrence.**
