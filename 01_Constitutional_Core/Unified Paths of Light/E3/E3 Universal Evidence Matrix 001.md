# Sacred Codes and Civilizational Longevity — E3
## Universal Evidence Matrix 001

**Principles:** Q001 Truthful Communication; Q006 Promise Integrity; Q009 Self-Critical Humility; Q011 Protection of Life.

---

## Method

This matrix upgrades the earlier plausibility review into an evidence record. The Qur'an is the anchor and is already established by E3. The seven comparison traditions must each stand on identifiable evidence.

The source-quality grade refers to provenance, not to the functional match grade.

## Q001 — Truthful Communication

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Biblical truth/non-false-witness material is directly identifiable. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Torah/Tanakh truth and false-witness prohibitions provide direct textual provenance. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | Canonical right-speech/false-speech material provides direct provenance. | SQ-A |
| Hinduism | Truthfulness (*satya*) is identifiable in foundational textual/virtue traditions; final table should name the exact passage used. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | Truthful living is a central Guru Granth Sahib ethical theme. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Analects 1.4 and related passages explicitly foreground trustworthiness in dealings with friends and self-examination. | SQ-A |
| African Traditional | The category must be specified. Akan/African ethics scholarship explicitly identifies truthfulness and honesty among social goods; Ubuntu-derived African moral theory also treats deception as wrongful while warning against generalising to all sub-Saharan societies. | SQ-B/C |

**Audit decision:** **Securely evidenced across the seven comparison families at functional level, but the African row must be labelled with its actual provenance rather than “African Traditional” generically.** Q001 remains a strong Universal candidate.

## Q006 — Promise, Covenant, and Commitment Integrity

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Promise/oath/covenant integrity has direct biblical provenance. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Covenant, oath and vow obligations have direct Torah/Tanakh provenance. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | Truthful speech and undertaking-based moral discipline provide strong functional support; exact promise formulation should be checked. | SQ-B |
| Hinduism | Duty, truth and vow/commitment traditions provide identifiable textual support; exact passage should be fixed in final bibliography. | SQ-B |
| Sikhism | Truthful living and keeping one's word are strongly represented in Sikh ethical interpretation. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Analects repeatedly treats *xin*—trustworthiness/fidelity—as a core social virtue; Analects 1.4 supplies direct evidence. | SQ-A |
| African Traditional | Ubuntu-inspired African moral theory explicitly uses breach of trust/breaking a promise as a paradigmatic wrong, while disclaiming universality across every African society. | SQ-B |

**Audit decision:** **Strong cross-traditional support.** Publication wording should identify the African evidence as Ubuntu-inspired/African moral philosophy rather than pan-African canon.

## Q009 — Self-Critical Humility

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Humility and anti-pride are directly biblical. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Humility/anti-arrogance have identifiable biblical and rabbinic provenance. | SQ-A/B |
| Buddhism | Non-conceit and disciplined self-examination have canonical support. | SQ-A/B |
| Hinduism | Humility/non-pride are identifiable virtues in foundational texts. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | Humility and overcoming ego (*haumai*) are central Sikh ethical themes; recent scholarly synthesis explicitly treats humility as a virtue flowing from truthful living. | SQ-A/B |
| Confucian | Analects 1.4 explicitly models repeated self-examination; anti-boastfulness/humility recur. | SQ-A |

**Audit decision:** **Strong candidate, not yet an unqualified 8/8 at the exact composite formulation.** Parent ethic of humility is much more securely universal than the narrower self-critical mechanism.

## Q011 — Sanctity and Protection of Life

| Tradition | Evidence status | Source-quality judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Christianity | Direct prohibition of murder and protection-of-life material. | SQ-A |
| Judaism | Direct Torah prohibition and extensive life-protection jurisprudence. | SQ-A |
| Buddhism | First precept against taking life gives direct canonical provenance. | SQ-A |
| Hinduism | *Ahimsa* and non-injury traditions provide strong identifiable provenance. | SQ-A/B |
| Sikhism | Compassion and disciplined restraint provide strong support; the exact unjust-killing formulation should be sourced directly. | SQ-B |
| Confucian | Humaneness and benevolent government strongly protect persons, though a universal non-killing formulation is less direct than in some traditions. | SQ-B |
| African Traditional | A documented Southern African Ubuntu teaching set explicitly includes “do not harm or kill” and the Shona maxim *Chikuru upenyu* (“life is paramount/what matters is life”). | SQ-B |

**Audit decision:** **Strong functional 8-family support for protection against unjust killing/harm.** Final wording should prefer **Protection of Life / prohibition of unjust killing** over assuming that every tradition shares the same theological meaning of “sanctity.”

## Matrix 001 Finding

This pass shows why provenance labels matter. The African comparison becomes much stronger when “African Traditional” is replaced by a specific evidentiary location—Akan ethics, Southern African Hunhu/Ubuntu, or Ubuntu-inspired African moral philosophy—without pretending that one source represents every African society.
