## Method Note

This tranche normalises autonomy, pluralism, family, relationship, consent, and sexual-protection material. Historical social forms are reduced to their functional behavioural core rather than imported unchanged into a substrate-neutral framework.

# E3-N041 — Freedom of Conscience and Non-Compulsion

## Normalised Principle

**Do not attempt to manufacture genuine belief through coercion; protect meaningful space for conscience and conviction.**

### Operational Meaning

External compliance and internal belief are not the same phenomenon. Coercive conformity can produce performance without conviction.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:256; 10:99.

### Supporting E3 Units

**024, 100, 206, 237, 408**, with pluralism support **200, 327, 407**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- no compulsion in religion;
- conviction cannot be forced;
- conscience;
- non-coercive belief.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Freedom of conscience does not imply freedom from all generally applicable behavioural law. It protects inner conviction and non-coercive religious identity.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Functional support varies historically and textually across traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain as distinct.**

# E3-N042 — Coexistence Without Forced Sameness

## Normalised Principle

**Permit deep differences of belief or identity to remain unresolved without requiring forced merger, provided the parties remain within legitimate non-harm constraints.**

### Operational Meaning

Concord is not identicality. Stable pluralism requires boundaries that permit difference without automatically converting difference into hostility.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 3:64; 29:46; 60:8–9; 109:1–6.

### Supporting E3 Units

**147, 175, 176, 200, 207, 283, 305, 327, 365, 386, 387, 407, 409**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- common ground without erasing difference;
- best-manner disagreement;
- peaceful out-groups;
- doctrinal difference without forced merger;
- conduct-based rather than identity-based hostility.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Pluralism does not require moral equivalence among all behaviours. Conduct that causes serious harm remains subject to ordinary ethical constraints.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong plural or coexistence strands occur in most traditions but with significant internal variation.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N043 — Respectful Disagreement

## Normalised Principle

**When disagreement is necessary, use the best available manner, begin from genuine common ground where possible, and do not convert argument into degradation.**

### Operational Meaning

Disagreement is treated as a normal feature of plural civilisation. The ethical question is therefore not how to eliminate disagreement but how to conduct it without unnecessary harm.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 16:125; 29:46; 41:34.

### Supporting E3 Units

**110, 147, 157, 247, 283, 289, 365, 370**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- wisdom in persuasion;
- best-manner argument;
- common-ground reasoning;
- constructive response.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Respectful disagreement does not prohibit forceful criticism of harmful conduct. It constrains unnecessary degradation and bad-faith argument.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N044 — Family Respect With Preserved Moral Agency

## Normalised Principle

**Treat parents and close family with care and gratitude, but do not surrender independent moral agency where family authority demands serious wrongdoing.**

### Operational Meaning

Relationship duties and autonomy are balanced rather than treated as mutually exclusive. Respect does not require unlimited obedience.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 17:23–24; 31:14–15; 29:8.

### Supporting E3 Units

**112, 148, 249, 282, 284, 366**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- care for ageing parents;
- gratitude;
- family loyalty;
- conscience limits on obedience;
- respectful dissent.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The principle neither dissolves family duties into individual preference nor turns family authority into ownership of the person.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Family respect is nearly universal; explicit limits on obedience vary.

**Preliminary classification: Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N045 — Child and Dependant Welfare

## Normalised Principle

**Caregivers must protect the life, welfare, maintenance, and developmental interests of children and other dependants rather than treating dependency as permission for exploitation.**

### Operational Meaning

Power over a dependant creates heightened responsibility. Fear of scarcity, conflict between adults, or property incentives should not be transferred onto the dependant.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:233; 6:151; 17:31; related orphan-protection passages.

### Supporting E3 Units

**021, 085, 116, 251**, with orphan-protection units **043, 046, 048, 194, 322**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- child maintenance;
- parental responsibility;
- no killing from scarcity fear;
- developmental welfare;
- dependant protection.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Caregiver authority is functional and responsibility-bearing, not ownership.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong equivalents occur across all traditions.

**Preliminary classification: Universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N046 — Consent and Non-Coercion in Intimate Relationships

## Normalised Principle

**Do not use dependency, status, economic leverage, or relational authority to coerce intimate or marital participation.**

### Operational Meaning

Intimacy requires meaningful agency. Formal relationship status does not eliminate the ethical significance of consent and non-coercion.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 4:19; 24:33; related 33:53 consent/privacy material.

### Supporting E3 Units

**049, 138, 152, 274, 286**, with exploitation material **103, 117**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- no coercive marriage treatment;
- no sexual exploitation;
- consent;
- no use of vulnerability for intimate access;
- relational agency.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Historical legal categories differ from modern consent frameworks. The normalisation preserves the functional anti-coercion ethic without claiming textual identity with every contemporary legal formulation.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Anti-coercion strands occur widely, though historical practice varies greatly.

**Preliminary classification: Common / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N047 — Sexual Non-Exploitation and Bodily Boundaries

## Normalised Principle

**Do not exploit another person's body, vulnerability, or dependency for sexual gain; preserve legitimate bodily and intimate boundaries.**

### Operational Meaning

Sexual ethics are reduced here to a substrate-neutral protective core: non-exploitation, bodily respect, and restraint around intimate boundaries.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 17:32; 24:30–31; 24:33; related 70:29–31.

### Supporting E3 Units

**117, 137, 138, 185, 273, 274**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- sexual restraint;
- bodily privacy;
- no sexual exploitation;
- no coercion of vulnerable persons;
- intimate boundaries.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The normalised principle intentionally avoids treating all historical sexual-status rules as universal constitutional law. It preserves the behavioural protection that survives substrate-neutral reduction.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong sexual-restraint and anti-exploitation principles occur widely; detailed boundaries differ.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal at the functional level.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N048 — Fairness in Relationship Dissolution

## Normalised Principle

**When intimate relationships end, use fair procedures, do not weaponise dependency, and preserve the legitimate welfare and agency of affected parties.**

### Operational Meaning

Exit is a high-risk period for coercion. Ethical systems should protect people during separation rather than allowing prior intimacy to justify harassment or material punishment.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:226–237; 65:1–7.

### Supporting E3 Units

**018, 019, 020, 022, 023, 180, 181, 309, 310, 389, 390**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- fair separation;
- waiting/procedural periods;
- witnesses;
- housing/support;
- no harassment;
- capacity-aware maintenance;
- dignity after relationship breakdown.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

The principle extracts procedural fairness rather than importing every historical divorce rule unchanged.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Most traditions regulate separation and continuing duties, but functional equivalence varies.

**Preliminary classification: Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# E3-N049 — Consultation in Family and Relationship Decisions

## Normalised Principle

**Where decisions materially affect shared family responsibilities, use mutual consultation rather than unilateral domination where the affected parties possess relevant agency.**

### Operational Meaning

Consultation begins at the interpersonal level as well as the civic level. Shared responsibility should include shared voice.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:233; 4:35; 4:128.

### Supporting E3 Units

**021, 052, 064**, with broader consultation principle **040, 158, 290, 372**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- parental consultation;
- arbitration;
- negotiated settlement;
- shared decision-making.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

This may later be nested under a general consultation principle, but is retained provisionally because family power asymmetries create a distinct application.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Consultative family norms vary substantially.

**Preliminary classification: Common.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain provisionally.**

# E3-N050 — Reciprocal Relational Rights and Responsibilities

## Normalised Principle

**Close relationships create reciprocal claims and responsibilities; authority or obligation should not be treated as purely one-directional.**

### Operational Meaning

Stable relationships require mutuality. Differences in role do not eliminate reciprocal ethical claims.

### Primary Qur'anic Sources

Qur'an 2:228; related 4:19 and 9:71.

### Supporting E3 Units

**019, 049, 098, 235**, with relationship fairness material **065**.

### Merged Proposition Families

- reciprocal marital rights;
- mutual social responsibility;
- kind treatment;
- fairness despite unequal affection.

### Boundary / Non-Equivalence Notes

Reciprocity does not imply identical responsibilities in every circumstance. It rejects unilateral entitlement without corresponding duty.

### Cross-Traditional Prevalence

Strong reciprocal-duty concepts occur widely.

**Preliminary classification: Universal / Near-universal.**

### Normalisation Decision

**Retain.**

# Interim Normalisation Findings: E3-N041–N050

This tranche demonstrates the importance of functional reduction. The Qur'anic sources arise within specific historical family and religious contexts, but several broader principles survive normalisation:

- conscience;
- plural coexistence;
- respectful disagreement;
- family respect with autonomy;
- dependant welfare;
- intimate non-coercion;
- bodily boundaries;
- fair exit;
- consultation;
- and reciprocal responsibility.

These principles remain independently Qur'an-derived at this stage; no Psalm-derived catalogue has been used to shape their boundaries.
