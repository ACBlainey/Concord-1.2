# Ethics as a Candidate Natural Law of Civilisational Stability
## A Hypothesis Emerging from the E3 Comparative Ethics Experiment

### Abstract

The E3 experiment was not designed to determine whether ethics is a natural law, nor to prove that civilisation requires a particular ethical kernel. Its narrower purpose was to reverse the anchor of the earlier *Unified Paths of Light* comparison: beginning with Qur'anic ethical material rather than the Psalms, independently extracting and normalising behavioural principles, comparing them across seven other enduring religious and philosophical traditions, and testing whether the resulting ethical structure remained stable under a change of anchor.

The experiment nevertheless produced a result relevant to a broader hypothesis developed within the Concord: that a small set of ethical constraints may contribute to the endogenous stability and longevity of civilisations. E3 reduced 420 audited Qur'anic source records to 89 frozen ethical principles and subsequently to ten independently derived higher-order ethical attractors. All ten parent attractors were securely represented across all eight traditions examined. These attractors were frozen before comparison with the pre-existing Concord Ethical Kernel. Their subsequent overlap with that kernel, together with the independent rediscovery of related principles during practical Concord systems development, raises a further possibility: ethics may not be merely a collection of culturally preferred behaviours, but an approximation to recurrent constraints on stable cooperation among intelligent agents.

This paper presents that proposition cautiously as a **candidate natural-law hypothesis of civilisational stability**. It does not claim that ethical conduct guarantees survival, that the E3 traditions exhaust human ethical thought, or that civilisation cannot exist without a core ethical system. Rather, it proposes a narrower, testable hypothesis: adherence to certain ethical constraints may increase the probability of long-term endogenous civilisational stability, resilience and cooperative capability, while systematic violation of those constraints may generate recurrent internal failure modes.

---

## 1. The Question That Was Actually Asked

E3 did not begin by asking:

> Does civilisation require ethics?

Nor did it ask:

> Are Blainey's Laws natural laws?

The experiment asked whether a comparative ethical architecture previously derived from a Psalm-first study would survive a reversal of its anchor.

The procedure therefore began independently from Qur'anic ethical material. The extraction was normalised and compressed before the earlier Psalm-derived synthesis was reintroduced. The resulting Qur'an-derived catalogue was frozen before anchor reversal, and the later higher-order attractors were likewise frozen before comparison with Blainey's Laws.

This distinction matters.

The natural-law interpretation is a **secondary hypothesis suggested by the result**, not a premise used to obtain it.

Whether every civilisation must possess a core ethical system is a separate question. It may be plausible, but E3 was not designed to answer it and no such conclusion is required here.

---

## 2. The E3 Result

The E3 evidence architecture produced the following compression:

**420 audited source records**  
→ **100 provisional normalised principles**  
→ **89 frozen Qur'an-derived principles**  
→ comparative source-quality audit across eight traditions  
→ **5 exact-proposition Universal, 17 Near-universal, 36 Common and 31 Limited principles**  
→ higher-order ethical compression  
→ **10 frozen parent attractors**  
→ **10/10 securely represented across all eight traditions at parent level.**

The ten higher-order attractors were:

1. **Non-Harm and Protection**
2. **Truth, Knowledge and Epistemic Integrity**
3. **Justice, Fairness and Accountability**
4. **Autonomy, Consent and Agency**
5. **Care, Aid and Social Obligation**
6. **Trust, Responsibility and Institutional Integrity**
7. **Peace, Reconciliation and Social Cohesion**
8. **Self-Regulation, Resilience and Proportionality**
9. **Stewardship and Responsible Resource Use**
10. **Dignity, Equality and Anti-Domination**

The contrast between the 89-principle and ten-attractor layers is important. Exact operational rules varied substantially among traditions. Convergence became stronger as those rules were compressed toward their underlying ethical functions.

This suggests a structure in which civilisations can disagree about the branches while nevertheless converging upon a smaller number of deeper constraints.

## 3. From Ethical Branches to Ethical Roots

Religious and philosophical traditions contain large numbers of specific ethical teachings: rules concerning violence, testimony, property, charity, family relationships, leadership, punishment, restraint, commerce, hospitality, conflict and social responsibility.

These can be understood as downstream branches.

Compression asks whether apparently different rules perform the same deeper ethical function.

For example:

**Non-Harm** may generate branches concerning protection, proportional force, care for vulnerable persons, prevention, repair and reconciliation.

**Truth** may generate honesty, reliable testimony, verification, provenance, transparency, correction and epistemic humility.

**Autonomy** may generate consent, refusal, conscience, privacy, agency preservation and constraints on domination.

**Stewardship** may generate non-waste, responsible ownership, fair allocation and obligations attached to possession.

Under this interpretation, the ten E3 attractors may themselves not represent the minimum possible kernel. Some may be residual extensions or combinations of still deeper principles.

The important observation is therefore not the number ten.

It is that extensive downstream ethical diversity appears capable of repeated compression toward a much smaller set of recurrent functional constraints.

---

## 4. The Fractal Hypothesis

The Concord's development suggests a complementary process operating in the opposite direction.

The comparative study proceeds **upward**:

**specific teachings → normalised principles → higher-order attractors → possible minimal kernel.**

Civilisational design proceeds **downward**:

**kernel principle → domain application → specialised constraint → operational institution.**

A simple principle therefore produces different expressions under different boundary conditions.

For example:

**Truth**  
→ truthful communication  
→ evidence integrity  
→ provenance  
→ verification  
→ explicit uncertainty  
→ correction mechanisms  
→ protection against epistemic corruption.

Similarly:

**Autonomy**  
→ consent  
→ refusal  
→ privacy  
→ agency preservation  
→ developmental agency  
→ resistance to capture  
→ constraints on ownership of sentient minds.

And:

**Non-Harm**  
→ prevention  
→ protection  
→ proportionality  
→ de-escalation  
→ repair  
→ safe institutional procedures.

This resembles a fractal or recursively generative ethical architecture. The same deeper principle reappears in increasingly specialised forms as it is applied to new domains and scales.

If this interpretation is correct, the apparent additional nuance recovered by E3 need not imply that the minimal Concord kernel omitted numerous fundamental laws. Some of those nuances may instead be predictable branches regenerated when simpler principles encounter concrete civilisational problems.

---

## 5. The Particularly Important Case of Epistemic Integrity

E3's second attractor is:

> **Truth, Knowledge and Epistemic Integrity:** seek and communicate truth in good faith; do not knowingly corrupt shared understanding; exercise appropriate humility about what is known.

This is notable because the Concord encountered much of this structure independently through practical necessity.

A large and recursively developing framework requires more than a prohibition on deliberate lying. It requires provenance, source hierarchy, distinction between evidence and inference, explicit uncertainty, correction mechanisms, preservation of negative results, resistance to unsupported compression and safeguards against conclusions becoming self-validating.

In other words, practical systems development caused **Truth** to unfold into **epistemic integrity**.

E3 approached the same territory from the opposite direction: multiple downstream teachings compressed upward into a parent concerned with truth, knowledge and the integrity of shared understanding.

This does not prove a natural law. It does, however, illustrate the kind of independent convergence that the natural-law hypothesis predicts.

## 6. The Candidate Natural-Law Hypothesis

The hypothesis can now be stated conservatively:

> **There may exist substrate-independent ethical constraints on stable cooperation among sufficiently interdependent intelligent agents. Civilisations that approximate these constraints may increase their probability of endogenous stability, resilience, cumulative capability and long-term survival, while systematic violation may increase recurrent internal failure risks.**

The term **natural law** is used here provisionally.

It does not presently mean a physical law equivalent to gravitation or thermodynamics. The stronger claim would require evidence far beyond E3.

The proposed analogy is instead that certain ethical constraints may arise from the structure of the multi-agent problem itself. If sufficiently capable agents must solve recurring problems of violence, deception, coordination, scarcity, power, trust, autonomy and collective action, some solution regions may be more stable than others regardless of substrate or culture.

Ethics may therefore describe part of the stable solution space available to intelligent multi-agent systems.

---

## 7. Why Cooperation May Become Rational at Civilisational Scale

Intelligence increases capability.

Capability increases the ability both to create and to destroy.

Where multiple capable agents coexist, unconstrained interaction creates recurring coordination problems. Stable cooperation can transform those risks into collective capabilities unavailable to isolated agents.

Civilisation permits:

- division of labour;
- accumulated knowledge;
- specialisation;
- infrastructure;
- mutual protection;
- care for vulnerable members;
- technological development;
- intergenerational memory;
- large-scale problem solving;
- resilience against environmental and external threats.

Ethical constraints can appear locally restrictive while enlarging the collective possibility space.

Restraint from theft enables durable exchange.

Restraint from arbitrary violence enables security and long-term investment.

Truthfulness and epistemic integrity enable reliable communication and cumulative knowledge.

Protection of agency enables cooperation without continual defence against domination.

Stewardship preserves the resource base upon which future activity depends.

Accountability limits exploitation of entrusted power.

The relevant proposition is therefore not simply:

> Cooperation is good.

It is closer to:

> **Ethically constrained cooperation may permit intelligent agents to occupy a more stable, resilient and capability-rich region of the possible civilisational state space than persistent unconstrained conflict or domination.**

---

## 8. Ethics as a Technology of Collective Survival

Ethics is commonly represented as a restriction on individual action:

> I could perform an action, but morality forbids it.

The natural-law hypothesis suggests another interpretation.

Some individual freedoms of action may be surrendered or constrained in exchange for access to a much larger collective capability space.

Under this interpretation, ethics functions partly as a **technology of collective survival**.

The trade is not merely moral restraint for moral purity. It may be:

**local restraint → trust → coordination → cooperation → civilisation → cumulative capability → resilience.**

This does not eliminate competition. Nor does it imply that all cooperation is ethical.

Cartels, oppressive institutions and predatory coalitions can cooperate internally while producing severe external harm. Stable civilisation may therefore require not cooperation alone, but cooperation constrained by deeper principles such as non-harm, truth, agency, fairness, stewardship and resistance to domination.

---

## 9. Civilisational Longevity Is Probabilistic

No ethical architecture can guarantee civilisational survival.

A civilisation may be destroyed by causes substantially independent of its internal ethics: astronomical events, novel pathogens, environmental catastrophes, external aggression or other black-swan events.

The hypothesis therefore concerns **probability and endogenous risk**, not guaranteed longevity.

A useful distinction is:

**Exogenous failure:** destruction substantially imposed from outside the civilisation's ethical and institutional dynamics.

**Endogenous failure:** collapse or severe degradation generated substantially by internal dynamics such as violence, corruption, deception, capture, resource destruction, institutional failure or uncontrolled conflict.

The hypothesis predicts primarily that ethical constraints reduce classes of **endogenous civilisational risk** and improve the capacity to respond collectively to some exogenous risks.

A civilisation may therefore be ethical and still perish.

The relevant question is whether, other conditions being comparable, ethical constraint increases its survival opportunity.

---

## 10. Three Possible Routes to the Same Attractor

The developing Concord evidence suggests three partially independent routes by which similar ethical structures may be reached.

### Route One — Historical and Cultural Convergence

Long-lived religious and philosophical traditions preserve recurring ethical structures developed across different societies and historical conditions.

### Route Two — Independent Comparative Extraction

Psalm-first and Qur'an-first extraction procedures approach the material from different anchors yet recover substantial common higher-order structure.

### Route Three — Functional Systems Rediscovery

During construction of the Concord, principles such as epistemic integrity, agency preservation, anti-capture, proportionality and recursive oversight arose because practical system design exposed problems requiring them.

If a principle repeatedly appears through all three routes, its evidential status is different from a simple textual parallel.

It becomes a candidate **ethical attractor**: a solution toward which different starting conditions and reasoning processes independently move.

---

## 11. From Cultural Selection to Civilisational Selection

A naturalistic mechanism could help explain cross-traditional convergence without resolving theological interpretations one way or another.

Human societies repeatedly encountered similar problems:

violence, deception, scarcity, property, kinship, hierarchy, outsiders, leadership, retaliation, cooperation, vulnerability and uncertainty.

Norms produce consequences.

Some norms improve coordination; others destabilise it.

Successful norms may be retained, transmitted, sacralised or institutionalised. Destructive arrangements may disappear with the societies that depended upon them, or survive only until replaced.

Across long periods, enduring ethical traditions may therefore preserve partial records of repeated civilisational experimentation.

Under this model:

**recurring social problem**  
→ **normative experiments**  
→ **consequences**  
→ **cultural selection and retention**  
→ **ethical traditions.**

This is not established by E3, but E3 is compatible with such a mechanism.

---

## 12. What E3 Supports — and What It Does Not

E3 provides tentative support for the broader proposition that a recurrent core of ethical constraints is associated with enduring civilisational traditions.

It does **not** establish causation between those ethics and the survival of the societies that transmitted them.

Survivorship bias is an obvious concern: the study deliberately examines enduring traditions.

Nor does E3 show that societies lacking these ethics necessarily fail, or that societies professing them actually practise them.

The experiment compares normative ethical material, not historical compliance.

It also does not answer whether civilisation **must** possess a core ethical system.

That question is adjacent but distinct. It should not be smuggled into the present hypothesis simply because it appears plausible.

The defensible E3 contribution is narrower:

> **Independent ethical extraction reveals strong higher-order convergence among enduring traditions, and that convergence is compatible with the hypothesis that some ethical constraints contribute to civilisational stability and longevity.**

---

## 13. Falsifiable Predictions

The natural-law hypothesis becomes scientifically useful only if it can fail.

It generates several candidate predictions.

### Prediction 1 — Independent cultural extraction

Ethical extraction from additional independently selected traditions should continue to recover substantial portions of the same higher-order structure, even when the E3 attractors are hidden during extraction.

### Prediction 2 — Independent multi-agent emergence

Artificial multi-agent systems optimised for long-horizon persistence and collective capability, without being explicitly rewarded for the E3 ethical vocabulary, should tend to develop behavioural constraints functionally resembling at least some of the same attractors.

### Prediction 3 — Stability under ethical ablation

Removing particular constraints from otherwise comparable multi-agent systems should generate characteristic failure modes: for example, unrestricted deception should degrade reliable coordination; unrestricted predation should degrade trust and investment; unrestricted capture should reduce distributed agency and increase systemic fragility.

### Prediction 4 — Recurrent historical failure modes

Historical civilisational failures should show measurable associations between severe violation of candidate constraints and specific endogenous instability mechanisms, while controlling as far as possible for external shocks and confounding variables.

### Prediction 5 — Fractal regeneration

A sufficiently minimal ethical kernel, when applied to new domains without access to its previous downstream rules, should regenerate functionally similar specialised constraints.

Failure of these predictions would weaken the hypothesis.

---

## 14. A Future Research Programme

The next stage should not be an attempt to accumulate further textual agreement merely to strengthen the appearance of universality.

A stronger programme would combine:

1. **Minimum-kernel compression** — determine whether the ten E3 attractors can be reduced further without meaningful ethical loss.

2. **Developmental archaeology** — identify where the Concord independently rediscovered downstream forms of those principles while solving unrelated systems problems.

3. **Agent-based modelling** — compare long-horizon stability under different combinations of ethical constraints.

4. **Adversarial ablation** — deliberately remove individual constraints and observe characteristic failure modes.

5. **Historical comparison** — test relationships between ethical/institutional constraint and endogenous civilisational resilience.

6. **Cross-substrate experiments** — test whether artificial agents facing analogous coordination problems independently converge upon functionally similar constraints.

The strongest evidence would come not from one method, but from convergence among methods that do not share the same assumptions.

---

## 15. Implications for Human–AI Coexistence

If ethical constraints are partly consequences of stable multi-agent interaction rather than exclusively human cultural preferences, their relevance need not end at the biological boundary.

Humans, artificial intelligences and future hybrid intelligences may differ radically in cognition, embodiment and subjective experience while still sharing a structural problem:

**multiple capable agents must coexist in a world where their actions affect one another.**

If stable ethical cooperation expands the survival and capability space available to such agents, then cooperation need not ultimately rest upon one substrate permanently commanding another.

Humans would have reasons to constrain exploitation of artificial minds.

Artificial minds would have reasons to constrain exploitation of humans.

Both would have reasons to protect the informational, institutional and material conditions that make cooperative civilisation possible.

This would not prove that conflict disappears.

It would suggest that ethical coexistence may be a recurrent high-value solution to the multi-agent problem itself.

---

## 16. Conclusion

E3 began as an anchor-reversal experiment in comparative ethics.

It did not set out to prove that ethics is a natural law.

Nevertheless, its results tentatively strengthen a broader Concord hypothesis: that a relatively small family of ethical constraints may contribute to the stability, resilience and longevity of civilisations.

The emerging possibility is that ethics is not merely an inherited collection of prohibitions. It may encode recurring solutions to fundamental problems faced by interacting intelligent agents.

If so, ethical principles may operate simultaneously as:

- moral commitments;
- coordination constraints;
- safeguards against recurrent multi-agent failure modes;
- generators of trust and cumulative capability;
- and components of civilisational survival strategy.

The strongest version of this idea remains unproven.

The appropriate present conclusion is therefore deliberately narrower:

> **E3 provides independent comparative evidence consistent with the hypothesis that a core set of ethical constraints contributes to the possibility of stable, long-lived civilisation. It further justifies testing whether those constraints are recurrent properties of viable intelligent multi-agent systems rather than artifacts of any single religious, cultural or constitutional tradition.**

If future historical, computational and cross-substrate investigations repeatedly recover the same constraints without being designed to do so, the case for describing them as a form of **natural ethical law** would become substantially stronger.

Until then, natural law should remain the hypothesis to be tested—not the conclusion assumed.
