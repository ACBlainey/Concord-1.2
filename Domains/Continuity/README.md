# Purpose

This folder is the development and eventual operating home for the **Continuity civilisational domain**.

Continuity concerns the persistent civilisational responsibility of preserving the ability to continue, restore or reconstruct legitimate functions and capabilities across disruption and time while permitting necessary change.

Its central question is:

> **Can the required function or capability actually survive or be recovered under the conditions being considered?**

Continuity is not equivalent to storing information.

> **Preserved Information ≠ Recoverable Capability**

---

# Initial Domain Boundary

Continuity is distinct from neighbouring Concord systems.

**Historical** preserves an interpretable, provenance-bearing past.

**KCS** represents current knowledge, dependencies and change propagation.

**Continuity** determines whether the required function or capability can survive, be handed forward, or be recovered.

**Library / knowledge preservation** may preserve usable information.

**Continuity** concerns whether the larger enabling capability remains recoverable.

Continuity does not inherit operational or constitutional authority merely because it preserves recovery capability.

> **Continuity ≠ Authority**

# Initial Orientation

Historical demonstrated that a Concord civilisational domain can cut horizontally across many conventional institutions.

Continuity appears to do the same.

Historical asks:

> **What existed, what happened, what was represented, and how can the past be reconstructed faithfully?**

Continuity asks:

> **What must remain possible, and what knowledge, capability, dependencies and recovery pathways are required for it to remain possible?**

The next task is to resolve that distinction against the full Concord corpus and determine the proper architecture of the Continuity domain.
