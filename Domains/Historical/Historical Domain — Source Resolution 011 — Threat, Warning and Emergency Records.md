# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 011 — Threat, Warning and Emergency Records

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / THREAT-AND-EMERGENCY PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines what the Historical Domain must preserve when civilisation identifies, predicts, investigates or responds to threats.

The problem is unusually important because threat systems operate under uncertainty and may justify high-consequence action. A historical system that preserves only confirmed threats or successful interventions would systematically erase the evidence needed to detect:

- false alarms;
- missed warnings;
- threat inflation;
- model failure;
- institutional capture;
- emergency overreach;
- successful prevention;
- justified but mistaken action;
- changing evidence;
- premature or delayed intervention.

Primary V1.2 sources include:

- `08_Active_Development/01_DEVELOPMENT_NOTES/04_GOVERNANCE_AND_STEWARDSHIP/Civilisational Threat Detection, Vector Triggers and Think-Tank Escalation — Preliminary System Architecture.md`;
- `08_Active_Development/01_DEVELOPMENT_NOTES/04_GOVERNANCE_AND_STEWARDSHIP/Integrated Threat-Governance Loop — Adversarial Attack and Falsification Audit.md`;
- `01_Constitutional_Core/Constitutional Emergency V2/Constitutional Emergency V2.md`;
- `08_Active_Development/01_DEVELOPMENT_NOTES/04_GOVERNANCE_AND_STEWARDSHIP/PH-02 — Emergency Governance and Emergency Operations.md`;
- `08_Active_Development/02_CANDIDATE_PRINCIPLES/CP-02 Outliers and Failure as Evidence.md`.

## 3. The Threat Architecture Already Requires Historical Memory

The preliminary threat architecture proposes:

`Civil Metrics → Direction Vector → Trigger → Threat Think Tank → Investigation → Findings → Governance Consideration → Authority Justification Chain → Bounded Action`

It repeatedly separates epistemic functions from authority:

> **Prediction ≠ Authority**

> **Think Tank Finding ≠ Governance Decision**

and:

> **The body that identifies a threat should not automatically acquire the authority required to respond to that threat.**

This creates a chain that Historical must preserve without collapsing its stages.

A later record must be able to distinguish:

1. what was observed;
2. what triggered attention;
3. what was predicted;
4. what investigators concluded;
5. what uncertainty or dissent existed;
6. what Governance decided;
7. what authority was actually granted;
8. what action was taken;
9. what subsequently happened;
10. what later review concluded.

Candidate principle:

> **Threat Observation ≠ Threat Prediction ≠ Threat Finding ≠ Governance Decision ≠ Emergency Authority ≠ Historical Outcome.**

---

## 4. False Alarms Are Historical Evidence

The threat architecture explicitly proposes meta-metrics including:

- false-positive rate;
- false-negative discoveries;
- number of no-threat findings.

It also requires review where a metric repeatedly triggers false alarms.

Therefore a no-threat conclusion is not disposable.

A warning that later proves unsupported can reveal:

- an over-sensitive trigger;
- a poor model;
- incomplete measurement;
- institutional bias;
- a legitimate inquiry that correctly terminated;
- changing conditions.

Candidate principle:

> **A False Alarm Is Not a Null Historical Event.**

Deleting false alarms would make the threat system appear more accurate than it was and remove the evidence required to improve it.

---

## 5. Missed Threats Are Also Historical Evidence

CP-02 states that outlier, contradictory and unsuccessful outcomes should be examined as potential evidence of hidden assumptions, missing pathways or structural weakness.

The Emergency architecture explicitly recognises false-negative emergencies.

Therefore Historical must preserve not only what triggered the system, but where later evidence shows that the system failed to trigger.

This may require retrospective linkage:

`Historical Event → Earlier Signals → Missed Trigger / Misclassification → Later Recognition`

Candidate principle:

> **Absence of Contemporary Recognition Does Not Mean Absence of Historical Signal.**

This is an ESCP-sensitive function: later investigation may reveal that relevant dimensions existed outside the contemporary measurement architecture.

---

## 6. Trigger History Must Survive

The adversarial audit asks who decides which warning deserves attention and concludes that this decision must be auditable.

It explicitly states that trigger history should preserve provenance.

Historical therefore needs to preserve enough to reconstruct:

- the metric or observation;
- its state at the time;
- trigger rule/version;
- threshold;
- evidence;
- source;
- confidence;
- decision to investigate or not investigate;
- responsible process;
- later correction where relevant.

This is especially important when trigger rules themselves change.

A later threshold must not be retroactively applied as though it governed the earlier decision.

---

## 7. Threat Assessments Need Temporal Meaning

The preliminary architecture distinguishes three broad threat families:

- projectable developing threats;
- known long-horizon threats;
- black-swan/deep-uncertainty threats.

For long-horizon threats it proposes:

`Threat Record(t) → Updated Evidence(t+1) → Updated Horizon → Updated Lead Time → Updated Current Action Value`

This is a direct Historical requirement.

A threat record cannot safely be stored as:

`Threat = Severe`

It needs temporal context:

- severity;
- probability/confidence;
- horizon;
- lead time;
- uncertainty;
- evidence state;
- action value at that time;
- current classification;
- prior classifications.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Threat Severity Without Historical Temporal Context Can Misrepresent the Decision Space That Existed at the Time.**

---

## 8. Threat Age Is Not Threat Urgency

The source explicitly states:

> **Threat Age ≠ Threat Urgency**

A long-known threat may remain distant.

A newly discovered threat may be immediate.

Historical must therefore not use duration in the archive as a proxy for urgency, importance or legitimacy.

Similarly:

> **Historical Persistence of a Threat Record ≠ Continuing Emergency Status.**

This is important because a permanent Historical record of an emergency must not make the emergency itself appear permanent.

---

## 9. Preserve Threat Contraction and Disappearance

The source explicitly allows that:

- evidence may move a threat further away;
- a distant threat may accelerate;
- an apparent threat may disappear.

Therefore Historical must preserve downward movement as carefully as escalation.

Candidate lifecycle:

`Signal → Inquiry → Assessment → Escalation / Stable Watch / Contraction / No Credible Threat / Reclassification / Closure`

Candidate principle:

> **De-escalation Is Historical Information, Not Deletion of the Earlier Threat State.**

Otherwise a system can retain the alarming state while losing the evidence that later reduced concern.

---

## 10. Threat Think-Tank Dissent Must Survive

The threat architecture calls for:

- explicit challenge functions;
- contradictory evidence;
- recorded dissent;
- adversarial analysis;
- alternative responses.

The adversarial audit further requires materially relevant commissioned findings to be preserved and discarded analysis not to disappear from provenance.

Therefore:

> **Historical Threat Custody Must Preserve Material Dissent Without Treating Dissent as Equivalent Evidence.**

Preservation does not imply equal evidential weight.

It preserves the fact that an alternative assessment existed, what evidence supported it, and how it was handled.

---

## 11. “No Credible Threat Found” Is a Valid Outcome

The proposed Threat Assessment Record includes explicit outcomes such as:

- NO CREDIBLE THREAT FOUND;
- INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE;
- CREDIBLE THREAT — PREPAREDNESS RECOMMENDED;
- SERIOUS THREAT — GOVERNANCE REVIEW RECOMMENDED;
- IMMINENT / ACTIVE THREAT — URGENT GOVERNANCE REVIEW.

Historical should preserve these as stateful conclusions, not reduce the record to “threat” versus “no threat.”

Particularly:

> **Insufficient Evidence ≠ No Threat**

and:

> **No Credible Threat Found ≠ Proof That the Trigger Was Irrational.**

The inquiry may have been legitimate even when it terminated without confirmation.

---

## 12. Emergency Records Have a Stronger Accountability Requirement

Emergency V2 states:

> **Emergency governance remains constitutional governance.**

It requires significant emergency decisions to remain reviewable and linked to evidence.

Its Emergency Decision Record asks what threat was addressed and what evidence was available at the time.

This establishes a strong Historical function.

Historical must be able to reconstruct emergency reasoning using the **contemporary evidence state**, not merely later knowledge.

Candidate principle:

> **Emergency Decisions Must Be Historically Reviewable Against the Information Reasonably Available at the Time.**

This does not prohibit later assessment using better information.

It requires the two views to remain distinguishable.

---

## 13. Contemporary Reasonableness and Retrospective Correctness Are Different

PH-02 asks post-event review to consider:

> Given the evidence, urgency, available alternatives and foreseeable consequences at the moment of decision, was the intervention reasonable and proportionate?

This produces a critical Historical distinction:

- **Outcome correctness** — what later turned out to be true or effective.
- **Contemporary decision quality** — whether the decision was reasonable given what was knowable then.

A good decision can have a bad outcome.

A poor decision can occasionally have a good outcome.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Outcome ≠ Historical Decision Quality.**

Historical must preserve enough information to evaluate both without conflating them.

---

## 14. Changing Course Must Not Be Rewritten as Consistency

Emergency V2 explicitly treats changing course in response to better evidence as legitimate rather than failure.

Therefore a later corrected decision must not overwrite the earlier decision.

The historical chain should preserve:

`Evidence State A → Decision A → New Evidence B → Review → Decision B`

rather than:

`Final Decision B`

Candidate principle:

> **Correction During Emergency Must Preserve the Earlier Decision State and the Evidence That Caused It to Change.**

This aligns directly with Source Resolution 004's metric-correction model.

---

## 15. Emergency Sunset Creates a Historical Boundary

Emergency V2 requires automatic Constitutional Emergency Sunset:

> **Emergency authority expires unless its continued necessity is constitutionally demonstrated.**

This creates an important historical transition.

Historical should preserve:

- activation;
- scope;
- authority;
- responsible actors;
- evidence;
- reviews;
- renewals;
- contractions;
- expiry;
- restoration of ordinary procedure.

Candidate principle:

> **Expiry of Emergency Authority Is a Positive Historical Event, Not Merely the Absence of Renewal.**

Without that event, future reconstruction may be unable to establish exactly when exceptional authority ceased.

---

## 16. Permanent Emergency Is a Historical Failure Mode

Emergency V2 states:

> **Permanent emergency is a form of silent constitutional change.**

Historical is therefore part of the defence against this failure.

It should make visible:

`Temporary Authority → Renewal → Renewal → Scope Change → Continued Use`

so that repeated temporary actions cannot disappear into disconnected records.

This is a relational-history problem.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Records Must Permit Repeated Temporary Authorities to Be Seen as a Sequence.**

Otherwise institutional persistence can hide behind individually valid-looking episodes.

---

## 17. Emergency Secrecy Does Not Cancel Historical Accountability

Emergency V1 and V2 recognise that some emergency information may require temporary confidentiality.

Previous Historical source resolutions established:

> **Historical Custody ≠ Public Accessibility**

This pass strengthens that principle.

Sensitive threat material may require:

- sealed custody;
- restricted discovery;
- delayed disclosure;
- redacted derivatives;
- protected evidence;
- later declassification review.

But temporary secrecy should not silently become historical disappearance.

Candidate principle:

> **Operational Secrecy May Restrict Historical Access Without Necessarily Eliminating Historical Custody.**

The exact precedence rules remain unresolved and must still be reconciled with privacy, dangerous-knowledge and lawful-destruction architecture.

---

## 18. Manufactured Emergencies Require Preservation Against the Power Holder

Emergency V2 explicitly defines the possibility of a Manufactured Emergency, including:

- exaggerating threats;
- concealing evidence of improvement;
- manipulating emergency metrics.

The threat adversarial audit similarly examines institutions that could produce the evidence, define the threat, justify authority and measure their own success.

This strongly reinforces the earlier Historical anti-capture principle:

> **The System Being Audited Should Not Possess Unilateral Control Over the Survival of the Record Required to Audit It.**

For high-consequence emergency records, independent or protected Historical custody is therefore particularly important.

## 19. Threat-System Self-Measurement Is Historical Data

The threat architecture proposes measuring the threat-detection system itself.

Relevant history may include:

- trigger count;
- false positives;
- false negatives discovered;
- no-threat findings;
- calibration changes;
- model changes;
- threshold changes;
- investigation duration;
- escalation rate;
- de-escalation rate;
- review findings.

Historical custody of these meta-metrics enables longitudinal examination of whether the warning system is becoming:

- more accurate;
- more alarmist;
- more blind;
- more centralised;
- more resistant to correction.

Historical does not itself make that governance judgment.

It preserves the longitudinal evidence.

## 20. Successful Prevention Creates a Counterfactual Problem

Threat systems face a special epistemic difficulty.

If intervention succeeds, the feared outcome may never occur.

A later observer may incorrectly conclude that:

- the threat was exaggerated;
- the intervention was unnecessary;
- or, conversely, that the intervention definitely prevented the outcome.

The sources do not provide a complete solution to this counterfactual problem.

However, they support preserving:

- pre-action evidence;
- threat model;
- predicted trajectories;
- alternatives;
- intervention;
- subsequent observations;
- uncertainty;
- later review.

Candidate principle:

> **Non-Occurrence After Intervention Does Not by Itself Establish Either That the Threat Was False or That the Intervention Prevented It.**

This should remain an epistemic constraint, not a historical verdict.

---

## 21. Long-Horizon Threat Registers Are Historical-Operational Hybrids

The preliminary architecture proposes a Civilisational Long-Horizon Threat Register to:

- preserve knowledge;
- preserve provenance;
- record scientific confidence;
- record horizon;
- record relevant capabilities;
- support periodic reassessment.

This is not purely Historical because it remains operationally active.

The likely boundary is:

### Threat system / planning
owns the current live threat entry, reassessment and action implications.

### Historical
preserves prior threat states, prior assessments, prior evidence states, changes, decisions and provenance.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Custody of Prior Threat States ≠ Ownership of Current Threat Assessment.**

## 26. Historical Must Preserve Failure Without Creating Threat Permanence

There is a tension.

Threat and emergency records are valuable because they support future learning.

But preserving every threat at permanent high salience could produce a civilisation psychologically or institutionally dominated by its past dangers.

Source Resolution 005 already distinguished archive, cold storage and active material.

This suggests:

`Active Threat → Resolved/Expired → Historical → Cold/Restricted where appropriate → Reactivation only through legitimate current evidence/process`

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of a Threat Must Not Preserve the Threat's Former Operational Authority.**

and:

> **Historical Retrieval of an Emergency Does Not Reactivate Emergency Power.**

## 27. Historical and Threat Governance Boundary

Historical should preserve:

- signals;
- trigger history;
- assessments;
- evidence states;
- dissent;
- forecasts;
- threat-state changes;
- governance referrals;
- emergency declarations;
- authority activations;
- decisions;
- renewals;
- expiry;
- outcomes;
- audits;
- lessons;
- failures.

Historical should not:

- declare a threat;
- set current threat level;
- declare an emergency;
- activate emergency power;
- choose mitigation;
- decide that a past warning is currently valid;
- convert archived threat assessments into present authority.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Threat Custody ≠ Threat Authority.**

## 28. Relationship to Civil Attention

Civil Attention can surface participant reports that reveal threats or institutional failure.

Historical may later preserve the issue/provenance chain.

But:

`Historical Record of Repeated Complaints`

does not itself become:

`Threat Declaration`

The legitimate current system must assess the evidence.

Historical provides memory and pattern visibility without inheriting operational authority.

## 29. Relationship to Metrics

Metrics may generate threat triggers.

Source Resolution 004 established that historical metrics require their definitions, methods, uncertainty and temporal context.

This pass adds:

> **A Historical Threat Trigger Is Not Intelligible Without the Historical State of the Metric and Trigger Rule That Produced It.**

Therefore threat history and metric history must be linkable.

## 30. Relationship to Research

Threat Think Tanks are research bodies.

Source Resolution 009 therefore applies directly.

Historical must preserve:

- hypotheses;
- models;
- evidence;
- negative results;
- failed predictions;
- alternative interpretations;
- uncertainty;
- changing epistemic state.

Threat research is not exempt from epistemic provenance merely because stakes are high.

Indeed, high stakes make it more important.

## 31. ESCP Implication

Threat systems are especially vulnerable to incomplete evaluation space.

A metric may miss a relevant population.

A model may omit a causal pathway.

An institution may suppress a signal.

An outlier may be the first evidence of a new failure mode.

Therefore Historical should preserve enough contradictory and failed material to support later reconstruction of what the contemporary system could not see.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Threat Records Must Preserve Evidence of the Limits of the Threat-Detection System, Not Merely the Threats It Successfully Detected.**

## 34. Current Finding

Threat and emergency history cannot safely be reduced to a list of disasters.

The historically important object is the complete evidential and authority chain:

`Observation → Trigger → Inquiry → Assessment → Dissent → Governance Consideration → Authority Justification → Action → Review → Correction / Renewal / Contraction → Sunset → Outcome → Audit → Learning`

including branches where:

- no threat was found;
- evidence remained insufficient;
- the warning was false;
- the warning was missed;
- the response was mistaken;
- the response was justified but unsuccessful;
- the threat disappeared;
- the emergency ended;
- the institution itself became part of the risk.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **A civilisation cannot learn reliably from danger if its history remembers only the dangers it correctly predicted and the actions it later judged successful.**

Historical therefore has an anti-selection-bias function: it must preserve enough of the failed, uncertain, dissenting and superseded threat record to make later learning possible without inheriting the authority of the past threat system.
