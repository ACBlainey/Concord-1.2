## 1. Purpose

This record begins source resolution for the proposed Historical Domain.

The starting hypothesis is that Historical is the Concord's durable epistemic memory domain. The purpose of source resolution is to determine which existing Concord systems already own parts of that function before a formal domain specification is written.

> **Domain Hypothesis ≠ Source-Resolved Domain**

> **Repository Location ≠ Functional Ownership**

## 2. First Confirmed Architectural Anchor — KCS

The current Knowledge Control System source family is the strongest confirmed architectural anchor found in this first pass.

The KCS back-propagation audit identifies the primary source as:

`03_Continuity_and_Memory/KCS KNOWLEDGE CONTROL SYSTEM.md`

and states that the original KCS already contains substantive architecture for:
- externalised memory;
- epistemic state;
- provenance;
- historical preservation;
- reasoning pathways;
- honest negative knowledge;
- active/dormant/archive distinctions;
- bounded retrieval;
- stewardship;
- attribution;
- contribution evidence;
- anti-oracle and anti-social-credit safeguards.

KCS Companion Upgrade 003 adds explicit treatment of independent epistemic, retrieval/activity, integrity, access/visibility and capability-operational states; state-assignment provenance; typed historical relations; bounded retrieval; negative-search records; reusable software/model/tool state; privacy, retention and redaction; and interfaces to Continuity and change propagation.

This strongly supports:

> **Historical should compose KCS rather than invent a second civilisational memory/provenance architecture.**

KCS also already establishes several Historical-domain constraints:
- archived does not mean false;
- active does not mean true;
- stored object available does not mean capability operational;
- not retrieved does not mean nonexistent;
- recorded knowledge is not a complete evaluation space;
- historical preservation does not imply unlimited retention or universal visibility;
- restricted/sealed/redacted/expired/policy-removed states can coexist with provenance;
- retrieval does not itself reactivate archived capability.

## 3. Continuity Protocol Interface

The Continuity Protocol source family is a second confirmed interface.

Its current companion distinguishes:
- RECORDED;
- ACCESSIBLE;
- INTERPRETABLE;
- ACTIONABLE;
- RECOVERABLE;
- OPERATIONAL.

It explicitly states:

> **Preservation of Information ≠ Preservation of Recoverable Capability**

and identifies KCS as the supplier of preserved knowledge/capability objects and represented dependency/provenance state.

This suggests a clean provisional boundary:

**Historical/KCS preserves the record and relevant capability artefacts → Continuity determines whether the required function can actually survive or be recovered.**

The Continuity recovery basis may include artefacts, provenance, instructions, tools, credentials, prerequisite capability, successor/handoff information, verification tests and known failure records.

This is directly relevant to the proposed storage of retired tools/code and potentially dormant computational states, but it does not yet establish that Historical owns AI reactivation or continuity decisions.

> **Historical Preservation ≠ Continuity Success**

> **Historical Custody ≠ Reactivation Authority**

## 4. Permission / Protected-Access Interface

The promoted Fractal Permission Architecture provides a confirmed general permission mechanism relevant to Historical access.

FPA asks:

> **Who may do what, where, why, when, under which conditions, by which legitimate basis, and until when?**

It separates entry, use, inspection, recording, modification, removal, retrieval and authorisation; supports nested contexts; records permission provenance; handles revocation/expiry; and explicitly consumes higher-order privacy, safe-space and consent constraints.

This supports the proposed Historical access pattern:

**Historical Object → Protected/Nested Context → Permission Claim → Minimum Necessary Permission → Bounded Action → Termination/Audit**

FPA does not manufacture the legitimacy of the underlying rule or authority. Historical therefore should not become its own unrestricted access authority merely because it has custody.

> **Custody ≠ Permission**

> **Capability to Retrieve ≠ Authority to Retrieve**

## 5. Exit Interview / Institutional Learning Records

The promoted Exit Interview Protocol provides a concrete example of information that may become Historical while preserving fine-grained access and retention conditions.

Its operating chain ends in:

**... → Optional Feedback → Provenance/Archive**

It also establishes:
- one interview may contain multiple information objects;
- those objects may have different purposes, consent, access and retention states;
- deletion/restriction should operate at information-object level where practicable;
- minimal deletion provenance may remain where legitimate;
- historical evidence may inform analysis while retaining its original epistemic status;
- confidentiality does not imply unlimited retention.

This supports Historical storing institutional-learning evidence without flattening consent, privacy, epistemic state or retention rules.

## 6. Civic Sensor / Camera Black-Box Candidate

The existing Active Development sketch **Privacy-Preserving Civic Sensor and Camera Evidence Architecture** directly supports the proposed black-box function.

It proposes:

**Camera / Sensor → Protected Raw Recording → Black-Box Evidence Store**

Ordinary use:

**Black-Box Raw Store → Authorised Extraction → Anonymisation / Redaction → Purpose-Bounded Civic Data**

Exceptional identified access:

**Legitimate Investigative Need → Required Authority → Bounded Request → Necessary Raw Evidence → Audit / Accountability**

The sketch explicitly separates:
- capability to record from authority to observe;
- operational metric need from authority to identify;
- possession of raw evidence from permission for routine access;
- anonymised civic use from identified investigative use.

However, this remains **SKETCH / UNTESTED / NON-CANONICAL** and requires source resolution against privacy, protected-space, governance, judiciary, authority, evidence and accountability architecture.

Historical is a plausible future owner of the black-box evidence store, but that ownership is not yet established by the source.

## 7. Initial Functional Composition

The first source pass therefore suggests a domain composition approximately like:

**Historical Domain**
- **KCS** — primary knowledge/memory/provenance substrate;
- **Protected/nested context architecture** — information-space boundary;
- **FPA / contextually bounded permission** — who may perform which action on which Historical object;
- **Continuity interface** — whether preserved knowledge/capability is recoverable or operational;
- **domain-specific retention/privacy/authority rules** — supplied by legitimate external owners;
- **Civil Attention interface** — reports/problems about Historical records or services;
- **candidate black-box evidence service** — subject to further development/source resolution.

This is composition, not yet final ownership.

## 8. Important Emerging Object Distinctions

Historical appears likely to require preservation of several independent dimensions rather than one generic archive status:

**Object identity + version/time + provenance + epistemic state + integrity state + access/visibility state + retention state + retrieval/activity state + capability-operational state + authority/permission state**

These dimensions should not be collapsed.

Examples:
- a record may be historically authentic but epistemically superseded;
- a file may be preserved but sealed;
- code may be retrievable but non-operational;
- a model may be stored but unauthorised for activation;
- a redacted derivative may be public while its source remains protected;
- a deleted-by-policy object may or may not retain minimal provenance depending on legitimate policy.

## 9. Candidate Historical Services Emerging From Sources

The first pass supports investigation of distinct services within Historical rather than one undifferentiated archive:

1. **Knowledge and Provenance Repository** — KCS-based durable civilisational memory.
2. **Archive and Version History** — superseded/corrected/historical states without erasure of developmental path.
3. **Protected Records Custody** — restricted, sealed and nested-access information.
4. **Public Knowledge / Library Surface** — candidate public-access projection of legitimate Historical holdings; source ownership not yet resolved.
5. **Black-Box Evidence Custody** — candidate protected raw sensor/evidentiary storage; currently sketch-level.
6. **Capability / Tool Archive** — preserved software, models, procedures, datasets or tools separated from operational activation.
7. **Continuity Holdings Interface** — preserved recovery-basis material supplied to Continuity without Historical deciding recoverability.
8. **Institutional Record Archive** — governance, voting, judiciary, Civil Attention, audit and similar records where their owning systems require durable preservation; individual source families still require resolution.

## 12. Current Finding

The Historical Domain hypothesis is strongly supported at the architectural level by existing KCS, Continuity and permission/protected-information mechanisms.

The most important first finding is that **Historical should not be designed as a new monolithic archive**. Much of its kernel already exists as distributed Concord architecture, especially KCS. The domain-definition task is therefore primarily one of **functional composition, custody boundaries and interfaces**.
