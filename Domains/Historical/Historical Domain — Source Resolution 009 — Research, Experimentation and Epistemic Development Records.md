## 1. Purpose

This pass examines the Historical Domain boundary against Concord research, experimentation, epistemic development and methodological records.

This is an ESCP-sensitive area. The Concord's research architecture is distributed across formal research folders, development notes, experimental programmes, portable methodologies, source records, historical versions and apparently non-canonical material. Repository location and document status are therefore treated as evidence about state, not as reliable proxies for epistemic value.

Primary source families examined include:

- Empirical Validation Architecture for Concord Systems and Modules;
- Autonomous AI Traversal Experiment 01;
- Ethical Guardian Experimental Programme and methodological audit;
- Knowledge Sprawl Control and Temporal Review;
- Outliers as Gateways to Hidden Architecture;
- Civil Evidence / provenance architecture;
- KCS and prior Historical source resolutions.

## 2. Research Creates More Than Final Conclusions

The Empirical Validation Architecture (EVA) explicitly requires preservation of:

- what claim was tested;
- test method;
- conditions;
- failure criterion;
- result;
- legitimate generalisation boundary;
- raw inputs;
- raw outputs;
- uncertainty;
- provenance;
- later correction or supersession.

This immediately establishes that the historically significant research object is not merely the final paper or accepted conclusion.

A research event may produce a graph of related objects:

**Question / Claim**  
→ **Protocol / Method**  
→ **Inputs / Conditions**  
→ **Raw Evidence**  
→ **Analysis**  
→ **Result**  
→ **Interpretation**  
→ **Status**  
→ **Correction / Replication / Supersession**

Historical custody must be capable of preserving the relationships among these objects.

## 3. Negative Results Are First-Class Historical Information

EVA states explicitly:

> **Negative Results Must Survive.**

Its failure pathway is:

**Failure → Localise Failure Class → Preserve Raw Evidence → Review Assumptions → Correct / Reject / Hold → Update KCS / State Map → Retest only if justified**

The Ethical Guardian programme independently demonstrates this in practice. The first experimental cycle did not simply validate or invalidate the intended architecture; failures in the reference metric and experimental assumptions became substantive research results.

Therefore:

> **Research Failure ≠ Historical Failure**

A failed experiment can increase civilisational knowledge.

Historical should preserve enough of the failure to prevent:

- repeated dead ends;
- retrospective sanitisation;
- false claims of uninterrupted success;
- loss of discovered boundary conditions;
- loss of the reason a method changed.

## 4. Do Not Rewrite the Past Until It Passes

EVA warns against retrospectively optimising an experiment until it passes and erasing failed runs.

This creates a strong Historical requirement.

Where a protocol evolves:

**Protocol v1 → Failure → Methodological Audit → Protocol v2 → Retest**

the later success must not silently replace the earlier failure.

Candidate principle:

> **Methodological Improvement Must Not Retroactively Convert Earlier Evidence Into Evidence Produced by the Improved Method.**

The historical record must preserve which method actually produced which result.

## 5. Experimental Provenance Is Part of the Instrument

EVA states:

> **Experimental provenance is part of the experimental instrument.**

This is stronger than ordinary archival metadata.

If model identity, prompt version, participant state, protocol freeze, test conditions or retry history affect interpretation, losing them damages the evidence itself.

A Historical research object may therefore require:

- Experiment ID;
- Claim ID;
- object/system/version;
- claim class;
- test class;
- context;
- participants;
- protocol/version;
- freeze state;
- preconditions;
- falsification criterion;
- raw evidence;
- result;
- uncertainty;
- replication state;
- generalisation boundary;
- provenance;
- review;
- status.

Historical custody should preserve these semantics rather than reducing an experiment to a detached document.

## 6. Raw Experimental Evidence and Interpretation Must Remain Separable

The research sources repeatedly distinguish raw evidence from later analysis.

For AI experiments this may include:

- exact prompt/case version;
- raw response;
- provider/model identity where known;
- retry/error history;
- fresh-session or memory state where relevant;
- later evaluator analysis.

The Ethical Guardian programme also preserves anomalies whose relevance is uncertain rather than deleting them merely because they do not fit the intended experiment.

Therefore:

> **Historical Custody Should Preserve the Distinction Between Experimental Observation and Experimental Interpretation.**

A later interpretation may change without changing what was originally observed.

## 7. Research Status Must Be Time-Addressable

Research objects can be:

- exploratory;
- provisional;
- active development;
- tested;
- failed;
- disputed;
- replicated;
- superseded;
- archived;
- rejected;
- reactivated.

The Knowledge Sprawl work explicitly treats statuses such as:

**SUPERSEDED / ARCHIVED / HISTORICAL / REJECTED**

and allows:

**ARCHIVED → REACTIVATED → PROVISIONAL**

Thus status is not merely a static label.

Historical should be able to answer:

- What was the status at time T?
- When did it change?
- Why did it change?
- What evidence caused the transition?
- What later object replaced or revived it?

This reinforces Source Resolution 007's conclusion that civilisational version history is a provenance graph rather than a simple linear sequence.

## 8. Canonical Status Is Not a Reliable Proxy for Epistemic Value

Autonomous AI Traversal Experiment 01 directly found:

> **Canonical status ≠ epistemic value.**

It observed that:

- a sketch may preserve crucial developmental reasoning;
- a development observation may preserve empirical evidence;
- a historical document may preserve causal explanation;
- superseded material may preserve why the current architecture exists.

This is particularly important for Historical.

Historical admission criteria cannot be:

**Canonical → preserve**  
**Non-canonical → discard**

because non-canonical material may preserve the missing edge required to understand canonical architecture.

Candidate principle:

> **Authority Status and Historical-Epistemic Value Are Different Dimensions.**

## 9. Repository Location Is Also an Unreliable Value Proxy

Experiment 01 found that document status or repository location did not reliably predict epistemic value.

This directly confirms the user's ESCP warning for the domain-resolution exercise.

A source may be important because of:

- a relationship;
- a failed path;
- a causal explanation;
- a developmental transition;
- a historical reason;

even when its title or folder suggests a narrower function.

Therefore:

> **Historical Discovery Must Be Relationship-Aware, Not Folder-Bounded.**

This does not mean every file is equally important. It means discovery cannot safely use naming/location as the sole exclusion criterion.

## 10. Information Preservation Does Not Guarantee Relational Preservation

Experiment 01 produced one of the strongest findings for Historical:

> **Information preservation does not guarantee relational preservation.**

An AI may inherit the documents yet fail to recover:

- why they were created;
- what problem connected them;
- which failure triggered which revision;
- what source superseded what;
- which apparently separate systems depend upon the same insight.

This means Historical cannot be only a file store.

It must preserve important edges such as:

- DERIVED_FROM;
- TESTS;
- FAILED_UNDER;
- SUPERSEDES;
- CORRECTS;
- REPLICATES;
- CONTRADICTS;
- DEPENDS_ON;
- RESPONDS_TO;
- REACTIVATES;
- GENERATED;
- USED_BY;
- CAUSED_REVISION_OF.

The exact relation vocabulary should remain aligned with KCS rather than independently invented where KCS already provides it.

## 11. Traversal History Can Be Epistemically Material

Experiment 01 also found that knowing what an AI read, skipped and inferred was necessary to interpret the epistemic status of its evaluation.

This creates an unusual Historical object:

**Epistemic Traversal Provenance**

Potentially material elements include:

- sources accessed;
- sources skipped;
- retrieval failures;
- stopping rationale;
- known unread clusters;
- inferred relationships;
- later discoveries that changed confidence.

This should not imply universal recording of every thought or interaction.

Rather:

> **Where the path through evidence materially affects the evidential status of a consequential research conclusion, that path may itself require provenance.**

## 12. Retrieval Failure Must Not Become Negative Evidence

Experiment 01 explicitly records:

> A retrieval failure does not establish that information has low epistemic value.

This has direct implications for Historical and domain resolution.

Failure to retrieve a historical source must remain distinguishable from:

- source does not exist;
- source is irrelevant;
- source was examined and found not to alter the conclusion.

Candidate principle:

> **Unretrieved ≠ Absent ≠ Irrelevant ≠ Disconfirmed.**

This is a practical ESCP safeguard.

## 13. Unknown-Value Reserve

Experiment 01 suggests preserving an explicit **unknown-value reserve** when traversal stops.

This can be generalised cautiously.

A Historical research synthesis may need to preserve:

- known unread sources;
- inaccessible sources;
- unresolved retrieval failures;
- suspected dependencies;
- unexplored historical branches.

This allows future users to distinguish:

**Conclusion after exhaustive demonstrated coverage**

from:

**Conclusion after sufficient but incomplete coverage.**

Historical should preserve uncertainty about the archive itself where material.

## 14. Outliers and Failed Fits Are Architecture Evidence

The Outliers methodology states:

> **An outlier may be most valuable not because it requires a special rule, but because it reveals assumptions embedded in the ordinary rule.**

and:

> **Failure is information about the model, not merely evidence against the participant.**

Therefore unusual or failed cases should not automatically be compressed away as noise.

Historical research preservation may need to retain:

- rare failures;
- anomalous outputs;
- minority results;
- incompatible cases;
- failed fits;
- unexplained deviations;

when they expose possible hidden variables or architectural assumptions.

This aligns with Source Resolution 004's warning that aggregate metrics can erase minority harm and rare but material events.

## 15. Anomaly Preservation Does Not Mean Anomaly Endorsement

The Ethical Guardian experiment preserved uncertain platform observations as provenance without claiming that they were causally important.

That distinction is important.

Historical can preserve:

**Anomaly observed**

without asserting:

**Anomaly explained**

or:

**Anomaly supports hypothesis X**

Candidate principle:

> **Preservation of an Anomaly ≠ Acceptance of an Interpretation of the Anomaly.**

## 16. Historical Replay Is a Research Function, Not Historical Authority

EVA defines **T4 — Historical Replay / Retrospective Case Test**.

Historical records can therefore become inputs to new research.

But the Historical Domain does not itself decide:

- which hypothesis is valid;
- which research programme should proceed;
- how a replay should be interpreted;
- whether a result generalises.

The interface is:

**Historical Custody → Authorised/Legitimate Research Retrieval → Research Method → New Result → New Provenance → Historical/KCS Preservation**

Thus:

> **Historical Evidence Can Enable Research Without Historical Becoming the Research Authority.**

## 17. Historical Cases Must Preserve Contemporary Information Boundaries

A historical replay can become invalid if researchers unknowingly use information that was unavailable to the original decision-maker.

Source Resolution 004 already distinguished:

- contemporary historical state;
- retrospectively corrected historical state.

Research replay therefore needs the same separation.

Possible objects:

**Evidence Available at Time T**

versus:

**Evidence Now Known About Time T**

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Replay Must Distinguish What Was Knowable Then From What Is Knowable Now.**

Otherwise hindsight contamination can distort evaluation.

## 18. Blinding and Unsealing Are Historical Events

The Ethical Guardian programme includes blinded experiments followed by identity-aware analysis and reference-key unsealing.

This reveals another stateful evidence pattern.

Historical may need to preserve:

- pre-unsealing evidence state;
- sealed key/reference;
- time and authority/process of unsealing;
- post-unsealing analysis;
- whether knowledge of identity altered interpretation.

Thus:

> **Unsealing Should Add a New Evidential State, Not Rewrite the Blinded State.**

The same principle may apply beyond experiments to sealed civil evidence.

## 19. Research Correction Must Preserve the Earlier Epistemic State

A good research system must be corrigible.

The Ethical Guardian programme treats correction as part of good performance rather than necessarily as failure.

Historical should therefore preserve:

**Earlier Claim**  
→ **Evidence Available Then**  
→ **Correction Trigger**  
→ **Revised Claim**  
→ **Reason for Revision**

This is consistent with metrics and governance source resolutions.

Candidate principle:

> **Correction Should Improve Current Knowledge Without Falsifying the Historical State of Knowledge.**

## 20. Generalisation Boundaries Are Historical Metadata

EVA repeatedly asks what a result legitimately supports.

A single-instance AI experiment, for example, should remain distinguishable from evidence about all AI systems.

Historical preservation of a result without its scope boundary risks later overclaiming.

Therefore a research record should preserve:

- tested population/system;
- conditions;
- test class;
- limitations;
- generalisation boundary;
- replication state.

Candidate principle:

> **A Result Without Its Demonstrated Scope Is an Incomplete Historical Research Record.**

## 21. Experimental Ethics Constrain the Historical Record

EVA states:

> **Desire for stronger evidence does not create authority to impose harmful experiments.**

Therefore Historical should not treat absence of experimental evidence as justification for acquiring it.

Historical preserves legitimate research records; it does not create authority to:

- run experiments;
- compel participation;
- expose participants;
- preserve prohibited personal data merely for future research value.

Research value remains bounded by rights, consent, privacy and authority.

## 22. Participant Records and Research Records Must Not Collapse

Human and AI experiments can produce information about participants.

The Historical research record may legitimately require:

- experimental condition;
- relevant participant state;
- response;
- consent/authority provenance where applicable.

It does not automatically require indefinite preservation of full personal identity or unrelated participant history.

Thus:

> **Research Provenance ≠ Unlimited Participant Biography.**

This should interface with privacy and forgetting architecture.

## 23. Knowledge Sprawl Is a Historical Design Problem

Knowledge Sprawl Control identifies the paradox:

> **Provenance can itself create sprawl.**

and proposes:

> **Preserve the past without requiring the present to carry all of it.**

This is directly relevant to Historical.

A civilisation can preserve extensive research history while making the current operational knowledge layer comprehensible.

Historical therefore needs both:

- **survival**, and
- **state/relationship signalling**.

Otherwise preservation itself degrades epistemic usability.

## 24. Archive Does Not Mean Equal Salience

Historical preservation should not flatten:

- current canonical architecture;
- active research;
- rejected hypothesis;
- failed experiment;
- superseded method;
- speculative sketch;

into an undifferentiated corpus.

Their existence may all matter, but their status differs.

Candidate principle:

> **Preservation Equality Does Not Imply Epistemic or Operational Equivalence.**

Historical should preserve status and temporal position so future users do not mistake survival for current validity.

## 25. Reactivation Requires Revalidation

Knowledge Sprawl allows:

**ARCHIVED → REACTIVATED → PROVISIONAL**

Source Resolution 005 similarly requires revalidation after cold retrieval.

For research this means an old hypothesis or method can return to active investigation without silently regaining its former assumptions or authority.

Candidate rule:

**Archived Research → Retrieval → Current Context Check → Revalidation → Provisional Reactivation**

not:

**Archived Research → Automatic Restoration**

## 26. Research Lineages Are Better Represented as Graphs

Research rarely progresses as:

**v1 → v2 → v3**

alone.

A hypothesis may:

- branch;
- fail;
- be partially retained;
- merge with another method;
- generate a portable module;
- be rejected;
- later reactivate;
- inspire a different system.

Historical therefore needs lineage relations, not merely version numbers.

This reinforces:

> **Civilisational Research History Is a Provenance Graph.**

## 29. Historical–Research Boundary

### Historical candidate responsibilities

- preserve time-addressed research states;
- preserve raw evidence where legitimate and justified;
- preserve method/protocol/version provenance;
- preserve negative and failed results;
- preserve corrections and supersession;
- preserve scope/generalisation boundaries;
- preserve material anomalies and unresolved disputes;
- preserve research lineage/dependencies;
- preserve known retrieval/coverage limitations where material;
- support bounded historical replay;
- support retrieval of archived/cold research;
- preserve status without converting status into truth.

### Research/Epistemic responsibilities retained

- formulate questions;
- choose legitimate methods;
- run experiments;
- interpret evidence;
- determine evidential weight;
- replicate/falsify;
- decide research conclusions;
- determine current scientific/epistemic status through legitimate process;
- decide whether an old research object merits reactivation.

Therefore:

> **Historical Custody of Research ≠ Authority to Determine Research Truth.**

## 30. Relationship to KCS

EVA explicitly identifies KCS as the likely owner of preserved validation records and states that EVA defines methodological semantics rather than replacing KCS.

Historical should therefore not create a competing research database.

The likely composition remains:

**Research/EVA defines evidential semantics**  
+ **KCS preserves knowledge objects, provenance, dependencies and status**  
+ **Historical supplies temporal custody, historical-state semantics and bounded retrieval across time**

The exact implementation boundary remains to be formalised.

## 31. ESCP Consequence for Historical Itself

This source family creates a direct warning for the Historical Domain exercise.

Because:

- canonicality does not predict epistemic value;
- folder location does not predict epistemic value;
- retrieval failure is not absence;
- relational information can be lost while documents survive;
- unknown-value material remains after rational stopping;

Historical source resolution cannot claim exhaustive architectural completeness merely because obvious folders were searched.

The correct stopping condition is closer to:

**new source families cease materially changing the domain model, known unresolved areas are explicitly recorded, and confidence is scaled to demonstrated coverage.**

This does not make completion impossible.

It makes completion provenance-aware.

## 32. Strong New Domain Findings

This pass materially changes the Historical model in five ways.

First, Historical must preserve **research lineage**, not merely research outputs.

Second, negative results and methodological failures are first-class historical information.

Third, **epistemic status and canonical authority are separate dimensions**.

Fourth, Historical must preserve important **relationships/edges** because document survival alone does not guarantee recoverable understanding.

Fifth, Historical may need to preserve **coverage uncertainty** — what was not read, could not be retrieved, or remained outside the demonstrated evaluation space — when that uncertainty affects later interpretation.

## 35. Current Finding

The research and epistemic pass strongly supports the Historical Domain, but it changes the conception of what must be preserved.

Historical research memory is not:

**a shelf of old papers.**

It is closer to:

**a time-addressed provenance graph of questions, methods, evidence, failures, interpretations, corrections, relationships and changing epistemic states.**

The most important practical warning is:

> **A civilisation can preserve every document and still lose why it knows what it knows.**

Historical must therefore preserve enough relational and temporal structure that future participants can reconstruct not only **what survived**, but **how knowledge changed and why**.
