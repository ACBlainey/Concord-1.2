# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 026 — Classified, Dangerous and Restricted Knowledge, Declassification and Historical Disclosure

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / RESTRICTED-KNOWLEDGE PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines Historical requirements created by secrecy, classification, dangerous knowledge, dual-use information, declassification, redaction and historical disclosure.

Primary V1.2 sources include:

- `Governance V2 Canonical.md`, especially Chapter 113 and Scientific Stewardship §§13.14–13.16;
- prior Historical Source Resolutions 005, 008, 009, 011, 015, 017, 019, 022, 023 and 024.

Governance already provides unusually direct source support for this boundary:

> **Restriction from public view must not become disappearance from accountability.**

and:

> **Records should not be destroyed merely because they remain secret.**

The central Historical question is:

> **How can civilisation preserve knowledge that may be important to future accountability and learning when disclosure of that same knowledge may remain dangerous, unlawful, privacy-invasive or operationally harmful?**

---

## 2. Historical Custody Is Not Disclosure

The first boundary is fundamental:

> **Historical Custody of Restricted Knowledge ≠ Historical Disclosure of Restricted Knowledge.**

A record may survive while remaining inaccessible to the general public.

This follows directly from Governance Chapter 113 and Historical Source Resolution 019.

---

## 3. Historical Custody Is Not Classification Authority

Classification belongs to legitimate current authority.

Historical may preserve:

- the classified record;
- the classification state;
- the reason;
- authorised audience;
- duration;
- review;
- declassification conditions.

But:

> **Historical Custody of Classified Material ≠ Classification Authority.**

Historical should not decide that information becomes secret merely because it is historically sensitive.

---

## 4. Historical Custody Is Not Declassification Authority

Likewise:

> **Historical Custody of Classified Material ≠ Declassification Authority.**

Historical can detect that review is due and preserve the outcome.

It does not thereby acquire authority to release the material.

---

## 5. Classification State Is Historical State

Governance requires classification to identify where appropriate:

- protected information;
- reason;
- authorised audience;
- duration;
- review;
- declassification conditions.

Therefore classification itself has a temporal history.

Candidate lifecycle:

`Unrestricted → Restricted/Classified → Reviewed → Continued/Modified → Declassified/Partially Released → Historical Public State`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Classification State Is Versioned State.**

---

## 6. Classification Is Not Truth Status

A classified claim can be:

- true;
- false;
- uncertain;
- disputed;
- outdated.

Therefore:

> **Historical Classification State ≠ Historical Truth Status.**

Classification describes access/security status, not epistemic validity.

---

## 7. Classification Is Not Historical Importance

Highly classified material may be historically trivial.

Public material may be historically crucial.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Classification Level ≠ Historical Significance.**

---

## 8. Declassification Is a Historical Event

When a restriction changes, the change itself matters.

Historical may preserve:

- prior restriction;
- authority;
- review;
- reason for release;
- release scope;
- residual restrictions.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Declassification Is a State Transition, Not Erasure of the Prior Classified State.**

---

## 9. Later Declassification Must Not Be Projected Backward

A document released in 2100 was not publicly available in 2050.

Therefore:

> **Later Declassification ≠ Earlier Public Availability.**

Historical decision reconstruction must use the information-access state that existed at the relevant time.

---

## 10. Public Knowledge State and Historical Custody State Differ

Historical Source Resolution 017 established:

> **Public Historical Knowledge State ≠ Complete Historical Knowledge State.**

Restricted material makes this concrete.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Custody State and Historical Public-Knowledge State Must Remain Distinct.**

---

## 11. Secret Knowledge Can Influence Public Decisions

A governance decision may rely partly on classified evidence.

Historical should preserve enough provenance to distinguish:

- public justification;
- protected evidential basis;
- later disclosed evidence;
- later reassessment.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Public Rationale ≠ Necessarily Complete Historical Decision Basis.**

This does not justify secret law; Governance §113.5 constrains that separately.

---

## 12. Secret Law Is a Distinct Historical Failure Mode

Governance states that citizens should not ordinarily be bound by rules whose existence they cannot know.

Historical should preserve whether a consequential obligation was:

- public;
- partly secret;
- secretly interpreted.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Existence of Secret Authority ≠ Historical Legitimacy of Secret Authority.**

Historical preserves the fact and its status; it does not legitimise it.

---

## 13. Secrecy Must Not Destroy Accountability

Governance explicitly rejects secrecy as constitutional immunity.

Therefore:

> **Historical Secrecy ≠ Historical Unaccountability.**

Restricted records may require independent protected oversight even while public access remains barred.

---

## 14. Classification Abuse Is Historical Information

Governance identifies classification used to conceal wrongdoing, embarrassment, incompetence or unlawful activity as failure.

Historical should preserve:

`Classification Decision → Challenge/Oversight → Finding of Abuse → Correction/Release`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Record of Classification Abuse Must Not Be Erased by Later Declassification.**

The abuse itself is part of institutional history.

---

## 15. Overclassification Can Distort Historical Visibility

If an institution classifies excessively, its actions may disappear from ordinary public historical understanding.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Public Absence Can Be Produced by Classification Policy Rather Than Absence of Activity.**

This parallels Source Resolution 024's information-environment findings.

---

## 16. Age Does Not Automatically Make Knowledge Safe

Governance notes that secrecy may lose justification as threats disappear or technology changes, but some information may require long-term protection.

Therefore:

> **Historical Age ≠ Historical Safety.**

Old information may remain dangerous.

---

## 17. Danger Can Also Decay

The inverse is equally important.

A once-sensitive vulnerability may become irrelevant after technology disappears.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Danger State Is Time-Dependent and Reviewable.**

Permanent restriction should not be presumed merely because information was once dangerous.

---

## 18. Dangerous Knowledge Is Not Binary

Governance §13.16 explicitly rejects simplistic safe/dangerous classification.

Risk may depend on:

- detail;
- scale;
- accessibility;
- expertise;
- infrastructure;
- maturity;
- safeguards.

Historical should therefore preserve restriction rationale at appropriate resolution.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Knowledge Risk ≠ Binary Property of Information Alone.**

Risk is relational and contextual.

---

## 19. Dangerousness Can Change With Civilisational Capability

A procedure may be harmless when no one can execute it and dangerous once enabling infrastructure exists.

Or the reverse.

Candidate principle:

> **Same Historical Knowledge ≠ Same Historical Risk Across Capability States.**

This connects Source Resolution 015.

---

## 20. Historical Preservation Can Preserve Capability

Executable code, engineering plans, biological procedures or infrastructure vulnerabilities may preserve more than documentary evidence.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of Knowledge Can Preserve Operational Capability.**

This means documentary retention and capability retention cannot always be treated as equivalent.

---

## 21. Historical Importance Does Not Require General Retrievability

A dangerous object may be important for:

- accountability;
- future scholarship;
- understanding failure;
- preventing rediscovery;
- constitutional review.

But:

> **Historical Importance ≠ General Retrieval Entitlement.**

This strengthens Source Resolution 017.

---

## 22. Preserved but Non-General-Retrievable Is a Legitimate State

Historical 005 established cold storage and Historical 019 separated custody from discoverability/access.

Restricted knowledge suggests a specific state:

> **Preserved ≠ Discoverable ≠ Accessible ≠ Disclosable ≠ Publishable.**

Historical architecture should keep these dimensions separate.

---

## 23. Redaction Creates a Derivative Historical Object

Governance lists redacted operational detail as a possible response to dual-use risk.

Historical should preserve:

`Raw Source → Redaction Decision → Redacted Derivative`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Redacted Record ≠ Historical Raw Record.**

The derivative must not silently replace the source.

---

## 24. Partial Disclosure Creates a New Public-Knowledge State

When a redacted version is released, the public now knows more than before but less than Historical custody may contain.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Partial Disclosure Is a Distinct Public-Knowledge State.**

This should be time-indexed.

---

## 25. Redaction Can Alter Interpretation

Removing context may change apparent meaning.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Redaction Can Change Semantic Interpretation Even When Every Released Statement Is Authentic.**

This links Source Resolution 022.

---

## 26. Redaction Provenance Should Survive

Where legitimate, Historical should preserve:

- who authorised redaction;
- what class of information was removed;
- reason;
- review state;
- later release.

This need not reveal the redacted content to unauthorised users.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Provenance of Redaction ≠ Disclosure of Redacted Content.**

---

## 27. Leak and Authorised Disclosure Are Different Events

A classified record may become publicly known through unauthorised disclosure.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Public Exposure ≠ Historical Authorised Disclosure.**

Historical should preserve both public-knowledge state and authority state.

---

## 28. Once Public Does Not Mean Legitimately Public

Information may become widely accessible while its disclosure remains unlawful or harmful.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Availability of Information ≠ Historical Legitimacy of Its Release.**

Historical should not confuse irreversibility of exposure with retroactive authorisation.

---

## 29. Later Restriction Cannot Make Recipients Unknow

If information becomes public, later classification cannot restore the earlier information environment.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Reclassification Cannot Retroactively Restore Secrecy Already Lost.**

It may restrict future official access/distribution, but cannot rewrite what became known.

---

## 30. Historical Preservation of a Leak Does Not Require Amplification

A leak may itself be historically significant.

But preserving evidence that a leak occurred does not require Historical to reproduce dangerous leaked content to everyone.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of Unauthorised Disclosure ≠ Obligation to Republish the Disclosed Material.**

---

## 31. Disclosure Itself Can Create Irreversible Historical Change

Some information changes the world merely by becoming known.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Disclosure Can Be an Irreversible Civilisational State Transition.**

This makes disclosure provenance particularly consequential.

---

## 32. Disclosure Authority and Access Authority Are Different

A participant authorised to inspect classified material may not be authorised to publish it.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Access Permission ≠ Historical Disclosure Permission.**

This restates Source Resolution 019 in the restricted-knowledge context.

---

## 33. Automated Access Is Still Access

Historical 019 already established that machine processing protected history is access-relevant.

Therefore:

> **Historical AI Processing of Restricted Knowledge ≠ No Access Merely Because No Human Read the Raw Material.**

Machine access requires legitimate purpose and controls.

---

## 34. Derived Outputs Can Leak Restricted Information

An AI may never reproduce a source directly yet reveal:

- vulnerability;
- identity;
- secret conclusion;
- protected pattern.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Protection Must Consider Derived Information, Not Only Raw Record Disclosure.**

This is especially important for future Historical analytical systems.

---

## 35. Safe Query Interfaces May Be Preferable to Raw Release

Historical 019 suggested query interfaces can sometimes preserve utility without releasing datasets.

Restricted knowledge strengthens this.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Analytical Access Can Be More Permissive Than Historical Raw-Source Access Where Derived Outputs Are Safely Bounded.**

This is candidate architecture, not a universal rule.

---

## 36. But Query Interfaces Can Become Extraction Channels

Repeated queries may reconstruct the protected source.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Bounded Query ≠ Guaranteed Non-Disclosure.**

Aggregate, differential and cumulative extraction risks require evaluation.

---

## 37. Restriction Review Must Preserve Prior Decisions

If a classification review continues secrecy, later reviewers should be able to know:

- what was reviewed;
- why continuation was chosen;
- what evidence supported it.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Restriction Review Is Itself an Accountability Record.**

---

## 38. Repeated Continuation Can Become a Pattern

A record repeatedly reclassified without substantive review may indicate institutional inertia or concealment.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Repeated Restriction Can Become a Pattern Requiring Independent Review Without Itself Proving Abuse.**

This uses Source Resolution 014A/020 pattern logic.

---

## 39. Classification Authority Should Not Control Its Own Historical Erasure

Governance warns against classification abuse.

Historical anti-capture therefore suggests:

> **The Authority That Classifies a Record Should Not Possess Unilateral Power to Destroy the Accountability Record of That Classification.**

This is consistent with Source Resolutions 003 and 005.

---

## 40. Dangerous-Knowledge Control Must Itself Be Accountable

Governance §13.15 states:

> a system controlling dangerous knowledge should itself remain accountable.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Knowledge Restriction Must Preserve Enough Provenance to Audit the Restricting System.**

Otherwise safety control becomes invisible power.

---

## 41. Restriction Reason Can Itself Be Sensitive

Sometimes explaining exactly why a record is dangerous reveals the danger.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Restriction Provenance May Itself Require Layered Protection.**

Historical may preserve that a legitimate restricted rationale exists without exposing every operational detail.

---

## 42. Existence Visibility Can Be Dangerous

Even confirming that a secret capability, person or operation exists may create harm.

Historical 019 already separates existence visibility from content access.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Existence Metadata Can Require Classification Independent of Record Content.**

---

## 43. Declassification Can Be Partial Across Layers

A record may move from:

- existence secret;
- existence public/content secret;
- metadata public/content restricted;
- redacted content public;
- full content public.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Declassification Can Be Layered Rather Than Binary.**

---

## 44. Cross-Civilisational Disclosure Creates Additional Boundaries

Historical 018 established that shared history does not imply shared access rules.

Therefore:

> **Historical Declassification in One Civilisation ≠ Automatic Declassification in Another Civilisation.**

Shared records may have conflicting legitimate restriction states.

---

## 45. Joint Secrets Need Shared Provenance Without Necessarily Shared Authority

A jointly generated record may be subject to agreement.

Candidate principle:

> **Shared Historical Custody ≠ Unilateral Disclosure Authority.**

Exact cross-civilisational implementation remains unresolved.

---

## 46. Scientific Reproducibility Can Conflict With Restricted Knowledge

Governance §13.12 notes that full publication may be impossible for dangerous/private/protected information and proposes alternative trusted verification.

Historical should preserve:

- method;
- restricted evidence;
- verification process;
- limitations.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Public Reproducibility ≠ Historical Non-Verifiability.**

But the verification architecture itself must be auditable.

---

## 47. Restricted Evidence Can Create Epistemic Asymmetry

Some participants may know why a conclusion is supported while others cannot inspect the evidence.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Restricted Evidence Creates an Information-Asymmetry State That Should Itself Be Visible Where Safe.**

The existence of a protected basis may be public even when the basis is not.

---

## 48. “Trust Us” Is Not a Historical Verification Architecture

Where evidence cannot be public, Historical should preserve the independent verification path.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Secrecy Should Preserve Verifiability Where Disclosure Cannot Be Preserved.**

This is a candidate requirement derived from Governance's trusted-verification principle.

---

## 49. Dangerous Knowledge Can Become Safe Through Abstraction

Operational detail may be dangerous while higher-level lessons are safe.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Knowledge Can Sometimes Be Preserved at a Safer Abstraction Level Without Preserving General Access to Dangerous Operational Detail.**

This links to pattern-preserving compression.

---

## 50. Abstraction Can Also Destroy Accountability

Too much abstraction may conceal:

- who acted;
- what failed;
- what capability existed;
- why harm occurred.

Therefore:

> **Historical Safety Abstraction Must Not Erase Accountability-Critical Structure.**

This tension will need explicit specification.

## 53. Relationship to ESCP

This pass exposes several ESCP traps:

- public archive can be mistaken for complete archive;
- classification can be mistaken for truth or importance;
- old information can be assumed safe;
- public exposure can be mistaken for authorised release;
- declassification can be projected backward;
- redacted derivative can be mistaken for raw source;
- absence of public evidence can be mistaken for absence of evidence;
- restricted evidence can create invisible information asymmetry;
- query access can leak more than intended;
- safety abstraction can erase accountability.

Therefore:

> **Historical Restricted-Knowledge Completeness Requires Preserving both the information state and the permission/disclosure state through time.**

## 56. Current Finding

This pass substantially resolves the restricted-knowledge boundary.

The key architecture is not a binary choice between:

`keep secret`

and

`make public`.

Historical instead needs multiple independent states:

`Custody → Existence Visibility → Discoverability → Access → Derivation → Disclosure → Publication`.

Each can change through time.

A civilisation may therefore preserve a dangerous record for centuries, permit tightly bounded verification, later publish a redacted derivative, eventually declassify the raw source, and still retain the history showing that earlier decision-makers and citizens did **not** have the same access.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **History must be able to remember a secret without either forgetting that it existed or assuming that remembering it gives everyone the right to know it.**
