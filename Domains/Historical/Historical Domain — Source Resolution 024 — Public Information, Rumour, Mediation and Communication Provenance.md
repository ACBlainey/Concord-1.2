# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 024 — Public Information, Rumour, Mediation and Communication Provenance

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / INFORMATION-ENVIRONMENT PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines Historical requirements created by public information, rumours, mediated testimony, informational intermediaries and official civil communications.

Primary V1.2 sources include:

- `E9-03 — Oral Source Provenance and Mediation Classification.md`;
- `E9-207 — Occurrence Pattern Note: Rumour, Information and Crisis Resilience.md`;
- `E9-257 — Occurrence Pattern Note: Power, Information and Candid Speech.md`;
- `E9-319 — Occurrence Pattern Note: Intermediaries, Information Dependence and Judgment.md`;
- `Concord Civil Submission, Receipt and Future Communications Layer — Revised Development Note.md`;
- prior Historical Source Resolutions 003, 009, 010, 011, 017, 018, 019, 020, 022 and 023.

The civil communications layer is explicitly active-development/non-canonical. E9 occurrence notes preserve source observations without automatically promoting them into constitutional rules.

The central Historical question is:

> **How can civilisation remember what information circulated, who could hear whom, what was officially communicated, and what people reasonably believed without turning circulation, repetition or official status into proof of truth?**

---

## 2. Historical Circulation Is Not Historical Truth

A rumour can be historically important even when false.

It may affect:

- behaviour;
- crisis response;
- trust;
- markets;
- migration;
- governance;
- conflict.

Therefore:

> **Historical Record That Information Circulated ≠ Historical Certification That the Information Was True.**

Historical may need to preserve both the content and its epistemic status.

---

## 3. False Information Can Produce Real Historical Effects

A false report may cause real action.

Candidate chain:

`Claim → Circulation → Belief/Uncertainty → Behaviour → Consequence → Correction`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Falsity of a Claim ≠ Historical Irrelevance of Its Effects.**

---

## 4. Correction Does Not Erase Prior Information State

If a rumour is corrected later, Historical should preserve:

- what circulated;
- what evidence existed;
- who reasonably knew what;
- when correction became available;
- what actions occurred before and after correction.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Correction of Public Information ≠ Earlier Availability of Correct Information.**

This extends Source Resolutions 004, 011 and 022.

---

## 5. Historical Belief and Historical Fact Are Different Objects

A population may believe X while X is false.

Historical should distinguish:

- event/fact claim;
- evidence state;
- circulating narrative;
- measured belief;
- later finding.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Belief State ≠ Historical Reality State.**

Yet belief state may itself be historically causal.

---

## 6. Historical Information Environment Is a First-Class Context

Source Resolution 023 established that power can shape the evidence environment.

This pass adds the public information environment.

For consequential events, Historical may sometimes need to preserve:

- available official information;
- major circulating alternatives;
- access barriers;
- known corrections;
- uncertainty;
- censorship/restriction where evidenced;
- communication outages;
- mediation dependencies.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Decision Context Can Depend on the Information Environment Available at the Time.**

---

## 7. Information Quantity Is Not Information Candour

E9-257 identifies a strong asymmetry:

> **information quantity and information candour are not the same property.**

A powerful office may receive many reports while suppressing direct truthful speech.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Volume of Reporting ≠ Historical Quality or Candour of Reporting.**

---

## 8. Power Can Distort the Surviving Record Without Explicit Falsification

If participants avoid candid speech around authority, official archives may contain a systematically filtered view.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Official Record Density ≠ Historical Completeness of Contemporary Opinion or Knowledge.**

The absence of criticism from an official channel does not prove criticism did not exist.

---

## 9. Silence Has Multiple Possible Causes

Historical silence may reflect:

- agreement;
- indifference;
- fear;
- censorship;
- lack of access;
- missing records;
- cultural convention;
- no opportunity to speak.

Therefore:

> **Historical Silence ≠ Historical Consent.**

And:

> **Historical Absence of Recorded Dissent ≠ Proof of Absence of Dissent.**

---

## 10. Mediation Is Provenance

E9-03 explicitly classifies oral sources by mediation grade.

Historical should generalise:

> **Historical Mediation Level Is Part of Evidential Provenance.**

A directly attested source and a secondary paraphrase should not silently become equivalent historical evidence.

---

## 11. Source Distance Must Remain Visible

A useful chain may be:

`Event/Tradition → Participant/Witness → Recorder → Translator → Editor → Publication → Digitisation → Analyst`.

Each stage can introduce:

- selection;
- omission;
- framing;
- translation;
- compression;
- error.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Source Distance Should Remain Visible Where It Materially Affects Evidential Interpretation.**

---

## 12. Collection Date Is Not Origin Date

E9-03 explicitly warns:

> collection date is not assumed to equal origin date.

Historical should generalise:

> **Historical Recording Time ≠ Historical Origin Time.**

The time something was documented may differ substantially from the time it originated.

---

## 13. Publication Date Is Not Event Date

Likewise:

> **Historical Publication Time ≠ Historical Event Time.**

A later publication can preserve an earlier witness without becoming contemporaneous evidence.

---

## 14. Oral Tradition Requires Different Historical Provenance

An oral source may have:

- no single author;
- multiple performers;
- variants;
- changing use context;
- later recorder.

Historical should not force it into a documentary provenance model designed for authored written texts.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Provenance Equality Does Not Require Uniform Provenance Structure Across Source Forms.**

This parallels multisubstrate representation in Source Resolution 021.

---

## 15. Variants Are Not Automatically Duplicates

E9-03 says near-identical variants should be linked, while materially different contextual uses may remain distinct.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Variant ≠ Historical Duplicate by Default.**

A variant may preserve contextual or functional information.

---

## 16. Duplicate Circulation Is Not Independent Corroboration

The inverse is also important.

Ten later reports may all derive from one original claim.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Repetition ≠ Independent Historical Corroboration.**

Historical provenance should preserve shared ancestry where discoverable.

---

## 17. Information Intermediaries Can Shape Reality Models

E9-319 observes that dependence on an interpreter creates dependence on that intermediary's selection, framing and transmission.

No deception is required.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Intermediary Dependence ≠ Historical Evidence of Intermediary Deception.**

But it is a material structural fact.

---

## 18. Intermediary Selection Is Part of the Information Path

A decision-maker may never see the full source population.

Historical may need to preserve:

`Source Population → Selected Information → Intermediary Framing → Decision-Maker Model → Decision`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Decision Provenance May Require the Information-Selection Path, Not Merely the Information Ultimately Received.**

---

## 19. Translation Is One Form of Mediation, Not the Only One

Source Resolution 022 addressed semantic translation.

This pass broadens mediation to include:

- editorial selection;
- summarisation;
- agenda setting;
- filtering;
- interpretation;
- source access;
- platform ranking;
- official briefing.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Mediation Can Alter Information Without Altering the Literal Words That Survive.**

---

## 20. Omission Can Be Historically Material

An intermediary may transmit accurate statements while omitting other material evidence.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Accuracy of Transmitted Information ≠ Historical Completeness of the Information Path.**

This is an ESCP issue.

---

## 21. Official Communication Is Not Automatically True

The civil communications development note proposes provenance proving that an authority issued a notice in a particular form at a particular time.

Historical should distinguish:

`Authentic Official Communication`

from:

`Truth of Communication Content`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Authentication of an Official Message ≠ Historical Certification of Its Factual Correctness.**

---

## 22. Official Status Is Historical Metadata

Historical may preserve:

- issuer;
- authority claimed;
- timestamp;
- version;
- recipient/destination;
- authentication;
- correction/supersession.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Official Status Is Provenance, Not Truth Status.**

---

## 23. Submission Receipt Is Not Acceptance of Claim

The proposed distributed civil receipt layer proves a submission occurred.

It does not prove the submission's contents are true.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Proof of Submission ≠ Historical Proof of Submitted Claim.**

This is important for complaints, petitions, appeals and evidence filings.

---

## 24. Receipt Is Not Resolution

Likewise:

> **Historical Proof That an Authority Received a Communication ≠ Historical Proof That the Authority Addressed It.**

The return path belongs to Civil Attention/operational process history.

---

## 25. Tamper Evidence Is Not Semantic Evidence

A cryptographic hash may prove that a document has not changed since commitment.

It does not prove:

- authorship beyond the credential;
- truth;
- completeness;
- interpretation;
- legality.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Integrity Proof ≠ Historical Truth Proof.**

---

## 26. Proof Can Outlive Payload

The development note proposes preserving a hash while the larger document remains elsewhere.

This creates a Historical edge case.

If the payload is later destroyed, the receipt may still prove that some specific object once existed if a copy can be matched, but may not reveal its content.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Provenance Survival ≠ Historical Content Survival.**

This complements Source Resolution 022.

---

## 27. Content Can Survive Without Authentication

The inverse can occur: a copy of a message survives but its signature/provenance does not.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Content Survival ≠ Historical Authentication Survival.**

These should be separate record dimensions.

---

## 28. Communication Transport Is Not Civil Meaning

The development note separates transport from the civil event.

Historical should preserve the civil event where relevant without necessarily preserving every network hop.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Communication Provenance ≠ Historical Retention of Complete Transport Metadata.**

This protects privacy and avoids unnecessary technical history.

---

## 29. Transport Metadata Can Still Become Historically Material

In some cases, routing outage, censorship, delay or interception may matter.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Transport Metadata Should Be Preserved Only Where Material to the Historical Question or Required Accountability.**

Historical should not default to permanent communications surveillance.

---

## 30. Logical Identity and Network Address Must Remain Distinct

The communications source states:

`CitizenID ≠ NetworkAddress`.

Historical should preserve that distinction.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Communication Endpoint ≠ Historical Participant Identity by Default.**

A device/address is not necessarily a person.

---

## 31. Communications Technology Changes Over Time

The source deliberately avoids constitutionalising email, IP, blockchain or quantum networking.

Historical should preserve technology context without confusing implementation with civil function.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Communication Mechanism ≠ Historical Communication Function.**

---

## 32. Technology Migration Can Alter Available Metadata

A future communication system may expose far less routing metadata than today's systems.

Historical should not treat differences in metadata availability as differences in participant behaviour.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Metadata Absence Can Reflect Communication Architecture Rather Than Historical Inactivity.**

---

## 33. Rumour During Crisis Has Distinct Historical Importance

E9-207 connects rumour to crisis resilience.

Historical 011 addressed threat and emergency records.

This pass adds:

> **Historical Crisis Reconstruction May Require the Information Environment, Not Merely the Physical Threat Environment.**

People act on what they know or believe, not only on what is objectively occurring.

---

## 34. Crisis Correction Timing Matters

A correction issued after action may be historically irrelevant to the original decision.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Availability of Correct Information Must Be Time-Indexed to the Decision It Could Have Informed.**

---

## 35. Repeated False Claims Can Become Historically Powerful Without Becoming Truer

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Influence of a Claim ≠ Historical Evidential Strength of the Claim.**

Popularity, repetition and reach are different from evidential support.

---

## 36. Historical Reach Is a Separate Dimension

For a public claim, Historical may distinguish:

- existence;
- publication;
- reach;
- repetition;
- belief;
- evidential status;
- consequence.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Publication ≠ Historical Reach ≠ Historical Belief ≠ Historical Truth.**

---

## 37. Corrections Can Have Less Reach Than Original Claims

Historical should not assume that publication of a correction restored the information environment.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Correction Issued ≠ Historical Correction Received or Believed.**

Where material and measurable, reach should remain separate.

---

## 38. Historical Narratives Can Change Without Underlying Events Changing

Later evidence, cultural framing or political interpretation may alter public narratives about a fixed past event.

Historical should preserve:

- underlying evidence;
- contemporary narratives;
- later narratives;
- reasons/evidence for revision.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Narrative Change ≠ Historical Event Change.**

---

## 39. Historical Narrative Is Still Historical Information

Narratives influence:

- identity;
- policy;
- trust;
- education;
- conflict;
- reconciliation.

Therefore:

> **Historical Narrative ≠ Historical Fact, But Historical Narrative Can Be a Historical Fact About What Was Believed or Communicated.**

---

## 40. Historical Should Not Become Narrative Arbiter

Historical may preserve evidence and provenance but should not inherit unlimited authority to impose one civil narrative.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Custody of Competing Narratives ≠ Authority to Manufacture a Single Official Memory.**

Where a legitimate factual finding exists, its status should be preserved; this does not require deleting the history of competing claims.

---

## 41. Proven Falsehood and Contemporary Uncertainty Are Different

A claim may have been genuinely uncertain at T1 and disproven at T2.

Historical should not retroactively label every T1 repetition as knowing falsehood.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Falsification ≠ Historical Proof of Earlier Bad Faith.**

Intent requires separate evidence.

---

## 42. Misinformation and Deception Must Remain Distinct

Historical may distinguish:

- false information;
- mistaken information;
- unverified information;
- deceptive communication where intent is established;
- satire/fiction;
- hypothesis;
- propaganda or persuasion where appropriately evidenced.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Falsehood ≠ Historical Deception Without Evidence of Intent.**

---

## 43. Public Information Provenance Can Reveal Capture

If a civilisation's official information repeatedly derives from one intermediary or institution, Historical may expose concentration.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Information Diversity ≠ Number of Messages If the Messages Share One Upstream Source.**

This is analogous to duplicate-source provenance.

---

## 44. Independent Channels Matter

Historical can preserve whether apparently separate reports were genuinely independent.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Source Count Should Not Be Used as Corroboration Count Without Provenance Independence.**

This has research, governance and crisis implications.

---

## 45. Candid Speech Can Require Protected Channels

E9-257 suggests power can suppress candour.

Historical may therefore need to preserve the existence and effectiveness of protected reporting channels without exposing reporters unnecessarily.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Evidence of Protected Dissent ≠ Requirement for Public Identification of the Dissenter.**

This interfaces with Source Resolution 019.

---

## 46. Anonymity Changes Evidential Structure, Not Necessarily Evidential Worthlessness

An anonymous report may be weaker on some provenance dimensions but still valuable as:

- trigger;
- pattern contribution;
- protected testimony.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Anonymity ≠ Historical Non-Evidence.**

Its limitations should remain explicit.

---

## 47. Communication Failure Can Be a Historical Cause

If a warning existed but did not reach the relevant authority, the history differs from a warning never being generated.

Candidate chain:

`Signal Generated → Transmission Attempted → Delivery Failed → Decision Without Signal`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Receipt ≠ Historical Non-Existence of Communication.**

---

## 48. Receipt Does Not Prove Comprehension

Even successful delivery may not mean the message was understood.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Delivery ≠ Historical Comprehension.**

Translation, accessibility, overload and context can intervene.

---

## 49. Comprehension Does Not Prove Acceptance

Likewise:

> **Historical Comprehension ≠ Historical Agreement or Action.**

This gives a richer communication-state chain.

---

## 50. Candidate Historical Communication-State Chain

A useful model is:

`Information/Event`
→ `Source Claim`
→ `Recording`
→ `Mediation/Selection`
→ `Transmission`
→ `Delivery`
→ `Comprehension`
→ `Belief/Disbelief/Uncertainty`
→ `Action/Non-Action`
→ `Correction/Revision`
→ `Historical Interpretation`.

No stage should silently substitute for another.

## 53. Relationship to ESCP

This pass exposes several ESCP failures:

- official archives can omit candid dissent;
- many reports can share one upstream source;
- accurate transmitted information can omit material alternatives;
- later correction can be projected backward;
- collection date can be mistaken for origin date;
- silence can be mistaken for consent;
- publication can be mistaken for reach;
- reach can be mistaken for belief;
- belief can be mistaken for truth;
- non-receipt can be mistaken for non-existence;
- communication receipt can be mistaken for issue resolution.

Therefore:

> **Historical Information Completeness Depends on the Provenance and structure of the information environment, not merely the volume of surviving messages.**

## 56. Current Finding

This pass adds the **historical information environment** as a distinct layer.

Civilisation does not act only on events.

It acts on:

- what was reported;
- what was omitted;
- what was translated;
- what reached decision-makers;
- what circulated publicly;
- what people believed;
- what authorities authenticated;
- what corrections arrived in time.

Those states are historically consequential even when the information itself was false.

At the same time, Historical must not confuse popularity, repetition, official status or cryptographic authenticity with truth.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **History must remember not only what happened, but what information about what happened was available, mediated, believed, doubted, corrected or never successfully received — without mistaking any of those states for the event itself.**
