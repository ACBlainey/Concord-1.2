## 3. Judicial Evidence Requires Historical-Grade Provenance

The Judiciary requires material evidence to retain sufficient provenance for legitimate examination. Its questions include:
- origin;
- collector;
- preservation;
- transformation;
- summarisation;
- computational generation;
- translation;
- retention of the original;
- chain of custody;
- producing methodology;
- uncertainty;
- contestability.

This strongly supports a Historical evidentiary-custody interface.

However:

> **Historical Custody of Evidence ≠ Judicial Determination of Evidence**

The Judiciary owns adjudicative use, evidential interpretation and legal consequence. Historical can preserve the evidentiary object, provenance, transformations and access history without becoming a court.

A likely interface is:

**Evidence Creation/Collection → Operational Evidentiary Use → Protected Historical Custody → Authorised Judicial Retrieval → Judicial Examination → Decision/Reasoning → Historical Preservation**

The exact transfer threshold remains domain-specific.

## 4. Judicial Reasoning Is Explicitly Civilisational Memory

The current Judiciary explicitly preserves dissent as part of constitutional memory.

Its rationale is not that dissent is true. It is that minority reasoning may later reveal alternative interpretation, overlooked evidence, different rights analysis, future consequences, weaknesses in majority reasoning, limitations of precedent or grounds for correction.

It states the distinction in substance:

> **Preservation Is Not Endorsement; It Is Information Retention.**

Judicial memory systems are expected to make minority reasoning discoverable so later Judicial Stewards can determine what was disputed, what alternative reasoning existed, why it was rejected, whether circumstances changed and whether later evidence justifies reconsideration.

This is a direct Historical function candidate.

## 5. Judicial Measurement and Historical Metrics

The Judiciary treats Constitutional Concordance as diagnostic rather than as a performance score.

It may examine:
- unanimous and divided determinations;
- recurring dissent;
- recurring interpretive divergence;
- differences across courts/jurisdictions;
- changes in reasoning over time;
- abstention patterns;
- preliminary/final reasoning differences;
- common-source dependence.

This is a useful example of the proposed metrics boundary:

**Operational system produces current measurements → domain uses them operationally → Historical preserves time-anchored measurement states and their provenance.**

Historical should not convert preserved metrics into authority, truth or performance ranking.

> **Historical Metric ≠ Normative Judgement**

> **Recorded Metric ≠ Authority to Optimise the Institution Around It**

## 6. Continuity and Archive Custody Are Explicitly Separated From Judicial Authority

The current Judiciary states that civilisation may require continuity mechanisms preserving:
- constitutional documents;
- judicial records;
- legal definitions;
- precedent;
- institutional procedures;
- identity of legitimate authority.

It then explicitly warns that continuity administrators do not thereby gain judicial authority:

> **Custody of law is not authority to determine law.**

This is exceptionally strong support for the Historical Domain model.

The same structural rule generalises:

> **Custody of a Record ≠ Authority of the System That Created or Uses the Record**

Historical can therefore be a common custodian without becoming Governance, Judiciary, Civil Attention, Epistemic review or operational management.

## 7. Legal and Semantic Versioning

The Judiciary also depends on temporally anchored legal definitions and provenance of semantic change.

This suggests Historical must preserve not only documents but **time/version context** sufficient to answer questions such as:
- which rule/version applied at a particular time;
- whether a definition was amended;
- what prior meaning existed;
- what later representation derives from which original;
- whether semantic drift occurred.

This aligns directly with KCS typed relations and state/version provenance.

## 8. V1 Historical Judiciary Confirmation

The earlier V1 Judiciary collection independently supports the same direction.

It treats each judgement as contributing to civilisation's constitutional knowledge, preserves minority opinions, uses patterns of agreement/disagreement as constitutional information, and frames judicial transparency as creating a durable body of reasoning for future constitutional learning.

This is therefore not merely a new interpretation created by the Historical-domain exercise. Historical-memory functions are already embedded in the Judiciary lineage.

## 9. Historical–Judiciary Boundary

### Historical candidate responsibilities
- preserve authoritative judicial records and versions;
- preserve submitted/accepted evidentiary objects where retention is legitimate;
- preserve chain-of-custody and transformation provenance;
- preserve judgements and reasoned opinions;
- preserve dissent/minority reasoning;
- preserve time-anchored precedent and legal/semantic versions;
- preserve historical judicial metrics and diagnostic states;
- enforce applicable protected-space/access conditions at the custody boundary;
- produce authorised/redacted/bounded retrievals;
- preserve audit history of consequential access/transformation where required.

### Judiciary responsibilities retained
- evidential admissibility and weight;
- findings of fact;
- legal interpretation;
- adjudication;
- remedies;
- judicial review;
- reopening decisions;
- jurisdiction;
- judicial authority;
- legal consequences of evidence;
- determination of what legal record must be retained where that is a judicial/legal requirement.

Historical therefore supports Judiciary without absorbing it.

## 10. Black-Box Evidence Consequence

The judiciary source strengthens the plausibility of the camera/sensor black-box proposal because it already requires preserved originals, transformation visibility and chain of custody.

A future Historical black-box service could potentially preserve:
- protected raw source;
- immutable/time-anchored provenance;
- transformation history;
- authorised extraction record;
- anonymised/redacted derivative;
- bounded relation between derivative and protected source.

But the camera architecture remains untested and the legitimate retention/access thresholds remain unresolved.

## 11. New Candidate Historical Principle

The Judiciary pass suggests a general domain rule:

> **Preserve the Record; Preserve Its Provenance; Preserve Its Historical State; Do Not Inherit the Authority of the System That Produced It.**

This appears compatible with KCS, Continuity and FPA and should be tested against other domains before becoming a formal Historical invariant.
