# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 023 — Investigation, Exceptional Access, Suspicion and Rehabilitation History

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / INVESTIGATIVE-POWER PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines Historical requirements created by investigation, exceptional access, predictive investigative modelling, evidentiary escalation and rehabilitation.

Primary V1.2 sources include:

- `Support-to-Investigation Firewall.md`;
- `Evidence-Conditioned Investigative Modelling — High-Risk Research.md`;
- `ETC01 Transgression Justice and Rehabilitation.md`;
- prior Historical Source Resolutions 002, 008, 011, 012, 019, 020 and 022.

The investigative-model sources are explicitly candidate/high-risk research. Historical uses them to resolve record and provenance requirements without treating the proposed investigative capability as adopted architecture.

The central question is:

> **How can civilisation preserve enough investigative history to audit exceptional power, correct false suspicion and learn from failure without converting Historical into a permanent suspicion database?**

---

## 2. Support Is Not Suspicion

The Support-to-Investigation Firewall states:

> **Support is not suspicion.**

Historical must preserve this category boundary.

A support record does not acquire investigative meaning merely because it later exists in Historical custody.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Custody of Support Information ≠ Historical Classification of the Participant as Suspicious.**

---

## 3. Historical Custody Must Not Collapse Purpose Boundaries

Support information may exist for:

- health;
- housing;
- financial support;
- maintenance;
- supported decision-making.

Its later historical survival does not erase its original purpose.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation ≠ Retrospective Repurposing.**

This strengthens Source Resolution 019.

---

## 4. Exceptional Access Is a Historical Event

The firewall explicitly requires exceptional access to be a discrete, authorised, provenance-recorded event.

Historical should preserve, where legitimate:

- authority basis;
- case/purpose;
- scope;
- time;
- data accessed;
- expiry;
- oversight;
- later correction.

Candidate principle:

> **Consequential Exceptional Access Should Leave Historical Provenance Even Where the Accessed Content Remains Protected.**

---

## 5. Provenance of Power Can Survive Without Universal Disclosure of Content

The source proposes dual provenance:

- restricted investigative provenance;
- external oversight provenance.

This yields:

> **Historical Auditability of Exceptional Power ≠ Public Disclosure of Protected Evidence.**

Historical can preserve that power was exercised without exposing all underlying identity/content.

---

## 6. Secrecy Increases the Need for Durable Oversight History

The source states that legitimate investigative secrecy should increase independent oversight rather than remove it.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Secrecy ≠ Historical Unaccountability.**

A temporarily secret act may still require durable restricted provenance.

---

## 7. Later Notification Is a Distinct Historical State

The source leaves later participant notification as a future legal question.

Historical should distinguish:

- access occurred;
- participant not notified because secrecy remained active;
- secrecy expired;
- notification decision;
- notification performed/withheld.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical End of Secrecy ≠ Automatic Historical Publication.**

Expiry may trigger review, not universal disclosure.

---

## 8. Access Without Copying and Access With Copying Are Different Histories

The firewall warns that copying protected data creates correction and deletion problems.

Historical should distinguish:

`bounded query/access`

from:

`derived/copy created and retained downstream`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Access Event ≠ Historical Replication Event.**

This matters for later correction, deletion and privacy accounting.

---

## 9. Replicated Evidence Creates Propagation Obligations

If a source record is corrected, downstream investigative copies may become stale.

Historical may need to preserve:

- source version;
- copy event;
- correction event;
- propagation state.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Correction of Source Evidence ≠ Automatic Correction of Every Derived Copy Unless Propagation Is Tracked.**

---

## 10. Evidentiary Status Must Survive Transfer

Support records may contain:

- observation;
- participant statement;
- support-worker inference;
- hypothesis;
- administrative classification.

Exceptional access must not silently transform those into established fact.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Transfer Into an Investigative Context Must Not Upgrade the Epistemic Status of the Transferred Information.**

---

## 11. Suspicion Is a Historical State, Not a Historical Identity

A participant may become a subject of investigation and later be cleared.

Historical should preserve the case state without turning temporary suspicion into permanent participant character.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Suspicion ≠ Persistent Participant Identity Attribute.**

---

## 12. Clearance Must Not Erase the Investigation

Deleting every trace of a false suspicion would prevent audit of:

- why suspicion arose;
- what power was exercised;
- what model failed;
- what harm occurred.

But retaining the suspicion as an active descriptor is also wrong.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Clearance Should End the Operational Force of Suspicion Without Erasing Accountability-Critical History of the Suspicion.**

---

## 13. Wrongly Suspected Cases Are High-Value Historical Evidence

The investigative-modelling source explicitly treats participants strongly modelled as suspicious but later cleared as model-failure evidence.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical False Suspicion Is Evidence About the Investigative System, Not Evidence Against the Cleared Participant.**

This is a crucial inversion.

---

## 14. Historical Must Preserve Who or What Was Wrong

Where a participant was cleared, later systems should not retrieve the old case as though it remains evidence of participant dangerousness.

Instead the historical value may attach primarily to:

- investigative process;
- evidence interpretation;
- model;
- authority decision;
- oversight failure.

Candidate principle:

> **The Historical Subject of a Failed Investigation May Shift From the Participant to the Investigative Process.**

---

## 15. Prediction Is Not Evidence of Completion

The source strongly distinguishes predicted branches from completed acts.

Historical should preserve:

> **Historical Prediction of Conduct ≠ Historical Record of Conduct.**

A modelled future must never silently enter biography as an event that occurred.

---

## 16. Unrealised Branches Need Explicit Ontological Status

If retained for audit/research, predicted branches should be labelled as:

- model output;
- hypothetical;
- counterfactual;
- unrealised.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Model Branch ≠ Historical Event.**

This links Source Resolution 022's representation-status requirement.

---

## 17. No Historical Precriminalisation

A participant should not acquire a permanent historical “risk identity” because a model once predicted wrongdoing.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Predictive Risk ≠ Historical Guilt.**

And:

> **Historical Risk Score ≠ Historical Character Record.**

---

## 18. Investigative Attention Alters the Evidence Environment

The source identifies circular evidence:

`Model Flags Participant → More Investigation → More Recorded Incidents → Model Confidence Increases`.

Historical must preserve exposure to observation/investigation where it materially affects data generation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Observation Density Can Be Partly Produced by Historical Surveillance Density.**

This is a new and important pattern constraint.

---

## 19. Recorded Incidence Is Not Necessarily Underlying Incidence

If one population is watched more heavily, it may generate more records.

Therefore:

> **Historical Recorded Event Frequency ≠ Historical Underlying Event Frequency Without Observation Context.**

This extends Source Resolution 014A.

---

## 20. Historical Data Can Be Endogenous to Authority

The exercise of authority can create the data later used to justify more authority.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Evidence Environment Can Be Altered by the Investigative Process Itself.**

Historical pattern analysis must preserve this possibility.

---

## 21. Authority Feedback Loops Need Provenance

A dangerous loop is:

`Prediction → Investigation → More Data → Higher Prediction → More Authority`.

The source rejects authority escalation through model recursion.

Historical should preserve each transition separately.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Recursion Must Not Be Misrepresented as Independent Evidence Accumulation.**

---

## 22. Model-on-Model Inference Compounds Uncertainty

The source gives:

`Digital Twin inference → investigative risk model`.

Historical should preserve inference lineage.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Derived Inference Should Preserve Its Upstream Model Dependencies.**

Otherwise a multi-stage inference can appear to be direct observation.

---

## 23. Model Version Is Part of Investigative History

A branch may depend on the model version used at the time.

Historical should preserve:

- model/version;
- inputs;
- assumptions;
- output;
- investigator interpretation;
- authority action.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Investigative Outcome Without Model-Version Provenance Can Be Incomplete.**

---

## 24. Investigator Interpretation Is Separate From Model Output

A model may suggest a branch; an investigator decides how to interpret it.

Historical should distinguish:

`Model Output → Human/Agent Interpretation → Evidential Action → Authority Decision`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Model Output ≠ Historical Investigator Conclusion.**

---

## 25. Investigative Hypotheses Need Contradictory Evidence

The source requires disconfirmation and multiple explanations.

Historical should preserve material evidence against the favoured branch.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Investigative Provenance Should Preserve Material Disconfirming Evidence, Not Merely the Evidence Supporting the Final Theory.**

This is anti-hindsight and anti-capture.

---

## 26. Final Finding Must Not Rewrite Earlier Hypothesis State

If branch C eventually proves correct, Historical should not make it appear that C was always obvious.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Resolution of an Investigation Must Not Erase the Genuine Alternative Hypotheses That Existed Under Earlier Evidence.**

---

## 27. Case-Specific Modelling and Population Profiling Are Different

The high-risk source prefers evidence-conditioned, case-specific modelling rather than population-conditioned suspicion.

Historical should preserve that distinction.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Case Analysis ≠ Historical Permission to Generalise Suspicion to Similar Participants.**

---

## 28. Case Learning Must Remove Investigative Force

Completed cases may legitimately produce structural knowledge.

But:

> **Historical Learning From an Investigation ≠ Preservation of Investigative Suspicion Against the Participant.**

The lesson and the participant case are different objects.

---

## 29. Investigative Data Must Not Silently Become Universal Training Data

The source explicitly rejects automatic secondary use.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Custody of Investigative Data ≠ Authority to Reuse It for General Predictive Training.**

Future research use requires its own legitimate basis.

---

## 30. Historical Testing Requires Temporal Blinding Discipline

The source proposes historical case testing of models.

A model tested on old cases can accidentally access outcome information that was unavailable during the original investigation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Investigative Replay Must Restrict the Test to Information Available at the Simulated Historical Time.**

This extends Source Resolution 011.

---

## 31. Historical Case Testing Can Leak the Answer

If a dataset includes post-resolution features, the model may appear highly predictive only because the future leaked into the past.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Predictive Validation Must Guard Against Temporal Outcome Leakage.**

This is a specific methodological Historical requirement.

---

## 32. Non-Deployment Can Be Successful Historical Learning

The source explicitly says rejection is a valid outcome.

Historical should preserve:

- tested;
- found too risky;
- not deployed.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Deployment of a High-Risk Capability Can Represent Successful Civilisational Learning.**

---

## 33. Historical Preservation of Rejected Models Does Not Authorise Reuse

A dangerous investigative model may be retained for research/accountability.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of a Rejected Investigative Capability ≠ Permission to Reactivate or Deploy It.**

This mirrors threat/emergency boundaries.

---

## 34. Executable Historical Models Require Special Treatment

If the historical artifact remains executable, retention can preserve capability as well as evidence.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Custody of Executable Investigative Technology ≠ Ordinary Documentary Custody.**

This interfaces with Source Resolution 015 and dangerous-knowledge questions.

---

## 35. Rights Cost Is Part of Historical Evaluation

The investigative source proposes evaluating not only detection but:

- privacy loss;
- false accusation;
- chilling effects;
- discriminatory impact;
- unnecessary investigation.

Historical should preserve material rights-cost evidence alongside performance.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Investigative Effectiveness ≠ Historical Net Legitimacy.**

The Judiciary/Governance determine legitimacy; Historical preserves the relevant evidence dimensions.

---

## 36. Chilling Effects Are Historical Outcomes

If support use falls after investigative access policy changes, that is historically relevant.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Policy Outcome Includes Behavioural Effects on Willingness to Seek Support Where Material.**

This may be observable only at aggregate level.

---

## 37. Absence of Support Use Can Be Produced by Fear of Investigation

Therefore:

> **Historical Reduction in Support Demand ≠ Historical Reduction in Support Need.**

This is a significant measurement warning.

---

## 38. Privacy Policy Can Alter the Observed Dataset

If people avoid a system because they distrust its privacy boundary, Historical records may become less complete.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Data Availability Can Be Endogenous to Historical Trust.**

This is another genuinely new relational dimension.

---

## 39. Enforcement Can Alter the Population Being Measured

Investigation, detention, sanction or exclusion can change later observed behaviour and participation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Post-Intervention Behaviour ≠ Untreated Baseline Behaviour.**

Historical causal analysis should preserve intervention state.

---

## 40. Rehabilitation History Must Not Become Permanent Stigma

ETC01 treats rehabilitation as restoration of cooperative agency, not reciprocal harm.

Historical may need to preserve enough to evaluate the justice process while avoiding permanent social punishment through archival exposure.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Record of Transgression ≠ Permanent Civil Disqualification.**

Any present consequence must come from current legitimate authority, not mere archival existence.

---

## 41. Offence Topology Is Not Person Diagnosis

ETC01 explicitly states:

> **Offence Topology ≠ Person Diagnosis ≠ Rehabilitation Prescription.**

Historical should preserve this distinction.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Classification of an Act ≠ Historical Diagnosis of the Participant.**

---

## 42. Rehabilitation Assessment Is a Separate Historical Object

A transgression may engage ethical dimensions, while individual rehabilitation requires evidence about:

- understanding;
- capability;
- behavioural regulation;
- environment;
- demonstrated restoration.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Transgression Record ≠ Historical Proof of Persistent Deficit.**

---

## 43. Restoration Changes Current State Without Erasing Past State

A participant may legitimately move from:

`Transgression → Accountability → Rehabilitation → Restored Standing`.

Historical should preserve the chain.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Restoration Must Be Able to Coexist With Historical Memory of the Earlier Transgression.**

But earlier history must not automatically override restored current standing.

---

## 44. Historical Memory Can Otherwise Become Extra-Judicial Punishment

If every past transgression remains universally discoverable and consequential forever, Historical itself becomes a sanction mechanism.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Discoverability Must Not Silently Create Punishment Beyond Legitimately Authorised Consequences.**

This is a major connection to Source Resolution 019.

---

## 45. Expired Sanction and Historical Record Are Different States

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Survival of a Sanction Record ≠ Continuing Sanction.**

The record may remain restricted for accountability while its operational effect ends.

---

## 46. Correction of Wrongful Findings Requires Strong Historical Separation

If a conviction/finding is overturned, Historical should preserve:

- original finding;
- authority and evidence at the time;
- challenge;
- new evidence;
- reversal;
- current status.

But retrieval should not present the overturned state as current.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Record of an Overturned Finding ≠ Current Finding Against the Participant.**

---

## 47. Historical Retrieval Context Matters

A raw search for a participant's name could surface obsolete suspicion or overturned findings without their current status.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Retrieval of Consequential Participant Records Should Preserve Material Current-Status Context.**

This is not historical rewriting; it prevents decontextualised retrieval.

---

## 48. Current Status Must Not Erase Historical State

The inverse remains true.

A correction layer should not delete the earlier state when accountability requires it.

Candidate principle:

> **Current Exoneration Context ≠ Erasure of the Historical Process That Required Exoneration.**

Both can coexist under access controls.

## 52. Relationship to ESCP

This pass exposes several ESCP traps:

- support data can be mistaken for suspicion data;
- surveillance density can be mistaken for event density;
- model output can be mistaken for observation;
- repeated investigation can manufacture apparent confirming data;
- final findings can erase genuine earlier uncertainty;
- reduced support use can be mistaken for reduced need;
- historical case replay can leak future information;
- offence classification can be mistaken for person diagnosis.

Therefore:

> **Historical Investigative Completeness Requires Preserving How the Evidence Environment Was Produced, Not Merely the Evidence That Survived.**

## 55. Current Finding

This pass reveals an important additional Historical problem.

History does not merely observe systems from outside them.

Sometimes the system being remembered **changed the evidence environment that history later sees**.

Investigation can increase observation.

Surveillance can increase recorded incidents.

Fear of investigation can reduce support-seeking.

A model can direct attention toward the data that later appears to confirm the model.

A sanction can change subsequent behaviour.

Therefore Historical must sometimes preserve not only:

`What evidence existed?`

but:

`What processes caused this evidence to exist, and what processes may have caused other evidence not to exist?`

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **A civilisation's historical record can be shaped by the powers it exercised; therefore the amount of recorded evidence is not always independent of who was watched, who felt safe enough to speak, and what the system expected to find.**
