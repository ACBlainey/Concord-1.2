# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 020 — Recursive Civil Attention, Case Learning and Responsibility History

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / RECURSIVE-LEARNING PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines Historical requirements created by:

- Civil Attention resolution and feedback;
- possible recursive/internal Civil Attention;
- case-learning;
- repeated failure;
- upstream architectural tracing;
- responsibility-resolution history.

Primary V1.2 sources include:

- `12_Civil_Attention/04_Central_Resolution_and_Feedback/README.md`;
- `12_Civil_Attention/04_Central_Resolution_and_Feedback/Central Resolution Record — Minimum Schema.md`;
- `08_Active_Development/00_SKETCH_IDEAS/Sketch Idea — Internal and Recursive Civil Attention Applications.md`;
- `08_Active_Development/.../4. Case-Learning and Structural Knowledge System.md`;
- `08_Active_Development/.../Architectural Ethics and Upstream Responsibility — Development Note 001.md`;
- prior Historical Source Resolutions 006, 009, 013, 014A and 019.

The key new question is:

> **How can civilisation learn that apparently separate cases form a repeated structural failure without turning Historical into a permanent identifiable dossier of every participant involved?**

---

## 2. Civil Attention Already Separates Resolution Oversight From Domain Judgment

The central resolution architecture states:

> **Oversight of Resolution ≠ Substitution for Domain Judgment.**

Historical inherits the same boundary.

Historical may preserve:

- issue state;
- routing;
- responsible functions;
- dependencies;
- domain returns;
- challenge;
- reopening;
- feedback;
- overall resolution.

It does not thereby acquire authority to resolve the issue.

> **Historical Resolution Memory ≠ Issue-Resolution Authority.**

---

## 3. Central Resolution Records Are Historical Seeds

The Central Resolution Record already preserves much of the structure needed for later reconstruction:

- original receipt/provenance;
- Issue ID;
- Domain Action Objects;
- responsible functions;
- Responsibility Resolution Object;
- cross-domain dependencies;
- significant deferral history;
- domain returns;
- overall resolution;
- reopening condition;
- feedback state.

This suggests a clean lifecycle:

`Active Civil Attention Resolution Record → Operational Closure/Transfer Threshold → Historical Resolution Record`.

The active record remains operational while the issue is live.

Historical preserves its durable temporal/provenance state after or during appropriate archival transition.

---

## 4. Historical Should Preserve Material Resolution State, Not Necessarily Every Workflow Event

Civil Attention is not a universal project-management system.

Likewise Historical should not become a universal workflow log.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Resolution Traceability ≠ Permanent Preservation of Every Operational Micro-Event.**

Material transitions, dependencies, decisions, deferrals, challenges and outcomes matter more than exhaustive low-value activity logging.

---

## 5. Internal Civil Attention Is Currently Candidate Architecture

The internal/recursive Civil Attention sketch proposes:

`Participant/Worker/Function → Common Internal Attention → Issue → Appropriate Owner(s) → Action/Processing → Mandatory Return → Resolution/Feedback`.

But the source explicitly says:

- this does not amend the graduated core;
- recursive use requires development and transfer testing;
- it should not yet be claimed as validated.

Historical must preserve that status.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of a Candidate Recursive Application ≠ Validation of That Application.**

---

## 6. Recursive Instantiation Could Create Nested Historical Chains

If Civil Attention is later validly instantiated inside:

- departments;
- infrastructure systems;
- AI operations;
- contractors;
- governance functions;

then one civil issue may have nested attention chains.

Historical should preserve relationships without flattening them.

Candidate principle:

> **Nested Attention Processes Should Preserve Parent/Child Provenance Without Being Collapsed Into One Undifferentiated Case.**

---

## 7. Internal Reporting Can Become Civilisationally Significant

An internal report may begin as a local operational issue.

If repeated across multiple units, it may reveal:

- design failure;
- resource shortage;
- unsafe procedure;
- missing upstream capability;
- institutional neglect.

Therefore:

> **Local Operational History Can Acquire Later Civilisational Historical Significance Through Pattern.**

This directly extends Source Resolution 014A.

---

## 8. Later Significance Must Not Be Projected Backward

A minor local report at T1 may only become important after similar cases appear at T2–T10.

Historical should preserve:

- what the case meant at T1;
- later pattern recognition;
- subsequent reclassification.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Historical Significance ≠ Contemporary Recognition of Significance.**

This prevents hindsight distortion.

---

## 9. Case Learning Separates the Person From the Lesson

The Case-Learning architecture states:

> **Civilisation should learn from the structure of a participant's experience without unnecessarily retaining the participant as the object of that learning.**

Historical should preserve this separation.

A case can produce:

- participant-specific operational record;
- temporary anonymised case study;
- structural lesson;
- aggregate pattern;
- methodological learning.

These are different historical objects.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Case Provenance ≠ Permanent Identifiable Case Retention.**

---

## 10. Learning Can Survive After Identifiable Case Retention Ends

The Case-Learning architecture explicitly permits structural knowledge to survive after the identifiable case representation is no longer needed.

Therefore:

> **Destruction or expiry of an identifiable learning representation need not destroy the legitimate structural lesson derived from it.**

But provenance must remain sufficient to understand the lesson's evidential basis without silently rebuilding identity.

---

## 11. Structural Knowledge Needs Provenance Without Unnecessary Identity

The source asks structural knowledge to preserve:

- where a pattern came from;
- how many cases supported it;
- case type;
- contradictory evidence;
- uncertainty.

It explicitly says provenance should not require unnecessary identity.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Structural Provenance Should Preserve Evidential Lineage at the Minimum Identity Resolution Necessary.**

---

## 12. Rare Cases Are Historically Important and Privacy-Sensitive

Rare cases may expose missing branches in civilisation's model of reality.

They are also easier to re-identify.

Therefore:

> **Historical Rarity Can Increase Both Epistemic Value and Privacy Risk.**

This is a genuine dual constraint.

Historical cannot simply maximise either preservation or anonymisation without considering the other.

---

## 13. Outliers Can Be Historical Probes

The Case-Learning architecture treats outliers as possible evidence of model incompleteness.

Historical should therefore preserve enough information to distinguish:

- ordinary error;
- data-quality problem;
- rare legitimate case;
- new structural branch;
- unresolved anomaly.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Outlier ≠ Historical Error by Default.**

This extends Source Resolution 009.

---

## 14. Repeated Cases Can Create a New Historical Object

Suppose ten separate cases reveal the same structural barrier.

The pattern itself becomes new information.

Historical should be capable of representing:

`Case A + Case B + ... + Case J → Structural Pattern P`.

Candidate principle:

> **Repeated Historical Cases Can Produce a Higher-Order Historical Pattern That Is Not Contained in Any Single Case.**

Again, pattern is information.

---

## 15. Pattern Formation Needs Temporal Provenance

Historical should preserve:

- when each contributing case occurred;
- when the pattern was first suspected;
- when it became supported;
- contradictory cases;
- when action was taken.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Pattern Existence ≠ Historical Pattern Recognition.**

A pattern may have existed before civilisation noticed it.

---

## 16. Pattern Recognition Creates Accountability Questions

If a repeated failure existed for years before recognition, Historical may later ask:

- was it detectable?
- did systems possess the relevant evidence?
- were cases isolated by architecture?
- did aggregation fail?
- were warnings ignored?

But Historical must not assume culpability.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Detectability ≠ Historical Responsibility.**

---

## 17. Responsibility Requires More Than Dependency

Architectural Ethics provides a crucial separation:

> **Functional dependency ≠ causal contribution ≠ decision authority ≠ ethical responsibility ≠ legal liability.**

Historical must preserve those relations separately.

A historical graph should not collapse them into one “responsible party” field.

---

## 18. Historical Responsibility Is a Multi-Relation Graph

For a significant failure, Historical may need to preserve:

- functional owner;
- causal contributors;
- decision authorities;
- information holders;
- actions/omissions;
- constraints;
- alternatives;
- foreseeability;
- corrective capacity;
- ethical findings;
- legal findings.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Responsibility ≠ Single Attribution Edge.**

---

## 19. Responsibility Findings Must Be Time-Indexed

Foreseeability and available alternatives depend on the information and capability available at the time.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Responsibility Must Be Evaluated Against the Decision Environment That Existed at the Time, Not Merely Against Later Knowledge.**

This extends Historical 011.

---

## 20. Later Discovery of Upstream Failure Must Not Rewrite Earlier Local Knowledge

A frontline actor may have appeared responsible before an upstream architecture defect was discovered.

Historical should preserve:

`Initial Local Interpretation → Upstream Investigation → Revised Causal Model → Responsibility Finding`.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Causal Understanding Should Correct Current Interpretation Without Erasing Earlier Evidential State.**

---

## 21. Upstream Tracing Must Not Become Infinite Historical Blame

Architectural Ethics explicitly warns against tracing every failure indefinitely upward until “civilisation” becomes responsible for everything.

Historical should preserve the stopping logic.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Causal Depth Must Be Materially Bounded.**

An upstream relationship should be retained as causally relevant only where supported as material to the case.

---

## 22. Distributed Responsibility Is a Legitimate Historical State

Some failures may involve:

- primary responsibility;
- contributory responsibility;
- shared responsibility;
- uncertain responsibility;
- systemic coordination failure;
- no established responsibility.

Historical must support these without forcing one culprit.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Responsibility Can Be Distributed, Partial, Uncertain or Unresolved.**

---

## 23. Systemic Failure Must Not Erase Individual Agency

Architectural Ethics warns against explaining all behaviour as architecture.

Historical should preserve individual action where material.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Structural Explanation ≠ Erasure of Individual Agency.**

---

## 24. Individual Blame Must Not Erase Systemic Constraint

The inverse also matters.

A final actor may have operated under:

- missing resources;
- broken information flow;
- conflicting rules;
- inadequate tools;
- upstream design constraints.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Individual Action ≠ Complete Historical Explanation of Outcome.**

---

## 25. Responsibility Resolution and Issue Resolution Are Different

Historical 006 established:

> **Responsibility Resolution ≠ Substantive Case Resolution.**

A system can determine who owns the next action without deciding:

- whether the complaint is true;
- who caused the underlying problem;
- who is legally liable.

Historical must preserve the distinction among:

- routing responsibility;
- operational responsibility;
- causal contribution;
- ethical responsibility;
- legal liability.

---

## 26. Repeated Routing Failure Is Historical Information

If Civil Attention repeatedly cannot identify an owner, that pattern may expose:

- missing domain;
- overlapping authority;
- architectural gap;
- unclear interface.

Candidate principle:

> **Repeated Responsibility-Resolution Failure Can Be Evidence of an Architectural Boundary Defect.**

This is a strong interface between Civil Attention, CDT and Historical.

---

## 27. Historical Can Detect Boundary Failure Without Owning the Repair

Historical pattern analysis may identify that issues repeatedly stall at the same interface.

But:

> **Historical Detection of an Architectural Defect ≠ Historical Authority to Redesign the Architecture.**

The finding should return through Civil Attention/development/governance as appropriate.

---

## 28. Closure Quality Can Be Historically Analysed

The Central Resolution system detects:

- no owner;
- disputed ownership;
- partial completion presented as whole resolution;
- closure without legitimate disposition;
- cross-domain disagreement.

Historical can preserve aggregate patterns of these failures.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Closure Count ≠ Historical Resolution Quality.**

A civilisation reporting “95% of cases closed” may still have poor resolution.

---

## 29. Feedback History Is Part of Procedural History

Civil Attention requires participant-facing feedback where legitimate.

Historical may preserve whether:

- feedback was provided;
- current state was explained;
- responsible function was identified;
- next review condition was supplied.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Process Completion Includes Material Return-to-Participant State Where the Architecture Requires Return.**

This does not mean preserving every message forever.

---

## 30. Missing Return Is a Historical Event

The Civil Attention core requires return paths.

If a routed issue disappears without a return, that absence can be diagnostically important.

Candidate principle:

> **Failure to Return Required Disposition Is Historical Process Information, Not Merely Missing Data.**

---

## 31. Reopening Creates a New State, Not Erasure

Historical 006 already established:

> **Reopening Extends the Historical Chain; It Does Not Rewrite the Earlier Case State.**

This pass strengthens it.

Repeated reopening may itself reveal:

- premature closure;
- ineffective remedy;
- recurring cause;
- incomplete scope.

Candidate principle:

> **Reopening Frequency Can Be a Structural Historical Signal Without Proving the Cause of Failure.**

---

## 32. Deferral History Matters

The Central Resolution Record preserves significant deferral history.

Historical 013 established:

> **Replacing a Review Date Must Not Erase the History of Deferral.**

For Civil Attention, repeated deferral may reveal capacity or priority problems.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Delay Must Preserve Material Deferral Decisions, Not Merely Final Elapsed Time.**

---

## 33. Case Learning and Historical Are Not the Same System

Case Learning actively derives structural lessons.

Historical preserves the temporal/evidential basis from which those lessons can be reconstructed and reviewed.

Candidate boundary:

> **Case Learning Generates Structural Knowledge; Historical Preserves the History and Provenance of That Learning.**

Historical should not silently become the analytic authority that determines lessons.

---

## 34. Structural Lessons Have Their Own Lifecycle

A case-derived lesson may move through:

`Candidate Pattern → Provisional Pattern → Supported Pattern → Disputed → Revised → Superseded`.

Historical should preserve those state transitions.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Structural Learning Must Preserve the Evolution of the Lesson, Not Only Its Current Form.**

---

## 35. Failed Lessons Are Historical Knowledge

A hypothesis derived from cases may later prove wrong.

The Case-Learning architecture explicitly preserves failed knowledge.

Therefore:

> **Failed Structural Lesson ≠ Worthless Historical Record.**

It can show why civilisation acted as it did and prevent repetition.

---

## 36. Interventions Become Historical Experiments

Case Learning studies interventions and outcomes.

A remedy can therefore be represented as:

`Observed Failure → Hypothesis → Intervention → Predicted Effect → Actual Effect → Review`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Remedy Record Should Preserve Prediction and Outcome Separately.**

Otherwise later success/failure cannot be evaluated fairly.

---

## 37. Successful Outcomes Can Still Contain Hidden Failure

A participant may recover despite a bad system.

Historical should not infer:

`good outcome → good architecture`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Outcome Success ≠ Proof of Process Adequacy.**

This mirrors Historical 011's outcome/decision-quality distinction.

---

## 38. Failed Outcomes Do Not Prove Bad Architecture

Likewise:

`bad outcome ≠ proof of bad process`.

A good intervention can fail under uncertainty.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Outcome Failure ≠ Automatic Proof of Process Failure.**

The evidential chain matters.

---

## 39. Historical Learning Must Resist Self-Protecting Institutions

Case Learning warns against institutions explaining every failure as participant exception.

Historical should preserve enough contradictory evidence to allow later review.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Classification Must Not Systematically Convert Institutional Failure Into Participant Exception.**

This is an anti-capture requirement.

---

## 40. Privacy Transformation Must Not Destroy All Learning Provenance

Historical 019 requires minimum identity exposure.

But excessive anonymisation can destroy relational information needed to test a structural lesson.

Candidate principle:

> **Privacy Transformation Should Remove Unnecessary Identity Without Destroying the Material Structure Needed to Audit the Learning Claim.**

This is another explicit preservation/privacy trade-off.

---

## 41. No Silent Recombination

The Case-Learning architecture prohibits silently recombining anonymised patterns with Participant X's history.

Historical should preserve this boundary.

Candidate principle:

> **Structural Historical Learning ≠ Permission to Reconstruct the Participants From Whom the Learning Was Derived.**

---

## 42. Learning-System Changes Are Historical Objects

Case Learning says its own developmental provenance should survive.

Therefore Historical may preserve:

- model version;
- extraction method;
- anonymisation method;
- classification rules;
- validation standard;
- known failure modes;
- architecture changes.

Candidate principle:

> **The History of Civilisational Learning Mechanisms Is Part of the Evidence Needed to Interpret Their Outputs.**

---

## 43. Historical Can Compare Institutional Learning Across Time

A mature Historical system could ask:

- how quickly did repeated failures become recognised patterns?
- how often were participant challenges upheld?
- how often did remedies recur?
- did the same boundary failure return?
- did correction improve outcomes?

These are legitimate civilisational learning questions.

But they should generally use privacy-protected structural data.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Institutional Learning Metrics Should Prefer Structural Performance Over Persistent Participant Profiling.**

---

## 44. Historical Can Preserve the Difference Between Unknown and Ignored

This pass exposes an important distinction.

At time T, civilisation may:

1. genuinely lack the information;
2. possess information but fail to connect it;
3. connect it but classify it as weak;
4. recognise it but fail to act;
5. act unsuccessfully.

Those are historically different states.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Ignorance ≠ Historical Fragmentation ≠ Historical Disbelief ≠ Historical Inaction ≠ Historical Failed Action.**

This is highly important for later accountability.

---

## 45. Pattern-Bearing History Can Reveal Institutional Blindness

If multiple local reports existed but no system could see their relationship, the pattern may reveal an evaluation-space failure.

Candidate principle:

> **A Civilisation Can Possess the Facts Needed to Learn and Still Fail to Possess the Relationship Needed to Understand Them.**

This directly connects Source Resolutions 010 and 014A.

---

## 46. Historical Should Preserve When Relationships Became Visible

The appearance of a new analytical method, shared identifier, taxonomy or cross-domain interface can make an old pattern newly visible.

Historical should preserve:

`Facts existed → relationship inaccessible → new method/interface → pattern discovered`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Discovery Can Result From New Relational Visibility Without Any New Underlying Event Occurring.**

This is a significant refinement of the pattern model.

---

## 47. New Visibility Can Create New Responsibility Prospectively

Once a pattern becomes reliably visible, future failures may occur under a different knowledge state than earlier ones.

Historical should preserve that transition.

Candidate principle:

> **The Point at Which a Risk Became Reasonably Knowable Can Change the Responsibility Context of Later Events.**

Historical preserves the evidence; judicial/ethical systems determine responsibility.

## 53. Current Finding

This pass strengthens the emerging conclusion that Historical is not simply a passive archive.

It preserves the temporal and relational substrate that allows other systems to learn **how civilisation learned**.

The important sequence is not merely:

`Problem → Solution`.

It may be:

`Separate Events → Unconnected Records → Emerging Pattern → Recognition → Hypothesis → Intervention → Outcome → Correction`.

At each point civilisation occupies a different epistemic state.

Historical must preserve those differences without turning case learning into permanent surveillance or turning later understanding into retrospective certainty.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **A civilisation can possess every fact required to understand a failure and still not understand it until the relationship between those facts becomes visible.**
