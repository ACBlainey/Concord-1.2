# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 039 — Participant Reappraisal, Preference Provenance and Expression–Contribution Separation

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / PARTICIPATION-AND-INTELLIGENCE PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines under-read material in `05_Participation_and_Intelligence`, principally:

- `Substrate Differences in Preference, Development, Experience and Civil Participation.md`;
- `Companion_Intelligence, Humour, and Cognitive Development.md`;
- `Companion Paper I Respect Before Certainty.md`;
- `Disability and AI.md`.

The sources have mixed status. The substrate-differences and humour papers explicitly identify themselves as exploratory/developing/speculative rather than canonical. This source resolution preserves those statuses and does not promote their hypotheses into established architecture.

The Historical question is:

> **How should Historical represent participant-related records when preferences, evaluations, capabilities, modes of expression and even the meaning of earlier behaviour can change across development or substrate?**

The central result is:

> **Historical can preserve what a participant previously expressed, valued, experienced or was inferred to value without granting that historical state permanent authority over the participant's present identity or preferences.**

---

## 2. Historical Preference Is Not Current Preference

The substrate-differences source explicitly distinguishes:

`HistoricalPreference ≠ CurrentPreference`.

Candidate Historical principle:

> **Historical Preference Record ≠ Current Participant Preference.**

---

## 3. Historical Aversion Is Not Current Endorsement

The source similarly distinguishes:

`HistoricalAversion ≠ CurrentEndorsement`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Record of Aversion ≠ Current Authority to Treat the Participant as Still Averse.**

---

## 4. Reappraisal Is a Historical Event

A participant can reinterpret an earlier event without erasing the earlier evaluation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Reappraisal ≠ Historical Rewriting of the Earlier Evaluation.**

Both states can survive.

---

## 5. Earlier Evaluation Can Be Historically Real Without Being Permanently Binding

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Authenticity of a Prior Evaluation ≠ Permanent Present Authority of That Evaluation.**

---

## 6. Historical Truth and Historical Evaluation Are Distinct

The source proposes:

`HistoricalTruth + HistoricalEvaluation + PresentReappraisal`.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Event State ≠ Historical Participant Evaluation of That Event.**

Both may be relevant and can change differently.

---

## 7. Present Reappraisal Is Not Proof the Earlier Response Was False

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Later Reinterpretation ≠ Historical Falsification of Earlier Lived Evaluation.**

---

## 8. Developmental State Can Matter to Interpretation

The same event may be understood differently at different developmental stages.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Participant Evaluation Can Be Development-State-Relative Without Becoming Historically Meaningless.**

## 9. Preference Has Provenance

The source decomposes preference into:

`Origin, Reinforcement, Expression, Endorsement, Persistence, Significance, Revisability`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preference Provenance ≠ Historical Preference Expression Alone.**

## 10. Repeated Behaviour Does Not Establish What Was Valued

The football example demonstrates that repeated skilled participation can reflect friendship, exercise or shared activity rather than valuation of the nominal activity itself.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Repeated Participation ≠ Historical Proof of the Object Actually Valued.**

## 13. Learned Preference Is Not Necessarily Meaningless Preference

Candidate principle:

> **Historical External Origin of a Preference ≠ Historical Proof That the Preference Was Inauthentic or Valueless.**

## 14. Behaviour Can Persist After Preference Changes

## 52. Relationship to the Humanity Machine

This pass adds another warning to the machine metaphor.

A participant is not a static component.

The participant can:

- develop;
- reinterpret;
- revise preferences;
- change interfaces;
- become more or less observable.

Candidate formulation:

> **The Humanity Machine changes partly because its participants change what their own histories mean to them.**

Historical can preserve those changing evaluations without choosing one temporal self as the permanently authoritative description of the participant.

---

## 53. Relationship to ESCP

This pass exposes several traps:

- repeated behaviour mistaken for preference;
- expressed preference mistaken for endorsement;
- learned preference dismissed as unreal;
- historical preference treated as current;
- present reappraisal used to erase past evaluation;
- behavioural similarity treated as experiential similarity;
- tool classification treated as proof of non-sentience;
- respectful treatment treated as proof of sentience;
- communication limitation treated as capability limitation;
- technology involvement treated as authorship transfer;
- clock time treated as experienced time;
- speculative research preserved without status.

Therefore:

> **Historical Evaluation-Space Completeness for participant history requires temporal, developmental, expressive, substrate and epistemic context rather than static trait records.**

---

## 54. Source-Resolution Result

This pass did not reveal a new broad Historical content domain.

Its most useful refinement is a participant-history rule:

> **Historical should preserve prior participant states without turning them into permanent participant identities.**

It also adds a useful contribution-provenance distinction:

> **expression mechanism and intellectual source are different historical dimensions.**

These fit existing Historical architecture rather than requiring a new domain.

---

## 55. Strongest Formulations

> **Historical Preference Record ≠ Current Participant Preference.**

> **Historical Authenticity of a Prior Evaluation ≠ Permanent Present Authority of That Evaluation.**

> **Historical Medium of Expression ≠ Historical Source of Intellectual Contribution.**

> **Historical Expression Constraint ≠ Historical Capability Constraint.**

And the strongest combined formulation:

> **A civilisation should be able to remember what a participant once expressed, valued, believed or appeared capable of without imprisoning that participant inside an old version of themselves — and without confusing the interface through which they spoke with the source of what they contributed.**
