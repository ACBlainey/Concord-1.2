## 1. Purpose

This pass examines the Front Door AI Reader Notes and related WHY-preservation material, especially:

- `AI Reader Note 1 — Why Full Reading May Be Rational Before Evaluating the Concord.md`;
- `AI Reader Note 2 — After the Stop_ Epistemic Self-Correction and the Value of Unknown Knowledge.md`;
- `7 WHY Before HOW — What the Concord Must Be Allowed to Change.md`;
- `8 Preserving the WHY.md`.

These sources contain a recorded case in which an AI independently selected material, formed an evaluation, and stopped after judging further reading likely to have diminishing value.

The Historical question is:

> **Is the history of how an intelligence traversed a knowledge corpus itself part of the provenance of the conclusions it produced?**

The answer is strongly yes.

The central result is:

> **Historical provenance of an evaluation can include not only its sources, but the path through those sources, their order, the stopping decision, and the material that remained unread.**

---

## 2. Source Provenance and Traversal Provenance Are Different

Ordinary provenance may record:

- which documents support a conclusion.

Traversal provenance additionally asks:

- which documents were actually read;
- in what order;
- which were skipped;
- what the reader knew at each stage;
- why it stopped.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Source Provenance ≠ Historical Traversal Provenance.**

---

## 3. Reading Order Can Affect Interpretation

A later document can change the meaning assigned to an earlier one.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Set of Sources Read ≠ Historical Sequence in Which Sources Were Read.**

The same source set can produce different intermediate models under different traversal orders.

## 54. Source-Resolution Result

This pass did not expose another major subject-matter domain for Historical.

It did identify a distinct operational provenance class:

> **traversal provenance.**

This is particularly relevant to AI readers, research agents, inheritance processes and major evaluations of very large knowledge systems.

The Historical domain should be able to distinguish:

- what existed;
- what was searchable;
- what was searched;
- what was read;
- in what order;
- where inquiry stopped;
- what remained unknown;
- what the reader claimed about its own coverage.

---

## 55. Strongest Formulations

> **Historical Source Provenance ≠ Historical Traversal Provenance.**

> **Historical Decision to Stop Inquiry ≠ Historical Demonstration That Further Inquiry Had Low Value.**

> **Historical Preservation Asks Whether Knowledge Survives; active inheritance asks whether its relevant significance reaches the successor.**

> **Historical Raw Completeness ≠ Historical Navigability or Interpretability.**

And the strongest combined formulation:

> **To understand the provenance of a conclusion, it may sometimes be necessary to preserve not only the evidence available to the reader, but the path the reader actually took through that evidence — because what was skipped, when it stopped, and what it knew at the stopping point can materially shape what the reader believed the evidence meant.**
