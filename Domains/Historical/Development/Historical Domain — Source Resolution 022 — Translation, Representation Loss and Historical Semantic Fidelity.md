## 1. Purpose

This pass examines Historical requirements created by translation and representation change.

Primary sources include:

- `V1.2a/00_Experimental_Protocol/Prospective Finding — Translation Topology, Relational Information Loss and Cognitive-Substrate Complementarity.md`;
- `V1.2a/00_Experimental_Protocol/Prospective Finding — Multi-Representation Civilisational Translation and the Machine-Readable Participation Requirement.md`;

- `E9-929 — Machine Readable Artifact Preservation Requirement.md`;
- `Ethical Kernel Translation, Interpretation and Machine Assurance — Problem Definition and Unknowns.md`;
- prior Historical Source Resolutions 009, 010, 017, 018 and 021.

Much of the V1.2a material is explicitly **experimental/non-canonical**. It is therefore used as evidence about a Historical problem, not silently promoted into canonical architecture.

The central Historical question is:

> **What must survive when civilisation changes the representation through which it remembers something?**

---

## 2. Object Preservation Is Not Information Preservation

The translation-topology work states:

> **Object Preservation ≠ Information Preservation.**

A translation may preserve:

- entities;
- names;
- local attributes;

while losing:

- relations;
- hierarchy;
- temporal sequence;
- causal structure;
- conditional dependence;
- contextual meaning;
- ambiguity;
- functional role;
- topology.

Historical therefore cannot evaluate preservation solely by asking whether the identifiable objects survived.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Content Survival ≠ Historical Information Survival.**

---

## 3. Historical Fidelity Includes Relations

Source Resolution 010 already established:

> preserve the nodes and preserve the important edges.

Translation research strengthens this.

A Historical transformation must preserve consequential relations where possible.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Semantic Fidelity Requires Preservation of Material Relationships, Not Merely Material Objects.**

---

## 4. Representation Change Is a Historical Event

Civilisational knowledge may move through:

- oral account;
- handwritten text;
- printed text;
- digitisation;
- OCR;
- database;
- graph;
- machine-readable schema;
- simulation;
- translated language;
- compressed summary.

Each transformation can alter what remains recoverable.

Therefore:

> **Historical Representation Change Is Itself Provenance.**

The representation path should be recoverable where material.

---

## 5. Translation Chains Can Accumulate Loss

The V1.2a finding models:

`R0 → R1 → R2 → ... → Rn`.

Loss can be path-dependent.

Once an early stage removes a relation, later stages cannot preserve what they never received.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Translation Loss Can Accumulate Irreversibly Across Representation Chains.**

This is especially important for very old or repeatedly mediated material.

---

## 6. Historical Should Preserve the Transmission Chain

For a culturally mediated source, a useful historical chain may be:

`Original Context → Original Language → Recorder/Editor → Published Source → Translation → Digitisation/OCR → Analytical Corpus → Interpretation`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Source Provenance Should Preserve Material Transmission Layers Where Those Layers Can Affect Meaning.**

This extends Source Resolution 017.

---

## 7. Accurate Extraction Does Not Prove Complete Recovery

The translation-topology work states:

> **Accurate Extraction from Available Corpus ≠ Complete Recovery of Original Ethical Topology.**

Historical should generalise this:

> **Accurate Historical Analysis of the Surviving Record ≠ Complete Recovery of the Original Historical Reality.**

This is an ESCP principle.

---

## 8. Translation Uncertainty Is Historical Information

Where translation is ambiguous, Historical should preserve:

- competing translations;
- disputed terms;
- confidence;
- unavailable source-language expertise;
- unresolved interpretation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Translation Uncertainty Should Be Preserved as Uncertainty Rather Than Collapsed Into a Single Retrospective Meaning.**

---

## 9. Later Translation Improvement Must Not Rewrite Earlier Knowledge State

Historical 018 already found this for intercivilisational agreements.

This pass generalises it.

If an improved translation appears at T2, Historical should preserve:

`Source → Translation T1 → Decisions/Interpretations under T1 → Translation T2 → Revised Understanding`.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Translation Improvement ≠ Earlier Availability of the Improved Meaning.**

---

## 10. Ambiguity Can Be Information

Translation systems often try to eliminate ambiguity.

But ambiguity may be inherent to the source.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Ambiguity ≠ Historical Defect by Default.**

Forcing one meaning can destroy information.

---

## 11. Ambiguity Collapse Is a Historical Transformation

If a source supports multiple plausible meanings and a later representation chooses one, Historical should record that interpretive narrowing.

Candidate principle:

> **Interpretive Resolution Should Not Erase the Historical Existence of Material Ambiguity.**

---

## 12. Machine Readability Is Not Historical Authority

The multi-representation work states:

> **Representation ≠ Constitution.**

and rejects machine readability as automatic epistemic priority.

Historical should apply the same rule.

Candidate principle:

> **Machine-Readable Historical Representation ≠ Authoritative Historical Reality.**

A graph or schema is an interface to history, not history itself.

---

## 13. Human-Readable Prose Is Also a Representation

The inverse is equally important.

Natural-language prose can:

- omit structure;
- imply relationships;
- compress uncertainty;
- depend on cultural context;
- be inaccessible to some participants.

Candidate principle:

> **Natural-Language Historical Representation ≠ Lossless Historical Reality.**

Historical should avoid privileging one representational substrate merely because it is familiar.

---

## 14. Multi-Representation Preservation Can Reduce Single-Representation Loss

The V1.2a hypothesis proposes representation plurality.

Historical may benefit from preserving materially different representations such as:

- original source;
- human-readable transcription;
- translation;
- structured graph;
- machine-readable dataset;
- visual topology;
- metadata/provenance.

Candidate principle:

> **Representation Redundancy Can Preserve Different Historical Information Dimensions.**

This is not a requirement to duplicate everything indefinitely.

---

## 15. Different Representations May Preserve Different Information

A prose document may preserve nuance.

A graph may preserve explicit relations.

A dataset may preserve exact membership.

A visual topology may preserve spatial or hierarchical structure.

Candidate principle:

> **Representational Equivalence Must Be Demonstrated for Consequential Information; It Should Not Be Assumed From Shared Subject Matter.**

---

## 16. Round-Trip Translation Can Test Historical Loss

The V1.2a work proposes:

`RA → RB → RA'`

and comparison of the original with the round-tripped representation.

Historical can use this as a candidate preservation audit.

Differences may expose:

- object loss;
- attribute loss;
- relation loss;
- topology loss;
- ambiguity loss;
- context loss.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation Testing Should Examine What Cannot Be Reconstructed After Representation Change.**

---

## 17. Round-Trip Success Does Not Prove Full Fidelity

A translation may round-trip vocabulary while losing information not represented by the comparison metric.

Therefore:

> **Successful Historical Round Trip Within Measured Dimensions ≠ Proof of Complete Semantic Preservation.**

This is another ESCP application.

---

## 18. Machine-Readable Artifact Preservation Can Be Necessary

E9-929 found that aggregate checkpoints were insufficient for exact set-difference closure.

It therefore required literal membership of mechanical sets and preservation of both R-ID and C-ID.

This is a concrete example where:

> **Aggregate Count ≠ Recoverable Historical Computation.**

Candidate principle:

> **Where Exact Historical Reproduction Depends on Member-Level Structure, Aggregate Preservation Alone Is Insufficient.**

---

## 19. Counts Can Preserve Result While Destroying Reproducibility

A historical record saying “820 edges” does not preserve which 820 edges.

Thus:

> **Historical Result Preservation ≠ Historical Reproducibility.**

This distinction applies to research, governance tallies, datasets, inventories and computational processes.

## 20. Machine-Readable Preservation Has Version Dependencies

A serialized dataset may become unusable if Historical loses:

- schema;
- field definitions;
- parser assumptions;
- encoding;
- software dependency;
- relation semantics.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Machine-Readable Data Without Its Interpretation Schema Can Become Semantically Unrecoverable.**

This links directly to Source Resolution 015.

## 21. Schema Migration Can Alter Meaning

Moving a dataset into a new schema can:

- merge fields;
- split categories;
- remove null states;
- change identifiers;
- alter relation types.

Historical should preserve migration provenance.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Schema Migration ≠ Semantically Neutral Copying.**

---

## 22. Identifiers Can Carry Hidden Relational Meaning

E9-929 required both R-ID and C-ID because a bare C-ID list lost duplicate and one-to-many provenance.

This yields a general principle:

> **Historical Identifier Simplification Can Destroy Relationship Provenance Even When Every Named Object Appears to Survive.**

This is a concrete instance of Source Resolution 010.

---

## 23. Compression Must Preserve a Route Back

Historical 010 already found:

> **Compression Is Safer When the Route Back to Material Reasoning and Provenance Survives.**

Translation work strengthens this into a representation rule.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Summary Should Not Become the Sole Surviving Representation Where Material Detail Cannot Be Reconstructed From It.**

---

## 24. Summaries Are Historical Derivatives

A summary should preserve provenance to its source.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Summary ≠ Historical Source.**

This seems simple but becomes critical when summaries are recursively summarised.

---

## 25. Recursive Summarisation Can Compound Loss

A chain:

`Source → Summary A → Summary B → Brief C`

can gradually remove:

- uncertainty;
- dissent;
- exceptions;
- causal links;
- status;
- scope.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Compression Loss Can Compound Across Derivative Generations.**

---

## 26. Derivative Lineage Must Be Explicit

Historical should preserve:

`Derivative → Immediate Source → Upstream Source(s)`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Derivative Provenance Should Permit Recovery of the Transformation Path, Not Merely the Earliest Source Name.**

This helps locate where loss or interpretation entered.

---

## 27. Ethical Translation Shows Why Provenance Matters

The machine-ethics development note proposes:

`Ethical Kernel → Interpretation → Domain → Assumptions → Derived Constraint → Implementation → Version → Test Evidence → Outcomes → Corrections`.

Historical should preserve such chains where consequential.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Operational Rule Without Translation Provenance Can Lose the Reason the Rule Exists.**

This applies beyond ethics to law, governance, safety and infrastructure.

---

## 28. Formal Correctness Can Preserve a Translation Error Perfectly

The machine-ethics source warns that a flawed interpretation can be perfectly formalised and implemented.

Historical therefore should not treat implementation consistency as evidence that the upstream meaning was preserved.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Implementation Fidelity ≠ Historical Semantic Fidelity.**

A system can perfectly execute the wrong translation.

---

## 29. Translation Error Can Become Systemic Historical Error

If one incorrect translation is reused across many systems, the resulting failures may appear independent while sharing one semantic ancestor.

Historical should preserve derivation lineage sufficiently to detect this.

Candidate principle:

> **Shared Historical Translation Ancestry Can Explain Distributed Downstream Failure.**

This links Source Resolution 020's case patterns with translation provenance.

---

## 30. Translation Versions Need Historical State

A consequential translation may move through:

- provisional;
- validated;
- disputed;
- corrected;
- superseded.

Historical should preserve the version used by each downstream decision/system.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Use of a Translation Must Be Linked to the Translation Version Actually Available at the Time.**

---

## 31. Source Text and Translation Must Remain Distinct Objects

A translation should not overwrite the source.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Translation ≠ Historical Replacement of Source.**

Where the source survives, both should remain related but distinct.

---

## 32. Lost Source Creates a Different Evidential State

Sometimes only a translation survives.

Historical should represent:

`Source unavailable / lost; derivative survives`

rather than silently treating the derivative as the original.

Candidate principle:

> **Surviving Translation Without Source ≠ Surviving Original Evidence.**

---

## 33. Cultural Context Can Be Lost Even With Linguistic Accuracy

A word-for-word translation may fail to preserve:

- social role;
- ritual context;
- historical usage;
- metaphor;
- normative weight.

Candidate principle:

> **Linguistic Accuracy ≠ Complete Historical Context Preservation.**

## 34. Translation Independence Can Reduce Confirmation Bias

E9 deliberately separated translation from predicted ethical function and used independent translation pathways.

Historical should preserve such methodological independence where it matters.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Translation Provenance Should Preserve Independence Conditions Where Independence Was Part of the Evidential Method.**

## 35. Blinding State Is Historical Method Information

If a translator was blinded to the expected result, that affects interpretation of the evidence.

Historical should preserve:

- what the translator knew;
- when unblinding occurred;
- which output was frozen first.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Method State Includes Information-Exposure State Where It Materially Affects Independence.**

This extends Source Resolution 009.

## 36. Translation Debt Is Historical Information

E9 explicitly tracked unresolved translation debt.

Historical should preserve unresolved transformation gaps rather than making the corpus appear cleaner than it was.

Candidate principle:

> **Unresolved Historical Translation Debt ≠ Completed Historical Understanding.**

## 37. Representation Accessibility Is Part of Historical Access

The multi-representation work notes that a participant may have formal access to information but be unable to use its representation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Access to Bytes ≠ Meaningful Historical Access.**

## 39. Translation Itself Can Introduce Authority Risk

If one translator, AI or institution controls the only usable representation, it can effectively shape what other participants can understand.

Candidate principle:

> **Control of Historical Translation Can Become Control of Historical Interpretation.**

## 40. Independent Representations Can Provide Cross-Checks

Where stakes are high, multiple translations or representation pathways can expose hidden loss or bias.

Candidate principle:

> **Independent Historical Representations Can Function as Mutual Error Detectors.**

This does not imply all interpretations are equally supported.

## 41. Disagreement Between Representations Is Information

If prose, graph and implementation disagree about a constitutional constraint, the discrepancy is itself important.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Representation Conflict Should Trigger Provenance Review Rather Than Silent Selection.**

## 55. Current Finding

This pass adds another major dimension to the Historical model: **semantic fidelity across representation change**.

Historical cannot merely preserve information-bearing objects.

It must also understand that every:

- translation;
- transcription;
- schema migration;
- summary;
- formalisation;
- digitisation;
- machine representation;
- reconstruction;

can alter the information available to future civilisation.

This does not imply preserving every representation forever.

It means that consequential transformations require enough provenance to know **what changed, what may have been lost, and which representation later decisions actually used**.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **A civilisation can preserve every word of a record and still lose part of its history if it loses the relationships, context or ambiguity that gave those words their meaning.**
