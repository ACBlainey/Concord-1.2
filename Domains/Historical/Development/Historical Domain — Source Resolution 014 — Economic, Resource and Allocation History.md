**Project:** The Concord

**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / ECONOMIC-RESOURCE PASS

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines what the Historical Domain should preserve from Concord systems concerned with:

- economic coordination;
- contribution;
- exchange;
- monetary experiments;
- resource allocation;
- treasury activity;
- stewardship;
- scarcity;
- predictive system steering.

The central danger is two-sided.

A civilisation that preserves too little economic history loses the evidence needed to understand shortages, allocation failures, capture, monetary experiments and long-run resource trajectories.

A civilisation that preserves too much raw economic activity can create a permanent transaction-and-behaviour surveillance system.

The Historical problem is therefore not:

> Store the economy.

It is:

> **Preserve enough economic and resource history to reconstruct consequential civil decisions, system states, experiments and failures without converting Historical into a universal financial dossier.**

## 2. Economic History Is Not Constitutional Worth

The current Coordination and Economy README explicitly states:

> **Contribution ≠ Moral Worth**

> **Economic Success ≠ Ethical Legitimacy**

It further states that usefulness, wealth, reputation, contribution and measured performance must not become substitutes for dignity, rights or legitimate authority.

Historical must preserve this distinction.

A historical economic record may show:

- wealth;
- contribution;
- market success;
- resource stewardship;
- transaction reliability;
- treasury responsibility.

It does not automatically establish the participant's civil worth or constitutional legitimacy.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Economic Success ≠ Historical Moral or Constitutional Worth.**

---

## 3. Historical Must Not Become a Universal Transaction Ledger

Ratchet recognises transaction records as possible provenance evidence.

But it also requires proportional provenance and privacy-preserving verification.

Not every low-risk activity requires maximal evidence.

Therefore Historical should not infer:

`transaction occurred → transaction must enter permanent Historical custody`.

Candidate principle:

> **Economic Activity ≠ Automatic Historical Retention.**

A large civilisation may produce enormous volumes of routine exchange with little enduring historical significance.

The eventual Historical specification needs thresholds based on consequence, accountability, system-state reconstruction, rights and legitimate retention purpose.

---

## 4. Aggregate Economic State Can Be Historically Important Without Preserving Every Transaction

The economic architecture may need historical reconstruction of:

- monetary supply;
- backing ratio;
- treasury condition;
- inflation;
- resource availability;
- scarcity;
- marketplace activity;
- system-level participation;
- allocation states.

These can often be preserved through state snapshots, metrics and bounded provenance rather than permanent participant-level transaction history.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Economic State Reconstruction Does Not Require Permanent Retention of Every Underlying Transaction.**

This extends Historical 006 and 008's anti-duplication principle.

---

## 5. Ratchet Is a Particularly Valuable Historical Development Case

V1 contains a recognisably more monetary/economic Ratchet architecture.

Ratchet V3 explicitly records that Ratchet was originally conceived as an interim digital economic system.

It then reframes Ratchet as:

> **a provenance-based coordination protocol**

with the chain:

`Provenance → Evidence → Contribution → Contextual Trust → Reciprocal Capability → Exchange → Optional Monetary Representation`

V4.2 develops this further into a general coordination, contribution, trust and reciprocal participation protocol.

This is not merely document versioning.

It is a change in the architecture's understood centre of gravity.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Versioning Should Preserve Changes in a System's Declared Purpose, Not Merely Changes in Its Mechanisms.**

---

## 6. V1 Preserves Economic Hypotheses That Later Become Subordinate

The V1 Economic & Monetary Architecture treats Progressive Monetary Backing as a central economic mechanism.

Ratchet V3 retains it as an important monetary hypothesis but no longer treats currency as Ratchet's defining purpose.

V4.2 further states that its economic architecture is an optional application layer built on the deeper coordination protocol.

This is a strong example of why V1 must remain available.

The earlier repository preserves not simply an obsolete file but a prior **architectural interpretation**.

Historical should be able to reconstruct:

`Economic/Monetary Core → Provenance-Based Coordination Reframing → General Coordination Architecture with Optional Economic Layer`.

## 7. Superseded Economic Hypotheses Are Historical Knowledge

Progressive Monetary Backing includes hypotheses concerning:

- provisional issuance;
- backing creation;
- supply;
- contribution;
- treasury;
- inflation;
- monetary resilience.

Whether these mechanisms are eventually adopted, rejected, modified or never deployed, their development history can remain valuable.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of an Economic Hypothesis ≠ Current Economic Endorsement.**

This directly parallels Historical 009's research-history rule.

---

## 8. Monetary Backing Must Not Be Rewritten as Physical Wealth

The archived monetary architecture explicitly states that newly created currency is not automatically equivalent to wealth.

Current Ratchet similarly distinguishes monetary representation from real resources.

Historical must preserve that distinction.

A historical record such as:

`Backing Ratio = 64%`

is an accounting/system state under a particular monetary architecture.

It should not be represented as:

`64% of civilisation's physical wealth existed`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Monetary Representation ≠ Historical Physical Resource Reality.**

---

## 9. Economic State Requires Definition and Model Context

Historical 004 established that metrics require their definitions and methodologies.

The same applies to economic indicators.

Historical values such as:

- backing ratio;
- contribution count;
- reputation;
- treasury health;
- resource utilisation;
- inflation;
- scarcity;

need the model/definition that gave them meaning.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Economic Metric Without Its Contemporary Definition Can Misrepresent the Economy It Describes.**

---

## 10. Contribution History and Value History Are Different

Multisubstrate Economic Participation explicitly states:

> **Provenance, Not Value**

and explains that the history of production is separate from the value of the work.

Ratchet similarly states:

> **Provenance Is Not Proof of Usefulness**

Historical therefore needs to distinguish:

- who contributed;
- what they contributed;
- how it was produced;
- what evidence existed;
- how usefulness/value was assessed;
- whether that assessment later changed.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Contribution Provenance ≠ Historical Proof of Value.**

---

## 11. Economic Reputation Is Contextual and Time-Sensitive

Ratchet defines reputation as a contextual record or assessment of observed behaviour.

It explicitly rejects universal social-credit logic and permanent reputational condemnation for ordinary failure.

Historical must therefore not preserve a historical reputation score as though it were a permanent participant property.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Reputation ≠ Permanent Participant Character.**

Where reputation history is legitimately retained, Historical should preserve:

- domain/context;
- evidence;
- effective period;
- assessment method;
- dispute/correction;
- later relevance state.

This is closely related to Historical 012's anti-dossier boundary.

---

## 12. Serious Historical Behaviour May Remain Relevant Without Becoming Universal Reputation

Ratchet notes that a historical pattern of serious fraud may remain relevant to financial stewardship.

This creates a bounded relevance principle.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Behaviour May Remain Relevant to a Defined Present Risk Without Becoming a Universal Civil Score.**

The legitimate present decision process determines relevance.

Historical provides evidence.

---

## 13. Economic Exit Creates Historical State Transitions

Ratchet requires meaningful economic exit and recognises that participants may leave and later return.

Historical may need to preserve consequential transitions such as:

`Participant enters economic protocol → acquires balances/obligations/claims → exits → settlement/state transition → later re-entry`.

But routine historical retention must remain privacy- and purpose-bounded.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Economic Participation State ≠ Permanent Economic Membership.**

A prior participant should not be represented as currently participating merely because the historical record survives.

---

## 14. Ownership and Stewardship Are Distinct Historical Claims

The Ownership, Stewardship and Cooperative Claims note explores movement from absolute-possession concepts toward stewardship.

Roadway Equilibrium similarly distinguishes private ownership from wider constitutional stewardship where resources have broader significance.

Historical must preserve what kind of claim existed.

A record should not collapse:

- ownership;
- stewardship;
- custody;
- access;
- contribution;
- authorship;
- resource allocation;
- temporary control.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Resource Claim Must Preserve the Type and Scope of the Claim.**

Otherwise later readers may convert temporary stewardship into ownership or contribution into possession.

---

## 15. Authorship Can Outlive Participation

The Ownership/Stewardship note explicitly proposes that authorship should remain recorded even where general participant access ends.

This produces a useful Historical distinction:

`Participation status`

and:

`Provenance/authorship status`

can diverge.

Candidate principle:

> **End of Participation Does Not Necessarily End Historical Authorship or Provenance.**

This must still be reconciled with privacy and lawful-removal rules.

---

## 16. Resource Allocation History Needs the Forecast That Informed It

The System-Steering and Predictive Resource Allocation architecture uses:

`Aggregate Metrics → Future Modelling → Resource Options → Governance Decision → Allocation → Outcome`.

Historical should preserve consequential allocations with enough information to reconstruct:

- demand evidence;
- forecast/model;
- model version;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- allocation proposal;
- legitimate decision;
- resources shifted;
- observed outcome;
- later review.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Resource Allocation Without Its Forecast and Alternatives Can Conceal the Decision Space That Existed at the Time.**

---

## 17. Prediction Does Not Become Allocation Authority

The resource architecture states:

> **The resource model advises. It does not govern.**

and:

> **Prediction informs allocation; it does not govern.**

Historical must preserve this separation.

Candidate chain:

`Forecast → Advice → Governance/Legitimate Allocation Decision → Implementation`

not:

`Forecast → Allocation`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Prediction Record ≠ Historical Allocation Authority.**

---

## 18. Failed Allocation Is Historical Evidence

The source explicitly states:

> **Failure is evidence.**

A failed allocation may reveal:

- wrong forecast;
- wrong model;
- poor implementation;
- missing dependency;
- capture;
- resource scarcity;
- unanticipated demand;
- accessibility failure.

Historical should preserve failure without automatically assigning cause.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Allocation Failure ≠ Historical Proof of Forecast Failure.**

The chain must preserve enough evidence to determine whether the failure arose in prediction, decision, implementation or external change.

---

## 19. Over-Allocation Is Not Automatically Failure

The resource architecture notes that unused capacity may be resilience.

Historical therefore should not infer inefficiency merely because resources were unused.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Use of Allocated Capacity ≠ Historical Proof of Waste.**

Context may show that reserve capacity was intentional.

This is another reason economic history requires decision rationale rather than only utilisation numbers.

---

## 20. Under-Allocation Requires Context Too

Repeated under-allocation may indicate:

- insufficient resources;
- poor forecasting;
- institutional neglect;
- bad routing;
- accessibility failure;
- unexpected demand.

Historical should preserve these possibilities rather than reducing the event to a single explanation.

This is an ESCP problem.

---

## 21. Resource Allocation Can Oscillate

The resource architecture explicitly identifies overcorrection:

`resources A → B → A`

as a potential failure mode.

Historical must preserve enough transition history for oscillation to become visible.

A sequence of isolated current-state snapshots may hide it.

Candidate principle:

> **Resource History Must Preserve Material Directional Transitions, Not Merely Periodic Totals.**

This is closely analogous to Historical 013's state-transition requirement.

---

## 22. Scarcity History Is Not Merely Price History

The Concord does not reduce resource reality to market price.

Scarcity may concern:

- housing;
- energy;
- compute;
- healthcare;
- transport;
- habitat;
- infrastructure;
- specialist capability;
- rare participant needs.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Price ≠ Historical Scarcity.**

Historical reconstruction may need both market and non-market evidence.

---

## 23. Rare Needs Must Not Disappear in Aggregate Economic History

The resource architecture warns that average-based planning can erase rare but severe needs.

Historical 004 similarly identified minority-harm compression risk.

Therefore:

> **Historical Aggregation of Resource Use Must Not Erase Material Outlier Need Where Its Preservation Is Legitimately Required.**

This does not imply retaining identifiable participant data indefinitely.

It may require privacy-preserving evidence that the outlier demand existed.

---

## 24. Multisubstrate Resource History Must Preserve Substrate Context

Different participants may require different resources.

The resource architecture specifically identifies:

- energy;
- compute;
- maintenance;
- habitat;
- biological needs;
- hybrid needs.

A future historical dataset that treats all resource demand as interchangeable could misrepresent earlier allocation conflicts.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Resource Demand Must Preserve Material Substrate Context Without Converting Substrate Into a Hierarchy of Worth.**

---

## 25. Treasury History Is Accountability-Critical

Ratchet describes the Treasury as stabilising rather than controlling and requires its authority to remain auditable.

Historical should preserve consequential treasury states and decisions sufficient to reconstruct:

- authority;
- reserve state;
- issuance/contraction where applicable;
- major transfers;
- intervention rationale;
- conflicts;
- audits;
- outcomes.

But:

> **Historical Treasury Custody ≠ Treasury Authority.**

Historical cannot issue currency, allocate reserves or retrospectively authorise a treasury action.

---

## 26. Economic Experimentation Requires Historical Separation From General Economy

Ratchet explicitly supports isolated experimentation so that failure becomes information rather than systemic damage.

Historical should preserve:

- experiment boundary;
- participants/scope where legitimately retainable;
- rules;
- economic mechanism tested;
- exposure;
- outcome;
- failure;
- lessons;
- whether findings were later adopted.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Record of an Economic Pilot Must Preserve That It Was a Pilot.**

Otherwise an experimental rule could later be mistaken for civilisation-wide economic policy.

---

## 27. Historical Must Preserve Economic Failure Without Freezing Participants in Failure

Ratchet states that a participant should not be permanently defined by previous failure.

This is an important Historical tension.

The system may need to remember an event while allowing its relevance to decay.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of Economic Failure ≠ Permanent Economic Disqualification.**

This likely requires contextual relevance, review and possibly cold-storage/restricted states from Historical 005.

---

## 28. Economic History Can Become a Surveillance System

If Historical permanently joins:

- transactions;
- location;
- identity;
- contribution;
- reputation;
- resource use;
- marketplace behaviour;
- treasury interactions;

it can reconstruct a highly invasive behavioural profile.

Therefore Historical 012's rule applies directly:

> **Civilisational Memory ≠ Permanent Universal Participant Profiling.**

This pass strengthens it:

> **Economic Historical Value Does Not Automatically Justify Participant-Level Financial Traceability Across All Time.**

---

## 29. Historical Economic Objects Should Prefer Consequential Events and State Changes

A candidate retention hierarchy may distinguish:

### Routine operational data
normally remains under operational/legal retention rules and need not enter permanent Historical custody.

### Aggregated historical state
may preserve economic/resource conditions without participant-level detail.

### Consequential decision records
major treasury, monetary, allocation or stewardship decisions may warrant durable Historical custody.

### Accountability-critical provenance
may require protected retention.

### Experimental/developmental history
may preserve economic hypotheses, pilots, failures and model changes.

This is candidate architecture, not a final retention rule.

## 34. Relationship to Roadway Equilibrium

Roadway Equilibrium explicitly warns:

> **A civilisation that repeatedly forgets previous failures will repeatedly approach the same cliffs.**

It proposes preserving previous decisions and trajectories so future stewards can recognise them.

Historical therefore provides an obvious temporal substrate for Roadway learning.

But Roadway remains a steering/interpretive method.

Historical does not itself determine where the current Roadway lies.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Trajectory Evidence ≠ Current Roadway Judgement.**

Resource scarcity may become a threat or emergency.

> **Historical Persistence of an Emergency Record ≠ Continuing Emergency Status.**

## 38. ESCP Implication

Economic systems are especially vulnerable to representational narrowing.

A civilisation may measure:

- transactions but not unpaid care;
- price but not scarcity;
- output but not resilience;
- utilisation but not accessibility;
- averages but not rare severe need;
- contribution but not invisible enabling work;
- monetary backing but not physical resources.

Historical should preserve enough model context to show what the economic system could and could not see.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Economic Records Should Preserve Material Blindness in the Economic Model, Not Merely the Values Produced by That Model.**

## 41. Current Finding

The economic/resource pass strongly supports the emerging view that Historical is not a universal archive of all operational data.

For economic systems in particular, the useful historical object is usually one of:

- a consequential state;
- a consequential decision;
- a resource transition;
- a model or metric state;
- a major experiment;
- a failure;
- an authority/provenance chain;
- a bounded participant-level evidentiary record where justified.

The emerging lifecycle is:

`Economic/Resource Activity → Operational Records → Metrics/Models → Consequential State or Decision → Outcome/Failure → Historical Handoff Assessment → Aggregate State / Decision Record / Protected Provenance / Experimental History → Later Review or Learning`

The V1 archive again proves especially valuable because it retains a prior economic conception of Ratchet that the current architecture alone cannot fully reconstruct.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **Historical should preserve enough of the economy to explain how resources, value, scarcity and consequential economic decisions changed — but not so much that civilisational memory becomes a permanent ledger of every participant's economic life.**
