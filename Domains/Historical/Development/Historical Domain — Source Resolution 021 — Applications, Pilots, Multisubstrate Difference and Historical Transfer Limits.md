# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 021 — Applications, Pilots, Multisubstrate Difference and Historical Transfer Limits

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / APPLICATION-TRANSFER PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines Historical requirements created by two source families that have not yet been treated directly as Historical domains:

- `05_Participation_and_Intelligence`;
- `07_Applications`;

with V1.2a used as a status-boundary comparison.

The sources are unusually explicit about non-equivalence:

> **Substrate neutrality does not require substrate uniformity.**

> **A failed application does not automatically invalidate the principle.**

> **A successful application does not automatically prove universal validity.**

> **A successful pilot would generate evidence, not sovereignty.**

These statements create an important Historical requirement: **the history of an implementation must preserve the scope in which its evidence was generated.**

---

## 2. Application History Is Not Principle History

The Applications area distinguishes:

`Principle ≠ Mechanism ≠ Architecture ≠ Implementation ≠ Outcome`.

Historical must preserve the same separation.

If a Mars implementation fails, the historical record should not silently become:

`Concord principle failed`.

Likewise, local success should not become:

`Concord principle universally validated`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Application Outcome ≠ Historical Verdict on the Underlying Principle.**

---

## 3. Historical Evidence Must Preserve Abstraction Level

A result can apply to:

- a principle;
- a mechanism;
- an architecture;
- an implementation;
- an environment;
- an outcome.

Historical should preserve which level was actually tested.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Evidence Must Preserve the Abstraction Level at Which It Was Generated.**

This prevents later scope inflation.

---

## 4. Pilot Context Is Part of the Historical Evidence

Applications can depend on:

- environment;
- participant population;
- infrastructure;
- resource conditions;
- governance state;
- technology;
- scale;
- time;
- implementation quality.

Therefore:

> **Historical Pilot Result Without Material Context Is Incomplete Evidence.**

A result cannot safely be detached from the conditions that produced it.

---

## 5. Transfer Is a New Epistemic Event

Suppose a mechanism succeeds on Mars and is later proposed for Earth.

The Earth proposal is not simply continuation of the Martian result.

It is a new transfer claim.

Candidate chain:

`Mars Architecture → Mars Implementation → Mars Evidence → Transfer Hypothesis → Earth Pilot → Earth Evidence`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Success in Context A ≠ Historical Validation in Context B.**

---

## 6. Reuse Must Preserve the Transfer Claim

When a mechanism is reused elsewhere, Historical should preserve:

- source context;
- claimed transferable features;
- known differences;
- predicted effects;
- adaptation;
- outcome.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Transfer Should Preserve What Was Assumed to Generalise.**

This makes later evaluation possible.

---

## 7. Failed Transfer Does Not Necessarily Falsify the Source Application

A mechanism may work in one context and fail in another.

Therefore:

> **Historical Transfer Failure ≠ Historical Failure of the Source Context.**

The failure may instead reveal a contextual dependency.

---

## 8. Successful Transfer Does Not Prove Universal Transferability

Repeated success strengthens evidence but does not establish unlimited scope.

Candidate principle:

> **Repeated Historical Transfer Success ≠ Universal Validity Without Demonstrated Scope.**

This extends Source Resolution 009.

---

## 9. Applications Are Evidence Generators

The Applications README gives:

`Architecture → Application → Pilot → Observation → Evidence → Evaluation → Correction`.

Historical should preserve this chain.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Application Records Should Preserve the Difference Between Design Intention, Observed Outcome and Later Evaluation.**

---

## 10. Unexpected Application Results Have Special Historical Value

The source explicitly says failure to fit the model may be information about the model.

Historical should preserve:

- unexpected failure;
- unexpected success;
- unexplained result;
- environmental anomaly;
- implementation deviation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Application Anomaly ≠ Noise by Default.**

This links directly to Historical 009 and 020.

---

## 11. Implementation Quality Is a Distinct Historical Variable

A failed implementation may reflect:

- poor execution;
- inadequate resources;
- training failure;
- local incompatibility;
- mechanism defect;
- principle defect.

Historical should not collapse these possibilities.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Implementation Failure ≠ Historical Mechanism Failure Without Further Evidence.**

---

## 12. Historical Must Preserve Implementation Drift

An application may diverge from its source architecture during deployment.

Historical should preserve:

`Specified Architecture → Implemented Architecture → Local Modifications → Outcome`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Outcome Must Be Linked to What Was Actually Implemented, Not Merely What Was Intended.**

---

## 13. Multisubstrate History Cannot Assume Uniform Participants

Folder 05 explicitly rejects simplistic categories such as:

- Human → one requirement set;
- AI → one requirement set;
- Hybrid → one requirement set.

Historical must avoid encoding these assumptions into its own schemas.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Substrate Classification ≠ Complete Participant Description.**

---

## 14. Substrate Neutrality Does Not Mean Historical Uniformity

Different participants may have materially different:

- continuity;
- embodiment;
- temporal experience;
- communication;
- resource dependency;
- vulnerability;
- identity;
- development;
- infrastructure.

Historical may therefore need different record structures or companion schemas while preserving common principles.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Equality Does Not Require Identical Historical Representation.**

This is compatible with contextual-wrapper architecture.

---

## 15. Historical Schema Should Preserve Relevant Difference, Not Category Stereotype

A participant's substrate category should not be used as a shortcut for assumed needs or capabilities.

Historical should preserve actual relevant attributes where legitimately required.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Category Membership ≠ Historical Inference of Individual Capability or Need.**

---

## 16. Unknown Intelligences Create an ESCP Problem

Folder 05 explicitly allows future intelligences whose characteristics do not fit present classifications.

Historical schemas therefore cannot assume the present ontology is complete.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Schema Completeness for Known Participants ≠ Historical Schema Completeness for Future Participants.**

Historical should support extension without rewriting old records as though new categories existed earlier.

---

## 17. New Participant Categories Must Not Be Projected Backward

If a new substrate or identity category is recognised at T2, older records may be re-analysed.

But Historical should distinguish:

- contemporary classification;
- later retrospective classification.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Participant Ontology ≠ Contemporary Historical Ontology.**

This mirrors Source Resolution 013.

---

## 18. Accessibility History Is Historically Material

Folder 05 identifies disability, communication differences and accessibility requirements as design concerns.

Historical may need to preserve whether an institution or participation mechanism was actually accessible to affected participants at the time.

Candidate principle:

> **Formal Availability ≠ Historical Accessibility.**

A service existing does not prove all eligible participants could meaningfully use it.

---

## 19. Participation Failure Can Be Interface Failure

A participant's non-use of a service may reflect:

- choice;
- lack of need;
- communication barrier;
- inaccessible interface;
- infrastructure dependency;
- cognitive mismatch;
- physical exclusion.

Historical should not infer motivation from absence.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Use ≠ Historical Refusal.**

This generalises the ballot distinction from Source Resolution 016.

---

## 20. Temporal Experience Can Affect Historical Meaning

Different intelligences may experience time, dormancy or processing cadence differently.

A delay that is trivial for one participant could be materially consequential for another.

Historical should preserve relevant temporal context where necessary.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Elapsed Time ≠ Uniform Participant Impact.**

This extends Historical 013.

---

## 21. Dormancy Is Not Ordinary Absence

Folder 05 identifies dormancy/reactivation as possible participant requirements.

Historical 012 already distinguishes participant continuity from data.

This pass adds:

> **Historical Dormancy ≠ Historical Non-Participation by Choice.**

A dormant participant may simply be unavailable under their continuity state.

---

## 22. Resource Dependency Changes Interpretation

An AI dependent on compute, power or network access may appear inactive because infrastructure failed.

A human may be unable to participate because of biological or accessibility needs.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Participant Behaviour Must Be Interpreted With Material Dependency Context Where That Context Is Relevant.**

This does not justify universal surveillance of participant dependencies.

---

## 23. Application History Can Reveal Hidden Human-Normative Assumptions

A system may work for the dominant participant class and fail for another.

Historical cross-case analysis can expose this pattern.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Majority Success ≠ Historical Evidence of Universal Accessibility.**

This is an ESCP consequence.

---

## 24. Rare Participant Requirements Must Survive Aggregation

Historical 014A and 020 already show that rare cases can carry high information value.

For multisubstrate civilisation this becomes especially important.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Aggregation Must Not Automatically Erase Low-Frequency Participant Requirements That Materially Affect Rights or Access.**

---

## 25. Historical Should Preserve Which Population Was Actually Tested

An application may claim to be multisubstrate but have been tested only with humans, or only with one AI architecture.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Test Population Must Be Preserved as Part of Evidential Scope.**

Without it, later users can accidentally universalise narrow evidence.

---

## 26. Absence of Failure in an Unrepresented Population Is Not Evidence of Suitability

Candidate principle:

> **No Observed Failure in an Untested Participant Class ≠ Evidence of Compatibility With That Class.**

This is a direct ESCP rule.

---

## 27. Cross-Substrate Transfer Is a Transfer Problem

A mechanism successful for humans may be adapted for AI, hybrids or unknown participants.

That adaptation is a new test, not mere deployment.

Candidate principle:

> **Cross-Substrate Application ≠ Evidence-Preserving Copy of the Original Application.**

The differences and adaptation assumptions must be preserved.

---

## 28. Historical Should Preserve Accommodation Evolution

A service may become more accessible over time through:

- interface changes;
- translation;
- assistive systems;
- infrastructure;
- policy;
- new participant models.

Historical should preserve major transitions.

Candidate principle:

> **Current Accessibility Must Not Erase Historical Exclusion or Earlier Accommodation State.**

---

## 29. Successful Accommodation Does Not Prove Prior Equality

If a later redesign solves a barrier, Historical should not retroactively imply that the system was always accessible.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Inclusion ≠ Earlier Inclusion.**

---

## 30. Application Evidence Can Feed Architecture Without Becoming Authority

The Applications README says applications generate evidence and questions for wider Concord development.

Historical should preserve the return path:

`Application Evidence → Evaluation → Development Proposal → Test → Architectural Revision or Rejection`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Application Evidence ≠ Automatic Architectural Amendment.**

Authority remains with the legitimate development/governance process.

---

## 31. Rejected Application Lessons Can Still Be Historical Knowledge

A proposal derived from an application may be rejected.

Historical should preserve why where material.

Candidate principle:

> **Rejected Application-Derived Change ≠ Historical Irrelevance.**

The rejection may later become useful evidence.

---

## 32. V1.2a Provides a Strong Status-Boundary Example

V1.2a explicitly states:

- experimental;
- non-canonical;
- isolated from V1.2;
- does not replace, amend or supersede V1.2.

Historical must preserve that status.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Proximity to Canonical Architecture ≠ Canonical Status.**

Repository co-location does not collapse experimental and canonical authority.

---

## 33. Counterfactual Experiments Require Frozen-State History

V1.2a requires derivations to be frozen before comparison with V1.2.

Historical should preserve:

- experimental protocol;
- starting kernel;
- frozen derivation;
- later comparison;
- revisions.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Counterfactual Integrity Depends on Preserving What Was Fixed Before Comparison.**

Otherwise hindsight contamination becomes difficult to detect.

---

## 34. Experimental Convergence Does Not Prove Uniqueness

If U1–U8 independently regenerate architecture similar to V1.2, that is evidence of convergence under the experiment.

It does not prove that no other architecture is possible.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Experimental Convergence ≠ Proof of Unique Necessity.**

Likewise divergence does not automatically invalidate either source architecture.

---

## 35. Experimental Isolation Is a Historical Relationship

V1.2a is intentionally isolated from V1.2.

Historical should preserve that isolation state because later integration could otherwise make it appear that ideas were always part of current architecture.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Experimental Isolation Is Provenance, Not Mere Folder Organisation.**

---

## 36. Later Integration Needs Explicit Lineage

If an experimental result later enters V1.2 or a future V1.3, Historical should preserve:

`Experimental Origin → Validation/Review → Adoption Decision → Current Architecture`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Adoption Must Preserve Experimental Origin Where Material.**

---

## 37. Application Success Can Create Political or Institutional Prestige Without Authority

The Mars-first source explicitly states that successful pilots generate evidence, not sovereignty.

Historical should preserve this boundary.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Success ≠ Authority Entitlement.**

An institution that ran a successful pilot does not acquire general authority merely from success.

---

## 38. Application Failure Should Not Be Hidden to Protect the Framework

The Applications source explicitly requires exposure to conditions capable of proving architecture wrong or incomplete.

Historical therefore has an anti-selection function.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Application Memory Must Preserve Material Failure and Contradiction, Not Only Demonstration Successes.**

This is necessary to prevent survivorship bias.

---

## 39. Pilot Selection Is Itself Historical Context

A civilisation may choose environments likely to succeed.

Historical should preserve why pilots were selected where that materially affects interpretation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Pilot Outcome Must Be Interpreted With Pilot Selection Context Where Material.**

Otherwise a curated test environment may be mistaken for general evidence.

---

## 40. Non-Deployment Is Also Historical Information

A proposed application may never be deployed because of:

- risk;
- cost;
- rights concerns;
- technical infeasibility;
- better alternatives;
- political rejection.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Deployment ≠ Historical Failure of the Underlying Idea.**

The reason for non-deployment matters.

---

## 41. Historical Applications Need Environment Versioning

A long-running application can experience major environmental change.

Historical should preserve material changes in:

- population;
- infrastructure;
- technology;
- resource availability;
- law;

## 42. Application Comparison Requires Comparable Context

Two sites using “the same” mechanism may not be directly comparable.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Application Comparison Must Demonstrate Material Comparability Rather Than Assume It From Shared Labels.**

## 43. Historical Should Preserve Adaptation, Not Treat It as Noise

Local adaptation can be the reason a mechanism succeeds.

Candidate principle:

> **Implementation Variation Can Be Historical Evidence About Contextual Requirements.**

This supports contextual-wrapper architecture.

## 47. Relationship to ESCP

This pass produces several direct ESCP warnings:

- testing only one environment does not establish universal suitability;
- testing only one participant class does not establish multisubstrate suitability;
- absence of recorded failure in an excluded population is not positive evidence;
- aggregate success can hide minority failure;
- current categories may omit future forms of intelligence;
- application labels can hide implementation differences.

Therefore:

> **Historical Evidence Space Must Preserve Who, Where, Under What Conditions and At What Architectural Level the Evidence Actually Applies.**

## 50. Current Finding

This pass reveals that Historical must preserve **scope** as a first-class part of evidence.

It is not enough to remember:

`X worked`.

Historical may need to remember:

`X, implemented as version Y, worked for population P, in environment E, under conditions C, with adaptation A, during time T, according to evidence method M.`

Only then can future civilisation distinguish legitimate learning from accidental over-generalisation.

The multisubstrate sources sharpen this further. A civilisation designed for many forms of intelligence cannot let the historical success of the majority erase the failure, exclusion or untested status of participants whose requirements differ.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **History should remember not only whether something worked, but for whom, where, under what conditions, and at what level of the architecture the evidence actually applies.**
