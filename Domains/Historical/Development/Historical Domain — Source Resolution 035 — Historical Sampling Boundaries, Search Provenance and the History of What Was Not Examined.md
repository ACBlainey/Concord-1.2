# Historical Domain — Source Resolution 035 — Historical Sampling Boundaries, Search Provenance and the History of What Was Not Examined

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN RESOLUTION / WHY-METHODOLOGY PASS  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This pass examines:

- `00_Front_Door/WHY?/3 Core Systems WHY Density — Bounded Sample.md`;
- related WHY extraction methodology and provenance.

The source is explicitly a bounded exploratory probe, not a canonical conclusion.

The Historical question is:

> **If later civilisation inherits a research conclusion or corpus summary, can it tell what was actually examined, what was only sampled, what was deliberately left unread, and why the search stopped?**

The central finding is:

> **Historical provenance of inquiry includes the boundary of the search, not merely the material that the search found.**

---

## 2. “Not Found” Has a Search History

A statement that something was not found can mean very different things:

- searched exhaustively and absent;
- searched deeply in one area only;
- sampled shallowly;
- searched by filename/path only;
- searched semantically;
- searched with a restricted vocabulary;
- deliberately left unexamined.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Discovery ≠ Historical Absence Without Search-Scope Provenance.**

---

## 3. Search Scope Is Part of Evidence

The bounded WHY probe carefully records that only opening samples of eight representative papers were examined.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Search Scope Is Part of the Provenance of a Search Result.**

---

## 4. Sampling Depth Matters

The source distinguishes:

- deeply examined and locally saturated;
- deeply examined but development-stage;
- shallowly sampled and deliberately not saturated;
- not examined.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Examination Status Should Preserve Depth, Not Merely Binary Read/Unread State.**

---

## 5. Local Saturation Is Not Global Saturation

The source explicitly demonstrates this.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Local Search Saturation ≠ Historical Corpus Saturation.**

This is a direct ESCP safeguard.

---

## 6. Deliberate Non-Examination Is Information

The core WHY sample intentionally leaves the unsampled corpus unknown.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Deliberate Non-Examination ≠ Historical Accidental Omission.**

The reason for the boundary can matter.

---

## 7. Stopping Rule Is Provenance

A search can stop because:

- diminishing returns were reached;
- time/resources ended;
- scope was deliberately bounded;
- a hypothesis was already supported;
- the method failed;
- access was unavailable.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Termination Reason Is Part of Methodological Provenance.**

---

## 8. Same Result Under Different Stopping Rules Has Different Evidential Meaning

Finding 36 candidate items and stopping because no more appear is different from finding 36 and deliberately stopping while more are still appearing.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Result Count Without Historical Stopping Rule Can Misrepresent Evidential Completeness.**

---

## 9. Unread Material Is Not Empty Material

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Unexamined Source Space ≠ Historical Null Evidence.**

It is an unresolved evaluation-space region.

---

## 10. Unknown Must Remain Unknown

The source deliberately refuses to extrapolate the remaining corpus.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Unknown Quantity Should Not Be Retrospectively Replaced by an Estimate Unless the Estimate and Method Are Explicitly Provenanced.**

---

## 11. Sampling Can Be Representative Without Being Exhaustive

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Representative Sample ≠ Historical Exhaustive Coverage.**

The two evidential claims must remain separate.

---

## 12. Selection of Representative Sources Is Itself a Choice

Why those eight papers?

Historical should preserve selection criteria where consequential.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Sample Result Depends Partly on Historical Sample Selection.**

---

## 13. Search Vocabulary Can Shape Discovery

A search for named terms may miss conceptual precursors using different language.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search-Term Absence ≠ Historical Concept Absence.**

This has already mattered repeatedly in V1/V1.1 source resolution.

---

## 14. Path Search Is Not Content Search

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Path-Level Non-Discovery ≠ Historical Content-Level Non-Discovery.**

---

## 15. Content Search Is Not Relational Search

Even reading every document may miss relationships among documents.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Content Coverage ≠ Historical Relational Coverage.**

This reinforces Source Resolution 010 and 033.

---

## 16. Relational Search Is Not Capability Search

Even perfect documentary and relational search may not expose tacit or live capability.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Relational Coverage ≠ Historical Capability Coverage.**

This reinforces Source Resolution 027.

---

## 17. Search Method Has an Evaluation Space

Historical inquiry therefore has layers:

`Path Search → Text Search → Semantic Reading → Relational Analysis → Temporal Comparison → Capability/Practice Examination`.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Completeness Is Method-Relative.**

---

## 18. Search Tool Changes Can Change What Becomes Findable

A future search system may expose relationships an earlier search could not.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Failure Under Method A ≠ Historical Search Failure Under Future Method B.**

---

## 19. Later Discovery Does Not Mean Earlier Search Was Dishonest

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Later Discovery ≠ Historical Proof of Earlier Negligence Without Evidence About Earlier Search Capability and Scope.**

---

## 20. But Search Limitations Should Be Declared

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Methodological Honesty Includes Declaring Known Search Boundaries.**

---

## 21. Search Provenance Prevents False Certainty

Without search provenance:

“not found”

can silently become:

“does not exist.”

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Provenance Is an Anti-False-Certainty Mechanism.**

---

## 22. Search Provenance Also Prevents False Reopening

If a region was already thoroughly searched to saturation, future researchers should know that.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Provenance Can Reduce Wasteful Rediscovery Without Preventing Re-Examination Under New Methods or Evidence.**

---

## 23. Saturation Is Time-Indexed

A corpus can change.

A search saturated on one repository version may not be saturated after new material is added.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Saturation Is Relative to Corpus State and Time.**

---

## 24. Saturation Is Question-Indexed

A corpus may be saturated for one question but not another.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Saturation for Question A ≠ Historical Search Saturation for Question B.**

---

## 25. Saturation Is Method-Indexed

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Saturation Under Method A ≠ Historical Search Saturation Under Method B.**

---

## 26. Search Results Need Corpus Version Provenance

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Corpus Search Without Corpus-Version Provenance Is Not Fully Reproducible.**

This matters strongly to evolving Concord repositories.

---

## 27. File Movement Can Break Naive Search History

A file can move while content remains identical.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Path Provenance Should Not Be Mistaken for Stable Content Identity.**

This reinforces Source Resolution 010 and 015.

---

## 28. Search Result Counts Are Not Stable Historical Objects by Default

Counts change when:

- corpus changes;
- classification changes;
- duplicates are resolved;
- search method improves;
- scope expands.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Count ≠ Timeless Property of the Corpus.**

---

## 29. A Count Can Still Be Historically Valid

A result such as “205 answers identified by this date under this method” can remain valid as historical state even if later work finds more.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Expansion of a Search Result ≠ Historical Falsification of the Earlier Bounded Result.**

---

## 30. Extraction Is an Interpretive Operation

Identifying a “distinct WHY answer” requires classification and judgement.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Extracted Object ≠ Raw Source Object.**

The extraction method is part of provenance.

---

## 31. Compression Can Alter Count

Two candidate answers may later be merged; one answer may later split.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Classification Count Depends on Historical Granularity Rules.**

---

## 32. Quantity Does Not Establish Truth

The WHY source explicitly warns against argument from quantity.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Number of Supporting Reasons ≠ Historical Strength of Conclusion by Default.**

---

## 33. Repeated Discovery Across Independent Areas Can Still Be Evidence

But only if independence and distinctness are established.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Cross-Domain Recurrence Can Be Evidentially Relevant Without Becoming Proof by Count.**

---

## 34. The History of Research Includes Negative Search Results

A well-conducted search that finds nothing can still constrain future inquiry.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Negative Search Result Can Be Knowledge When Its Search Space and Method Are Preserved.**

---

## 35. Failed Searches Can Reveal Vocabulary Problems

Repeated inability to find a named concept may later reveal that the concept existed under another name.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Search Failure Can Be Evidence About Terminology Rather Than About Conceptual Absence.**

---

## 36. Search History Can Reveal Concept Formation

If a term begins appearing only after a certain date while precursor functions existed earlier, Historical can distinguish:

- conceptual precursor;
- named formalisation;
- mature architecture.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Terminological Emergence ≠ Historical Conceptual Origin.**

## 37. Search History Can Reveal Architectural Migration

Repeated searches across V1 → V1.1 → V1.2 can show that a function:

- disappeared;
- moved;
- split;
- merged;
- was renamed;
- became operationalised.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Source-Resolution History Can Itself Become Evidence of Architectural Development.**

## 44. Relationship to ESCP

This pass is almost a direct operationalisation of ESCP.

An evaluator can be completely correct within the examined space and still be wrong about the larger corpus.

More strongly:

> **The boundary of the examined space must itself be historical information if later users are to know what the conclusion actually covers.**

---

## 45. Relationship to Source-Resolution Saturation

This is directly relevant to the present Historical project.

A future declaration of saturation should specify at minimum:

- which repository generations were examined;
- which areas were deeply read;
- which were sampled;
- which search methods were used;
- what remained inaccessible/unread;
- what stopping rule was applied;
- whether later passes were yielding new categories or only refinements.

Therefore:

> **Historical Source-Resolution Saturation Should Be a Provenanced Claim About a Defined Search Space, Not a Claim of Omniscience.**

---

The sign warns that Historical may never capture the whole machine.

> **Historical should remember which parts of the machine it actually looked at.**

Without that, future users cannot distinguish:

- absence;
- invisibility;

- failed search;
- deliberate non-examination.

This pass produced a modest but operationally important addition.

> “nothing was found”
