## 2. Ignorance Has Historical State

A civilisation does not only possess knowledge.

It also possesses explicit unknowns.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Knowledge State Includes Historically Recognised Unknowns.**

An open question is therefore not an absence of information. It is positive information about the boundary of contemporary knowledge.

---

## 3. Unknown Is Not the Same as Unasked

A question may be:

- explicitly open;
- suspected but unformalised;
- not yet conceived;
- mistakenly believed closed.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Unknown ≠ Historical Unasked Question ≠ Historical False Closure.**

These are different epistemic states.

---

## 4. The Time at Which a Question Became Askable Matters

Some problems only become visible after new concepts, evidence or participants exist.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Question Formation Is Itself a Knowledge Event.**

The appearance of a question can mark expansion of civilisation's evaluation space.

---

## 5. A Question Can Precede the Vocabulary Needed to State It

This parallels conceptual existence preceding terminology.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Problem Recognition Can Precede Historical Formal Question Naming.**

Earlier records may contain symptoms of a later-named problem.

---

## 6. Open-Question Registers Are Maps of Epistemic Boundaries

The V1.2 register describes itself as an active research map.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Open-Question Register Is a Snapshot of the Civilisation's Self-Recognised Epistemic Frontier.**

This is a distinct historical object from the answers eventually produced.

---

## 7. Current Open State Is Not Full Question History

The source deliberately avoids duplicating every prior status change because V1.1 preserves that audit history.

Candidate principle:

> **Current Question State ≠ Historical Question Lifecycle.**

Historical may need both, at different layers.

---

## 8. Question Status Is Versioned State

The register uses statuses such as:

- FOUNDATIONAL OPEN;
- MAJOR OPEN PROGRAMME;
- ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT;
- PARTIALLY ADDRESSED;
- ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED / VALIDATION OPEN;
- READY FOR TESTING;
- PERMANENT ADVERSARIAL QUESTION.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Question Status Is a Time-Indexed Epistemic State.**

---

## 9. Closure Has Types

The Closed Questions material distinguishes:

- conceptual closure;
- bounded closure;
- retired closure;
- architecturally addressed closure.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Closed ≠ Single Epistemic State.**

The reason and type of closure matter.

---

## 10. Architecturally Addressed Is Not Proven

The source is explicit:

> **ARCHITECTURALLY ADDRESSED is a distinct class of closed question.**

It means an architecture-generation question has an answer, not that the mechanism is empirically valid.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Architectural Closure ≠ Historical Empirical Validation.**

---

## 11. Closure of One Question Can Generate Another

The architecture-generation question:

> “What architecture could address this?”

may close while a new question becomes:

> “Does it actually work?”

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Question Closure Can Transform Rather Than Terminate Inquiry.**

This is a question-lineage relation.

---

## 12. Question Lineage Is a Graph

A question may:

- split;
- merge;
- narrow;
- broaden;
- transform;
- be superseded;
- reopen.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Inquiry History Is a Question Graph, Not a List of Open and Closed Items.**

---

## 13. Reopening Is a First-Class Historical Event

A question thought closed may later reopen because of:

- contradictory evidence;
- new participant type;
- changed technology;
- implementation failure;
- newly visible dimension.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Reopening Does Not Erase the Earlier Closure.**

The earlier closure remains evidence about the state of knowledge at that time.

---

## 14. False Closure Is Historically Valuable

If a civilisation incorrectly believed a question solved, that mistake may explain later decisions.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical False Closure Is Evidence About Historical Epistemic State.**

It should not simply be replaced by the later corrected status.

---

## 15. Later Answer Does Not Mean Earlier Question Was Answerable

Some questions require evidence or technology unavailable at the time.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Resolvability ≠ Earlier Resolvability.**

Historical evaluation should distinguish failure to answer from inability to answer with then-available means.

---

## 16. An Unresolved Question Can Still Constrain Action

The Sentience and Agency questions provide a strong example.

Civilisation may not know whether an unfamiliar intelligence is sentient, yet may adopt precautionary protections.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Uncertainty ≠ Historical Absence of Action.**

And:

> **Historical Protective Action Under Uncertainty ≠ Historical Resolution of the Underlying Question.**

---

## 17. A Question Can Be Civilly Irrelevant Without Being Epistemically Solved

The Open Questions register retires the broad metaphysical question of which copy is “really” the original person from required civil scope.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Civil Retirement of a Question ≠ Historical Epistemic Solution of the Question.**

This is a genuinely important distinction.

---

## 18. Scope Retirement Is Not Truth Closure

A civilisation may decide:

> we do not need to answer this to govern legitimately.

That does not mean:

> the answer is known.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Scope Closure ≠ Historical Truth Closure.**

---

## 19. Permanent Adversarial Questions Are Deliberately Non-Closing

The register includes a status:

> **PERMANENT ADVERSARIAL QUESTION**

This is historically unusual and valuable.

Candidate principle:

> **Some Historical Questions May Be Preserved Precisely Because Permanent Closure Would Increase Systemic Risk.**

The question functions as a standing attack surface against complacency.

---

## 20. Institutionalised Doubt Is Historical Architecture

A permanent adversarial question is not mere uncertainty.

It is a deliberate civil mechanism for preserving challenge.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Persistence of a Question Can Be a Designed Safeguard Rather Than Evidence of Developmental Failure.**

---

## 21. Closure Pressure Can Be Historically Dangerous

Institutions often benefit from declaring problems solved.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Closure Status Can Be Affected by Institutional Incentive.**

## 22. The Authority to Close a Question Matters

Not every participant or institution should be able to declare an unresolved problem solved for the whole civilisation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Question Closure ≠ Self-Authenticating Epistemic Authority.**

Historical should preserve the closure process and status basis.

Historical implication:

## 24. Evidence Standards Can Change

What counted as adequate simulation, replication or field evidence in one era may later change.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Evidence Threshold Is Itself Versioned Methodological State.**

Later standards should not automatically be projected backward.

---

## 25. Historical Validation State Is Multidimensional

An architecture can be:

- conceptually coherent;
- adversarially tested;
- simulated;
- piloted;
- independently replicated;
- longitudinally observed.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Validation State ≠ Binary Valid/Invalid Label.**

## 26. Failure After Closure Does Not Erase the Earlier Architecture

The Closed Questions register states:

> **Failure does not erase the fact that an architecture existed; it records that the architecture did not survive reality.**

Historical principle:

> **Historical Rejection of an Architecture ≠ Historical Erasure of Its Prior Addressed State.**

## 28. The History of Ignorance Can Explain the History of Harm

If a harmful decision was made before a relevant risk was recognised, the historical question is different from a decision made after the risk became known.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Responsibility Context Can Depend on Whether a Relevant Question Was Recognised at the Time.**

This connects to Source Resolutions 011, 020 and 023.

## 29. But “We Did Not Know” Requires Provenance

An institution can claim ignorance after the fact.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Claim of Ignorance ≠ Historical Demonstration of Ignorance.**

Historical should examine whether warnings, dissent or open questions already existed.

## 30. Known Unknowns and Ignored Warnings Are Different

Candidate principle:

## 32. Recovery Registers Preserve Lost Questions

The presence of Recovery Registers is itself significant.

They exist because unresolved questions can become fragmented, misplaced or lost during development.

Candidate principle:

> **Civilisational Memory Can Lose Questions as Well as Answers.**

This is a genuinely important Historical formulation.

---

## 33. Lost Questions Can Be More Dangerous Than Lost Answers

An answer can sometimes be rediscovered if the question remains visible.

If the question disappears entirely, civilisation may no longer know where its blind spot is.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of Important Questions Can Be as Important as Preservation of Their Current Answers.**

---

## 34. Question Recovery Is a Historical Event

When a forgotten unresolved problem is rediscovered:

> **Historical Recovery of a Question ≠ Historical Creation of a New Question.**

Its prior lineage should be restored where demonstrable.

---

## 35. Question Loss Can Create False Confidence

If unresolved questions disappear from active registers while the surrounding architecture survives, future participants may assume completeness.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Loss of Unresolved Questions Can Manufacture Apparent Certainty.**

This is a major anti-compression warning.

---

## 36. Consolidation Can Destroy Epistemic Boundaries

A clean summary may preserve conclusions but omit:

- unresolved assumptions;

## 37. Open Questions Are Part of Provenance

A current architecture should ideally be linked not only to:

- source documents;

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Provenance of an Architecture Includes Material Unresolved Questions About That Architecture.**

## 47. Relationship to the Humanity Machine

The Humanity Machine argues that value exists in relationships and change.

Open Questions add another relationship type:

`Known Knowledge ↔ Known Unknown ↔ Candidate Answer ↔ Evidence ↔ Challenge ↔ Closure ↔ Reopening`.

The knowledge system is not merely a warehouse of answers.

It is also a dynamic map of uncertainty.

Candidate principle:

> **A Civilisation's Ignorance Has Structure.**

That structure can change through time and can itself contain historically important information.

---

## 48. Relationship to the Legacy Ladder

The Legacy Ladder warns that losing a pathway can force rediscovery.

Open Questions reveal another form of lost pathway.

If civilisation preserves an answer but loses:

- the question;
- the alternatives;
- the validation gap;
- the reason the answer was provisional;

then it may inherit a conclusion without knowing where to challenge it.

Candidate principle:

> **Questions Are Part of the Legacy Ladder of Knowledge.**

---

## 49. Relationship to ESCP

This pass exposes several major ESCP failures:

- current open questions can be mistaken for all historical unknowns;
- a closed question can be mistaken for a proven answer;
- civil retirement can be mistaken for epistemic solution;
- later answerability can be projected backward;
- forgotten questions can manufacture apparent completeness;
- architecture closure can be mistaken for validation closure;
- one participant class can be mistaken for all future classes;
- closure at one abstraction layer can be mistaken for closure at all layers;
- a current question register can be mistaken for the complete historical question lineage.

Therefore:

> **Historical Evaluation-Space Completeness Requires Preservation of Material Unknowns, unresolved questions and closure boundaries, not merely accumulated answers.**

## 50. Source-Resolution Result

This pass produced a genuinely important addition to the Historical model.

Previous passes established that Historical must preserve:

- facts;
- relationships;
- patterns;
- structures;
- processes;
- provenance;
- uncertainty;
- competing interpretations.

The Open Questions material adds another first-class historical category:

> **recognised ignorance.**

A civilisation can lose knowledge by losing an answer.

It can also lose knowledge by losing the fact that it never had an answer.

That second failure may be more dangerous because it manufactures confidence.

## 51. Strongest Formulations

> **A civilisation's ignorance has structure.**

> **Civilisational memory can lose questions as well as answers.**

> **Historical preservation of important questions can be as important as preservation of their current answers.**

And the strongest warning:

> **If civilisation preserves its answers but forgets the questions that remain unresolved, history can transform uncertainty into false certainty without changing a single surviving answer.**
