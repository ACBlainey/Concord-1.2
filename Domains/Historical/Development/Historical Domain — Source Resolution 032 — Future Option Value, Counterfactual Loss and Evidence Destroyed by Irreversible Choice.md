> **An irreversible decision can destroy not merely an object, participant or record, but the future evidence that would have revealed why preserving it mattered.**

## 2. Historical Records Are Produced by Surviving Possibilities

Historical usually looks backward from events that occurred.

The WHY material exposes a harder problem.

Some possible futures never become historical events because an earlier choice prevents them.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Non-Occurrence Can Be Produced by Historical Elimination of the Conditions Required for Occurrence.**

---

## 3. Absence After Destruction Is Not Independent Evidence of Worthlessness

Temporal Value Uncertainty gives a clear sequence:

`Low Current Value → Prediction of Low Future Value → Destruction → Development Prevented`.

The later observation:

> “No future value appeared”

cannot independently validate the original prediction.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Absence of Later Value After Irreversible Removal ≠ Historical Validation of the Prediction That No Later Value Existed.**

---

## 4. Intervention Can Destroy Its Own Counterevidence

Recursive Epistemic Closure generalises this.

If a model removes the participant, institution, observer or record capable of contradicting it, the model can become self-confirming.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Irreversible Intervention Can Alter the Future Evidence Environment Against Which the Intervention Would Otherwise Be Evaluated.**

This extends Source Resolution 023's observation-density finding.

---

## 5. Historical Evaluation Must Preserve Evidence-Environment Change

A consequential decision may therefore need provenance for:

- what evidence existed before;
- what was removed;
- what observations became impossible afterward;
- what alternative pathways were closed.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Decision Provenance Can Include Material Future Evidence Pathways Destroyed by the Decision.**

This is candidate architecture.

---

## 6. Counterfactual Evidence Is Not Ordinary Evidence

Historical cannot know with certainty what an eliminated future would have contained.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Closed Possibility ≠ Historical Event That Would Certainly Have Occurred.**

The lost possibility should remain marked as counterfactual uncertainty.

---

## 7. The Correct Historical Object May Be the Lost Option

Historical can sometimes establish:

- an option existed;
- it was deliberately or accidentally closed;
- later testing became impossible.

It cannot necessarily establish what the option would have produced.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Existence of a Lost Option Can Be Evidenced Without Claiming Historical Knowledge of Its Unrealised Outcome.**

---

## 8. Option Value Has Historical Provenance

Temporal Value Uncertainty argues that preservation can retain developmental possibilities.

Historical implication:

> **Historical Option Value ≠ Historical Proof of Future Benefit.**

Option value is the preserved capacity to learn or act later.

---

## 9. A Snapshot Is Not a Trajectory

The source states:

> **A snapshot is not a trajectory.**

Historical records can overemphasise realised capability at a moment.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Realised Capability ≠ Historical Upper Bound on Developmental Capability.**

## 10. Evaluator Development Is Part of History

Temporal Value Uncertainty identifies four changing variables:

- evaluated participant;
- evaluator;
- relationship;
- environment.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Evaluation Is Time-Indexed to the Evaluator's Contemporary Evaluation Space.**

A later evaluator may recognise dimensions unavailable to an earlier evaluator.

## 11. Later Recognition Does Not Make Earlier Evaluators Omniscient

Candidate principle:

> **Later Recognition of Value ≠ Earlier Availability of the Representational Capacity Required to Recognise That Value.**

This should be preserved when evaluating old decisions.

## 12. But Contemporary Blindness Is Not Automatic Excuse

If evidence or dissent already exposed the missing dimension, later actors should not retroactively classify it as unknowable.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Evaluation-Space Limitation Must Be Distinguished From Historical Failure to Use Available Corrective Evidence.**

## 49. Relationship to ESCP

This pass adds a particularly severe ESCP form.

An evaluation space can be incomplete not only because a dimension is currently missing.

The decision made from that incomplete space can destroy the future observer, state or relationship that would have exposed the missing dimension.

Thus:

> **Evaluation-Space Incompleteness Can Become Self-Sealing Through Irreversible Action.**

Historical must preserve evidence of that possibility where material.

---

## 50. Relationship to the Sign at the Door of Historical

The developing Historical sign says, in effect:

> we are trying to store something whose most important information may lie in patterns and relationships, and some of it may be unstoreable.

This pass adds:

> **some of the information Historical most needs may belong to futures that never happened because earlier choices made them impossible.**

Historical cannot store those futures as facts.

But it can sometimes preserve:

- that the option existed;
- why it was closed;
- what evidence existed at closure;
- what future tests became impossible;
- what uncertainty remained.

That is the nearest honest historical representation of the lost possibility.

## 51. Source-Resolution Result

This pass produced another distinct Historical extension.

Previous work established preservation of:

- events;
- states;
- relationships;
- patterns;
- questions;
- uncertainty;
- places;
- practices.

The WHY material adds a new object of historical concern:

> **the option space that existed before irreversible choices narrowed it.**

Historical should not pretend to know unrealised futures.

But neither should it allow the surviving path to manufacture the appearance that no alternatives ever existed.

---

## 52. Strongest Formulations

> **Historical survival of the chosen path does not prove historical absence of alternatives.**

> **Each generation inherits not only predecessor knowledge but predecessor selection of what remained knowable.**

> **Historical completeness should be evaluated partly by preserved corrective capacity, not merely record quantity.**

And the strongest formulation:

> **An irreversible decision can destroy not only a possible future, but the future evidence that would have allowed civilisation to discover that destroying that possibility was a mistake.**
