## 2. The Concord Explicitly Separates Stable Source From Correctable Architecture

The Constitutional Core states that its central problem is:

> **How can a civilisation preserve stable ethical and constitutional identity while remaining capable of learning, correcting failure and adapting its mechanisms over time?**

Its hierarchy is:

**Source-Anchored Ethical Kernel**  
↓  
**The Concord — Master Civilisational Volume**  
↓  
**Derived Principles**  
↓  
**Constitutional Constraints**  
↓  
**Specialist Constitutional and Operational Architectures**  
↓  
**Mechanisms and Portable Modules**  
↓  
**Applications**  
↓  
**Experiments, Evidence and Validation**

The further a system sits below the Ethical Kernel, the greater the legitimate scope for correction, replacement, experimentation and redesign.

Historical therefore cannot treat all Concord change as one homogeneous kind of version change.

The historical record must preserve **what changed, at what architectural layer, under what authority, and with what status**.

## 3. The Ethical Kernel Creates a Special Historical Class

The current Ethical Kernel is explicitly:

**CORE / FOUNDATIONAL / SOURCE-ANCHORED / CONSTITUTIONALLY FIXED**

The ten extracted principles are not subject to ordinary amendment by Governance, Judiciary, Recursive Constitutional Architecture, Ratchet, later operational systems or the preferences of the author.

But their:

- interpretation;
- application;
- empirical effects;
- historical provenance;
- downstream mechanisms;
- derived constitutional constraints

remain open to examination and correction.

This produces a crucial Historical distinction:

**Fixed Source Object ≠ Fixed Interpretation ≠ Fixed Implementation**

Historical must be capable of preserving changes in interpretation and implementation without falsely representing the source kernel itself as having changed.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Versioning Must Respect the Constitutional Mutability Class of the Object Being Versioned.**

## 4. Canonical Does Not Mean Timeless

Several Concord systems explicitly use the term **canonical**.

Ratchet V4.2 provides an especially clear definition: canonical means that the document is intended to represent the **current integrated architecture** at that time. It does not mean that every mechanism is empirically validated.

Therefore:

> **Canonical ≠ Eternal**

and:

> **Canonical ≠ Empirically Proven**

Historical records must preserve both the canonical status and its effective period.

A document can truthfully have been canonical at time T and later be superseded without becoming historically false.

Candidate principle:

> **A Superseded Canonical Record Remains Evidence of What Was Canonical During Its Effective Period.**

## 5. Version Status Is Part of Meaning

The same text can carry very different civilisational meaning depending on whether it is:

- current canonical;
- superseded canonical;
- candidate;
- experimental;
- rejected;
- archived;
- fixed source;
- interpretation;
- implementation;
- external adaptation.

Therefore a historical copy without status metadata is incomplete.

A minimum historical architectural record should preserve:

**Object + Version + Status + Effective Period + Architectural Layer + Provenance + Supersession/Relationship Links**

This is stronger than simply retaining old files.

## 6. V1 → V1.1 → V1.2 Is a Real Historical Test Case

The repository sequence itself demonstrates the problem.

V1 contains earlier forms of:

- The Concord master volume;
- Recursive Constitutional Architecture V1;
- Governance;
- Ratchet;
- the Ethical Kernel;
- numerous companion and developmental papers.

V1.1 contains later canonical integrations including Recursive Constitutional Architecture V2, Governance V2 and Ratchet V4.2.

V1.2 retains much of this canonical architecture while adding substantial new structure, development, source resolution, portable modules, Civil Attention, Domains and other work.

Some files are byte-identical across V1.1 and V1.2. Others have changed, moved, been replaced, expanded or reclassified.

Therefore repository version alone is not enough to infer document version.

> **Repository Version ≠ Document Version ≠ Architectural Version ≠ Constitutional Status**

This is a major Historical finding.

## 7. Identical Survival Across Repository Versions Is Historically Meaningful

Several canonical files in V1.1 and V1.2 have identical blob identities.

This means the transition from one repository generation to another did not necessarily alter those architectural objects.

Historical should therefore distinguish:

- **continued unchanged**;
- **moved unchanged**;
- **renamed unchanged**;
- **reclassified unchanged**;
- **modified**;
- **superseded**;
- **forked**;
- **withdrawn**.

A repository transition must not automatically be represented as a substantive constitutional change.

Candidate principle:

> **Container Change ≠ Content Change.**

## 8. Conversely, Similar Names Do Not Prove Continuity

V1 has `Recursive Constitutional Architecture V1.md`.

Later repositories contain `Recursive Constitutional Architecture V2 Canonical.md`.

The shared system identity suggests lineage, but Historical should not infer exact continuity merely from naming.

The historical relationship needs explicit provenance:

**Predecessor → Development/Revision Process → Successor**

Where that chain cannot be fully reconstructed, the uncertainty should remain visible.

> **Apparent Version Lineage ≠ Proven Version Lineage.**

## 9. Recursive Constitutional Architecture Makes History Operationally Necessary

The Recursive Constitutional Architecture exists because stable principles must coexist with continual learning and correction.

It treats constitutional development as recursive rather than as isolated amendment.

Operational systems generate evidence. Evidence informs review. Review may justify correction of downstream mechanisms while the stable source remains bounded.

This means Historical is not merely a passive archive beside recursive constitutional development.

Recursive development depends on reliable memory of:

- prior architecture;
- reasons for change;
- evidence available at the time;
- observed failures;
- rejected alternatives;
- previous interpretations;
- outcomes after modification.

Without this, recursion becomes amnesiac iteration.

Candidate principle:

> **Recursive Constitutional Development Requires Recoverable Constitutional Memory.**

## 10. The Historical Record Must Preserve Why, Not Only What

A simple diff can show that text changed.

It cannot necessarily show:

- why it changed;
- what problem triggered the change;
- what evidence was available;
- what authority approved it;
- what alternatives were rejected;
- whether the change was experimental or canonical;
- what outcome followed.

Historical therefore requires more than file snapshots.

For material constitutional or architectural change, the ideal chain is:

**Prior State → Trigger/Problem → Evidence → Deliberation → Authority/Decision → New State → Effective Time → Outcome → Later Review**

This mirrors the Governance and Civil Attention findings from Source Resolutions 003 and 006.

## 11. Ratchet Adds Persistence and Reversal History

Ratchet states:

> **Changes that demonstrate beneficial outcomes should be capable of persisting, while harmful, obsolete or failed changes must remain reversible.**

Its process is:

**Proposal → Action → Observation → Evidence → Evaluation → Provisional Persistence → Continued Measurement → Review → Persist / Modify / Reverse / Replace**

Historical therefore needs to preserve not merely successful persistent states but also:

- provisional changes;
- evidence used to justify persistence;
- reversals;
- replacements;
- failed mechanisms;
- reasons for abandonment.

Otherwise later observers can see only the surviving architecture and may falsely infer that it was inevitable or always present.

Candidate principle:

> **Survival of an Architecture Must Not Erase the Alternatives, Failures and Reversals That Explain How It Survived.**

## 12. Epistemic Status Must Survive Versioning

Ratchet V4.2 distinguishes:

- established Concordian principles;
- derived Ratchet principles;
- candidate mechanisms;
- open questions;
- experimental propositions.

It explicitly warns that appearing in a canonical paper does not make a proposed mechanism established.

Historical must preserve such internal epistemic classification.

A historical snapshot labelled merely “canonical Ratchet V4.2” could otherwise falsely upgrade experimental mechanisms into established civilisational facts.

Thus:

> **Document Status ≠ Status of Every Proposition Inside the Document.**

Historical representation may require proposition-level or section-level status where the source architecture itself makes that distinction material.

## 13. Supersession Must Preserve the Prior Effective State

Source Resolution 003 established:

> **Operational Supersession ≠ Historical Erasure**

The constitutional/versioning pass strengthens this.

When version B supersedes version A, Historical should preserve:

- A's content;
- A's effective period;
- A's status during that period;
- the event/process that superseded A;
- B's effective start;
- the relationship between A and B.

This allows a later observer to answer two different questions:

1. **What is the current architecture?**
2. **What architecture governed at the historical time being examined?**

These must not be conflated.

## 14. Historical State Must Be Time-Addressable

For constitutional and architectural records, “current” is not enough.

A historical query may need to ask:

> What was the canonical governance architecture on date T?

or:

> Which version of a mechanism was in force when decision D was made?

Historical therefore needs effective-time semantics, not only storage timestamps.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Constitutional Meaning Is Time-Indexed.**

This parallels Source Resolution 004's finding that historical measurement must preserve temporal meaning, not merely historical values.

## 15. A Decision Must Be Interpretable Against the Architecture Then in Force

Governance, Judiciary, Civil Attention and other systems can only be fairly reconstructed if the relevant constitutional/architectural state at the time is recoverable.

A later version may change:

- authority boundaries;
- evidence requirements;
- review mechanisms;
- terminology;
- permitted actions;
- institutional interfaces.

Using the current architecture to interpret an older action can create anachronism.

Therefore:

> **Historical Evaluation of an Action Should Preserve the Governing Architectural Context Available at the Time, While Allowing Later Reassessment to Be Clearly Marked as Retrospective.**

This is the constitutional analogue of the “contemporary historical state” versus “retrospectively corrected historical state” distinction established for metrics.

## 16. Current Truth and Historical Authority Must Be Separated

A superseded architecture may no longer possess present authority.

Yet it remains authoritative evidence about its own historical effective period.

This produces:

> **Current Authority ≠ Historical Evidential Authority**

Historical custody must not reactivate superseded law or architecture merely by preserving it.

Equally, current systems must not erase prior authority states merely because they have been replaced.

## 17. The Ethical Kernel Needs Provenance Even Though It Is Fixed

“Fixed” does not mean “ahistorical.”

The Ethical Kernel document itself says its name anchors the extraction work to provenance and the Concord framework in which the principles were assembled.

Its historical record should therefore preserve:

- source-extraction provenance;
- canonical formulation;
- effective status;
- interpretive history;
- challenges and tests;
- applications;
- external adaptations;
- any attempted modifications clearly classified as external/different-framework proposals rather than silent amendments.

This is particularly important because the licence permits adaptation while the Concord's constitutional classification separately constrains what can be represented as the unchanged canonical kernel.

Thus:

> **Legal Permission to Adapt ≠ Constitutional Continuity of Identity.**

Historical should preserve that distinction.

## 18. Forks Must Not Be Mistaken for Successive Versions

A future derivative may alter the Ethical Kernel or another architecture under the licence.

That does not necessarily make it Concord version N+1.

Historical needs a relationship type such as:

- successor;
- predecessor;
- fork;
- adaptation;
- experiment;
- external derivative;
- rejected proposal;
- alternative implementation.

Candidate principle:

> **Change Relationship Must Be Recorded; Sequence Alone Does Not Establish Succession.**

## 19. Git History Is Useful but Insufficient

Git provides valuable technical provenance:

- commits;
- timestamps;
- file paths;
- diffs;
- hashes;
- branches;
- repository history.

But civilisational Historical needs semantic provenance that Git alone cannot guarantee:

- constitutional status;
- authority;
- effective date;
- reason;
- evidence;
- predecessor/successor meaning;
- whether a move was merely housekeeping;
- whether a text was canonical, candidate or experimental;
- whether a change was actually adopted.

Therefore:

> **Repository Provenance ≠ Constitutional Provenance**

Git can be one evidential substrate of Historical, not the whole architecture.

## 20. Historical Must Preserve Negative Developmental Knowledge

The architecture repeatedly values correction and reversibility.

Therefore failed, rejected and superseded systems can contain high-value information:

- why an approach failed;
- what risk it created;
- what dependency it lacked;
- what evidence overturned it;
- why it was rejected.

Erasing this creates a risk that later generations repeat already-discovered failures.

Candidate principle:

> **Rejected and Failed Architecture Can Be Civilisational Knowledge Even When It Has No Current Authority.**

This is consistent with Governance's preservation of failed ideas and reasons.

## 21. Constitutional Change Records Need Independent Survival

Source Resolution 003 established an anti-capture principle:

> **The System Being Audited Should Not Possess Unilateral Control Over the Survival of the Record Required to Audit It.**

This applies strongly to constitutional change.

A system capable of changing its own rules should not be able to erase:

- the prior rules;
- the authorisation for change;
- the evidence and reasoning;
- dissent;
- the exact transition point.

Historical therefore provides an independent custody function for constitutional provenance.

This does not give Historical veto or amendment authority.

## 22. Historical Custody Does Not Confer Constitutional Authority

Historical may preserve:

- constitutions;
- canonical architectures;
- amendments/revisions;
- superseded systems;
- evidence;
- interpretive histories.

It does not thereby gain authority to:

- amend the Constitution;
- decide which interpretation is legally binding;
- reinstate a superseded architecture;
- certify a candidate as canonical;
- determine governance policy.

Thus:

> **Custody of Constitutional History Is Not Constitutional Authority.**

This is a direct specialisation of the general Historical non-authority principle.

## 24. Candidate Lifecycle

A provisional constitutional/architectural lifecycle is:

**Proposal / Existing Architecture**

↓

**Evaluation / Development / Evidence**

↓

**Authorised Adoption or Canonicalisation**

↓

**Effective Architecture**

↓

**Operational Evidence / Review**

↓

**Persist / Clarify / Modify / Replace / Reverse / Fork**

↓

**New Effective State**

while Historical preserves:

**Prior Effective State + Transition Provenance + Reason/Evidence + Authority + Relationship to New State**

This creates a version graph rather than a simple overwrite chain.

## 25. Version Graph, Not Merely Version List

The archive search demonstrates that Concord development is not always linear.

Objects can:

- persist unchanged across repository generations;
- be moved;
- be renamed;
- be rewritten;
- be split;
- be integrated;
- be superseded;
- be forked;
- remain experimental beside canonical material.

Therefore Historical should model architectural history as a graph.

A simple:

**V1 → V1.1 → V1.2**

is useful at repository level but insufficient at object level.

Candidate principle:

> **Civilisational Version History Is a Provenance Graph, Not Necessarily a Linear Sequence.**

## 26. ESCP Implication

Version loss creates an evaluation-space failure.

If later evaluators can see only the current architecture, they may not know:

- what alternatives existed;
- what earlier actors knew;
- what constraints were then in force;
- what changed;
- why it changed;
- what evidence was absent;
- which failures had already been discovered.

They may therefore produce an internally accurate evaluation of the current state while drawing invalid conclusions about historical decisions or developmental causation.

The ESCP implication is:

> **A Current-State Corpus Can Be Complete for Present Operation Yet Incomplete for Historical Evaluation.**

Historical version preservation is therefore part of maintaining a sufficiently complete evaluation space.

## 27. Important Gap Exposed

The current Concord has strong ingredients for constitutional evolution:

- stable source principles;
- recursive constitutional development;
- governance decision provenance;
- Ratchet persistence/reversal;
- KCS provenance;
- repository version history.

But this pass has not located a single dedicated **Constitutional Amendment / Architectural Change Register** that unifies:

- exact effective versions;
- adoption authority;
- reasons;
- evidence;
- predecessor/successor relationships;
- effective dates;
- supersession;
- historical status.

This is not proof that no such mechanism exists elsewhere; ESCP applies.

It is, however, a sufficiently strong gap candidate to carry forward.

## 29. Current Finding

This pass substantially strengthens the Historical Domain hypothesis.

Historical is not merely responsible for storing old constitutional documents.

It needs to preserve the **time-addressable provenance graph of civilisational architecture** so that a future participant can determine:

- what architecture existed;
- what status it held;
- when it was effective;
- what it replaced;
- why it changed;
- who or what had authority to change it;
- what evidence supported the transition;
- what alternatives or dissent existed;
- what later outcomes were observed;
- whether the object was current, superseded, fixed, experimental, rejected or forked.

The V1 → V1.1 → V1.2 repositories provide a live demonstration of why this distinction matters.

The next Historical source-resolution pass should follow one of the remaining high-value unresolved interfaces rather than immediately writing a final domain specification. Strong candidates include black-box/sensor evidence, AI/tool retirement and cold storage, threat-intelligence records, or public historical access/library architecture.
