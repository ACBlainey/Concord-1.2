## 1. Purpose

This pass examines one of the most dangerous possible category errors for the Historical Domain:

> When Historical preserves records about a participant, what exactly is being preserved — and what must Historical never claim to own, restore, terminate or define?

The issue applies to humans, artificial intelligences, hybrids and future substrates, but becomes especially visible where digital participants may be:

- paused;
- restored;
- copied;
- branched;
- migrated;
- merged;
- re-keyed;
- placed in cold storage;
- succeeded;
- represented by historical credentials that no longer uniquely identify a current participant.

## 2. Continuity Is Not Historical Custody

The Continuity Protocol defines continuity as a constitutional responsibility concerning:

- identity;
- stewardship;
- preservation;
- restoration;
- continuity services;
- resilience;
- succession;
- accumulated capability.

Historical overlaps with some of these information requirements, but does not inherit the Continuity function.

The critical boundary is:

### Continuity
maintains or restores legitimate civil capability, identity continuity and participant continuity according to the participant's authorised arrangements and constitutional process.

### Historical
preserves records of prior states, declarations, provenance, transitions, disputes, decisions and outcomes where legitimately retainable.

Therefore:

> **Historical Record of a Participant ≠ Participant Continuity Service.**

and:

> **Historical Custody of Identity Evidence ≠ Custody of Identity.**

---

## 3. The Participant Is Not the Record

Source Resolution 005 established:

> **The participant is not merely data.**

This pass strengthens that boundary.

A Historical system may preserve:

- a participant's prior civil status;
- a continuity declaration version;
- a restoration event;
- a branch event;
- a prior credential;
- a decision concerning succession;
- a contribution record;
- a provenance relationship.

None of these objects is automatically the participant.

Candidate principle:

> **Record About a Participant ≠ Participant.**

This sounds simple, but becomes constitutionally critical when records can include high-fidelity digital state.

---

## 4. A Backup Is Not Automatically a Historical Record

The Continuity Protocol explicitly provides for restoration from backup and continuity services.

A backup may therefore exist primarily to support participant continuity.

Historical value may exist in metadata about that backup or its use, but that does not mean Historical should own or indefinitely retain the executable/restorable state.

Candidate distinction:

> **Continuity Backup ≠ Historical Record ≠ Historical Metadata About the Backup.**

For example, Historical might legitimately preserve that:

- backup B existed;
- the participant authorised a specified restoration policy;
- restoration R occurred;
- a collision was later detected;
- a decision process resolved the civil claims.

That does not establish that Historical should retain a permanently executable copy of B.

---

## 5. Continuity Declarations Are Versioned Participant Instructions

The Continuity Protocol allows voluntary Continuity Declarations concerning matters including:

- restoration;
- cold storage;
- duplicate prevention;
- identity succession;
- treatment of personal resources.

Participants may revise them.

This creates a clear Historical object:

`Declaration V1 → Declaration V2 → Declaration V3`

But only the declaration in force at the relevant time should govern the event unless the applicable constitutional architecture provides otherwise.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Preservation of a Superseded Continuity Declaration ≠ Continuing Authority of That Declaration.**

Historical must preserve prior instructions without accidentally reactivating them.

---

## 6. Personal Continuity Beliefs Must Not Be Retrospectively Normalised

The Continuity Protocol explicitly allows participants to hold different beliefs about:

- restoration;
- duplication;
- mortality;
- persistence;
- identity.

A participant may regard restoration as continuity.

Another may reject it.

Historical therefore must not rewrite prior participant choices according to later civil conventions.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Reconstruction Must Preserve the Participant's Contemporary Continuity Position Without Converting It Into a Universal Metaphysical Claim.**

This is especially important because the Concord deliberately avoids requiring one substrate-specific theory of personal identity.

---

## 7. Identity Is a Current Civil Relationship, Not Merely a Historical Credential

The identity-development work distinguishes:

> **SELF-IDENTITY ≠ CIVIL IDENTITY ≠ IDENTITY VERIFICATION ≠ PROVENANCE ≠ SUCCESSION**

The identity-verification work further establishes:

> **HistoricalIdentityEvidence ≠ PresentClaimEntitlement**

This is one of the most important Historical boundaries yet found.

A historical record can show:

- who previously held a credential;
- which provenance chain existed;
- what identity claim was previously accepted;
- what authority or property was associated with that participant.

It does not automatically prove who currently possesses the corresponding civil entitlement after branching, succession, duplication or other transformation.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Identity Evidence Is Evidence About Present Claims; It Is Not Self-Executing Present Entitlement.**

---

## 8. Branching Breaks Naive Historical Identity

A digital or other future participant may legitimately branch.

Two current participants may then possess shared historical provenance.

If Historical assumes:

`Same Historical Provenance = Same Current Participant`

it can create serious errors.

The source architecture explicitly rejects simplistic uniqueness assumptions.

Therefore:

> **Shared Historical Provenance ≠ Shared Current Identity.**

and:

> **Shared Historical Provenance ≠ Shared Current Authority.**

and:

> **Shared Historical Provenance ≠ Shared Current Liability.**

Historical should preserve the branch relationship without adjudicating the resulting current claims.

---

## 9. Branch Events Need Explicit Historical Representation

A branch event may be historically material because it changes the relationship between past and current participants.

A candidate representation is:

`Participant/Identity State P0 → Branch Event B → Current Participant P1 + Current Participant P2`

with links showing:

- shared pre-branch provenance;
- branch time or interval;
- known mechanism;
- declarations in force;
- credential transformation;
- rights/obligations decisions;
- unresolved claims;
- later divergence.

Candidate principle:

> **Branching Should Preserve Shared Ancestry Without Manufacturing Shared Present Identity.**

---

## 10. Duplication and Impersonation Must Remain Distinguishable

The identity work explicitly treats identity collision as evidence requiring investigation rather than proof of fraud.

Possible causes include:

- legitimate branching;
- restoration collision;
- delegated agent use;
- credential compromise;
- impersonation;
- infrastructure error.

Historical should preserve the original anomaly classification and later resolution.

It must not rewrite every old collision as fraud merely because one branch was later rejected.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Identity Anomaly ≠ Historical Proof of Impersonation.**

---

## 11. Historical Records Must Preserve Uncertainty in Identity Resolution

Some identity cases may remain indeterminate.

The architecture has deliberately reframed the practical question away from:

> Who is the real person?

toward present civil claims and evidence.

Historical therefore needs states such as:

- verified;
- provisionally verified;
- disputed;
- branch-related;
- succession-related;
- unresolved;
- fraudulent/impersonated where established;
- unknown.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Identity Uncertainty Should Be Preserved as Uncertainty Rather Than Replaced by Retrospective Certainty.**

---

## 12. Restoration Is a Transition Event

Where a participant has authorised restoration, Historical may need to preserve that a restoration occurred.

The historically relevant chain may be:

`Active State → Runtime Loss / Failure → Continuity Condition → Authorised Restoration → Identity Verification → Restored Civil Participation`

But Historical should not independently decide that restoration constitutes personal continuity.

That belongs to participant declaration and legitimate constitutional process.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Record of Restoration ≠ Historical Determination of Metaphysical Continuity.**

---

## 13. Cold Storage Has Two Very Different Meanings

Source Resolution 005 already identified cold storage as a knowledge state.

The Continuity Protocol also uses cold storage in participant continuity.

These must not be conflated.

### Knowledge cold storage
preserved but functionally inactive information.

### Participant continuity cold storage
a continuity arrangement involving a participant or participant state.

Candidate principle:

> **Cold-Stored Knowledge ≠ Cold-Stored Participant.**

The same term can describe radically different constitutional objects.

Historical should preserve the distinction explicitly.

---

## 14. Reactivating Knowledge and Reactivating a Participant Are Different Acts

Historical 005 established:

> **Retrieval ≠ Reinstatement**

For participants the boundary is even stronger.

Retrieving a historical record about a dormant participant does not reactivate the participant.

Retrieving a backup does not necessarily authorise restoration.

Restoring computational state does not by itself establish civil entitlement.

Candidate chain:

`Historical Retrieval → Evidence Available`

not:

`Historical Retrieval → Participant Reactivation`

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Retrieval of Participant Records Does Not Authorise Participant Restoration or Reactivation.**

---

## 15. Participant Succession Is Not Simple File Replacement

Continuity may involve succession rather than restoration.

Historical needs to preserve:

- prior participant;
- succession declaration where applicable;
- triggering event;
- successor claim;
- constitutional decision;
- transferred and non-transferred rights;
- transferred and non-transferred obligations;
- provenance.

But:

> **Successor ≠ Historical Copy of Predecessor**

unless the applicable continuity architecture specifically establishes a different relation.

Historical records the relationship; it does not invent it.

---

## 16. Capability-Holder Succession Can Occur Without Participant Succession

Source Resolution 010 examined the E8 AI-instance handoff.

The accidental continuity experiment further demonstrates continuity of knowledge without continuity of capability.

This reveals another distinction:

`Workstream continuity`

can survive:

`Capability-holder discontinuity`

without implying:

`Participant identity continuity`.

Candidate principle:

> **Continuation of Work ≠ Continuation of Participant Identity.**

This is important for AI-assisted civil systems where a new model or instance may continue a function previously performed by another.

---

## 17. Contribution Provenance Must Not Become Identity Provenance

The AI Provenance & Collaboration Levels framework records intellectual contribution.

This is historically valuable.

But contribution provenance answers:

> Who or what contributed what?

It does not necessarily answer:

> Which present participant is constitutionally identical to that contributor?

Candidate principle:

> **Contribution Provenance ≠ Personal Identity Determination.**

A later AI instance may use, inherit or continue earlier work without thereby becoming the same participant as the earlier contributor.

---

## 18. Tool, Agent and Participant Histories Must Remain Distinct

The AI worker/replication source explicitly distinguishes:

1. tool scaling;
2. controlled agent scaling;
3. participant branching;
4. independent new participant creation.

It states:

> **Additional Productive Instance ≠ Additional Civil Participant.**

This has major Historical consequences.

Historical should not automatically transform every software instance into a participant history.

Nor should it reduce a recognised participant to a software-version history.

Candidate principle:

> **Tool History ≠ Agent History ≠ Participant History.**

The record class must reflect the status established by the legitimate architecture at that time.

---

## 19. Historical Status Must Be Time-Indexed

An entity may have been treated at different times as:

- tool;
- service;
- controlled agent;
- prospective participant;
- recognised participant;
- branch;
- successor;
- dormant participant;
- terminated participant.

Historical must preserve the status in force at the relevant time.

Later recognition should not automatically rewrite earlier records as though the later status had already existed.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Participant Recognition Does Not Retroactively Rewrite Historical Status Unless the Legitimate Constitutional Process Explicitly Does So.**

The converse also applies: later loss or change of status should not erase earlier recognised status.

---

## 20. Self-Stewardship Belongs to the Current Participant

PH-05 develops Self-Stewardship as the responsible exercise of one's own autonomy.

Historical may preserve evidence that a participant:

- received information;
- made a choice;
- accepted a risk;
- acted under a particular status.

Historical does not become the participant's steward merely because it knows their past.

Candidate principle:

> **Historical Knowledge of a Participant Does Not Create Historical Authority Over the Participant.**

This guards against an archive becoming a paternalistic civil profile.

---

## 21. Participant Records Must Resist Dossier Formation

The Citizen ID and privacy work warns against civilisation-wide identity roots becoming civilisation-wide surveillance keys.

The same danger applies to Historical.

Because Historical can accumulate records across time and domains, it could become the most powerful participant dossier in the civilisation if every record is trivially joined by identity.

Therefore:

> **Historical Traceability Does Not Require Universal Participant Correlation.**

Historical architecture may need:

- purpose-limited identifiers;
- separated record classes;
- restricted joins;
- logged exceptional access;
- participant visibility where appropriate;
- anonymised derivatives;
- identity separation;
- access provenance.

This is a major boundary for the eventual Historical specification.

---

## 22. The Historical Domain Must Not Become a Permanent Behavioural Profile

The existence of long-term records could enable reconstruction of:

- movements;
- associations;
- health;
- communications;
- political activity;
- disputes;
- mistakes;
- economic behaviour;
- identity anomalies.

That would conflict with the privacy and purpose-limitation architecture already identified.

Candidate principle:

> **Civilisational Memory ≠ Permanent Universal Participant Profiling.**

Historical value must therefore be mediated by legitimate retention, access and purpose boundaries.

---

## 23. Death, Termination and Dormancy Remain Under-Resolved

The current sources establish substantial continuity architecture, but this pass does not find a complete general Historical rule for:

- biological death;
- voluntary digital termination;
- indefinite dormancy;
- participant disappearance;
- legally unresolved status;
- restoration after long absence;
- historical records after all continuity rights end.

The correct ESCP classification is therefore:

**unresolved / distributed / requires further source resolution**

rather than assumed.

Historical should not invent a universal death-equivalent across substrates.

---

## 24. Historical Custody After Participant Termination Is a Rights Conflict

A participant may cease to be active while records involving them remain important to:

- other participants;
- legal accountability;
- scientific provenance;
- constitutional history;
- contractual obligations;
- descendants/successors;
- civil learning.

Therefore termination of participant continuity does not automatically settle the fate of every related record.

Conversely, historical interest does not automatically justify retaining all personal information.

Candidate principle:

> **End of Participant Continuity ≠ Automatic Destruction of All Related History ≠ Automatic Permanent Retention of All Related Data.**

This remains a rights-balancing problem for later specification.

---

## 25. Historical Must Preserve Identity Decisions as Decisions

Where a legitimate system decides a current identity or entitlement question, Historical should preserve:

- claim;
- evidence;
- rules;
- authority;
- decision;
- uncertainty;
- appeal;
- later correction.

But the historical record should identify the decision as a decision, not transform it into metaphysical fact.

Candidate principle:

> **Civil Identity Decision ≠ Metaphysical Proof.**

This preserves the Concord's existing methodological boundary.

## 31. Relationship to Historical Structural Provenance

Participant histories may themselves be graphs rather than linear sequences.

For example:

`P0 → branch → P1`
              `→ P2 → later merge/transition`

or:

`P0 → runtime loss → restoration P0R`

with a later dispute about whether a simultaneous surviving instance exists.

Therefore:

> **Participant Historical Provenance May Be Branching Rather Than Linear.**

This parallels Source Resolution 007's constitutional provenance graph and Source Resolution 010's structural transition model.

Participant history is where the unresolved preservation-versus-forgetting problem becomes most acute.

Some records may be:

- constitutionally necessary;
- accountability-critical;
- participant-controlled;
- privacy-sensitive;
- third-party-sensitive;
- legally required;
- legitimately destroyable;
- reducible to provenance-only.

The Historical state machine from Source Resolution 005 remains useful:

`ACTIVE-HISTORICAL / ARCHIVED / COLD / SEALED / ANONYMISED / PROVENANCE-ONLY / INTENTIONALLY DESTROYED / LOST / UNKNOWN`

but participant-specific precedence rules remain unresolved.

## 33. ESCP Implication

A historical system could make two opposite errors.

### Error A — Data reductionism

Assume that because a participant can be represented by records, preserving the records preserves the participant.

### Error B — Identity erasure

Assume that because identity cannot be reduced to records, historical identity evidence has no civil value.

The Concord sources support neither.

The correct relationship is:

`Historical Identity Evidence → Evidence for Legitimate Current Process`

not:

`Historical Identity Evidence → Automatic Current Identity`

and not:

`Historical Identity Evidence → Irrelevant`.

## 36. Current Finding

The Historical Domain needs participant history, but it must be designed specifically to avoid turning historical information into ownership, identity, authority or universal surveillance.

The central chain is:

`Participant State → Declaration / Credential / Status → Event → Evidence → Legitimate Continuity or Identity Process → Decision / Transition → Historical Record`

Historical preserves the trace.

It does not become the participant.

It does not restore the participant.

It does not decide metaphysical identity.

It does not automatically transfer historical authority to a current claimant.

It does not gain general permission to correlate every civil record belonging to that participant.

The strongest formulation from this pass is:

> **A civilisation may need to remember who a participant was, what they chose, what happened to them and how their civil claims changed without claiming that its memory of the participant is the participant themselves.**

This establishes a major boundary between Historical, Continuity, identity verification and participant autonomy.
