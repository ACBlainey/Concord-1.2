## 1. Core finding

The existing README resolves the architectural question directly:

> **The Concord is multisubstrate by design; Folder 05 investigates where differences between participants may require that design to become more precise.**

Folder 05 is explicitly an **Active Research and Development Sandbox**.

Therefore **Participation and Intelligence is not a top-level operational civil domain**.

Its primary V1.3 destination is the **Research domain**, as a specialised programme concerned with participant/substrate differences and their implications across all civil domains.

> **Researching Participation != Owning Participation**
>
> **Researching Intelligence Differences != Governing Intelligent Participants**

Multisubstrate participation applies across:

**SUBSTRATE-NEUTRAL PRINCIPLE**
-> **OBSERVED/PLAUSIBLE PARTICIPANT DIFFERENCE**

## 8. Naming implication

This folder provides a strong example of why V1.3 needs a Domain Naming and Expectation Audit.

The name **Participation and Intelligence** sounds like an operational domain that might own participation or intelligent participants.

The actual material says the opposite: it is a research programme investigating differences that must be reflected throughout the civilisation.

Therefore:

> **Repository Name -> Expectation -> Possible False Ownership**

The migration should instead expose the real function:

> **Research / Participation and Intelligence**

This supports the wider rule:

> **Define the function first. Derive the boundary second. Name the domain last.**

## 9. Finding

V1.3 does not require a Participation and Intelligence civil domain.

It requires a civilisation-wide commitment to multisubstrate participation plus a specialised Research programme capable of discovering where substrate-neutral abstractions are insufficient.

That is a stronger architecture because participant difference remains everybody's responsibility where relevant rather than becoming the responsibility of one specialist domain.
