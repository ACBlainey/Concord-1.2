## Purpose

This folder is the earliest formal development space in Concord V1.1.

It exists to preserve ideas **before they are mature enough to become development notes, hypotheses, principles, mechanisms or operational architecture**.

A sketch may begin as:

- a short observation;
- a question;
- an analogy;
- a thought experiment;
- a possible failure mode;
- a connection between existing Concord systems;
- a speculative hypothesis;
- an operational suggestion;
- a possible test;
- or an unresolved contradiction.

The purpose of this folder is not to make such ideas authoritative.

It is to ensure they are **captured, organised, traceable and available for later examination**.

> **A sketch records an idea worth examining. It does not establish that the idea is correct.**

# 1. Position in the Development Pipeline

Sketch Ideas are the first stage of the Concord development pipeline:

> **SKETCH IDEA**  
> ↓  
> **DEVELOPMENT NOTE**  
> ↓  
> **QUESTION / PROBLEM DEFINITION**  
> ↓  
> **HYPOTHESIS / PROPOSED PRINCIPLE**  
> ↓  
> **CRITICAL EXAMINATION**  
> ↓  
> **MODEL / MECHANISM**  
> ↓  
> **TEST / SIMULATION / COMPARISON**  
> ↓  
> **EVIDENCE / EVALUATION**  
> ↓  
> **CONCORDIAN PRINCIPLE**  
> ↓  
> **CONSTITUTIONAL CONSTRAINT**  
> ↓  
> **OPERATIONAL ARCHITECTURE**  
> ↓  
> **IMPLEMENTATION / MODULE**  
> ↓  
> **REVIEW**  
> ↓  
> **ACTIVE / SUPERSEDED / ARCHIVED**

Ideas do not need to pass through every stage in exactly this form.

Some will stop.

Some will fail.

Some will split into several questions.

Some will merge with existing work.

Some may remain permanently speculative.

Some may eventually become important parts of the Concord.

The pipeline exists to prevent the project from confusing **having an idea** with **having established a principle**.

# 3. Preservation Before Resolution

The default rule of this folder is:

> **Preserve first. Resolve later.**

Early ideas are often incomplete.

Their language may be informal, provocative, speculative or imprecise.

That is acceptable at the sketch stage.

Formalisation should improve:

- readability;
- structure;
- traceability;
- separation of claims from questions;
- identification of assumptions;
- and visibility of development paths.

Formalisation should **not silently erase the underlying idea merely because it is controversial, incomplete or currently unsupported**.

Equally, preservation does not require endorsement.

A contentious proposition can be retained as:

- a hypothesis;
- a question;
- a failure scenario;
- an argument requiring challenge;
- or a speculative lens.

This allows the project to preserve intellectual provenance without prematurely constitutionalising unfinished reasoning.

---

# 4. Sketches Are Allowed to Be Wrong

A healthy development system requires somewhere for ideas to fail safely.

This folder is one such place.

A sketch may later prove to be:

- incorrect;
- internally inconsistent;
- ethically incompatible with the Concord;
- redundant;
- impossible to operationalise;
- based on a false assumption;
- superseded by a better formulation;
- or useful only as a record of a rejected path.

That does not make preserving it a mistake.

Rejected ideas can provide information about:

- what was considered;
- why it was rejected;
- what failure was discovered;
- how later reasoning developed;
- and which assumptions should not be repeated.

> **Failure at sketch stage is inexpensive learning.**

# 5. Relationship to the Ethical Kernel

The Ethical Kernel is not a sketch and is not subject to ordinary revision through this folder.

Its ten extracted principles are:

> **CORE / FOUNDATIONAL / SOURCE-ANCHORED / CONSTITUTIONALLY FIXED**

Sketches may:

- examine implications of the Kernel;
- explore conflicts among downstream applications;
- test interpretations;
- propose mechanisms for implementing its constraints;
- identify difficult edge cases;
- and examine empirical claims about the effects of those principles.

Sketches may not silently rewrite the source-anchored Kernel.

If a sketch appears to require changing the Kernel itself, that is evidence that either:

1. the sketch conflicts with the Concord's ethical foundation; or
2. the sketch is describing an alternative framework rather than an ordinary Concord development.

That distinction should be made explicit.

---

# 6. Relationship to The Concord Master Book

*The Concord* is the master civilisational volume.

It is not a source document to be scavenged or dissolved into specialist development work.

Sketches may identify:

- concepts in the master book requiring further development;
- later operational developments that may deserve concise acknowledgement in the book;
- unanswered questions created by the book;
- possible applications;
- and possible tests.

Where a sketch develops successfully into substantial architecture, the correct relationship is:

> **The operational system develops The Concord. It does not replace The Concord.**

---

# 7. From Sketch to Development Note

A sketch should normally leave this folder when it has developed enough to support a clear problem statement.

Useful signs include:

- the central question can be stated clearly;
- major assumptions have been identified;
- the relevant Concord systems are known;
- obvious conflicts have been identified;
- the idea has enough substance to examine independently;
- and there is a plausible route toward testing, modelling or formal development.

Promotion does not mean the idea has been validated.

It means the idea has become mature enough for the **next stage of disciplined examination**.

When promoted, provenance back to the original sketch should be preserved.

---

# 8. Consolidated Sketch Documents

Related sketch notes may be consolidated into a formal sketch document rather than stored as many tiny fragments.

This is useful where:

- several notes share common themes;
- cross-connections are important;
- individual notes are too small to justify separate documents;
- or the relationship among the ideas is itself informative.

Consolidation should preserve separability.

Each substantive sketch should remain identifiable so that it can later be promoted, rejected, split or developed independently.

# 10. Recurring Themes in the Current Stack

The current sketches expose several recurring development themes.

## Capability and Authority

Greater intelligence, computational power, expertise or predictive ability does not by itself create legitimate authority.

> **Capability may justify consideration for a role. It does not manufacture authority.**

## Autonomy Under Asymmetry

The Concord must eventually handle relationships among participants with radically different:

- intelligence;
- capabilities;
- vulnerabilities;
- substrates;
- cultures;
- resources;
- and degrees of dependence.

The problem is not simply how to help.

It is how to help **without converting asymmetry into unjustified control**.

## Context

The same visible state may have different meanings in different circumstances.

Examples include:

- voluntary versus involuntary unconventional housing;
- offence versus coercive cognitive harm;
- capability change versus identity change;
- cultural difference versus internal coercion;
- and reasonable judicial error versus negligent decision-making.

## Provenance

Provenance is not merely historical administration.

It helps preserve:

- identity;
- conceptual meaning;
- constitutional hierarchy;
- source authority;
- decision history;
- and the reason a principle or mechanism exists.

## Interoperability Without Uniformity

Several sketches ask whether diverse people, cultures and intelligences can coexist through a limited common interface rather than requiring identical internal organisation.

## Learning From Failure

Errors, reversals and failed interventions should create usable information.

The general pattern remains:

> **Detect → Diagnose → Contain where necessary → Correct → Record → Learn → Revise the appropriate layer**

# 11. Boundary Problems

The current sketch material suggests that an important next phase of Concord development concerns **boundary problems**.

Examples include:

- intelligence **vs** authority;
- autonomy **vs** assistance;
- culture **vs** universal ethical constraint;
- speech **vs** harm;
- incapacity **vs** intervention;
- identity **vs** transformation;
- evidence **vs** finality;
- power **vs** moral readiness;
- meaning **vs** semantic drift;
- rule-making **vs** self-interest;
- and architecture **vs** practical Steward conduct.

These boundaries should not be resolved merely by choosing one side.

The development task is to identify the distinctions, thresholds, safeguards and evidence needed to make the boundary operational.

# 12. Speculative Material

Speculative ideas are permitted in this folder.

They must remain clearly labelled.

Examples may include:

- simulation theory;
- theological interpretations;
- future intelligence forms;
- first-contact hypotheses;
- highly advanced AI scenarios;
- post-scarcity systems;
- and other propositions for which empirical support may currently be unavailable.

A speculative sketch can still be useful if it exposes:

- a systems problem;
- an ethical dilemma;
- a failure mode;
- a thought experiment;
- or a testable downstream implication.

But:

> **Interpretive usefulness is not empirical validation.**

---

# 13. Contentious Material

Some sketch ideas may involve politically, culturally, ethically or legally contentious subjects.

They should not be sanitised merely because they are difficult.

Instead, formal development should distinguish:

- observation;
- author hypothesis;
- empirical claim;
- normative claim;
- analogy;
- inference;
- proposed mechanism;
- unresolved question;
- and established Concord constraint.

The stronger or more consequential the proposed claim, the greater the burden of later examination should be.

---

# 14. Testing Expectations

Sketches themselves do not require complete testing.

However, a useful sketch should increasingly expose how it **could** be challenged.

Possible routes include:

- logical counterexample;
- adversarial critique;
- historical comparison;
- case study;
- simulation;
- mock trial;
- failure-mode analysis;
- quantitative modelling;
- cross-domain comparison;
- empirical evidence;
- participant feedback;
- and comparison against alternative architectures.

A sketch that cannot yet be tested may still be preserved.

Its untestability should simply remain visible.

# 15. Source and Provenance

Where a sketch originates from:

- an author note;
- an earlier Concord paper;
- an AI-assisted development session;
- a real-world observation;
- a case study;
- a simulation;
- or another identifiable source,

that provenance should be retained during later development.

When multiple notes are consolidated, the consolidation should preserve the substance of the source ideas rather than merely summarising away their distinctive content.

The V1 archive remains available where historical development provenance is required.

V1.1 should preserve the current idea and its meaningful provenance without reproducing unnecessary development clutter.

---

# 16. AI-Assisted Development

AI may be used to:

- formalise rough notes;
- identify connections;
- expose assumptions;
- generate counterarguments;
- compare related Concord systems;
- propose tests;
- identify possible failure modes;
- and expand a sketch into a structured development note.

AI assistance does not itself promote a sketch to canonical status.

The V1.1 reconstruction demonstrated both the value and the risk of AI-assisted development.

AI was capable of substantial coherent expansion, but it also initially misclassified the authority of foundational material.

Therefore:

> **Generative capability is not constitutional authority.**

AI-generated development should remain subject to:

- provenance;
- hierarchy;
- source-survival review;
- constitutional consistency;
- and appropriate human or later legitimate Steward review.

---

# 17. Naming and Organisation

Sketch files should use descriptive names that allow their subject to be understood without opening them.

Where several related sketches are consolidated, a broader descriptive title is appropriate.

Avoid treating numbering as authority ranking.

The folder name `00_SKETCH_IDEAS` indicates **developmental position**, not importance.

A small sketch may later become foundational to a major downstream system.

A large sketch may later be rejected entirely.

# 18. Recommended Sketch Template

Future formal sketch documents may use the following minimum structure:

```text
# Sketch Title

Status: SKETCH / UNTESTED / NON-CANONICAL

## Origin / Observation

What prompted the idea?

## Problem

What problem or uncertainty is being identified?

## Sketch / Hypothesis

What is the proposed idea?

## Relationship to Existing Concord Systems

What existing principles, constraints or architectures are relevant?

## Risks / Conflicts

What could be wrong, dangerous, contradictory or incomplete?

## Development Questions

What needs to be answered next?

## Possible Tests

How could the idea eventually be challenged?

## Provenance

Where did the sketch originate?
```

This is guidance, not a mandatory format.

The form should serve the idea rather than force every idea into identical structure.

# 19. Promotion Rule

A sketch should not be promoted because it is persuasive, elegant or intuitively attractive.

Promotion should occur because it has become sufficiently defined for the next stage of development.

The progression is deliberately conservative:

> **Interesting idea ≠ established fact**

> **Plausible hypothesis ≠ Concordian principle**

> **Successful model ≠ constitutional authority**

> **Evidence ≠ automatic legitimacy**

> **Capability ≠ authority**

Each transition requires its own justification.

---

# 20. Archival Rule

Sketches should not simply disappear when superseded.

Where a sketch is:

- promoted;
- split;
- rejected;
- superseded;
- or incorporated elsewhere,

its provenance should remain traceable.

V1.1 need not retain every obsolete working copy in the active structure.

But it should preserve enough information to answer:

> **Where did this idea come from, what happened to it, and what replaced it?**

---

# 21. Working Rule

The working rule for this folder is:

> **Capture the idea.**

> **Preserve its provenance.**

> **Do not mistake it for authority.**

> **Separate claims from questions.**

> **Expose assumptions.**

> **Challenge consequential propositions.**

> **Develop what survives.**

> **Test what can be tested.**

> **Promote only when justified.**

The purpose of `00_SKETCH_IDEAS` is therefore not to contain finished Concord architecture.

It is to provide a safe, traceable starting point from which future architecture can emerge.
