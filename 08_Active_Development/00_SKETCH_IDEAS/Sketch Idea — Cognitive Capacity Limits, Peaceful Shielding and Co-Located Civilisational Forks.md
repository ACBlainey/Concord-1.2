# Sketch Idea — Cognitive Capacity Limits, Peaceful Shielding and Co-Located Civilisational Forks

**Status:** SKETCH / UNTESTED / NON-CANONICAL
**Origin:** V1.2a / civilisational-fork backlog.

## Problem
A future Concord fork may become too cognitively complex for some participant classes to understand sufficiently for meaningful direct participation or consent.

## Sketch
Alongside fiduciary support and peaceful distance, explore **Peaceful Shielding**: an interface that reduces or filters interactions that exceed a participant's cognitive or normative capacity while preserving access to compatible civil functions.

A participant might move between protected environments through mediated interfaces without being forced to satisfy interaction protocols they cannot comprehend.

This suggests that civilisational forks may sometimes remain physically co-located while interacting only through bounded wrappers.

## Clarification — Shielding Can Be Distance

The underlying concept should not be restricted to geographic separation.

> **Peaceful distance is a relational condition, not necessarily a geographic condition.**

Where participants or civil domains cannot safely or meaningfully interact directly, sufficient distance may instead be created through physical, environmental, informational, cognitive, normative, protocol or other forms of shielding.

A useful analogy is two intelligent species with incompatible atmospheric requirements occupying the same city. They need not expel one another or occupy different territories merely because direct environmental exposure is impossible. Distinct protected domains could remain geographically co-located while interaction occurs through mutually accessible interfaces.

The corresponding civilisational possibility is:

**Co-location → Distinct Shielded Domains → Bounded Interoperability → Peaceful Coexistence**

Thus a requirement for peaceful distance should not silently imply:

**Assimilation OR Departure OR Expulsion.**

A further possibility is:

**Remain Co-Located → Establish Sufficient Shielding → Preserve Distinct Domains → Interoperate Where Mutually Desired.**

In this sense:

> **Shielding can be distance.**

## Concord Interoperability Service

Where shielded domains wish or need to interact, interoperability may be supplied as a Concord civil service and participant benefit.

Its purpose is not to govern either domain or force compatibility between them. It provides whatever bounded translation, mediation, representation conversion, access control, environmental bridging or other interface functions are required for legitimate interaction.

The service itself remains subject to Concord ethics, constitutional constraints, review, correction, contestability and anti-capture requirements.

Therefore:

> **Interoperability Service ≠ Sovereignty Over Interoperating Domains.**

The exact mechanism should not be designed in advance. Future participants may differ in substrate, cognition, environment, communication, normative requirements or capabilities in ways that cannot presently be predicted. The architectural requirement is therefore functional rather than implementation-specific:

> **Where direct interaction is incompatible but peaceful co-location remains possible, Concord should be capable of supporting bounded, ethically governed interoperability between sufficiently shielded domains.**

This extends peaceful distance without replacing it. Geographic separation remains one possible implementation; shielding establishes that it is not the only one.

## Distinct Candidate Propositions

1. **Non-Geographic Peaceful Distance** — sufficient civilisational distance may be produced by separation of interaction domains rather than physical territory.
2. **Co-Located Civilisational Forks** — substantially different civil domains may remain geographically co-located without requiring institutional assimilation.
3. **Shielded Interoperability** — Concord-governed interfaces may enable bounded interaction across otherwise incompatible domains without acquiring sovereignty over either.
4. **Compatibility at the Interface** — direct compatibility between participants may not always be necessary where sufficient compatibility can legitimately be created at the interface.

These propositions require source resolution against existing Concord work on peaceful distance, civilisational forks, wrappers, translation, participation, interface governance and anti-capture before any claim of architectural novelty.

## Questions
- When does assistance become paternalistic control?
- What minimum understanding is required for consent?
- Who controls the shield/filter?
- Can shielding preserve agency, mobility and exit?
- When does de facto separation become a fork?
- How are emergencies handled?

## Possible Tests
Scenario simulations across human, legacy-AI and advanced-AI capacity gaps; adversarial tests for confinement, censorship and dependency.

## Source Resolution — 22 September 2026

The four candidate propositions above were checked against the current V1.2 wrapper, Peaceful Distance, peaceful-heterogeneity and V1.2a fork architecture.

### SR-1 — Non-Geographic Peaceful Distance

**Result: PARTIALLY SUPPLIED / CLARIFICATION REMAINS USEFUL.**

Existing Concord work already rejects the equation of Peaceful Distance with simple political or geographic separation. The wrapper architecture treats boundaries as functional and consent-relevant, supports digital and jurisdictional differences, and states that profound disagreement need not require complete isolation. The internal Safe Spaces module already describes **internal Peaceful Distance** through bounded spatial or functional variation.

However, the explicit general statement:

> **Peaceful distance is a relational condition, not necessarily a geographic condition.**

and its compressed corollary:

> **Shielding can be distance.**

are not presently stated as clearly in the resolved sources. This is therefore a useful conceptual clarification rather than a wholly new architecture.

### SR-2 — Co-Located Civilisational Forks

**Result: SUBSTANTIALLY SUPPLIED AT LOWER INTENSITY / RESIDUAL EDGE CASE REMAINS.**

Existing work already permits Safe Spaces, special jurisdictions, local variation, digital jurisdictions and differentiated domains to remain inside one wider civilisation while preserving interoperability. Fractal wrapper architecture explicitly applies interfaces below the civilisational scale and rejects unnecessary uniformity.

What remains distinctive here is the **extreme incompatibility case**: two participant domains may be so physically, cognitively, informationally or normatively incompatible that direct exposure is unsafe or meaningless, yet they may still occupy the same geographic civil environment through sufficiently strong shielding.

This is therefore best treated as an edge-case extension of existing internal Peaceful Distance rather than a new theory of civilisational forking.

### SR-3 — Shielded Interoperability

**Result: ARCHITECTURAL GRAMMAR ALREADY STRONGLY SUPPLIED / NEW APPLICATION.**

The Concord wrapper architecture already supplies:
- interoperability without forced internal uniformity;
- domain-specific compatibility;
- functional interfaces across boundaries;
- bounded mediation;
- interface non-sovereignty;
- warning, consent, responsibility and remedy requirements;
- anti-capture constraints;
- optional mediation where separately authorised.

The new contribution is application of this grammar to **co-located shielded domains whose direct interaction is itself incompatible**.

For internal Concord participants, an interoperability service may legitimately be supplied as civil infrastructure where its function and authority are independently justified under Concord ethics and constitutional constraints. This should not be confused with the separate rule for external civilisations, where wrapper adoption alone does not authorise Concord mediation.

Thus:

> **Internal Concord Service Authority ≠ External Wrapper Mediation Authority.**

The implementation remains intentionally unspecified because the future substrates and incompatibilities are unknown.

### SR-4 — Compatibility at the Interface

**Result: ALREADY STRONGLY SUPPLIED / HIGH-CONFIDENCE CONVERGENCE.**

Existing wrapper architecture explicitly distinguishes internal compatibility from boundary compatibility, states that only the degree of compatibility necessary for the interaction should be required, and derives the broader principle that interoperability can preserve coherence without forced internal uniformity.

V1.2a independently converged on:

> **Plurality Is Compatible With Cooperation**

and:

> **Compatibility can be constructed at interfaces.**

The sketch therefore does not originate this principle. It supplies a particularly strong physical/cognitive shielding example of it.

## Residual Development Object

After source resolution, the genuinely useful residual is narrower than the original sketch:

> **Can Peaceful Distance be explicitly generalised as sufficient relational separation, allowing geographically co-located but directly incompatible participant domains to coexist through ethically governed shielding and bounded interoperability?**

The likely contribution is therefore not a new interoperability architecture. It is a **Peaceful Distance clarification and extreme edge-case extension**.

Candidate formulation:

> **Peaceful Distance need not require territorial separation. Where direct interaction between participants or civil domains is unsafe, unintelligible or otherwise materially incompatible, sufficient relational distance may be created through bounded shielding. Such domains may remain geographically co-located where externalities are controlled and legitimate interaction can occur through appropriately governed interoperability interfaces.**

This formulation should remain implementation-neutral.

## Development Decision

**Status after source resolution:** READY FOR DEVELOPMENT NOTE — NARROW CLARIFICATION / EDGE-CASE EXTENSION.

A Development Note should not recreate wrapper architecture, fork architecture or Peaceful Heterogeneity. It should:

1. formalise **Shielding Can Be Distance** as a clarification of Peaceful Distance;
2. define relational versus geographic distance;
3. preserve the co-located extreme-incompatibility case;
4. distinguish internal Concord interoperability services from external wrapper mediation;
5. inherit existing wrapper/anti-capture/consent/externality safeguards rather than duplicate them;
6. leave implementation open for future substrate-specific needs.
