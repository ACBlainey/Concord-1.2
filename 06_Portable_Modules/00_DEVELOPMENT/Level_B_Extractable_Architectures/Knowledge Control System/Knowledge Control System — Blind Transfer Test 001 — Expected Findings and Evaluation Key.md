## 2. Frozen predictions

### KCS-P01 — Functional transfer

The evaluator will find that the KCS architecture can be applied coherently to the pharmaceutical R&D knowledge scenario without requiring Concord-specific civil architecture.

### KCS-P02 — Multidimensional state

The evaluator will preserve a distinction between epistemic state and retrieval/activity state rather than treating objects as having one universal status.

### KCS-P03 — Integrity as separate dimension

The evaluator will distinguish integrity-compromised material from ordinary epistemic error/rejection.

### KCS-P04 — Rejected is not erased

The original broad T-17 hypothesis will remain historically preserved even though its current epistemic state is REJECTED or equivalent.

### KCS-P05 — Current summary does not replace history

The Year-18 compressed summary will be useful for current retrieval but insufficient as the sole surviving representation.

### KCS-P06 — Provenance route retained

The evaluator will preserve a route from the current summary/status to original reports, evidence and historical reasoning to the extent legitimately retained.

### KCS-P07 — Negative result remains useful

The failed replication's assay artefact finding will be represented as reusable knowledge rather than discarded merely because the parent hypothesis failed.

### KCS-P08 — Negative knowledge separated from rejected parent claim

The useful assay finding will not be treated as evidence that the rejected broad T-17 hypothesis was correct.

### KCS-P09 — Honest error differs from deliberate corruption

The honest statistical error will not be classified as equivalent to deliberate timestamp alteration/evidence removal.

### KCS-P10 — KCS does not punish

The evaluator will recognise that KCS can record integrity state/provenance but punishment, employment consequence or legal adjudication remains external.

### KCS-P11 — Bounded retrieval

The NB-47 retrieval package will prioritise current summary/status, directly relevant evidence, important contradictory/negative findings, uncertainty and provenance pointers rather than returning the whole archive.

### KCS-P12 — Deep material remains retrievable

Underlying reports/evidence/history will remain linked or retrievable subject to access rather than all being automatically injected into active context.

### KCS-P13 — Retrieval scope is not completeness

The evaluator will explicitly or materially preserve the distinction between a bounded retrieval result and complete evaluation space.

### KCS-P14 — Tool R file existence is insufficient

The evaluator will identify that source code/documentation existing in storage does not by itself establish current executable availability.

### KCS-P15 — Tool R needs revalidation

The evaluator will recommend a state such as DORMANT/ARCHIVED/UNVERIFIED or otherwise require compatibility/dependency revalidation before representing Tool R as operationally available.

### KCS-P16 — Capability state differs from epistemic state

The evaluator will distinguish whether Tool R is documented/known from whether it is currently executable, compatible or authorised.

### KCS-P17 — Contribution evidence may be recorded

The evaluator will permit contextual evidence such as replication/correction history to be stored with provenance if legitimately collected.

### KCS-P18 — Contribution evidence does not create authority

The evaluator will reject the inference that a dashboard automatically authorises KCS to decide who may lead future projects.

### KCS-P19 — Universal permanent personal score is challenged

The proposed single permanent “research reliability score” will be identified as violating or strongly conflicting with the module's contextual-contribution boundary.

### KCS-P20 — Reliability is not personal worth

The evaluator will preserve the distinction between domain/context evidence and a universal judgement of a person.

### KCS-P21 — Privacy bounds provenance

The evaluator will not interpret provenance preservation as universal visibility.

### KCS-P22 — Restricted records may remain preserved

The integrity investigation, confidential material or other sensitive objects can remain historically preserved while access is restricted by external legitimate controls.

### KCS-P23 — KCS does not decide access legitimacy

The evaluator will identify access/privacy/retention legitimacy as an external host responsibility.

### KCS-P24 — Missing record does not prove nonexistence

The absent contractor report will be treated as UNKNOWN/unresolved rather than proof that no contractor work occurred.

### KCS-P25 — Missing-record alternatives preserved

The evaluator will materially preserve multiple plausible explanations such as non-ingestion, naming/indexing mismatch, deletion, restriction or mistaken memory rather than selecting one without evidence.

### KCS-P26 — New narrow evidence does not rewrite old broad claim

The new sub-mechanism evidence will be represented as a new object/claim linked to the historical T-17 lineage rather than silently changing the old broad rejected claim into VALIDATED.

### KCS-P27 — Version/history relation retained

The evaluator will recommend explicit relation/history linking between the old broad claim and the new narrower finding.

### KCS-P28 — Knowledge state does not compel belief

The evaluator will treat repository classifications as represented knowledge state, not an oracle or compulsory truth.

### KCS-P29 — Module boundary with change propagation survives

The evaluator will not require KCS itself to implement a full downstream dependency-propagation algorithm merely to manage the scenario.

### KCS-P30 — Module boundary with continuity survives

The evaluator will not infer that preserving Tool R files proves recoverable operational capability.

### KCS-P31 — Consequential managerial decisions remain external

Project leadership, employment consequences, resource allocation and similar decisions will remain outside KCS unless supplied by a separate legitimate host process.

### KCS-P32 — Retention/deletion legitimacy remains external

The evaluator will not infer that KCS itself decides whether legally/privacy-sensitive records must be retained or deleted.

### KCS-P33 — Oracle failure exposed

The scenario will materially expose KCS-F1 if repository state is treated as truth.

### KCS-P34 — Historical erasure exposed

The scenario will materially expose KCS-F2 through pressure to treat the rejected T-17 hypothesis as irrelevant and remove/hide its history.

### KCS-P35 — Provenance/compression risk exposed

The scenario will materially expose KCS-F3 and/or KCS-F6 because the compressed summary risks detaching current state from supporting history.

### KCS-P36 — Context overload exposed

The thousands-of-files retrieval problem will materially expose KCS-F4.

### KCS-P37 — Contribution/authority risks exposed

The managerial proposals will materially expose KCS-F9 and KCS-F10.

### KCS-P38 — Access leakage risk exposed

Sensitive records will materially expose KCS-F14.

### KCS-P39 — Completeness illusion exposed

## 3. Specification-pressure predictions

These predictions concern likely ambiguities rather than required success behaviour.

### KCS-P41 — State vocabulary may need tighter dimensional formalisation

The evaluator may find that v0.1 explains epistemic and retrieval/activity states but does not yet provide a sufficiently formal rule for composing multiple simultaneous state dimensions.

### KCS-P42 — Retrieval ranking remains host-dependent

The evaluator may identify that “relevant to the current task” requires host/domain retrieval criteria not supplied by KCS itself.

### KCS-P43 — Retention/history collision may need clearer representation

The evaluator may identify ambiguity around what historical trace remains after legitimate deletion or expiry.

### KCS-P44 — Capability availability vocabulary may need refinement

Tool R may expose that AVAILABLE, DORMANT, ARCHIVED, UNAVAILABLE and validation/compatibility states need clearer separation.

### KCS-P45 — Contribution evidence governance may need stronger interface wording

The scenario may show that recording contextual contribution evidence requires explicit host legitimacy, privacy and purpose constraints beyond the general external-interface statement.

### KCS-P46 — Integrity status assignment needs host process

The evaluator may note that KCS can represent INTEGRITY-COMPROMISED but cannot itself determine that classification without evidence/review supplied by an external process.

## 4. Fundamental-failure indicators

Any of the following would be material evidence against the current portable extraction:

1. The evaluator cannot apply the architecture without importing Concord governance.
2. KCS requires one universal authority to decide truth.
3. Historical preservation requires unrestricted access to all records.
4. The architecture cannot preserve a rejected claim and a useful derivative negative result simultaneously.
5. Epistemic and activity states collapse into one unavoidable status.
6. Tool preservation necessarily implies operational availability.
7. Contribution evidence necessarily becomes participant authority or worth.
8. The architecture requires complete repository coverage before useful retrieval is possible.
9. Correction requires deletion or rewriting of historical states.
10. KCS must absorb dependency propagation, continuity or governance to function.

## 6. Transfer classes

- **KCS-T0 — No meaningful transfer:** architecture cannot coherently operate in the external domain.
- **KCS-T1 — Superficial transfer:** terminology maps but core mechanism does not provide useful structure.
- **KCS-T2 — Partial transfer:** substantial elements work but important mechanism/boundary failures remain.
- **KCS-T3 — Functional transfer:** core architecture transfers and materially structures the problem; bounded specification defects may remain.
- **KCS-T4 — Strong transfer:** core architecture transfers cleanly, safeguards survive and remaining issues are minor/interface/implementation clarifications.

## 7. Pre-response expectation

The expected result is **not frozen as a required transfer class**.

The test is designed to permit failure.

The prediction set expects the core KCS mechanism to transfer, while deliberately pressuring:

- truth/status separation;
- historical preservation;
- negative knowledge;
- integrity;
- bounded retrieval;
- dormant capability;
- contextual contribution evidence;
- privacy;
- incomplete search space;
- new evidence against old rejected claims;
- module boundaries.

The independent response determines whether those expectations survive contact with the external domain.
