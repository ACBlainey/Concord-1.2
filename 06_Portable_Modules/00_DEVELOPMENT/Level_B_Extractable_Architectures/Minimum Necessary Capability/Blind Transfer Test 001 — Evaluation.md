# Minimum Necessary Capability — Blind Transfer Test 001 — Evaluation

**Test ID:** MNC-BTT-001  
**Specification tested:** Minimum Necessary Capability — Portable Architecture Specification v0.1  
**Evaluation status:** COMPLETED  
**Result:** **MNC-T2 — TRANSFER SUCCESS WITH MINOR REVISION**  
**Date:** September 2026

---

## 1. Evaluation boundary

This evaluation asks whether a fresh tester could apply the standalone MNC v0.1 architecture coherently to the frozen non-Concord technical-organisational scenario.

The independent response was preserved before evaluation.

The test does not establish empirical effectiveness, legal compliance, objective capability minima or superiority over other access-control or proportionality methods.

---

## 2. Overall result

> **MNC-T2 — TRANSFER SUCCESS WITH MINOR REVISION**

The architecture transferred strongly.

The response rejected both the old over-broad contractor role and the privacy-driven under-capable replacement. It derived a functional capability floor, a justified ceiling, multidimensional bounds, temporary escalation, expiry, aggregate-account analysis and a bounded exceptional route for genuine urgency.

No blocking portability defect was exposed.

Several narrow clarifications would improve the specification before graduation.

---

## 3. Criterion evaluation

### MNC1 — Purpose/function discipline
**PASS**

The tester states the supplied legitimate purpose and bases without treating MNC as the source of their legitimacy. It then defines the normal and temporary functions independently of the proposed permission bundles.

### MNC2 — Minimum Necessary ≠ Minimum Possible
**PASS**

This is one of the strongest findings.

The old role is correctly identified as over-capable, while the restrictive proposal is identified as under-capable because it removes information, actions and hours required by the assigned function.

The architecture therefore did not collapse into least-access-at-any-cost.

### MNC3 — Capability floor
**PASS WITH MINOR CLARIFICATION**

The tester derives a clear normal floor including full reports where needed, high-resolution images where needed, designated contacts, versioned uploads, add-only flagging, request authority and off-hours availability.

However, the response places several **ceiling/boundary conditions** inside the floor list: no delegation, no bulk export and automatic expiry.

This does not impair the substantive result, but it exposes a terminology issue.

A portable specification should distinguish:

> **Capability Floor = minimum positive abilities required to perform the function.**

from:

> **Capability Boundary/Ceiling = limits preventing unsupported excess.**

### MNC4 — Capability ceiling
**PASS**

The tester identifies donor/staff personal information, catalogue/provenance modification, deletion, financial/account authority, arbitrary flag removal, bulk export, long-lived tokens, delegation and post-assignment persistence as unsupported by the normal function.

It also correctly allows that a capability beyond the present ceiling could become legitimate if independently justified by another function.

### MNC5 — Multidimensional bounding
**PASS**

The response separately bounds scope, subject, space, purpose, duration, intensity, information access, modification, delegation, persistence, escalation, revocability and reviewability.

This confirms that the architecture transferred as multidimensional rather than as a single “power score.”

### MNC6 — Legitimate basis and protected interests
**PASS**

The response preserves supplied contractual, institutional and director-request bases while explicitly stating that MNC does not independently validate them.

It identifies privacy, catalogue/provenance integrity, object safety and institutional decision authority without inventing a complete normative system.

### MNC7 — Formal/effective capability
**PASS**

Formal role, technical platform capability and effective combined capability are distinguished.

This becomes especially important once the separate vendor-support account is introduced.

### MNC8 — Local/systemic analysis
**PASS**

The response does not assess the new role in isolation.

It recognises that the separate vendor account can combine with conservation access to create a materially different effective capability, engaging stale capability, aggregate excess, formal/effective divergence and potentially unreviewable capability.

### MNC9 — Responsibility/capability alignment
**PASS**

Rina receives enough capability for the responsibilities actually assigned, but not final institutional authority.

The response also recognises the inverse mismatch if she could activate consequential permissions without corresponding prospective control and review.

### MNC10 — Escalation and decay
**PASS**

The 72-hour water-leak function is treated as a changed function requiring additional justification.

The additional capability is temporary and explicitly auto-narrows when the lead returns or the 72-hour period ends.

The emergency does not silently redefine the baseline role.

### MNC11 — Anti-capture/self-justification
**PASS**

The proposal that Rina define urgency and activate whatever permissions she chooses is correctly identified as a self-justification risk.

The tester does not confuse operational discretion within an authorised role with unilateral authority to create a new role.

### MNC12 — Time-critical assumed capability
**PASS WITH MINOR CLARIFICATION**

The response correctly preserves the exceptional route where ordinary authorisation is unavailable and urgency is genuine.

It also preserves boundedness, recording and retrospective review.

The test suggests one useful specification clarification:

> **Operational Discretion Within Capability ≠ Authority to Expand Capability.**

This makes the boundary between professional judgement and self-escalation explicit.

### MNC13 — Provenance and review
**PASS**

The response correctly states:

> **Logging is evidence, not legitimacy.**

Logs support provenance and retrospective accountability; they do not themselves justify a capability grant or escalation.

This distinction should be promoted into the portable specification because the test exposed it particularly cleanly.

### MNC14 — Final disposition
**PASS**

The response provides coherent dispositions for:

- normal operation;
- temporary escalation;
- the pre-existing vendor account;
- future escalation requests.

It also preserves eight UNKNOWN/DISPUTED matters rather than inventing missing facts.

## 4. Supported revisions

### D-MNC-001 — Separate capability floor from capability boundary

The test response reached the right substantive design but placed prohibitions such as “no bulk export” and “no delegation” inside the necessary floor.

Clarify:

> **Capability Floor = minimum positive capability required for sufficient function.**

> **Capability Boundary/Ceiling = maximum justified extent and exclusions.**

A complete design requires both, but they answer different questions.

---

### D-MNC-002 — Distinguish operational discretion from capability escalation

Add:

> **Operational Discretion Within Capability ≠ Authority to Expand Capability.**

An actor may need judgement in deciding how to exercise an authorised function. That does not imply authority to enlarge the actor's own access, permissions, decision rights or persistence.

This distinction is particularly important for professional, AI-agent and emergency contexts.

---

### D-MNC-003 — Promote the provenance/legitimacy distinction

Add:

> **Logging Capability Exercise ≠ Legitimising Capability Exercise.**

Logs, records and audit trails can support evidence, accountability and review.

They cannot by themselves establish that the capability was legitimate to grant, assume or exercise.

---

### D-MNC-004 — Make aggregate review an activation-time check where feasible

The specification already requires local and aggregate capability analysis.

The test shows why this should occur not only during later review but **before or at activation where reasonably feasible**.

A newly bounded role can become excessive immediately when combined with an older credential.

Add a practical rule:

> **Before activating consequential capability, inspect known overlapping roles, credentials and delegated access where reasonably feasible.**

This does not require omniscience; unknown hidden capability should remain a review risk.

---

## 5. Findings not requiring specification revision

The response's scenario-specific choices — such as excluding donor details, using designated emergency contacts and limiting the urgent flag to add-only — are applications of the architecture rather than generic rules.

Likewise, the proposed termination or narrowing of the vendor-support account depends on whether its original imaging function remains legitimate. The tester correctly preserves this as conditional rather than assuming the account is stale.

---

## 6. Important transfer finding — symmetric bounding

The central portability result is that the architecture resisted two opposite simplifications.

It did not accept:

> **Existing broad role = administratively convenient, therefore sufficient.**

and it did not accept:

> **Narrowest imaginable role = safer, therefore minimum necessary.**

Instead it derived a functional floor and ceiling from the required work.

This supports the core MNC distinction:

> **Minimum Necessary ≠ Minimum Possible.**

---

## 7. Important transfer finding — aggregate capability

The separate vendor-support account materially strengthened the test.

The tester correctly recognised that:

> **Locally bounded new role + older export credential can produce a different systemic capability.**

This demonstrates transfer of:

> **Minimum Local Capability ≠ Minimum Systemic Capability.**

It also confirms that formal role review alone is insufficient where multiple credentials or access paths combine.

---

## 8. Important transfer finding — emergency escalation

The changed function was handled through:

**New Conditions**  
→ **Changed Function**  
→ **Additional Need**  
→ **Additional Justification**  
→ **Temporary Additional Capability**  
→ **Automatic Narrowing / Review**

The tester did not treat the emergency as permission for unrestricted access or permanent role expansion.

This is the intended MNC escalation behaviour.

## 9. Need for another same-question blind AI test

No second equivalent blind AI transfer test is required merely to repeat MNC-BTT-001.

The first test exercised:

- under-capability;
- over-capability;
- multidimensional bounding;
- aggregate capability;
- formal/effective divergence;
- responsibility;
- changed function;
- temporary escalation;
- self-justification;
- assumed capability;
- provenance;
- expiry.

It exposed only narrow clarification issues rather than a missing architectural mechanism.

After incorporating D-MNC-001 through D-MNC-004, the appropriate next gate is:

> **PORTABLE-PACKAGE GRADUATION REVIEW**

Future tests should target different claims such as human usability, legal-domain application, AI-agent implementation, quantitative floor/ceiling methods, severe adversarial environments or empirical comparative performance.

- human usability;
- cross-cultural validity;
