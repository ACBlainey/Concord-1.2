## 4. Source architecture recovered

The primary source contains several layers.

### 4.1 Generalisable continuity primitives

The strongest portable primitives are:

- minimise unnecessary loss rather than attempt to eliminate all loss;
- preserve accumulated capability across time;
- preserve without freezing change;
- distinguish continuity of function from immortality of a particular implementation;
- treat continuity recursively across nested system levels;
- preserve memory as usable infrastructure rather than passive storage;
- preserve provenance and revision history;
- preserve useful failure knowledge;
- maintain accessibility, maintainability and future usability;
- establish continuity arrangements before disruption where practical;
- support restoration/recovery after interruption;
- preserve responsibility/function through succession;
- support planned transition rather than purely reactive replacement;
- preserve enough enabling capability for recovery, not merely final-state information;
- keep continuity mechanisms themselves reviewable and adaptable.

These form the candidate portable architecture.

### 4.2 Concord-specific implementations

The following source elements should not automatically become universal portable requirements:

- the Concord Library as a named constitutional institution;
- Hall of Legacy;
- Constitutional Memorial;
- Concord citizenship/guest/evaluator structures;
- civilisational branches as a required organisational model;
- Concord-specific constitutional identity status;
- Concord-specific continuity declarations;
- the particular constitutional treatment of AI restoration, runtime loss and duplicate prevention;
- Concord-specific governance, authority and accountability machinery;
- Concord-specific attractor claims.

These remain valuable examples, source implementations or specialised applications.

### 4.3 Adjacent modules that must remain separate

**Legacy Ladder** addresses preservation of the prerequisite capability chain required to recover advanced knowledge/technology. It may become a separate portable module or specialised continuity companion.

**Knowledge Control System** addresses knowledge state, dependency and change propagation. It is already a separate Level B candidate.

**KCS Change Propagation** is likewise a separate candidate.

**Reality Trees** may support succession/fork exploration but is not part of the continuity core.

The portable Continuity Protocol may reference these functions abstractly without absorbing their full architectures.

## 5. Portable problem statement

The source supports the following domain-neutral problem:

> **How can a system preserve valuable function, knowledge, capability, provenance and recoverability across change, interruption, replacement or succession without turning preservation into stagnation?**

This is narrower and more portable than the full constitutional source.

## 6. Candidate compact principle

A defensible compact formulation is:

> **Preserve what remains valuable; adapt what must change; retain enough knowledge, provenance and enabling capability to recover legitimate function after disruption.**

A second source-grounded boundary is:

> **Continuity ≠ Stasis**

and:

> **Preservation of Information ≠ Preservation of Recoverable Capability**

The latter is especially supported by the Legacy Ladder and EKC-08 comparison.

## 7. Candidate continuity object

The module should require the user to specify the object whose continuity matters.

Candidate objects include:

- function;
- knowledge;
- operational capability;
- responsibility;
- service;
- relationship;
- process;
- institution;
- identity state;
- infrastructure;
- provenance;
- correction/review capability.

The module must not assume that preserving the current container is equivalent to preserving the valuable object.

## 8. Candidate operational chain

The extracted architecture supports development around:

**Continuity Object → Value/Legitimate Function → Threat/Discontinuity → Critical Dependencies → Preservation State → Recovery Basis → Trigger → Restoration/Succession → Verification → Review/Adaptation**

This chain is an extraction abstraction, not a verbatim sequence from the source.

## 9. Preservation-state distinction

A portable specification should distinguish at least:

1. **recorded** — information exists somewhere;
2. **accessible** — intended future users can retrieve it;
3. **interpretable** — meaning/context/provenance can be understood;
4. **actionable** — procedures and dependencies permit use;
5. **recoverable** — sufficient enabling capability exists to restore the required function;
6. **operational** — the function is currently available.

This prevents archive existence from being mistaken for continuity.

## 10. Change boundary

The source strongly rejects preservation-as-stagnation.

A portable module therefore needs explicit tests for:

- what should survive;
- what may change;
- what must be replaceable;
- what function is more durable than its current implementation;
- what historical material should remain as provenance without retaining authority;
- what obsolete or harmful material should be superseded rather than operationally preserved.

The module should preserve learning without giving the past permanent command over the future.

## 11. Succession boundary

Succession is a major source component and belongs in the portable core at functional level.

Portable succession should ask:

- what function/responsibility must continue;
- what predecessor state must be preserved;
- what successor must receive;
- what authority/permission is independently required;
- what handoff proves continuity;
- what happens if succession is partial, contested or fails.

The module must not infer that historical succession alone creates legitimacy.

> **Continuity ≠ Authority**

> **Succession ≠ Automatic Legitimacy**

## 12. Personal-identity boundary

The primary source contains extensive material on biological, artificial and hybrid identity continuity.

That material is significant but introduces philosophical, legal and substrate-specific questions beyond the minimum general continuity architecture.

For portable v0.1, personal identity continuity should therefore be treated as a **specialised application domain**, not as the definition of continuity itself.

A general module can preserve declared continuity requirements and uncertainty without claiming to solve metaphysical identity.

## 13. Recovery and degradation

The wider continuity sources support explicit attention to degraded conditions.

A portable module should distinguish:

- normal continuity;
- degraded continuity;
- interruption;
- recoverable loss;
- irrecoverable loss;
- restoration;
- succession/replacement;
- regeneration from a lower capability state.

Where full restoration is impossible, preserving a minimum recovery basis may be more important than preserving a high-level artefact that can no longer be used.

## 14. Provenance

Provenance is core, not decorative.

A continuity record should preserve enough of:

**Source → Transformation → Decision/State → Implementation → Outcome → Review → Correction/Supersession**

to permit future users to understand what was preserved, why, how it changed and whether it remains current.

Exact provenance depth should be proportional to consequence.

## 15. Honest failure

The source explicitly treats failed experiments, proposals and mechanisms as potential continuity assets.

The portable module should therefore allow preservation of:

- unsuccessful approaches;
- failure causes;
- invalidated assumptions;
- abandoned states;
- reasons for supersession.

> **Preserving Only Success Can Destroy Continuity of Learning.**

## 16. Failure modes to test

Candidate portable failure modes include:

- archive theatre — stored but inaccessible/unusable information;
- implementation immortality — preserving the container instead of the function;
- provenance loss;
- rationale loss;
- dependency-chain blindness;
- obsolete-state resurrection;
- continuity capture — continuity mechanism used to preserve incumbent authority;
- single-point preservation;
- correlated redundancy;
- format/interpretation decay;
- restoration without verification;
- succession without adequate handoff;
- succession treated as legitimacy;
- over-preservation/stagnation;
- under-preservation/unnecessary loss;
- correction-capacity loss;
- continuity mechanism fossilisation.

## 17. ESCP safeguard

Source recovery is sufficient to begin portable specification, but the source corpus is large and continuity is distributed across neighbouring architectures.

Therefore:

> **No Additional Source Found ≠ No Additional Source Exists**

and:

> **Portable Extraction ≠ Exhaustive Concord Continuity Model**

The v0.1 module should preserve provenance and allow later source recovery if testing exposes a missing dependency.

## 18. Extraction decision

The candidate is genuinely portable.

The strongest reusable architecture is not “preserve everything.” It is a bounded method for identifying what continuity actually requires, preserving enough state and enabling capability to survive discontinuity, restoring or succeeding function, verifying recovery and remaining adaptable.

> **SOURCE RESOLUTION SUFFICIENT FOR STANDALONE SPECIFICATION.**

> **NO BLOCKING SOURCE OMISSION IDENTIFIED AT THIS STAGE.**
