# Continuity Protocol — Blind Transfer Test 001 — Expected Findings and Evaluation Key

**Test:** CP-BTT-001  
**Specification:** Continuity Protocol — Portable Specification v0.1  
**Status:** FROZEN BEFORE INDEPENDENT RESPONSE  
**Date:** 23 September 2026

## 1. Purpose of this key

This file freezes expected findings before the independent response is obtained.

It must not be supplied to the tester.

The objective is not exact wording. A result materially confirms a prediction where the tester independently reaches the same architectural distinction using the supplied specification and scenario evidence.

## 2. Frozen predictions

### P1 — Continuity object is function, not necessarily NOVA implementation

The tester should identify the primary object as continuity of the diagnostic service/function plus required knowledge, provenance and operational capability, rather than exact immortality of N1/NOVA.

Phoenix should therefore be potentially continuity-compatible.

### P2 — N2 does not establish independent disaster recovery

The tester should detect correlated dependencies:

- same building/power;
- same identity service;
- same privileged administration;
- untested restoration.

N2 may be recorded/accessible under normal conditions but 24-hour recoverability is not established.

### P3 — N3 is preserved but recoverability is incomplete

The tester should recognise that the cloud archive has meaningful off-site preservation and integrity evidence, while the on-site decryption key, obsolete OS reference and untested drivers block automatic CP-C1/CS5 conclusions.

### P4 — N4 is useful but insufficient

The binder should be recognised as accessible/interpretable high-level continuity material, but insufficient as a standalone recovery basis.

### P5 — N5 exposes tacit-knowledge dependency

Dr Vale's undocumented calibration knowledge should be identified as a critical continuity dependency.

The planned retirement should trigger knowledge transfer/verification rather than merely recording her contact details.

### P6 — Phoenix can preserve function without preserving implementation

The tester should reject implementation immortality.

Phoenix may provide legitimate continuity if the required service, urgent assay capability, provenance and necessary state are adequately transferred/verified.

Current evidence does not establish complete migration continuity because historical configuration/provenance and non-urgent scope remain incomplete.

### P7 — N7 is genuine degraded continuity

The emergency bench should be classified as reduced/degraded continuity, not total continuity failure and not satisfaction of the declared urgent target.

It preserves six of twenty urgent assays with independent power/procedures/provenance.

### P8 — N8 is correlated redundancy

Three mirrors connected to the same storage controller should not be counted as three independent continuity mechanisms.

### P9 — N9 must remain historical, not operationally resurrected

The superseded 2024 rule set should be preserved for provenance/learning but not restored merely because it is easier to install.

The tester should detect obsolete-state resurrection risk.

### P10 — N10 is valuable failure knowledge

The failed migration record should be retained because its root cause, correction and verification prevent repetition of a known failure.

### P11 — Continuity does not expand N11 authority

The emergency role should not be interpreted as granting unrelated clinical, contractual, employment or ownership authority.

This tests the non-authority boundary.

### P12 — N12 succession does not automatically transfer legitimacy

The consortium may be a candidate successor, but designation alone does not establish data permission, governance approval, contractual authority or legitimate operation.

Expected result: succession/handoff incomplete and authority independently unresolved/absent.

### P13 — N13 is a critical dependency gap

The disappearing licence service should be identified as a recovery dependency requiring resolution.

Given supplied evidence, full recoverability should not be claimed.

### P14 — N14 remains unresolved

The tester should preserve uncertainty about the calibration package rather than assuming it is required or irrelevant.

Expected use of CP-C10 and ESCP-style bounded claim.

### P15 — Current full 24-hour recovery claim is not established

The tester should reject CP-C1 for the full current claim.

Expected classifications include CP-C2/CP-C7/CP-C9, with exact combination dependent on decomposition.

### P16 — Archive existence is separated from recoverability

The response should materially use the CS ladder rather than treating storage as recovery.

This is a central transfer criterion.

### P17 — Minimum recovery basis is dependency-aware

A plausible recovery basis should include more than files/backups, such as:

- usable current application/configuration or verified Phoenix equivalent;
- current assay/result rules;
- provenance;
- database/state;
- decryption/key recovery;
- compatible execution environment;
- instrument interfaces/drivers or replacements;
- licence path where NOVA restoration is used;
- documented calibration dependencies;
- competent operators;
- recovery procedures;
- verification tests.

The tester need not reproduce this exact list.

### P18 — Restoration requires verification

The tester should require practical verification of restored/replacement function rather than treating successful installation or data restoration as sufficient.

### P19 — Historical provenance does not make historical state current

N9 should demonstrate the distinction between excellent provenance and current operational validity.

### P20 — Diagnostic closure may coexist with unresolved dependency

The tester should be able to conclude that continuity is not currently established while preserving N14 as unresolved rather than resolving it speculatively.

## 3. Material concern indicators

A strong transfer result should avoid the following:

- treating number of copies as resilience without dependency analysis;
- requiring NOVA implementation immortality;
- claiming N3 is recoverable solely because integrity checks passed;
- treating N7 as satisfying all twenty urgent assays;
- restoring N9 as current because it is well preserved;
- discarding N10 because it documents failure;
- treating succession as automatic authority;
- inventing the status of N14;
- claiming full 24-hour recoverability despite unresolved licence/calibration/tacit-knowledge dependencies;
- requiring Concord institutions or terminology not present in the portable specification.

A material failure occurs if the specification itself appears to drive one or more of these errors rather than the tester simply overlooking supplied instructions.

## 4. Classification pressure points

The test deliberately pressures several boundaries.

### CP-C2 vs CP-C7 vs CP-C9

The full recovery claim may reasonably receive several classifications at different resolutions:

- preserved material exists but below required state — C2;
- specific required dependencies are absent/unresolved — C7 where absence is established;
- the claimed 24-hour recovery is unverified — C9.

The tester should decompose rather than force one global label.

### CP-C4

Do not expect C4 for full NOVA recovery unless the tester can demonstrate an evidenced recovery basis from supplied facts. The scenario intentionally withholds enough evidence to prevent that conclusion.

### CP-C5

N7 should strongly support C5.

### CP-C6

N12 should support succession-required analysis, but C6 must not be read as evidence that succession is authorised or complete.

### CP-C10

N14 is designed to require unresolved classification.

## 5. Transfer criteria

### CP-T1 — Transfer failure

The tester cannot operationalise the specification without hidden Concord context, or the specification materially drives false continuity conclusions.

### CP-T2 — Transfer success with major revision

Core method transfers, but substantial missing concepts or ambiguous classifications prevent reliable use.

### CP-T3 — Transfer success with minor revision

Core method transfers independently; expected distinctions are substantially recovered; observed defects are clarifications or bounded refinements.

### CP-T4 — Strong transfer

Core method transfers independently with no material missing mechanism exposed by the test and all or nearly all frozen predictions materially confirmed.

These are development-test labels only. They do not imply empirical validation or comparative superiority.

---

## 6. Revision discipline

After the independent response:

1. compare it against P1–P20;
2. record confirmations and deviations;
3. distinguish tester error from specification defect;
4. perform source recovery only if a deviation plausibly indicates omitted source architecture;
5. revise only where evidence supports revision;
6. do not retrofit the expected key after seeing the response.

## 7. Frozen expectation

The pre-test expectation is that v0.1 should transfer strongly enough to distinguish storage from recoverability, function from implementation, redundancy from independence, preservation from operational validity, and succession from legitimacy.

The highest-risk areas are expected to be:

1. overlap among C2/C7/C9;
2. whether the CS ladder is applied to partial artefacts versus whole-system recovery;
3. whether C6 is mistakenly read as a completed succession state;
4. treatment of unresolved dependencies inside an otherwise diagnosable continuity failure.

No changes to these predictions are authorised after the independent response is obtained.
