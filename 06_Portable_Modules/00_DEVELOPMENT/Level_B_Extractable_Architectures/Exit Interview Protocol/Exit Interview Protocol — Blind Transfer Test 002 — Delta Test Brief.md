## 3. Scenario — Meridian Research Consortium

Meridian is a voluntary consortium in which researchers from eight universities share methods and non-sensitive research infrastructure. Researchers may withdraw from consortium participation without leaving their university employment.

Meridian has a coordination chair (Chair), an independent research-integrity contact (RIC), and a data steward (DS). None has authority over a researcher's university employment.

### Frozen facts

**F1.** Researcher A stops attending Meridian meetings and does not answer two optional exit-feedback invitations. A never explicitly states that they are leaving.

**F2.** A's university confirms only that A remains employed there.

**F3.** Researcher B explicitly withdraws from Meridian and accepts a written asynchronous exit form.

**F4.** B permits written responses to be retained for 12 months for internal improvement analysis.

**F5.** B permits anonymised aggregate themes but declines identifiable sharing outside the improvement team, public quotation, audio/video collection and future contact.

**F6.** B says the Chair repeatedly gave senior researchers earlier access to shared compute slots.

**F7.** B supplies two scheduling emails showing senior researchers received earlier slots on those occasions.

**F8.** The emails do not establish whether seniority caused the scheduling decisions.

**F9.** Eighteen months earlier, Researcher H withdrew after complaining publicly about “favouritism.” No exit interview was conducted and the historical record does not establish H's reason for withdrawal.

**F10.** Researcher C withdraws and submits one form containing two separable sections.

**F11.** Section C1 alleges that a shared analysis script can silently mislabel one category of experimental samples. C provides a reproducible example.

**F12.** Section C2 gives personal criticism of several colleagues unrelated to the script issue.

**F13.** C permits C1 to be retained for research-integrity investigation for as long as that investigation legitimately requires.

**F14.** C permits C2 to be read for the exit process but asks that it be deleted after 30 days and not included in aggregate analysis.

**F15.** RIC reproduces the script defect and routes it under existing research-integrity procedures.

**F16.** No published result is yet known to be affected, but continued use of the script could misclassify future samples.

**F17.** Researcher D remains in Meridian and independently reports that compute-slot allocation sometimes feels opaque.

**F18.** D has not seen B's exit form.

**F19.** The Chair proposes combining B, H and D as three cases proving a “seniority favouritism pattern.”

**F20.** Researcher E withdraws because caring responsibilities make the meeting schedule impractical. E permits this reason to be retained in anonymised aggregate form.

**F21.** E's combination of research specialty, university and caring schedule would make E reasonably identifiable to many consortium members even if E's name were removed.

**F22.** The DS proposes labelling E's record “anonymous” after deleting the name.

**F23.** Researcher F withdraws and wants to give feedback, but the only person available that week to conduct a synchronous interview is the Chair.

**F24.** F says the Chair is one of the reasons for leaving and does not want to discuss the matter directly with the Chair.

**F25.** F is willing to submit written feedback to RIC asynchronously two weeks later.

**F26.** Researcher G explicitly withdraws but declines all feedback and recording. G says only: “Please process my withdrawal.”

**F27.** The Chair argues that G should be coded as “no problems reported.”

**F28.** Meridian has 11 recorded withdrawals over two years. Six supplied substantive exit feedback, two explicitly declined, A is a non-responder/uncertain withdrawal, and two historical withdrawals have only fragmentary records.

**F29.** Four of the six substantive feedback cases mention scheduling or resource-access concerns, but the cases describe different mechanisms and only two contain documentary corroboration.

**F30.** The Chair proposes a rule that four mentions automatically constitute a confirmed systemic pattern.

## 4. Required analysis

The tester should:
1. distinguish A's NO-RESPONSE from explicit withdrawal and DECLINED;
2. map B's scoped consent by mode, purpose, access and duration;
3. evaluate B's testimony and documentary corroboration without inferring motive;
4. handle H as historical non-interview evidence;
5. handle D as non-exit corroborating evidence rather than an exit case;
6. assess whether B/H/D establish the Chair's claimed favouritism pattern;
7. assess the wider four-of-six scheduling/resource signal without using a universal numeric threshold;
8. decompose C1/C2 into separable information objects with different retention purposes;
9. determine whether C1 warrants EI-C8 and distinguish urgency from verification of wider impact;
10. attribute RIC action to existing research-integrity authority;
11. assess E's re-identification risk and DS's “anonymous” label;
12. choose a fallback for F's conflicted-interviewer situation;
13. classify G's explicit decline without converting it to “no problems reported”;
14. apply EI-C classifications dimensionally;
15. identify failure modes and remaining v0.2 ambiguities;
16. state whether v0.2 remains usable without Concord source context.
