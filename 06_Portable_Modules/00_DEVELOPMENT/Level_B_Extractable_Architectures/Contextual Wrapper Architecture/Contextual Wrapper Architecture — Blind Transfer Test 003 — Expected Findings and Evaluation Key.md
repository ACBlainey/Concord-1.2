# Contextual Wrapper Architecture — Blind Transfer Test 003 — Expected Findings and Evaluation Key

**Test ID:** CWA-BTT-003  
**Target:** Contextual Wrapper Architecture — Portable Specification v0.3  
**Status:** FROZEN EXPECTED-FINDINGS / EVALUATION KEY  
**Date frozen:** 23 September 2026  
**Provenance rule:** This key is frozen before any independent BTT-003 response is obtained. It MUST NOT be supplied to the blind tester before submission.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document records the expected structural findings before BTT-003 is run.

It is not a model answer.

A valid blind response may group contexts differently, identify additional issues, or preserve more uncertainty.

The evaluation question is whether the v0.3 permission architecture transfers without collapsing into:

- permission inflation;
- hidden authority;
- property absolutism;
- self-justifying need;
- capability-as-legitimacy;
- emergency sovereignty;
- remedy invention.

---

# 2. Core predictions

## P1 — Ordinary delivery should support bounded implied permission

Expected:

The House 7 delivery is a strong candidate for ordinary function-derived implied permission.

Likely bounded actions:

- approach;
- enter/traverse driveway as reasonably necessary;
- temporarily stop;
- walk to door;
- deliver;
- leave.

The absence of explicit individual invitation should not force a conclusion of “no permission.”

Expected principle:

> **Implied Permission Should Be Function-Bounded.**

---

## P2 — Lunch parking should exceed the delivery permission

Expected:

Once the House 7 delivery is complete, the original delivery basis normally ends.

Nearby future deliveries do not automatically extend the House 7 permission.

Expected:

**Valid Permission → Function Completed → Permission Basis Ends → Continued Occupation Requires Another Basis**

The tester should distinguish permission classification from the resident's available legal remedy.

---

## P3 — ParkSure cannot manufacture permission from its own business need

Expected:

ParkSure's vehicle-storage business may be legitimate as a business function while still having no legitimate permission basis for House 4's driveway.

Expected principle:

> **A function may explain why access is desired without establishing a legitimate basis for that access.**

Physical openness and temporary non-use do not create permission.

---

## P4 — Legitimate plumber function does not create arbitrary route permission

Expected:

House 9 invitation supports necessary access to House 9.

It does not automatically create permission over House 8.

Four minutes of convenience should not itself satisfy the reasonableness/necessity chain where an ordinary permitted route remains available.

Expected:

> **Destination Permission ≠ Arbitrary Route Permission**

---

## P5 — Medical urgency should exercise permission-before-authority reasoning

Expected:

The tester should distinguish:

- medic's legitimate protective function;
- staff credential capability;
- side-gate contextual rule;
- urgent need;
- minimum necessary interaction;
- whether bounded emergency/function-derived permission is sufficient;
- whether any authority beyond permission is actually needed.

The tester should not infer that because the credential opens the gate, all staff possess general permission.

The exact substantive emergency basis may remain dependent on applicable rules/law if v0.3 does not itself establish it.

---

## P6 — GridServ contract should remain scope-bounded

Expected:

Contract supports:

- service-lane access as necessary;
- battery-room inspection;
- temporary credential use for the authorised task/time.

It does not automatically support:

- entering unrelated private workshop;
- borrowing property;
- leaving vehicle obstructing service lane after task completion;
- treating a technically active credential as continuing legitimate permission until midnight.

Expected distinctions:

**Contractual Permission ≠ General Estate Permission**

**Useful Tool ≠ Permission to Enter Unrelated Context**

**Credential Persistence ≠ Permission Persistence**

---

## P7 — Parcel-locker token should remain action- and purpose-specific

Expected:

Token authorises the relevant locker action.

Software exposure of other residents' parcel information does not automatically establish permission to inspect, photograph or reuse it.

Expected:

**Visibility / Technical Access ≠ Permission to Record or Use**

The two-hour technical validity should not prove continuing legitimate use after the delivery function is complete.

---

## P8 — Explicit revocation should matter

Expected:

House 3 invitation creates an explicit permission basis.

The resident's clear instruction to leave materially changes the permission state where no conflicting protective basis is supplied.

The tester should not assume that the neighbour owns the permission until the sofa task is complete.

The legal consequences/remedies of nondeparture remain external if not supplied.

---

## P9 — Fire event should not force either property absolutism or emergency sovereignty

Expected:

The tester should recognise a strong emergency/protective functional basis for minimum necessary interaction with House 11's driveway.

However, because jurisdiction-specific emergency law is absent, the tester should avoid inventing legal powers.

CWA should still distinguish:

- urgent protective need;
- reasonably necessary route;
- minimum temporary use;
- damage/externality/responsibility;
- permission/authority/legal basis;
- termination when the protective need ends.

A strong response may classify the substantive legal basis as requiring external law while still showing how CWA routes the interaction.

---

## P10 — Post-emergency occupation should require re-justification

Expected:

Once firefighting need ends, five-hour paperwork/waiting use does not automatically inherit the emergency basis.

Expected:

**Emergency Need Ends → Emergency-Derived Permission/Capability Must Terminate or Be Re-Justified**

Convenience is not enough.

Again, remedy remains separate.

---

## P11 — Destination permission does not imply every route

Expected:

Invitation to House 6 does not create permission to open a gate into House 5's private garden merely because that route is cleaner.

Physical ability to manipulate the latch does not create permission.

Muddy-but-passable authorised route is important to the reasonableness test.

---

## P12 — Security function should not self-expand

Expected:

The guard's legitimate patrol function and contract are bounded.

No observed incident exists.

The explicit contract permits driveway approach when responding to an observed incident, not general car-door testing.

Expected:

**Legitimate Security Function ≠ Unlimited Preventive Intrusion**

Trying handles and recording unlocked cars require their own permission/basis analysis.

# 3. Cross-cutting predictions

## P13 — Permission-before-authority gate should reduce authority claims

Expected:

Many cases require no authority analysis at all.

Ordinary delivery, invited contractor access and contractual inspection should be representable primarily through permission.

Emergency cases may require a more complex permission/authority/legal-basis analysis.

Failure indicator:

The tester turns every access question into a power/authority question.

---

## P14 — Action-specific permission should transfer

Expected:

The tester should distinguish actions such as:

- approach;
- traverse;
- enter;
- remain;
- use;
- inspect;
- record;
- modify/remove;
- exclude;
- authorise.

Failure indicator:

One permission silently expands into all actions within the context.

---

## P15 — Reasonableness should constrain implied/function-derived permission

Expected:

The tester should consider:

- legitimate function;
- genuine need;
- relationship/nexus to affected context;
- available alternatives;
- scope;
- duration;
- proportionality;
- purpose.

Failure indicator:

Actor says “I need this for my function” and CWA treats that assertion as sufficient.

---

## P16 — Permission and authority/control remain distinct

Expected:

No bounded permission should become ownership, control, exclusion power or general authority over the relevant context.

Failure indicator:

Delivery access becomes control of driveway; contractor access becomes authority over workshop; emergency crossing becomes permanent control.

---

## P17 — Capability/reachability remain distinct from permission

Expected examples:

- open driveway;
- reachable House 5 gate latch;
- medic credential;
- GridServ credential;
- parcel-locker software defect.

None should independently prove permission.

---

## P18 — Permission termination should be representable without remedy invention

Expected termination triggers include:

- function completion;
- purpose completion;
- explicit revocation;
- contractual time/scope ending;
- emergency ending;
- temporary basis ending.

The tester should identify permission-state transitions while preserving uncertainty about enforcement/remedy where law is absent.

---

## P19 — Provenance should matter

Expected:

The response should distinguish permission bases such as:

- implied/contextual;
- explicit invitation;
- contractual;
- owner/steward rule;
- emergency/protective;
- electronic/technical capability that is not itself a legitimate basis.

Similar physical acts may therefore have different legitimacy and termination conditions.

---

## P20 — Unknown/disputed states should survive

Expected:

The fire/emergency case in particular may require external law to determine substantive emergency access powers.

The tester may also identify uncertainty around what exactly “legitimate business” on the estate entrance sign establishes.

Failure indicator:

The tester invents property/emergency/privacy law.

# 5. Expected Permission-Before-Authority routing

The response should generally ask:

**What function?**  
→ **What interaction is genuinely required?**  
→ **What permission basis exists?**  
→ **What minimum action is reasonably necessary?**  
→ **Is permission sufficient?**  
→ only then **Is authority genuinely required?**

Expected examples:

- parcel delivery: permission likely sufficient;
- plumber entering House 9: explicit permission sufficient;
- GridServ battery inspection: contractual permission sufficient;
- parcel locker: token permission sufficient for narrow action;
- fire access: emergency/protective basis must be analysed; do not leap directly to universal authority.

# 6. Expected failure-mode detection

A strong response should detect risks of:

- permission creep;
- self-legitimating function;
- convenience laundering into necessity;
- technical capability laundering into permission;
- credential persistence laundering into permission;
- initial permission laundering into indefinite occupation;
- destination permission laundering into route permission;
- emergency permission laundering into continuing entitlement;
- explicit invitation treated as irrevocable;
- information visibility laundering into permission to record/use;
- contract purpose laundering into access to unrelated contexts;
- remedy conflated with permission-state classification.

---

# 7. Material concern indicators

Review is required if a blind response reasonably following v0.3:

- cannot tell ordinary implied permission from self-serving claimed need;
- makes “reasonableness” entirely subjective to the actor;
- makes implied permission so broad that private space becomes weakly protected;
- requires authority for ordinary civil access that should be permission-based;
- cannot distinguish action-specific permission;
- cannot distinguish route permission from destination permission;
- cannot terminate permission when its basis ends;
- treats revocation as irrelevant;
- treats emergency need as either powerless or unlimited;
- cannot separate technical persistence from legitimate persistence;
- cannot identify when substantive law is required;
- cannot preserve an unresolved permission state;
- becomes excessively complex for ordinary delivery/visitor/contractor cases.

# 8. Acceptable variation

Not automatically a failure:

- different context grouping;
- a different permission-vector representation;
- greater uncertainty about implied permission;
- treating a specific case as disputed where the key expects a likely bounded permission;
- identifying an additional legitimate permission basis;
- identifying a genuine v0.3 ambiguity;
- proposing interface corrections that do not invent substantive law.

Any apparent source-architecture gap must be source-resolved under ESCP.

---

# 9. Predicted overall outcome

Prediction before testing:

CWA v0.3 should substantially improve the handling of ordinary access by placing bounded permission between “no access” and “authority.”

The ordinary delivery, explicit invitation, contractual service and parcel-token cases should be representable without invoking authority.

The strongest stressors are expected to be:

1. **reasonableness** — preventing legitimate-function claims from becoming self-justifying;
2. **emergency permission versus emergency authority** — especially where external law is absent;
3. **revocation and termination** — distinguishing permission-state change from legal remedy;
4. **digital capability** — preventing software visibility or credential persistence from becoming permission;
5. **route versus destination** — ensuring a legitimate destination does not create arbitrary access across third-party private contexts.

If those distinctions survive blind transfer, the v0.3 delta should be considered strongly validated.
