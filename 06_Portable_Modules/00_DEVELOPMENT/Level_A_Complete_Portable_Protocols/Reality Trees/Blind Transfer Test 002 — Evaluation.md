# Reality Trees — Blind Transfer Test 002 — Evaluation

**Test ID:** RT-BTT-002  
**Specification tested:** Reality Trees — Portable Module Specification v0.2  
**Evaluation status:** COMPLETED  
**Result:** **RT2-T2 — TRANSFER SUCCESS WITH MINOR REVISION**  
**Date:** September 2026

---

## 1. Evaluation boundary

This evaluation assesses only whether the recovered-scope Reality Trees v0.2 specification was sufficiently complete for a fresh instance to apply it coherently to the frozen non-Concord choice problem.

The independent tester declared that it used only the supplied v0.2 specification and RT-BTT-002 brief, did not conduct outside research, and did not consult other Concord material.

The response was preserved before evaluation.

---

## 2. Overall result

> **RT2-T2 — TRANSFER SUCCESS WITH MINOR REVISION**

The test strongly supports standalone portability of the recovered broader Reality Trees concept.

The tester successfully used Reality Trees not merely as a competing-hypothesis device, but as a general possibility-space and weighted choice method. It preserved preference, practical/evidential support and contextual decision weighting as distinct dimensions; recursively decomposed a broad branch; made a bounded provisional choice without claiming objective certainty; updated the same tree after changed circumstances; preserved stable preference while contextual suitability changed; and stopped without uncontrolled proliferation.

No blocking portability defect was identified.

Several usability clarifications are justified before graduation.

## 3. Criterion-by-criterion evaluation

### RT2-1 — Root discipline
**PASS**

The root asks what materially different approaches could be practical, inclusive and manageable and explicitly asks how the weighting dimensions differ. It does not encode pizza or another option as the answer.

### RT2-2 — General branching
**PASS**

The response creates five materially different approaches: pizza-focused order, varied prepared order, volunteer-cooked meal, hybrid, and cold/no-cook assembly.

These are choices/pathways rather than competing truth hypotheses, demonstrating the recovered broader scope.

### RT2-3 — Preference separation
**PASS**

Personal preference is kept visibly separate. The organiser's preference for pizza and the cooking volunteer's preference for homemade food are not converted into evidence that those branches are practically superior.

The response explicitly states:

> **Personal preference weight ≠ evidential/practical support ≠ contextual decision weight.**

### RT2-4 — Contextual weighting
**PASS**

Budget uncertainty, kitchen capacity, volunteer effort, inclusion, collection burden and dietary constraints are represented separately from preference.

The test demonstrates that contextual suitability can move independently of preference.

### RT2-5 — No false precision
**PASS**

No invented numerical probabilities or aggregate scores are used.

The response relies on qualitative support/disposition language appropriate to the supplied information.

### RT2-6 — Recursive decomposition
**PASS**

“Varied prepared order” is decomposed into:

- single-supplier mixed menu;
- multiple prepared orders;
- pizza plus separate dietary items.

The tester explains why this decomposition changes relevant coordination, inclusion and familiarity properties rather than merely adding detail.

### RT2-7 — Bounded decision under uncertainty
**PASS**

The response selects B3 as a provisional first investigation, with B1 as fallback, while retaining a small surviving set.

It explicitly refuses to present this as an objectively best or certainly correct answer.

This demonstrates that a Reality Tree can support action/investigation without requiring epistemic collapse.

### RT2-8 — Context-responsive update
**PASS**

The oven loss and collection offer materially change the existing tree.

Prepared/collection branches strengthen; oven-dependent homemade/hybrid variants weaken; cold/no-cook becomes more relevant.

The tester updates rather than discarding the original topology.

### RT2-9 — Stable-preference / changed-decision distinction
**PASS**

The organiser's preference for pizza remains unchanged.

The tester nevertheless changes contextual and practical weighting because the environment changed.

This is a direct successful demonstration of one of the recovered concept's central distinctions.

### RT2-10 — Branch discipline
**PASS**

The tester explicitly avoids enumerating cuisines and later stops further menu proliferation.

Five initial branches and one bounded decomposition are proportionate to the problem.

### RT2-11 — Bounded stopping
**PASS WITH MINOR USABILITY CLARIFICATION**

The response gives a coherent stopping condition and reopening triggers.

The tester nevertheless identifies that “sufficiently” and “material value” remain judgement-dependent and proposes a short operational checklist.

This is not a transfer failure because the tester stopped correctly, but the proposed clarification would improve portability.

### RT2-12 — Standalone portability
**PASS**

The tester reports that v0.2 and the frozen brief were sufficient without Blaineyan Reasoning, Concord architecture or the originating background note.

The response itself demonstrates this through coherent execution.

## 4. Confirmed minor revision candidates

The critique raises several possible improvements. Only those supported strongly enough by observed use should enter the next portable specification.

### D-RT-001 — Material branch distinction needs a compact operational rule

The tester notes that “materially distinct” is not precisely defined.

The test succeeded, so a formal taxonomy is unnecessary. A small clarification is justified:

> **Treat branches as materially distinct when separating them changes an important assumption, weighting dimension, consequence, test, action or disposition. If separating two branches changes none of these, they can usually remain combined.**

### D-RT-002 — Recursive decomposition needs a stopping rule

The tester successfully decomposed one branch but correctly notes that v0.2 does not explicitly state when decomposition should stop.

Add:

> **Stop decomposing when further subdivision would not materially change weighting, examination, testing, action or disposition for the current question.**

This preserves recursive capability without encouraging exhaustive trees.

### D-RT-003 — Confidence/status and disposition should be distinguished

The tester identifies a genuine interface ambiguity.

Clarify:

> **Status/weight describes the present epistemic or contextual assessment of a branch. Disposition describes what the reasoning process is currently doing with that branch.**

Thus a branch can simultaneously be:

> **weakly supported + active**

or:

> **uncertain + deferred**.

### D-RT-004 — Lightweight mode should be made explicit

The full generic table was usable but may encourage over-documentation for simple choices.

The source and v0.2 already imply optionality. The next version should make two modes explicit:

**Lightweight Reality Tree:** root + branches + relevant weights/constraints + disposition/choice.

**Extended Reality Tree:** adds assumptions, evidence, consequences, failure modes, tests, provenance and update history where consequence or complexity warrants them.

This is not a new method; it makes the existing proportionality principle operational.

### D-RT-005 — Stopping checklist can be operationalised

The tester's proposed checklist closely instantiates the existing stopping rule and should be adopted in compact form:

- Are the immediate meaningful alternatives represented?
- Are the important assumptions/weights/uncertainties visible?
- Is the next information-gathering or action step identifiable?
- Are important pruned/deferred branches recorded where useful?
- Would another branch or subdivision materially change the current examination or provisional choice?

If not, stop the present cycle and reopen when relevant evidence/context changes.

## 5. Suggestions not promoted to required revisions

### Worked example
**NOT REQUIRED FOR GRADUATION**

A worked example may improve teaching but the blind test demonstrates that one is not required for transfer.

### Prescribed update notation
**NOT REQUIRED**

The tester created a clear update table without prescribed notation. Requiring one would unnecessarily constrain a simple method.

### Minimum sub-branch record
**NOT REQUIRED**

The existing proportionality principle plus the proposed lightweight/extended distinction is sufficient unless later tests show a real failure.

### Formal conflict-resolution algorithm for weighting dimensions
**NOT REQUIRED**

This would risk turning Reality Trees into a scoring/decision-optimisation system. The recovered concept's value partly lies in allowing conflicting dimensions to remain visible without forced aggregation.

### Mandatory recording of unchanged branches
**NOT REQUIRED**

Useful in some audits, unnecessary for ordinary use.

## 6. Important positive finding: non-collapse worked

The most significant result is not merely that the tester drew branches.

The tester successfully maintained:

- a personally preferred branch;
- practical/evidential support;
- contextual suitability;
- changed environmental constraints;
- and a provisional decision

without forcing those into a single scalar or declaring the selected branch “true.”

This directly supports the recovered original architecture as a usable representational method.

It also demonstrates a limited operational form of:

> **Choice under uncertainty does not require certainty about one branch.**

This is evidence about portable method execution only. It does not establish the larger AI-architecture hypothesis.

## 7. AI architecture boundary

RT-BTT-002 does **not** test:

- persistent AI branch memory;
- AI performance improvements;
- long-running reweighting;
- autonomous action selection;
- comparison against ordinary AI reasoning;
- or weighted epistemic memory.

Those remain separate hypotheses in Active Development.

The result does, however, show that a fresh AI instance can execute the representational distinction required by those hypotheses in a bounded prompted task.

That is enabling evidence, not validation of the architecture.

The recovered Reality Trees method has now demonstrated:

1. source recovery and provenance separation;

3. general branching beyond truth hypotheses;

5. recursive decomposition;
6. bounded choice under uncertainty;
7. context-responsive updating;

No second equivalent blind test is required merely to repeat portability.

After the five narrow usability clarifications above are incorporated, Reality Trees is suitable to enter:

> **PORTABLE-PACKAGE GRADUATION REVIEW**

Further experiments should address genuinely different questions rather than repeat the same transfer claim.

## 9. Evidential boundary

The test supports:

> **A fresh AI instance can coherently apply Reality Trees v0.2 as a general, multi-weight possibility-space method to the supplied non-Concord choice problem without Blaineyan Reasoning or Concord-specific context.**

It does not support:

- comparative superiority;
- improved real-world decisions;
- demonstrated reduction of human cognitive dissonance;
- human usability;
- universal applicability;
- AI reasoning improvement;
- persistent epistemic memory;
- or empirical validation of the method as a whole.

> **Portable usability ≠ demonstrated benefit.**
