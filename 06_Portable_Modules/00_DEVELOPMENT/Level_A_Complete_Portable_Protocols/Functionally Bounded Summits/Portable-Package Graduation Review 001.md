# Functionally Bounded Summits — Portable-Package Graduation Review 001

**Specification reviewed:** Functionally Bounded Summits — Portable Module Specification v0.2  
**Status:** COMPLETED

---

## 1. Review question

Has Functionally Bounded Summits reached the point where the current portable-package development cycle can close and a standalone v1.0 release can be created without overstating the evidence?

---

## 2. Source basis

The candidate has:

- a direct developed source protocol in the Concord intercivilisational architecture;
- surrounding source architecture supporting bounded interoperability, non-governance, contextual procedure and minimum necessary capability;
- a completed source-resolution/extraction audit;
- no identified blocking Concord-specific dependency.

The source audit concluded:

> **SOURCE RESOLUTION SUFFICIENT FOR STANDALONE SPECIFICATION / NO BLOCKING SOURCE OMISSION IDENTIFIED.**

---

## 3. Standalone extraction

The portable specification now defines:

- applicability and non-applicability;
- problem and function resolution;
- participant-role discipline;
- evidence discipline;
- Minimum Necessary Negotiation;
- interaction grammar;
- dependency-sensitive scope expansion;
- bounded outcomes;
- Functional Failure Containment;
- non-inference;
- bounded authority;
- third-party facilitation;
- functional trust;
- failure classification;
- emergency use;
- repeated-use transition;
- implementation record;
- evaluation questions;
- evidential boundary.

Concord-specific wrapper institutions are not required to operate the protocol.

---

## 4. Transfer evidence

FBS-BTT-001 tested the standalone specification in a fictional non-Concord, non-state organisational setting.

Result:

> **FBS-T2 — TRANSFER SUCCESS WITH MINOR REVISION**

All twelve test criteria passed.

The test demonstrated that a fresh AI instance could:

- define a bounded problem without embedding a preferred mechanism;
- identify the required function;
- distinguish negotiating participants from wider actors;
- preserve known/disputed/missing evidence;
- resist negotiation contagion;
- recognise genuine dependencies;
- expand scope narrowly when needed;
- update the same process when new information arrived;
- distinguish non-agreement from protocol failure;
- preserve non-inference;
- and diagnose failure without importing Concord-specific machinery.

---

## 5. Test-supported revisions

Four bounded clarifications were incorporated into v0.2:

### R1 — Evidence-source distinction

> **Necessary Evidence Source ≠ Necessary Negotiating Participant.**

### R2 — Staged scope handling

> **Out-of-Scope Proposal ≠ Protocol Violation by Default.**

Proposed linkage is dependency-tested before persistence, bypass or coercion is classified as violation.

### R3 — Evidential discipline around exploitation

> **Exploitative Structure ≠ Proven Malicious Intent.**

### R4 — Independent authority after non-agreement

> **Negotiated Agreement ≠ Independent Exercise of Pre-Existing Authority.**

No revision changes the core mechanism.

---

## 6. Core mechanism stability

Across source extraction, standalone specification and blind transfer, the central architecture remained stable:

> **Bound the problem. Bound the negotiation. Bound the failure. Expand only when the function genuinely requires it.**

The blind test did not expose a contradiction between boundedness and dependency recognition.

Instead, it supported the intended distinction:

> **Functional Boundedness ≠ Functional Isolation.**

---

## 7. Portability finding

The available evidence supports a bounded claim:

> **Functionally Bounded Summits can be specified independently of Concord-specific civilisational machinery, and a fresh AI instance successfully transferred the specification to the tested non-state organisational scenario.**

This is sufficient for **specification-level portable graduation**.

It is not sufficient for empirical validation.

---

## 8. Claims that remain open

Graduation must not imply that the protocol:

- improves agreement rates;
- reduces escalation in real-world disputes;
- outperforms other negotiation methods;
- is robust under severe power asymmetry;
- prevents coercion;
- works equally across cultures;
- is usable by humans without adaptation;
- works in every diplomatic or organisational setting;
- or produces legitimate substantive outcomes merely because its procedure was followed.

Those require different evidence.

---

## 9. Need for another same-question blind AI test

A second equivalent blind AI transfer test is not required merely to repeat FBS-BTT-001.

The first test exposed only minor specification clarifications and no blocking transfer defect.

Further testing should target materially different questions, for example:

- human usability;
- repeated summit sequences;
- high-stakes emergency compression;
- severe asymmetric power;
- multi-party scope capture;
- long-running adversarial exploitation;
- comparative performance against alternative negotiation procedures;
- real-world implementation.

---

## 10. Authority and legitimacy boundary

The portable module remains procedural.

It does not decide who legitimately holds authority in an external system.

It preserves:

> **Forum Existence ≠ Standing Authority.**

> **Protocol Design ≠ Authority Over Protocol Users.**

> **Successful Negotiation ≠ Permanent Jurisdiction.**

> **Negotiated Agreement ≠ Independent Exercise of Pre-Existing Authority.**

This boundary is necessary for portability.

It does not mean:

> **proven effective, universally applicable, empirically validated or permanently fixed.**
