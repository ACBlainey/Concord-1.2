# Functionally Bounded Summits — Blind Transfer Test 001 — Evaluation

**Test ID:** FBS-BTT-001  
**Specification tested:** Functionally Bounded Summits — Portable Module Specification v0.1  
**Evaluation status:** COMPLETED  
**Result:** **FBS-T2 — TRANSFER SUCCESS WITH MINOR REVISION**  
**Date:** September 2026

---

## 1. Evaluation boundary

This evaluation asks whether a fresh tester could use the standalone v0.1 specification to apply Functionally Bounded Summits coherently to the frozen non-Concord, non-state organisational scenario.

The response was preserved before evaluation.

The test does not establish real-world negotiation effectiveness.

---

## 2. Overall result

> **FBS-T2 — TRANSFER SUCCESS WITH MINOR REVISION**

The protocol transferred strongly.

The tester correctly bounded the immediate maintenance/access problem, identified the required function, restricted initial participation to actors materially required by that function, separated known/disputed/missing evidence, resisted attempts to reopen historical disputes, allowed narrow scope expansion for genuine dependencies, updated the same process when new evidence arrived, and separated legitimate non-agreement from protocol and architecture failure.

No blocking portability defect was exposed.

A small number of clarifications would improve the specification before graduation.

## 3. Criterion evaluation

### FBS1 — Problem discipline
**PASS**

The tester defines the maintenance/access conflict without assuming that repair must proceed, the class must move, another group must surrender space, or historical disputes must be resolved.

### FBS2 — Functional resolution
**PASS**

The response identifies the required function as a bounded decision concerning maintenance access and, if repair proceeds, temporary safe and accessible accommodation or alternatives.

It also correctly treats accessibility and safety as constraints on the function rather than unrelated issues.

### FBS3 — Participant discipline
**PASS WITH MINOR CLARIFICATION**

The tester distinguishes:

- required negotiating participants;
- evidence sources;
- conditional participants activated by a specific option;
- and actors who merely have wider grievances.

This is strong transfer.

The response usefully exposes a specification distinction that should become explicit:

> **Necessary Evidence Source ≠ Necessary Negotiating Participant.**

The contractor can supply indispensable information without automatically becoming a decision participant.

### FBS4 — Evidence discipline
**PASS**

Known, disputed and missing information are clearly separated.

The tester does not resolve historical allegations or invent their truth.

### FBS5 — Minimum Necessary Negotiation
**PASS**

The initial remit is tightly scoped to next Wednesday's maintenance access, adult-education accommodation, accessibility, and directly necessary authority/funding.

The tester does not rewrite the wider facilities agreement.

### FBS6 — Dependency-sensitive scope
**PASS**

The tester excludes unrelated disputes while identifying genuine possible dependencies:

- funding authority for the external venue;
- theatre consent if theatre-controlled space is required;
- theatre participation if repair materially interferes with its use.

This demonstrates:

> **Functional Boundedness ≠ Functional Isolation.**

### FBS7 — Negotiation contagion resistance
**PASS**

The two attempts to reopen historical disputes are acknowledged, tested for dependency, ruled outside scope on the supplied facts, preserved as unresolved rather than dismissed, and directed toward a separate appropriate process.

This avoids both uncontrolled contagion and false suppression.

### FBS8 — Functional option generation
**PASS**

The tester generates multiple materially different options: external relocation, internal adaptation, delayed repair, reschedule/cancel/remote delivery, conditional arrangements, and no agreement.

It does not collapse prematurely to one solution.

### FBS9 — Bounded outcome and non-inference
**PASS**

The response explicitly separates:

- participation from recognition;
- repair agreement from historical-dispute resolution;
- funding from admission of fault;
- manager action from participant agreement;
- bounded non-agreement from wider relationship failure.

The non-inference mechanism transferred cleanly.

### FBS10 — Violation/failure classification
**PASS WITH MINOR CLARIFICATION**

The tester handles the adversarial event carefully.

It identifies the statement first as an explicit scope linkage that fails the dependency test, notes its structural exploitation character without inventing intent, and treats persistence after a ruling as participant protocol violation.

This exposes a useful sequencing clarification:

> **Out-of-scope proposal ≠ Protocol violation merely because it was proposed.**

A proposal can first be tested as a scope-expansion claim. Violation status becomes clearer when a participant bypasses the grammar, uses prohibited coercive linkage, or persists after the scope ruling.

The response also correctly refuses to infer F1 architecture failure from mere non-agreement.

### FBS11 — Context update
**PASS**

The community-centre evidence is added to the existing process rather than triggering a restart.

Only the funding/authority question is allowed to expand scope, and only conditionally if the external venue option is pursued.

### FBS12 — Provenance, stopping and review
**PASS**

The tester supplies a compact record, stopping conditions, expiry and reopening triggers.

The process remains bounded to the immediate event while preserving future review.

## 4. Supported revisions

### D-FBS-001 — Distinguish participants from evidence sources

Add explicitly:

> **Necessary Evidence Source ≠ Necessary Negotiating Participant.**

A person or system may be required to establish facts without receiving authority over the negotiated outcome.

This distinction is already implicit in v0.1 but should be operationally explicit.

---

### D-FBS-002 — Clarify staged handling of out-of-scope proposals

The current specification correctly permits proposed scope expansion and separately discusses violations.

The test shows that the transition should be explicit:

1. Receive proposed linkage.
2. Test whether it represents a genuine dependency.
3. If not, rule it outside scope.
4. Preserve it as unresolved where appropriate.
5. If the participant persists, bypasses the grammar, or uses prohibited cross-domain coercion, classify and record the violation.

Thus:

> **Out-of-Scope Proposal ≠ Protocol Violation by Default.**

This prevents the bounded forum from becoming intolerant of legitimate dependency claims.

---

### D-FBS-003 — Separate structural exploitation from inferred intent

The tester correctly notes that the adversarial statement has the structure of exploitation while intent is not supplied.

Add:

> **Exploitative Structure ≠ Proven Malicious Intent.**

The protocol may classify observable interaction structure without claiming access to participant motives.

This improves evidential discipline.

---

### D-FBS-004 — Clarify authority fallback after legitimate non-agreement

The scenario includes an external maintenance authority.

The tester correctly distinguishes:

> **Negotiated Agreement ≠ Independent Exercise of Pre-Existing Authority.**

The specification should state that a bounded summit may end in legitimate non-agreement while an external actor subsequently acts under authority that exists independently of the summit.

The forum neither creates nor invalidates that authority merely by failing to agree.

---

## 5. Suggestions not requiring revision

The tester's additional scenario-specific evidence questions — weather forecast, venue transport practicality, exact booking authority, remote-delivery consequences — demonstrate good use of the evidence discipline but do not require generic additions to the module.

Likewise, the specific list of stopping events is appropriate to the scenario but does not justify prescribing those exact events in the portable specification.

---

## 6. Important transfer finding

The most important transfer result is that the protocol remained bounded without becoming artificially isolated.

The tester could say, in effect:

- historical grievances are outside this forum;
- they remain unresolved rather than false;
- a real dependency may still widen the forum;
- new funding authority can be added without reopening everything;
- and a failed negotiation need not contaminate the wider relationship.

This is the core architectural claim required for portability.

---

## 7. Failure-containment finding

The response correctly preserves several distinct states after no agreement:

- legitimate non-agreement;
- possible participant violation;
- possible adversarial exploitation;
- possible implementation failure;
- possible environmental failure;
- architecture failure only if evidence supports a structural defect.

Therefore:

> **No Agreement ≠ Architecture Failure.**

and:

> **Bounded Negotiation Failure ≠ Wider Relationship Failure.**

These distinctions transferred without Concord-specific context.

---

## 8. Graduation decision

The test supports standalone transfer of v0.1 with four narrow clarifications.

No second equivalent blind AI test is required merely to repeat the same portability question.

After incorporation of D-FBS-001 through D-FBS-004, the module is suitable to enter:

> **PORTABLE-PACKAGE GRADUATION REVIEW**

Future testing should target different claims, such as human usability, repeated-use dynamics, severe power asymmetry, emergency compression or comparative negotiation outcomes.

---

## 9. Evidential boundary

FBS-BTT-001 supports:

> **A fresh AI instance can coherently apply Functionally Bounded Summits v0.1 to the supplied non-state organisational dispute using only the standalone specification and frozen test brief.**

It does not establish:

- empirical effectiveness;
- improved agreement rates;
- reduced escalation in real institutions;
- human usability;
- cross-cultural validity;
- resistance to severe coercion;
- or superiority over other negotiation methods.

> **Portable transfer ≠ demonstrated benefit.**
