## 4. Core portable mechanism

The source supports the following portable primitive:

> **Where a specific interaction problem can be meaningfully separated from a wider contested relationship, negotiate at the minimum functional scope needed to address that problem, expand scope only when a genuine dependency requires it, and presumptively contain success or failure to the scope actually negotiated.**

Compact form:

> **Bound the problem. Bound the negotiation. Bound the failure. Expand only when the function genuinely requires it.**

This mechanism does not require adoption of Concord governance, ethics, wrapper institutions or civilisational architecture.

---

## 5. Essential components

The following are source-supported and appear necessary to preserve in extraction:

1. **Problem definition** — identify the practical issue.
2. **Functional domain** — identify the function actually requiring negotiation.
3. **Minimum necessary participants** — include actors materially required to establish or operate that function.
4. **Relevant evidence** — define what information bears on the function.
5. **Bounded remit** — state what the forum may and may not decide.
6. **Interaction grammar** — agenda, decision rules, records, time horizon, pause/withdrawal conditions and other proportionate procedures.
7. **Explicit scope expansion** — dependencies may widen the remit only when demonstrated and recorded.
8. **Outcome classes** — accept, reject, modify, trial, conditional acceptance, request relevant evidence, or no agreement.
9. **Failure containment** — unresolved Domain A does not automatically contaminate Domains B–D.
10. **Violation separation** — distinguish negotiation failure from participant protocol violation.
11. **Failure classification** — architecture, implementation, participant violation, adversarial exploitation, environmental failure.
12. **Non-inference** — participation/agreement has only the implications explicitly established by the bounded forum.
13. **Bounded success** — success in the function does not imply wider reconciliation or authority.
14. **Anti-capture** — the forum does not acquire standing authority merely through use or success.
15. **Provenance** — record remit, participants, evidence, scope changes, outcomes, violations and non-inference conditions.
16. **Review/reuse** — recurring successful patterns may inform later protocols but do not automatically become permanent authority.

## 6. Core distinctions that must survive extraction

> **Functional Boundedness ≠ Functional Isolation**

> **Explicitly Agreed Scope Linkage ≠ Uncontrolled Negotiation Contagion**

> **Wrapper/Protocol Failure ≠ Participant Protocol Violation**

> **Repeated Participant Violation ≠ Architecturally Irrelevant**

> **Low General Trust ≠ Zero Functional Trust**

> **Functional Success ≠ General Agreement**

> **Forum Existence ≠ Standing Authority**

> **Successful Negotiation ≠ Permanent Jurisdiction**

These distinctions prevent the portable abstraction from becoming either unrealistically narrow or institutionally expansive.

## 7. Concord-specific elements to abstract

Several source terms should not become mandatory dependencies in the portable release.

### “Wrapper”
In the source, the summit sits within Concord wrapper architecture. The portable version can use **protocol**, **forum**, **interface** or **facilitating architecture** without requiring a Concord wrapper.

### “Ratchet”
The portable requirement is durable provenance, retrieval and review of prior bounded agreements. A user may implement this through any suitable recordkeeping/knowledge system.

### “Peaceful Distance”
This is an important Concord relationship but not a prerequisite for operation. The portable protocol can state the narrower function directly: participants need not resolve every wider disagreement in order to address one separable problem.

### “Authority Justification Chain / Minimum Necessary Capability”
These provide architectural analogies. The portable protocol needs its own minimum-negotiation rule and need not import the full Concord authority architecture.

### Concord as mediator
Not required. A third-party facilitator may be used only where participants authorise it under an independently legitimate scope.

## 8. Minimum standalone inputs

A portable implementation minimally requires:

- two or more participants with a shared or interacting practical problem;
- a problem capable of at least provisional functional separation;
- identifiable actors capable of negotiating or implementing the relevant function;
- enough information to propose an initial scope;
- willingness to use a bounded forum, even where wider trust is low.

The protocol is not applicable where the supposed functional domain cannot meaningfully be separated from the wider issue without hiding a necessary dependency.

---

## 9. Minimum standalone outputs

A bounded summit should produce a record containing:

- defined problem/function;
- authorised remit;
- participants/roles;
- relevant evidence or unresolved evidence needs;
- explicit dependencies and any scope changes;
- proposals considered;
- outcome;
- protocol violations where applicable;
- non-inference conditions;
- follow-up/review conditions;
- and a statement of what remains outside the result.

## 10. Failure states

The source supplies five useful failure classes:

1. **Architecture failure**
2. **Implementation failure**
3. **Participant protocol violation**
4. **Adversarial exploitation**
5. **Environmental failure**

These should be retained because “no agreement” alone is too coarse to diagnose whether the protocol itself failed.

A sixth operational condition should remain distinct from failure:

**Legitimate non-agreement** — the process worked as intended but participants did not find mutually acceptable terms.

This is an extraction clarification rather than a claim that the source treated all no-agreement outcomes as failures; the source explicitly permits rejection/no agreement.

## 12. Portability finding

The mechanism is more portable than its intercivilisational framing suggests.

Its abstract problem is:

> **How can participants with a wider contested relationship solve one separable practical problem without requiring the forum to absorb the whole relationship or allowing failure to contaminate unrelated functions?**

This can potentially apply to:

- organisations;
- institutions;
- federated systems;
- communities;
- technical consortia;
- multi-agent systems;
- jurisdictions;
- or civilisations.

This broader portability is an extraction hypothesis to be tested. It is not yet demonstrated.

---

## 13. Dependency finding

No Concord-specific architectural dependency appears necessary for the core protocol.

The portable package should remain compatible with external:

- governance systems;
- legal systems;
- recordkeeping systems;
- mediation systems;
- authority structures;
- and dispute-resolution systems.

The protocol determines **how to bound the negotiation**, not who ultimately possesses legitimate authority in every external context.

---

## 14. Evidential status

The source is a developed candidate architecture, not an empirically validated negotiation method.

The extraction may preserve claims such as:

- the protocol is designed to reduce uncontrolled issue linkage;
- it attempts to contain failure;
- it makes scope expansion explicit;
- it separates functional agreement from wider political inference.

It should not claim without later evidence that it:

- improves agreement rates;
- prevents escalation;
- outperforms general negotiation;
- neutralises power asymmetry;
- eliminates coercion;
- or is suitable for every low-trust conflict.
