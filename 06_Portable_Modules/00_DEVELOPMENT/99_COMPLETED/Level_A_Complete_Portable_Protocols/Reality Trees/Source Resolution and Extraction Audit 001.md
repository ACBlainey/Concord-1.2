## 3. Core source definition

A Reality Tree is a representation of multiple possible realities, hypotheses, explanations, consequences or pathways arising from a present state.

Its purpose is not to determine truth by visualisation. It is to:

- make alternatives explicit;
- preserve uncertainty;
- reduce premature commitment;
- expose assumptions and causal relations;
- support investigation and testing;
- retain unsuccessful branches as information;
- and revise the represented possibility space as evidence changes.

The source's governing epistemic boundary is:

> **Reality remains the final arbiter.**

A branch is therefore a possibility to investigate, not automatically a conclusion.

---

## 4. Minimum supported protocol

The source gives an explicit eight-step method:

1. **Identify the present state.**
2. **Generate possible explanations, consequences, or pathways.**
3. **Branch where meaningful alternatives exist.**
4. **Examine the assumptions and causal relationships behind each branch.**
5. **Assess evidence, uncertainty, benefits, harms, and possible failure modes.**
6. **Test promising branches where practical.**
7. **Retain the results, including unsuccessful branches.**
8. **Refine the model as new evidence becomes available.**

This is the minimum portable procedural core. Packaging should preserve it rather than replace it with a larger invented workflow.

---

## 5. Confidence weighting

The source permits branches to receive **confidence weighting**.

The weighting is explicitly not necessarily a mathematical probability. It may represent the degree to which a branch is currently believed, supported or considered plausible.

Therefore:

> **Confidence weighting ≠ objective probability.**

A portable package must not silently convert subjective or evidence-relative confidence into probabilistic precision.

Weights may change as evidence, reasoning or discussion develops.

---

## 6. Branch epistemic status

The source allows a branch to be:

- strongly believed;
- weakly believed;
- uncertain;
- plausible but unsupported;
- not believed;
- actively dismissed;
- or retained as a devil's-advocate branch.

A branch need not be endorsed in order to be represented.

The devil's-advocate function is:

> **“If this were true, what would follow?”**

This establishes an important boundary:

> **Representation ≠ endorsement.**

and:

> **Possibility ≠ truth.**

---

## 7. Belief-to-hypothesis bridge

Reality Trees originated partly as a personal method for separating belief from certainty.

The portable function is broader: competing interpretations can remain visible while confidence differs between them.

A branch may move from personal belief or speculative possibility toward formal hypothesis only through additional examination and evidence. The tree itself does not validate that transition.

---

## 8. Testing hierarchy

The source extends the same branch logic into:

> **Speculation → Simulation → Limited Pilot → Real-World Branch → Knowledge Integration**

This is a supported experimental hierarchy, but it is not mandatory for every Reality Tree.

A simple cognitive Reality Tree may never progress beyond conceptual examination.

Where testing does occur, stronger and more consequential tests should follow appropriate evidence, safeguards and domain methods.

---

## 9. Failure as information

The source treats unsuccessful branches as potentially informative.

Where experimental branches are appropriately isolated, failure can produce local learning rather than requiring system-wide commitment.

This supports:

> **Branch failure ≠ tree failure.**

However, Reality Trees do not themselves supply the governance, safety or authority architecture needed to authorise consequential real-world experiments.

---

## 10. Documentation and provenance

Where branches develop into real-world experiments, the source calls for preservation, where practical, of:

- provenance;
- assumptions and methodology;
- reasoning and experimental rationale;
- confidence assessments;
- measured outcomes;
- unintended consequences;
- successful discoveries;
- unsuccessful discoveries;
- lessons learned.

This documentation function is part of the method's learning loop.

---

## 11. Relationship to Blaineyan Reasoning

The source states that Reality Trees form part of the broader Blaineyan Reasoning approach and can support:

- hidden-assumption exposure;
- competing explanations;
- failure-mode analysis;
- abstraction movement;
- consequence exploration;
- resistance to premature conclusions;
- research-question generation;
- progressive pruning.

The portable boundary should remain:

> **Blaineyan Reasoning → may use → Reality Trees**

Reality Trees are a branch-representation and exploration method. They do not reproduce the whole Blaineyan Reasoning cycle.

In particular, the Reality Trees core should not import the complete Blaineyan Reasoning machinery for recursive abstraction, Scout/Cartographer roles, full epistemic labelling, anticipatory reasoning, specialist handoff or its larger stopping procedure unless independently supported by the Reality Trees source.

Conversely, the two completed Blaineyan Reasoning blind tests demonstrate that Blaineyan Reasoning can operate without the Reality Trees specification. Reality Trees are therefore useful but non-mandatory infrastructure for Blaineyan Reasoning.

## 13. Minimum standalone inputs

A Reality Tree minimally requires:

1. a present state, observation, question or uncertainty;
2. at least two materially distinct possibilities, explanations, consequences or pathways worth representing.

Useful but non-mandatory inputs include:

- evidence;
- assumptions;
- causal claims;
- confidence assessments;
- known constraints;
- testing opportunities.

---

## 14. Minimum standalone branch representation

The source does not mandate a rigid visual notation.

A minimum portable branch record can therefore remain simple:

| Branch | Possibility / pathway | Current confidence or status | Key assumptions | Evidence / uncertainty | Consequences / failure modes | Test or next examination | Current disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The visual tree form is useful but not mandatory. A table, graph or nested text representation may instantiate the same method if branch relationships remain visible.

This representation is a packaging abstraction of source-supported elements, not a claim that the source prescribed this exact table.

---

## 15. Minimum outputs

A completed bounded Reality Tree cycle should leave:

- the present state or root question;
- the branches considered;
- branch relationships where relevant;
- assumptions and causal claims examined;
- current evidence and uncertainty;
- optional confidence/status assessments;
- identified consequences, benefits, harms or failure modes where relevant;
- branches selected for testing or further investigation;
- pruned, rejected, deferred or unresolved branches retained with reasons where practical;
- results from any tests performed;
- an updated tree/model when evidence changes.

---

## 16. Failure and misuse modes supported or directly implied by the source

### Premature collapse
Selecting one branch as certain before alternatives have been adequately examined.

### False equivalence
Treating all represented branches as equally plausible merely because all are visible.

### Confidence-as-probability error
Presenting subjective confidence weights as objective mathematical probabilities.

### Branch proliferation
Generating possibilities without proportional examination or testing.

### Exploration-as-endorsement
Treating a devil's-advocate or speculative branch as the user's belief.

### Visualisation-as-proof
Treating the tree itself as evidence that a branch is true.

### Failure erasure
Deleting unsuccessful branches and losing what was learned.

### Evidence resistance
Protecting a preferred branch from revision when reality contradicts it.

### Unsafe escalation
Moving from speculation to consequential real-world experimentation without appropriate domain safeguards, authority or specialist methods.

---

## 17. Stopping condition

The source does not specify a formal stopping algorithm.

A conservative portable stopping rule can be inferred from its bounded purpose:

A Reality Tree cycle may stop when the current meaningful alternatives have been represented sufficiently for the immediate question, promising branches have identifiable next examinations or tests, unsupported branches have a recorded disposition, and further expansion would add no material value without new evidence.

This is a packaging clarification, not a source quotation.

The tree remains revisable when new evidence appears.

The canonical source is unusually close to a finished portable module already.

No blocking conceptual omission was identified for packaging.

The work required is therefore primarily:

The method should **not** be expanded into a general decision-tree, forecasting, Bayesian, causal-inference or governance framework without separate evidence and development.
