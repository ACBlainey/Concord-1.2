# Contextual Wrapper Architecture — Source Resolution and Extraction Audit 001

**Candidate:** Contextual Wrapper Architecture (CWA)  
**Development level:** Level B — Extractable Architectures  
**Audit status:** COMPLETED — SOURCE RESOLUTION SUFFICIENT / PORTABLE CORE IDENTIFIED / UNIVERSALITY CLAIM REMAINS UNVALIDATED  
**Date:** September 2026

---

## 1. Audit purpose

Determine whether the Concord's Fractal Contextual Wrapper architecture contains a coherent portable mechanism that can be extracted without requiring adoption of Concord constitutional institutions, civilisational wrappers or domain-specific law.

The candidate function identified during the portable-module scan was:

> **A bounded-interface grammar for making changes in rules, rights, responsibilities, permissions, risks, roles and expectations legible without requiring uniform internal systems.**

This audit tests that proposition against the developed source architecture.

---

## 2. Primary source

Primary source:

`11_Intercivilisational_Architecture/01_Concord_Civilisational_Wrapper/01_Core_Wrapper_Architecture/Fractal Contextual Wrappers — A Universal Concord Grammar for Bounded Rule, Behavioural and Jurisdictional Variation.md`

The source defines a contextual wrapper as:

> **A bounded interface architecture that makes a material change in rules, rights, responsibilities, permissions, risks, roles, expectations or behavioural norms legible to participants while preserving the relationship between the bounded context and its surrounding civil framework.**

The source distinguishes:

- **Contextual Wrapper** — primarily defines a bounded environment in which conditions differ from the surrounding environment;
- **Interoperability Wrapper** — primarily defines how distinct bounded systems interact;
- **Hybrid Wrapper** — performs both functions.

This portable candidate concerns the first of these.

## 4. Problem being solved

The source identifies a recurring structural problem:

> **How can a bounded environment legitimately operate under materially different conditions from its surroundings while remaining intelligible, responsible and connected to the wider system?**

Examples in the source range from:

- boxing rings;
- sports venues;
- laboratories;
- workplaces;
- medical environments;
- Safe Spaces;
- special districts;
- digital environments;
- local jurisdictions;
- civilisational boundaries.

The substantive rules differ dramatically.

The hypothesised reusable element is the **grammar by which material contextual difference is made legible and bounded**.

---

## 5. Portable primitive

The portable primitive can be stated without Concord-specific institutions:

> **Where a participant enters, occupies or acts within a bounded context whose material rules, permissions, restrictions, risks, roles, responsibilities or authority differ from the surrounding/default context, make the boundary and material variation sufficiently legible, establish the applicable legitimate basis, bound context-specific permissions and authority, protect affected nonparticipants, provide appropriate remedy/exit/emergency handling, and terminate the contextual variation when its legitimate context ends.**

A shorter form is:

> **Standardise the interface more strongly than the outcome.**

The objective is not uniform substantive rules.

It is **comprehensible difference**.

---

## 6. Core grammar

The source supplies the following universal-wrapper grammar:

**Identify Context**  
→ **Identify Boundary**  
→ **Identify Participant / Role**  
→ **Identify Applicable Wider Framework**  
→ **Identify Material Variation**  
→ **Communicate Material Variation**  
→ **Warn Where Necessary**  
→ **Establish Consent or Other Legitimate Basis**  
→ **Activate Contextual Permissions / Restrictions**  
→ **Identify Responsibility and Local Authority**  
→ **Operate Within Context**  
→ **Provide Remedy / Emergency Override**  
→ **Permit Withdrawal / Exit Where Applicable**  
→ **Terminate Context**  
→ **Revert to Surrounding Norms**  
→ **Record / Review Where Necessary**

For portability, Concord's Ratchet can be abstracted to **evidence-responsive review and revision**.

## 7. Irreducible distinctions

### Context ≠ physical place

> **Physical Boundary ≠ Complete Contextual Boundary**

A context may begin before a physical threshold, continue across several locations, be digital, be role-defined or be activity-defined.

### Contextual permission ≠ general permission

> **Contextual Permission ≠ General Permission**

A permission legitimate inside one context does not automatically survive outside it.

### Presence ≠ role permission

> **Presence in Context ≠ Possession of Every Contextual Permission**

Different participants may occupy the same context under different roles.

### Shared context ≠ shared consent

> **Shared Context ≠ Shared Consent**

Consent, exposure, responsibility and permission can be role-specific.

### Bounded context ≠ sovereignty

> **Bounded Context ≠ Sovereign Jurisdiction**

> **Contextual Authority ≠ Political Sovereignty**

A local interface does not manufacture general political authority.

### Boundary ≠ externality boundary

> **Contextual Boundary ≠ Externality Boundary**

Consequences can escape the formal context.

### Formal exit ≠ meaningful exit

> **Formal Exit ≠ Meaningful Exit**

Nominal ability to leave may not amount to practical ability to withdraw.

### Declared context ≠ reality

> **Declared Context ≠ Observed Reality**

Signage or metadata cannot establish facts merely by declaring them.

## 8. Material-variation test

Not every difference requires a full wrapper.

The architecture should activate proportionately where the context materially changes one or more of:

- rules;
- rights/protections;
- responsibilities;
- permissions;
- restrictions;
- risks;
- participant roles;
- authority;
- privacy/data conditions;
- behavioural expectations;
- remedy;
- exit;
- exposure to external consequences.

The information burden should scale with material difference and risk.

> **Information Burden Should Be Proportionate to Material Contextual Difference and Risk.**

This prevents the wrapper itself becoming unusable through information overload.

---

## 9. Legibility interface

The source proposes a reusable information grammar.

A participant entering a context should, where materially relevant, be able to determine:

- What context am I entering?
- What changes here?
- What remains protected or unchanged?
- What role do I have?
- What permissions do I gain?
- What restrictions apply?
- What risks materially change?
- Who has authority here?
- What authority do they actually possess?
- Who is responsible if something goes wrong?
- How do I withdraw?
- What happens in an emergency?
- How do I seek remedy?

The content is context-specific.

The interface grammar can remain familiar.

---

## 10. Domain-specific rules remain necessary

The source explicitly rejects replacing substantive rules with the wrapper grammar.

> **Common Grammar ≠ Elimination of Domain-Specific Rules**

Boxing still needs boxing rules. Laboratories need safety rules. Workplaces need actual procedures. Digital systems need actual permission schemas.

Therefore the portable wrapper should expose and organise material rules; it should not pretend to generate them.

---

## 11. Legitimate basis and consent

Where voluntary entry materially changes conditions, boundary crossing can be consent-relevant.

A useful chain is:

**Approach Context**  
→ **Recognise Boundary**  
→ **Receive Relevant Information**  
→ **Understand Material Variation**  
→ **Receive Proportionate Warning Where Required**  
→ **Choose**  
→ **Enter / Participate** or **Decline / Avoid**

But:

> **Consent Activates Permissible Variation; Consent Does Not Manufacture Legitimacy for Impermissible Variation.**

And:

> **Clear Signage ≠ Legitimate Rule**

> **Entry ≠ Waiver of Fundamental Rights**

For portability, the applicable wider normative/legal framework supplies the legitimate bounds. The wrapper does not create legitimacy by presentation alone.

---

## 12. Roles and contextual authority

Where local authority is required:

**Contextual Problem / Function**  
→ **Need for Local Authority**  
→ **Minimum Necessary Authority**  
→ **Role-Bounded Authority**  
→ **Context-Bounded Authority**  
→ **Authority Ends When Function / Context Ends**

Core boundary:

> **Contextual Authority Should Not Escape Its Context Without Independent Justification.**

The portable wrapper need not implement the complete Minimum Necessary Capability module, but it must record:

- what authority exists;
- who holds it;
- for what function;
- over whom/what;
- when it activates;
- and when it ends.

---

## 13. Temporal boundaries

Contextual variation may be temporary.

The architecture should make material context start and end conditions legible.

> **Context Start and Context End Should Be Legible Where Material.**

At context termination:

- temporary permissions end;
- temporary restrictions end where appropriate;
- contextual authority ends unless independently justified;
- surrounding/default norms resume.

This is a major anti-creep mechanism.

---

## 14. Nested contexts

Contexts may be nested.

Example structure:

**Wider System → Facility → Restricted Area → Activity → Special Event**

A participant may simultaneously occupy several contexts.

The wrapper therefore requires explicit identification of:

- applicable layers;
- material rules at each layer;
- inherited rules;
- overridden defaults;
- and conflict/priority rules.

The source does not yet provide a universally validated precedence algorithm.

That limitation must remain visible.

---

## 15. Overlapping contexts and composition

Contexts can overlap without clean nesting.

The source example is a medical emergency during a boxing match:

**Sporting Context + Medical Emergency Context → Temporary Composite State**

Therefore:

> **Wrapper Composition Requires Explicit Conflict and Priority Rules.**

A portable implementation must not assume that the newest, smallest or most local wrapper automatically wins.

Conflict resolution must come from the applicable legitimate wider framework.

---

## 16. Emergency override

A bounded context should not make participants unreachable by legitimate emergency action merely because local rules exist.

> **Contextual Consent ≠ Irrevocable Exposure to Harm**

Emergency override must itself remain:

- justified;
- bounded;
- role/function-specific;
- reviewable;
- temporary where appropriate.

The contextual wrapper exposes the emergency relationship; it does not supply unlimited emergency authority.

---

## 17. Withdrawal and exit

Where participation is voluntary and withdrawal is applicable, the wrapper should identify:

- how to withdraw;
- what conditions apply;
- what consequences follow;
- what assistance/remedy exists;
- whether exit is practically meaningful.

> **Formal Exit ≠ Meaningful Exit**

Some contexts, especially employment, residence, custody, dependency or essential infrastructure, may not permit simple immediate exit. The architecture should expose that condition rather than falsely label participation voluntary.

---

## 18. Nonparticipants

Context-specific permissions do not automatically apply to people merely because they are nearby or affected.

The wrapper should distinguish:

- participants;
- role-holders;
- observers;
- adjacent nonparticipants;
- third parties affected by externalities.

> **Contextual Permission Ends Where Its Legitimate Scope Ends.**

This prevents local permission from being projected onto people who did not receive, accept or legitimately fall under it.

---

## 19. Externalities

A formal wrapper cannot contain all consequences.

Potential externalities include:

- physical harm;
- noise;
- pollution;
- financial liability;
- disease;
- data flows;
- infrastructure effects;
- reputational effects;
- cross-system dependencies.

> **Contextual Boundary ≠ Externality Boundary**

The portable wrapper must identify material effects that cross its boundary and the responsible interface for handling them.

---

## 20. Human-readable and machine-readable equivalence

The source explicitly extends contextual legibility into digital and AI environments.

A wrapper may have:

- human-readable signs/instructions;
- machine-readable contextual metadata;
- or both.

Where both exist, they should represent the same underlying context.

A critical failure mode is divergence between human-readable and machine-readable rules.

For portability:

> **Different Interface Representations Should Not Silently Encode Different Contexts.**

This is particularly relevant to AI agents operating across changing permission/risk environments.

---

## 21. Reality correction

A wrapper is an interface description, not proof that the described environment is real.

> **Declared Context ≠ Observed Reality**

A system claiming “safe environment” or “all participants consent” must remain corrigible by evidence.

Portable review chain:

**Context Rule / Declaration**  
→ **Observed Outcome / Evidence**  
→ **Discrepancy or Harm**  
→ **Review**  
→ **Rule / Interface Revision**  
→ **Re-evaluation**

## 25. Enumeration failure

A critical source warning is:

> **Universal Grammar Must Not Replace Necessary Specificity.**

The wrapper should reduce structural duplication.

It should not reduce an actual rule to a vague category that participants cannot act on.

This is particularly important for portable extraction: portability must not be achieved by abstracting away the substantive information required for safe participation.

## 28. Portability finding

The contextual-wrapper architecture appears strongly portable as a **legibility and bounded-context architecture**.

Its portable value does not depend on whether the source's stronger “universal civil grammar” hypothesis is ultimately true.

A weaker, testable claim is sufficient:

> **The same structured interface may be reusable across materially different bounded contexts even when their substantive rules differ.**

Candidate application domains include:

- workplaces;
- laboratories;
- sports;
- events;
- digital platforms;
- AI-agent environments;
- controlled data spaces;
- temporary project teams;
- shared facilities;
- special-access areas;
- multi-organisation collaborations.

These are candidate use domains, not validated domains.

---

## 29. Universality hypothesis must remain separate

The source proposes:

> **A sufficiently general contextual-wrapper grammar may describe a large proportion of legitimate bounded variation in law, behaviour, risk, authority and social expectation across a multisubstrate civilisation without requiring every context to possess an unrelated interface architecture.**

This is explicitly a research hypothesis.

Portable extraction must not upgrade it into an established result.

The hypothesis weakens if:

- domains require incompatible grammars;
- standardisation increases confusion;
- users ignore contextual information;
- wrapper overhead exceeds benefits;
- material local differences are dangerously simplified;
- machine-readable representation creates capture risk;
- wrapper boundaries manufacture false consent;
- or the architecture creates more ambiguity than domain-specific approaches.

---

## 30. Evidential boundary

The source architecture is highly developed conceptually but remains developmental.

The portable package may claim to provide a structured method for making bounded contextual variation legible.

It may not yet claim that contextual wrappers:

- improve compliance;
- improve safety;
- create meaningful consent;
- reduce disputes;
- outperform domain-specific interfaces;
- work universally;
- are equally understandable to humans and AI;
- or solve rule precedence automatically.

Those require additional evidence.

---

## 31. Audit conclusion

**SOURCE RESOLUTION SUFFICIENT FOR STANDALONE SPECIFICATION.**

**PORTABLE CORE IDENTIFIED.**

**NO BLOCKING SOURCE OMISSION IDENTIFIED.**

However, the source's **universality hypothesis remains unvalidated** and must not be bundled into portable graduation as though demonstrated.

The portable core is:

> **Make material contextual difference legible; preserve the surrounding/default framework; distinguish roles; establish legitimate contextual variation; bound permissions, restrictions and authority; protect nonparticipants; expose externalities; provide emergency/remedy/exit relationships; terminate contextual variation when its basis ends; and remain corrigible to evidence.**

The next artifact should be:

> **Contextual Wrapper Architecture — Portable Specification v0.1**

A blind transfer test should deliberately choose a non-Concord context unlike the source's recurrent boxing/jurisdiction examples and should include:

1. multiple participant roles;
2. a material rule change;
3. a participant who does not share another role's consent;
4. a nested or overlapping context;
5. an externality escaping the formal boundary;
6. a machine-readable/human-readable mismatch or equivalent interface inconsistency;
7. and a context ending so reversion can be tested.

> **Common Grammar ≠ Uniform Rules.**

> **Portable extraction ≠ universal validation.**
