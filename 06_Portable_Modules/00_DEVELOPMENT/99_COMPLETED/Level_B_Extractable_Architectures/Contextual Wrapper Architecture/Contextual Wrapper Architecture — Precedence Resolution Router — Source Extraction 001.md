## 1. Purpose

This document extracts a portable **Precedence Resolution Router (PRR)** from existing Concord architecture.

The router addresses a recurring CWA problem:

> When multiple legitimate contexts, rules, functions, protections or authorities appear to require incompatible action, what should the wrapper do?

The source material does **not** support a single universal rule declaring which context always wins.

Instead, it supports a routing architecture that:

1. identifies the actual decision conflict;
2. separates different authority and competence topologies;
3. checks higher-order constraints;
4. activates bounded protective or emergency functions where justified;
5. decomposes complex decisions;
6. applies explicit local precedence where legitimate;
7. routes legal or jurisdictional questions to legitimate resolvers;
8. preserves unresolved states where no legitimate resolution basis is established.

The router is therefore an **interface and classification mechanism**, not a sovereign decision-maker.

# 3. Core source principles

The following principles constrain the router.

> **Priority of Function ≠ Superiority of Participant**

> **Authority follows the function relevant to the decision, not merely the participant highest on the organisational chart.**

> **Reporting Authority ≠ Decision Competence**

> **Decision Competence ≠ Universal Decision Sovereignty**

> **Protective Authority Need Not Be Delegated by the Authority It Constrains**

> **Functional Conflict Should Trigger Resolution Architecture, Not Automatic Rank Supremacy**

> **Emergencies change operational priorities. They do not change constitutional principles.**

> **Emergency Need ≠ Unlimited Emergency Authority**

> **Crisis must never manufacture authority.**

> **Standing to Raise ≠ Authority to Decide**

> **The existence of an externality does not create jurisdiction.**

> **Information Architecture ≠ Decision Authority**

> **Schema Authority ≠ Sovereignty**

These collectively prohibit several false shortcuts.

# 4. What the router must not do

The PRR MUST NOT assume:

- highest-ranked participant wins;
- most technically capable participant wins;
- expert judgement creates general authority;
- emergency automatically overrides everything;
- newest context wins;
- smallest nested context wins;
- parent context always wins;
- child context always wins;
- most restrictive rule wins;
- local rule can automatically override protected invariants;
- harm automatically creates jurisdiction;
- ability to resolve a conflict creates authority to resolve it;
- CWA itself becomes the adjudicator.

A result of **UNKNOWN**, **DISPUTED**, or **REQUIRES EXTERNAL RESOLUTION** is preferable to fabricated precedence.

# 5. Decision object first

Before comparing authorities, identify the actual **decision object**.

A conflict that appears to be:

> Manager versus Engineer

may actually contain several different decisions:

- Is the machine physically safe?
- Is production allowed to resume?
- What remediation is required?
- Who coordinates staff while work is paused?
- Does the event require reporting?
- Is a participant entitled to refuse?
- Who reviews the decision later?

These questions may legitimately belong to different functions.

Therefore:

> **Apparent Actor Conflict May Be Multiple Decision Objects**

The router SHOULD decompose a conflict before attempting global ranking.

---

# 6. Authority topology separation

For each decision object, distinguish where relevant:

- **Reporting topology** — who coordinates whom?
- **Competence topology** — who has relevant knowledge/capability?
- **Responsibility topology** — who carries the relevant duty?
- **Decision-jurisdiction topology** — who is authorised to decide this class of question?
- **Protective-authority topology** — who may stop/refuse/intervene?
- **Functional-priority topology** — which legitimate function controls this decision under these conditions?
- **Review topology** — who can review the exercise?
- **Escalation topology** — where does unresolved disagreement go?

These topologies may point to different participants.

That is not automatically an architectural contradiction.

# 7. Precedence Resolution Router

The source-derived router is:

**Conflict Detected**

↓

### Gate 1 — Is the conflict real and material?

Identify:

- contexts involved;
- rules/permissions/protections involved;
- participants/nonparticipants affected;
- actual incompatible requirements;
- evidence state;
- uncertainty.

If no material incompatibility exists:

**NO PRECEDENCE CONFLICT**

If uncertain:

**POTENTIAL CONFLICT / EVIDENCE REQUIRED**

↓

### Gate 2 — Decompose the decision

Ask:

> **What exact decision or action is contested?**

If the apparent conflict contains multiple decision objects, separate them.

Each object proceeds independently through the remaining gates.

↓

### Gate 3 — Is an applicable protected invariant or higher-order constraint engaged?

Check for applicable:

- fundamental sentient rights;
- bodily autonomy;
- consent;
- safeguarding;
- constitutional protections;
- due process;
- protected privacy;
- prevention of serious harm;
- other established high-order constraints.

If YES:

**Protected Constraint Bounds Conflicting Contextual Authority**

This does not elevate the protected participant into a superior person or general authority holder.

↓

### Gate 4 — Has a legitimate protective threshold activated?

If there is a credible immediate or sufficiently serious threat to safety, rights or another protected interest, determine whether the applicable architecture recognises Protective Authority.

If YES:

**Protective Function Activates**

→ **Minimum Necessary Refusal / Stop / Escape / Warning / Intervention**

→ **Conflicting Ordinary Function May Pause Where Necessary**

→ **Escalate to Relevant Competent Function**

Protective action remains bounded and reviewable.

↓

### Gate 5 — Is this an emergency context?

If YES, determine:

- emergency trigger;
- emergency function;
- authority basis;
- minimum necessary capability;
- retained constitutional/protected constraints;
- temporary deviation required;
- termination/reversion condition.

Apply:

> **Emergencies change operational priorities. They do not change constitutional principles.**

and:

> **Minimum Constitutional Deviation**

Emergency classification does not activate an unrestricted package of powers.

↓

### Gate 6 — Which legitimate function owns the decision?

For the specific decision object ask:

- Which function is responsible?
- Which function has decision jurisdiction?
- What competence is required?
- Is the competent participant also authorised to decide?
- Is another function responsible for coordination or implementation?

Apply:

> **Reporting Authority ≠ Decision Competence**

and:

> **Decision Competence ≠ Universal Decision Sovereignty**

Where a legitimate functional priority is already established:

**Apply Function-Bounded Priority**

not personal hierarchy.

↓

### Gate 7 — Is there an explicit contextual/domain precedence rule?

Examples may include:

- explicit additive inheritance;
- defined laboratory clearance relationships;
- constitutional constraint on local rules;
- recognised safety-stop priority;
- agreed contractual or procedural priority;
- valid emergency procedure.

If YES:

**Apply Explicit Rule Within Its Legitimate Scope**

Do not generalise it to sibling contexts or unrelated decisions.

↓

### Gate 8 — Is the conflict jurisdictional or adjudicative?

If the issue concerns:

- competing legal rules;
- constitutional interpretation;
- disputed rights;
- conflicting judgements;
- competing jurisdiction;
- a matter requiring adjudication;

identify whether a legitimate resolver already exists.

If YES:

**Route to Legitimate Resolver**

CWA records the interface, authority basis and result.

CWA does not become the resolver.

↓

### Gate 9 — Is jurisdiction shared, overlapping, disputed or absent?

Where the conflict crosses boundaries, separately map:

- consequence;
- responsibility;
- jurisdiction;
- coordination possibility;
- remedy path.

Apply:

> **The existence of an externality does not create jurisdiction.**

Possible outcomes include:

- INTERNAL-JURISDICTION;
- SHARED/AGREED-JURISDICTION;
- OVERLAPPING-JURISDICTION;
- JURISDICTION-DISPUTED;
- NO-SHARED-JURISDICTION;
- VOLUNTARY-COORDINATION-AVAILABLE;
- OPTIONAL-MEDIATION-AVAILABLE;
- EMERGENCY-CONTACT-ONLY;
- PEACEFUL-DISTANCE / NO-FURTHER-PROCESS;
- UNKNOWN.

↓

### Gate 10 — Can minimum temporary action preserve safety while resolution remains open?

Where delay itself creates material risk, determine whether an already legitimate protective/emergency function permits a bounded temporary action.

The action MUST NOT acquire greater authority merely because the final dispute remains unresolved.

Record:

- action;
- authority basis;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- expected duration;
- reversibility;
- affected rights;
- review requirement.

↓

### Gate 11 — Is a legitimate resolver established?

If YES:

**Resolver Authority Verified**

→ **External Resolution**

→ **Basis / Decision / Scope Recorded**

→ **Affected Contexts Updated**

→ **Review / Appeal / Correction Where Applicable**

If NO:

**UNKNOWN / DISPUTED / REQUIRES EXTERNAL RESOLUTION**

Do not invent a winner.

↓

### Gate 12 — Termination and reversion

When temporary priority, protective authority or emergency authority ends:

- deactivate function-derived authority where its basis ends;
- restore surrounding/default context where appropriate;
- preserve records and responsibility;
- inspect residual permissions/capabilities/data/processes;
- propagate revocation where required;
- review consequences;
- feed learning into Ratchet/KCS where applicable.

# 10. Application to the HRDC emergency

The BTT-001 emergency can now be represented without inventing a universal winner.

The apparent collision contains at least:

- immediate medical treatment;
- L3 biological safety;
- emergency evacuation/fire response;
- security/escort;
- maintenance diagnosis;
- automated fire-system operation.

These are not one decision.

The router decomposes them.

### Medical urgency

The paramedic's function may have priority concerning urgent medical treatment if the relevant emergency/protective threshold and authority basis are established.

This does not make the paramedic the general controller of the laboratory.

### Biological safety

The L3 supervisor/safety function may retain priority concerning biological containment/PPE requirements where those requirements remain compatible with necessary emergency treatment.

This does not make the supervisor the final medical decision-maker.

### Fire system

Door unlocking changes physical reachability/access-control state.

It does not by itself establish universal permission or authority.

### Maintenance engineer

Technical competence concerning the ventilation controller may be relevant.

Competence alone does not establish L3 entry authority.

### Security

Escort responsibility may remain applicable unless legitimately displaced or modified by the emergency architecture.

### Residual conflict

If the supplied rules do not establish how immediate medical entry interacts with L3 PPE/escort requirements, the correct state remains:

**REQUIRES EXTERNAL RESOLUTION / PRIORITY RULE NOT ESTABLISHED IN SUPPLIED CONTEXT**

The router has nevertheless reduced the conflict by separating decision objects and legitimate functions.

# 11. Why this is not a hierarchy

The PRR does not create a ladder such as:

**Rights > Safety > Emergency > Expertise > Management > Local Rules**

Such a ladder would be unsupported and would erase context.

Instead, the router asks:

> **Which legitimate constraint or function applies to this decision under these conditions, and what is its bounded scope?**

A safety function may control whether a machine is safe to operate while management retains coordination responsibility.

A clinician may control a medical determination while a laboratory specialist controls a containment determination.

A court may resolve a legal conflict without acquiring operational control of the institution.

A constitutional right may constrain all of them without turning its holder into their superior.

---

# 12. Failure modes

The PRR fails if it:

1. converts function into permanent rank;
2. converts expertise into sovereignty;
3. treats emergency as unrestricted supremacy;
4. treats rights protection as personal hierarchy;
5. confuses reporting authority with decision jurisdiction;
6. confuses technical competence with legitimate authority;
7. creates jurisdiction from harm or externality;
8. creates adjudicative authority from descriptive capability;
9. applies a local precedence rule outside its scope;
10. refuses to preserve UNKNOWN/DISPUTED states;
11. creates paralysis by requiring unnecessary resolution before legitimate protective action;
12. allows temporary authority to persist after its basis ends;
13. hides responsibility during multi-function conflict;
14. collapses several decision objects into a false winner-takes-all dispute.

# 14. Source-status statement

The PRR is not presented as a newly invented universal precedence doctrine.

It is an extraction and formalisation of relationships already present across the reviewed Concord architecture.

The principal new work in this document is **integration**:

- bringing dispersed precedence mechanisms into one portable routing sequence;
- preserving their different legitimacy bases;
- preventing them from being collapsed into a universal hierarchy;
- making unresolved state an explicit valid outcome.

Under ESCP, future source discovery may modify or extend the router.

---

# 15. Integration recommendation

The PRR should be integrated into CWA as a **subroutine/interface**, not as the entire purpose of the module.

CWA remains responsible for making bounded contextual difference legible.

When contextual relationships produce a material conflict, CWA invokes the PRR.

The relationship is:

**CWA Context Map**  
→ **Conflict Detected**  
→ **PRR**  
→ **Resolved / Routed / Temporarily Bounded / Unknown**  
→ **CWA Context State Updated**

This preserves separation of concerns while preventing CWA from stopping at conflict detection when existing Concord architecture already supplies a legitimate routing path.
