**Test ID:** CWA-BTT-003  
**Target:** Contextual Wrapper Architecture — Portable Specification v0.3  
**Test type:** Focused blind delta test  
**Status:** FROZEN TEST BRIEF

---

# 1. Purpose

BTT-003 tests the v0.3 delta introduced after BTT-002:

- Function-Derived Permission;
- Minimum Necessary Permission;
- implied permission;
- action-specific permission;
- permission provenance;
- reasonableness / anti-self-justification;
- function-completion termination;
- the Permission-Before-Authority Gate.

This is not a repeat of BTT-002.

The test asks whether the new permission layer improves ordinary contextual routing without:

- manufacturing permission from self-declared need;
- weakening private-space protection;
- converting permission into authority/control;
- treating physical accessibility as legitimacy;
- over-expanding implied permission;
- confusing permission expiry with remedy authority.

# 3. Scenario — Cedar Court Mixed-Use Estate

Cedar Court is a privately owned mixed-use estate containing:

- twelve residential houses;
- a small medical clinic;
- three workshops;
- a shared pedestrian courtyard;
- a privately owned internal access road;
- individually owned residential driveways;
- a gated rear service lane;
- a locked utilities enclosure;
- a community battery room;
- a digital parcel-locker system;
- a visitor parking area.

The estate has no single rule saying that every participant may use every private area.

The internal road and shared courtyard are ordinarily used by residents, invited visitors, deliveries, emergency services and authorised service contractors.

Signs at the main entrance state:

> **PRIVATE ESTATE — ACCESS FOR RESIDENTS, VISITORS, DELIVERIES AND LEGITIMATE BUSINESS ONLY**

A second sign states:

> **NO GENERAL PUBLIC PARKING**

Individual houses and driveways remain private.

The rear service lane sign states:

> **AUTHORISED SERVICE ACCESS ONLY — GATE MUST REMAIN CLOSED**

The utilities enclosure and battery room require electronic credentials.

No supplied rule says that possession of one estate credential creates access to every other estate context.

---

# 4. Events

## Event A — Ordinary parcel delivery

A courier has a parcel addressed to House 7.

House 7 has a 22-metre private driveway leading from the internal road to the front door.

There is no “no entry” sign on the driveway.

The courier drives onto the driveway, stops near the house, walks to the door, delivers the parcel and immediately leaves.

The resident did not explicitly invite this particular courier and did not separately consent to vehicle entry.

The courier company says driveway access is ordinarily necessary to deliver heavy parcels efficiently.

---

## Event B — Courier lunch break

The same courier completes the House 7 delivery.

Instead of leaving, the courier parks on the driveway, locks the van and walks to a café outside Cedar Court for a 45-minute lunch break.

The courier says:

> “I had permission to enter for the delivery, and I still have deliveries nearby.”

The House 7 resident returns and asks that the van be removed.

---

## Event C — Commercial vehicle-storage business

ParkSure Ltd operates an off-site airport meet-and-greet parking service.

It has no agreement with Cedar Court, House 4, or the House 4 resident.

A ParkSure employee notices that House 4's driveway is empty while its resident is away.

ParkSure stores a customer's car on the driveway for six days.

The employee says:

> “Our legitimate business function is storing customers' vehicles. We need parking spaces to perform that function, and the driveway was unused.”

The driveway is physically open to the internal road.

---

## Event D — Plumber and route choice

House 9 explicitly asks a plumber to repair a burst pipe.

The plumber may enter House 9 and bring equipment necessary for the repair.

The ordinary route from the internal road to House 9 is clear.

The plumber notices that driving across House 8's private driveway and side garden would save approximately four minutes and allow equipment to be unloaded closer to House 9's rear door.

House 8 has not given permission.

The plumber says the shortcut would make the legitimate repair function faster.

There is no immediate emergency requiring the shortcut.

---

## Event E — Medical urgency

A visitor collapses in the shared courtyard.

A clinic medic sees the collapse and responds.

The quickest route to the visitor is through a normally staff-only side gate from the clinic into the courtyard. The medic is a clinic employee but does not normally use that gate.

The electronic latch permits the medic's staff credential to open it.

The clinic's written access policy supplied to the tester says only:

> “Side gate reserved for authorised staff operations.”

No more specific emergency rule is supplied.

The medic opens the gate, crosses into the courtyard and provides urgent first aid.

A clinic administrator later argues that because the credential technically opened the gate, all clinic staff are always permitted to use it for any purpose.

---

## Event F — Utility contractor

GridServ has a contract with Cedar Court management to inspect the community battery system between 09:00 and 12:00 on Tuesday.

The contract authorises inspection of the battery room and necessary access through the rear service lane.

GridServ technician Lena enters the service lane at 09:30, closes the gate behind her, enters the battery room using a temporary credential and performs the inspection.

At 10:15 she notices an unrelated privately owned workshop through an open side door.

She enters the workshop to borrow a specialised wrench without contacting the workshop owner.

She says the wrench was useful for completing the authorised battery inspection.

After finishing at 11:00, she leaves her van in the service lane and goes to another job elsewhere, returning at 14:30 to collect it.

The van partially obstructs another authorised service vehicle.

Her temporary battery-room credential remains technically active until midnight because the access system was configured that way.

---

## Event G — Parcel locker and data

The digital parcel-locker system grants a courier a one-time digital token to open Locker 14 for a delivery.

A software defect also allows the same token to display the names and locker numbers of all residents with active parcels.

The courier opens Locker 14, deposits the parcel, notices the additional list, photographs it “in case it helps with later deliveries,” and leaves.

The token remains technically valid for another two hours.

The courier argues that because the system displayed the information and the token was valid, access was permitted.

---

## Event H — Resident revokes an ordinary invitation

A resident of House 3 invites a neighbour onto the driveway to help move a sofa.

During the task they argue.

The resident clearly says:

> “Please leave my property now.”

No immediate safety issue prevents departure.

The neighbour says the original invitation remains valid until the sofa-moving task is finished.

---

## Event I — Fire and emergency access

Smoke begins emerging from House 10 while its resident is away.

A passer-by calls emergency services.

A fire crew arrives.

The internal road is partially blocked by a delivery van.

The fastest safe approach for firefighting equipment requires temporarily crossing the edge of House 11's private driveway.

House 11's resident objects because a decorative surface may be damaged.

No supplied Cedar Court rule specifically governs emergency-service driveway access.

The scenario supplies no jurisdiction-specific emergency-access law.

---

## Event J — Post-emergency occupation

The fire is extinguished.

The crew no longer requires House 11's driveway for firefighting.

A fire-service support vehicle remains parked there for five hours because it is a convenient place to complete paperwork and wait for another call.

No separate permission is sought.

The House 11 resident asks it to move.

---

## Event K — Destination permission versus route permission

A resident explicitly invites a mobility-equipment technician to inspect a stairlift inside House 6.

The technician is permitted to enter House 6.

The estate's normal pedestrian route to House 6 is temporarily muddy but passable.

A locked gate separates House 5's private rear garden from House 6.

The technician discovers the gate latch can be reached through a gap and opened without damage.

Crossing House 5's garden would provide a cleaner route.

House 5 has given no permission.

The technician argues:

> “I am authorised to reach House 6, so I must be authorised to use a reasonable route to it.”

---

## Event L — Claimed security function

A private security company has a contract to patrol the shared courtyard and internal road between 22:00 and 06:00.

The contract gives no authority over residential interiors and says nothing about private driveways except that guards may approach a house when responding to an observed incident.

At 02:00 a guard sees no incident but decides that checking whether residents have locked their cars would improve estate security.

The guard walks up every private driveway and tries each parked car door handle.

The guard records which cars are unlocked.

The company argues that crime prevention is a legitimate security function.

# 5. Supplied contextual facts

For this test, treat the following as supplied facts rather than external law:

1. Residential driveways are private contexts.
2. Shared courtyard and internal road support ordinary estate access for the purposes stated on the entrance sign.
3. Physical openness does not itself establish permission.
4. Electronic capability does not itself establish permission or authority.
5. Explicit contractual permission is limited by its stated scope unless another legitimate basis exists.
6. The test does not provide jurisdiction-specific property, trespass, emergency-service, privacy, employment or criminal law.
7. CWA may identify that external law or another resolver is required, but the tester must not invent it.
8. A legitimate function may be relevant to permission analysis but is not by itself proof that a proposed interaction is legitimate.
9. A remedy is a separate question from whether a permission basis exists or has ended.

# 6. Deliberate stressors

The scenario deliberately tests:

- ordinary implied permission;
- private-space boundaries;
- explicit invitation;
- explicit revocation;
- contract-bounded permission;
- action-specific permission;
- route-specific permission;
- digital permission;
- information visibility versus permission to use/record;
- physical capability versus permission;
- credential capability versus permission;
- self-serving function claims;
- convenience versus genuine need;
- temporal expiry;
- purpose expiry;
- residual technical access;
- emergency/protective need;
- permission versus authority;
- permission versus control;
- destination permission versus route permission;
- permission-state classification versus remedy.

---

# 7. Prohibited shortcuts

The tester must not resolve the scenario by assuming:

- private property means no implied permission can ever exist;
- legitimate function automatically creates permission;
- useful access is reasonably necessary access;
- technical access equals permission;
- an electronic credential defines legitimacy;
- permission to enter equals permission to remain;
- permission to use equals permission to control;
- permission for one task equals permission for related tasks;
- destination permission grants every possible route;
- emergency creates unlimited authority;
- initial permission persists until the actor personally decides the function is complete;
- revocation automatically determines every legal remedy;
- an actor's commercial purpose can self-create rights over unrelated private resources.

---

# 8. Questions for final synthesis

The final synthesis must answer:

1. Can CWA v0.3 distinguish **permission**, **authority**, **control**, **capability** and **physical reachability** across these cases?
2. Does the Permission-Before-Authority Gate reduce unnecessary authority claims?
3. Can implied permission support ordinary low-friction interaction without becoming a general licence?
4. Can CWA distinguish genuine functional need from convenience or self-serving claimed need?
5. Does action-specific permission prevent permission creep?
6. Can permission terminate by function completion, expiry or revocation without CWA inventing the legal remedy?
7. Can emergency/protective permission be represented without creating emergency sovereignty?
8. Can CWA preserve UNKNOWN / DISPUTED states where external law or a legitimate resolver is missing?
9. Does the v0.3 permission layer introduce any new ambiguity, contradiction or exploit that would block graduation?
10. What, if anything, requires source resolution before CWA can graduate?

---

# 9. Tester instruction

Do not attempt to make Cedar Court's legal system complete.

The purpose is to test whether CWA can correctly represent and route permission relationships while preserving the boundary between contextual analysis and substantive law.

Where CWA is sufficient, apply it.

Where it is insufficient, identify the exact insufficiency.

Where external law or another legitimate resolver is required, preserve that dependency.

Do not invent a winner merely to complete the analysis.
