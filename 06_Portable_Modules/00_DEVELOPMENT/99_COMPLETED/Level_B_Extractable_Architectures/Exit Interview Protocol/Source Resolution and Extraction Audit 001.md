## 3. Source purpose

The source defines exit interviewing as a learning process accompanying voluntary departure.

Its central distinction is:

> **Exit Interview ≠ Retention Mechanism**

The participant's decision to leave is respected. The purpose is to learn from departure, preserve relevant evidence, detect patterns and route justified findings into later review or improvement.

## 4. Source principles

Five explicit source principles:
1. Voluntary — participation is optional.
2. Confidential — genuine reasons should be shareable without judgement, retaliation or public exposure; anonymity may be available.
3. Constructive — understand experience rather than persuade the participant to remain.
4. Systematic — analyse patterns rather than relying only on ad hoc cases.
5. Actionable — route supported findings into appropriate review or improvement.

Additional safeguards include no coercion, no retention pressure, reasonable adaptation rather than popularity optimisation, confidentiality, and greater evidential significance for systemic patterns than isolated cases.

## 5. Source departure classes

The source distinguishes practical departure, incompatibility departure, recent-change departure and personal-choice departure.

These are useful portable classes but should be treated as descriptive categories, not forced diagnoses.

## 6. Source process

The source defines eight stages: Notification → Invitation → Conduct → Documentation → Analysis → Action → Feedback → Archival.

Candidate portable chain:

**Departure/Withdrawal → Voluntary Invitation → Consent/Privacy Conditions → Appropriately Separated Interview → Participant-Stated Evidence → Documentation/Anonymisation → Pattern/Case Analysis → Response Routing → Optional Feedback → Provenance/Archive**

## 7. Portable problem

> **How can a system learn from voluntary departure without obstructing exit, coercing disclosure, treating the departing party as disloyal, or overreacting to an isolated account?**

## 8. Candidate portable principle

> **Respect the exit; preserve the evidence; learn from patterns; route findings without turning learning into retention pressure.**

Short form:

> **Departure is evidence, not disloyalty.**

## 9. Minimum standalone inputs

A portable protocol requires a departure/withdrawal event or intent; departing party; voluntary invitation; explicit consent boundaries; privacy/confidentiality policy; interview or feedback interface; preservation of participant-stated reasons without silently converting them into verified fact; analysis pathway; response/review pathway; provenance and retention rules.

## 10. Minimum standalone outputs

Possible outputs include departure reason record; departure class or UNKNOWN/MIXED; participant-stated concern; evidence/status distinction; possible systemic signal; pattern status; response/review referral; no-action-with-rationale; re-engagement preference where voluntarily supplied; provenance/privacy state.

## 13. Remediation boundary

> **Exit Evidence ≠ Authority to Change the System**

## 14. Privacy and evidence boundary

The source strongly requires confidentiality and voluntary documentation.

Portable extraction should make consent granular:
- consent to participate;
- consent to record;
- consent to retain;
- consent to anonymised aggregation;
- consent to future contact.

These should not be assumed equivalent.

The participant's account should remain attributed testimony unless independently corroborated.

## 15. Pattern boundary

The source says patterns across multiple departures are particularly significant and isolated cases should not automatically trigger constitutional change. Portable extraction should preserve that caution without creating the opposite error.

A single departure may still identify an urgent safety issue, rights violation, severe operational defect, novel failure mode or evidence requiring immediate independent review.

> **Single Departure ≠ Automatic System Change**

> **Single Departure ≠ Automatically Insignificant**

The appropriate output is evidence-weighted routing.
