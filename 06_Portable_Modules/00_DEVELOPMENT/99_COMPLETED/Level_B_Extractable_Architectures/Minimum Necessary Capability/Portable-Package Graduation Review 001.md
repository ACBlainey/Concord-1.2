## 6. Test-supported revisions incorporated in v0.2

### R1 — Floor versus boundary

> **Capability Floor ≠ Capability Boundary/Ceiling.**

The floor describes minimum positive abilities required for sufficient function. Restrictions and exclusions belong to the boundary/ceiling.

### R2 — Operational discretion versus escalation

> **Operational Discretion Within Capability ≠ Authority to Expand Capability.**

Professional judgement about exercising an authorised capability does not automatically include authority to enlarge that capability.

### R3 — Logging versus legitimacy

> **Logging Capability Exercise ≠ Legitimising Capability Exercise.**

Auditability supports evidence and accountability; it does not itself justify a grant, assumption or exercise of capability.

### R4 — Activation-time aggregate review

Where reasonably feasible, known overlapping roles, credentials, delegated access and persistent permissions should be inspected before or at activation of consequential capability.

This does not require omniscience; hidden capability remains a review risk.

## 8. Important transfer findings

### Symmetric capability discipline

The tester rejected both the broad legacy role and the excessively restrictive alternative.

This supports the intended distinction:

> **Minimum Necessary ≠ Minimum Possible.**

### Aggregate capability

The tester combined the new role with a pre-existing vendor credential.

This supports:

> **Minimum Local Capability ≠ Minimum Systemic Capability.**

### Effective capability

Formal role descriptions were not treated as the whole capability state.

### Emergency decay

A temporary change of function justified temporary additional capability without converting it into the baseline role.

### Self-justification boundary

The capability holder was not permitted to define urgency and unilaterally activate arbitrary additional capability merely because activity would later be logged.

## 9. Portability finding

The evidence supports the bounded claim:

> **Minimum Necessary Capability can be specified independently of Concord-specific institutional machinery, and a fresh AI instance successfully transferred the standalone specification to the tested non-Concord technical-organisational capability problem.**

This is sufficient for **specification-level portable graduation**.

It is not sufficient for empirical validation.

---

## 10. Claims that remain open

Graduation must not imply that MNC:

- identifies objectively correct capability minima or maxima;
- reduces abuse in practice;
- improves cybersecurity;
- improves institutional performance;
- is legally compliant in any particular jurisdiction;
- outperforms least-privilege, proportionality or zero-trust frameworks;
- is equally usable by humans and AI systems;
- is cross-culturally validated;
- or supplies a universal theory of legitimate authority.

The source itself also leaves open quantitative methods for capability floors/ceilings, aggregate-capability measurement, evidential thresholds, reviewer capture and related implementation questions.

---

## 11. Need for another same-question blind AI test

A second equivalent blind AI transfer test is not required merely to repeat MNC-BTT-001.

The first test already exercised the principal architecture under:

- under-capability;
- over-capability;
- multidimensional bounds;
- aggregate access;
- formal/effective divergence;
- responsibility;
- changed function;
- escalation;
- decay;
- self-justification;
- assumed capability;
- provenance;

Further testing should target materially different claims such as:

- human usability;
- AI-agent implementation;
- legal/regulatory use;
- severe adversarial environments;
- quantitative methods;
- long-duration capability accumulation;
- or comparative empirical performance.
