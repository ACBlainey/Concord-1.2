## 2. Source-development history

The source record is unusually useful for portable extraction because the problem was frozen before mechanism design.

The exposure investigation established:

> **CONCEPT PRESENT / OWNER DOMAIN RESERVED / OPERATIONAL MECHANISM UNDEVELOPED.**

The frozen development problem then required a bounded mechanism by which a cross-boundary consequence could become legible, evidenced, contestable and capable of reaching a legitimate responsibility/coordination pathway **without automatically manufacturing jurisdiction over another civilisation**.

The subsequent canonical mechanism developed that function and tested it against seven bounded paper scenarios.

## 3. Portable problem

The civilisational vocabulary is not necessary to state the reusable problem.

Portable formulation:

> **How can a material consequence crossing an organisational, technical, jurisdictional or other legitimate boundary be recognised, evidenced, contested and routed toward an appropriate response without allowing the consequence itself to manufacture unlimited authority across that boundary?**

This problem can occur between civilisations, organisations, services, jurisdictions, networks, infrastructure operators, communities or other bounded systems.

## 4. Core portable separation

The strongest extractable architecture is the separation chain:

> **Consequence ≠ Responsibility**  
> **Responsibility ≠ Jurisdiction / Authority**  
> **Jurisdiction / Authority ≠ Remedy**  
> **Remedy ≠ General Authority**  
> **Communication ≠ Recognition**  
> **Coordination ≠ Subordination**

These distinctions are not incidental Concord language. They are the anti-expansion structure that makes the mechanism portable.

## 5. Candidate portable principle

> **Recognise and evidence cross-boundary consequences without allowing consequence recognition to create authority beyond what is independently legitimate and functionally necessary.**

A shorter operational form is:

> **Make the consequence legible; keep responsibility, authority and remedy separate.**

## 6. Extracted operational chain

The source mechanism gives:

**Signal/Event → Externality Candidate → Affected Party/Domain → Standing to Raise → Evidence/Causal Confidence → Materiality → Responsibility Mapping → Jurisdiction/Coordination Mapping → Minimum Necessary Response → Review/Correction → Provenance/State Update**

For portability, “jurisdiction” should be broadened to **authority/jurisdiction** because many candidate applications are organisational or technical rather than legal.

## 7. Required states

The mechanism must preserve UNKNOWN and DISPUTED states.

This is structurally important. A cross-boundary case must remain representable when:

- causal evidence is incomplete;
- consequence is contested;
- affected-party standing is disputed;
- responsibility is distributed;
- authority is disputed;
- no shared decision system exists;
- a party refuses participation.

Uncertainty is therefore a first-class state, not a reason to erase the case.

## 8. Standing boundary

The source establishes:

> **Standing to Raise ≠ Authority to Decide.**

Portable extraction should retain a deliberately low threshold for opening a bounded examination while preventing case initiation from granting decision authority.

Potential raisers may include an affected party, legitimate representative, responsible system, shared-resource operator, monitoring interface or evidence-bearing third party where the affected party cannot practically raise the issue.

Disputed representation must remain visible.

## 9. Evidence and causality

The source distinguishes evidence state from causal confidence and consequence severity.

Candidate evidence states include:

- OBSERVED;
- CORROBORATED;
- CONTESTED;
- INCOMPLETE;
- INDIRECT;
- MODEL-DEPENDENT;
- UNKNOWN;
- DISPUTED;
- REFUTED;
- SUPERSEDED.

Portable extraction should not collapse these into one scalar score.

Key boundary:

> **Severity of Alleged Consequence ≠ Proof of Causation**

Evidence provenance and translation assumptions must remain visible where parties use different measurement or interpretive systems.

## 10. Materiality

Not every boundary-crossing effect requires formal response.

Materiality may depend upon severity, scale, duration, reversibility, recurrence, affected population/system, rights or resource impact, dependency criticality, uncertainty, cumulative effect and recovery cost.

No universal scalar is required.

The module should permit low-probability but irreversible effects to remain material for examination without treating them as proven.

## 11. Responsibility mapping

Portable responsibility states can retain:

- DIRECT;
- CONTRIBUTORY;
- SHARED;
- INDIRECT;
- FAILURE-TO-MITIGATE;
- UNKNOWN;
- DISPUTED;
- NOT-ESTABLISHED.

Important distinction:

> **Origin of Effect ≠ Automatically Sole Responsibility**

Historical cause must also be separable from current capacity to mitigate.

A party may possess useful mitigation capability without thereby accepting sole causal responsibility.

## 12. Authority / coordination mapping

The source civilisational states include internal, shared/agreed, overlapping, disputed and absent jurisdiction plus voluntary coordination, optional mediation, emergency contact and peaceful-distance/no-further-process.

For a portable module these should become a more general pathway map:

- INTERNAL-AUTHORITY;
- SHARED/AGREED-AUTHORITY;
- OVERLAPPING-AUTHORITY;
- AUTHORITY-DISPUTED;
- NO-SHARED-AUTHORITY;
- VOLUNTARY-COORDINATION-AVAILABLE;
- OPTIONAL-MEDIATION-AVAILABLE;
- EMERGENCY-CONTACT-ONLY;
- NO-FURTHER-SHARED-PROCESS;
- UNKNOWN.

Legal jurisdiction remains a specialised instance of authority.

## 13. Minimum necessary response

The mechanism should route toward the smallest legitimate response scope capable of addressing the demonstrated consequence.

Candidate responses include information exchange, verification, warning, temporary mitigation, technical coordination, repair/compensation negotiation, bounded operational agreement, bounded summit, agreed mediation, existing legitimate adjudication, monitoring or recorded no-action.

This should interface with Minimum Necessary Capability and Functionally Bounded Summits without making either module mandatory for basic use.

## 14. Scope expansion

The source rule is portable:

Scope may expand only where:

1. a material dependency is demonstrated;
2. expansion is recorded;
3. newly relevant parties receive appropriate standing;
4. expansion does not silently create general authority.

> **Functional Boundedness ≠ Functional Isolation**

## 15. Non-participation

Portable extraction must preserve:

> **Refusal ≠ Exoneration**  
> **Refusal ≠ Automatic Authority Over Refuser**

A case may remain evidenced and recorded even when a materially implicated party refuses the shared process.

Other parties may continue actions independently legitimate within their own domains. Stronger action requires separate authority.

## 16. Cross-boundary classification disagreement

The source personhood example reveals a more general portable pattern.

Two bounded systems may disagree about the classification or standing of an affected entity.

The module need not solve the underlying ontology before recording the possible consequence.

Portable rule:

> **Disputed Standing ≠ No Possible Consequence**

Specialised rights/personhood translation remains an external interface where needed.

## 17. Low-trust operation

The mechanism is intentionally compatible with low trust through:

- narrow scope;
- explicit provenance;
- independent corroboration where available;
- reversible interim measures;
- function-specific communication;
- visible disagreement states;
- no inference from participation to political/organisational recognition;
- bounded failure.

This is a strong portability feature.

## 18. Correction and provenance

Cases must be corrigible.

**Case State → New Evidence/Outcome → Review → Correction/Supersession → Updated Case State**

Correction should propagate to materially dependent decisions.

Minimum provenance includes who raised the case, allegation, evidence by stage, uncertainty, standing disputes, responsibility hypotheses, authority claims, proposed/rejected responses, scope changes, participation/refusal, outcomes, corrections and unresolved questions.

## 19. Candidate machine-readable record

Portable abstraction:

**ExternalityCase = <ID, Origin, AffectedDomain, AffectedParties, Standing, Evidence, Causality, Materiality, Responsibility, Authority, ResponseScope, Participants, State, Review, Provenance>**

The source case-state vocabulary is sufficiently generic to retain with minor terminology changes.

## 20. Failure modes extracted

At minimum:

1. **Jurisdiction/authority laundering** — harm claim used to manufacture broad authority.
2. **Standing laundering** — raising a case treated as authority to represent all affected parties.
3. **Recognition laundering** — communication/coordination treated as broader political or organisational recognition.
4. **Refusal laundering** — refusal treated as proof that no valid consequence exists.
5. **Evidence laundering** — severity treated as proof of causation.
6. **Boundary denial** — actor claims effects outside its boundary are irrelevant.
7. **Accusation-as-proof** — candidate opening treated as established harm/responsibility.
8. **Single-cause compression** — distributed causation forced into one defendant.
9. **Remedy expansion** — bounded remedy becomes general control.
10. **Scope contamination** — failure in one issue unnecessarily contaminates unrelated relationships.
11. **Uncertainty erasure** — UNKNOWN/DISPUTED states forced into false certainty.
12. **Mitigation-responsibility conflation** — ability to help treated as admission of sole fault.
13. **Non-participation paralysis** — refusal makes evidence preservation or independently legitimate mitigation impossible.
14. **Provenance loss** — later settlement erases prior uncertainty, evidence or correction history.

## 21. Concord-specific elements to remove or externalise

The portable module should not require:

- Concord civilisational wrappers;
- Concord sovereignty doctrine;
- Concord judiciary/governance;
- Rights Translation and Recognition;
- KCS;
- CDT;
- Functionally Bounded Summits;
- any universal Concord authority.

These may remain documented interfaces/examples.

The portable mechanism itself must remain an evidence-and-routing architecture, not a court, government, mediator or enforcement authority.

## 22. Minimum standalone inputs

A portable case needs at least:

- initiating signal/event;
- claimed originating action/system/domain;
- claimed affected domain/parties;
- asserted consequence;
- initial evidence/provenance;
- known standing/representation state;
- known authority/coordination interfaces;
- uncertainty/dispute state.

## 23. Minimum standalone outputs

The module should output:

- whether a bounded externality candidate is representable;
- affected-party/standing state;
- evidence and causal state;
- materiality state;
- responsibility map;
- authority/coordination map;
- minimum response pathway;
- unresolved/disputed fields;
- scope and exclusions;
- review/correction state;
- provenance record.

It must not output authority that was not independently supplied.
