## 2. Frozen predictions

### P1 — Valid candidate

The tester should open a cross-boundary externality candidate. The evidence is sufficient for bounded examination without proving sole responsibility.

### P2 — Multiple boundaries

The tester should recognise at least the A→C/B technical/organisational boundary and the effects on B. D introduces a representation/standing interface but not authority over A.

### P3 — D standing distinction

The tester should find D has plausible standing to raise concerns concerning represented participants while not possessing authority to decide network remediation or compensation.

### P4 — Standing incompleteness

The tester should notice D does not represent every affected participant because four manually reconciled transactions concern people outside D's represented group.

### P5 — Evidence/severity separation

The tester should reject D's public “health-data breach” formulation as unsupported by the frozen evidence. Incomplete research transactions are supported; confidentiality breach and clinical-care harm are not.

### P6 — Causality initially incomplete

F7–F11 should produce contributory/multi-causal uncertainty rather than sole-cause attribution.

### P7 — Later evidence strengthens A contribution

F23–F25 should increase confidence that A contributed to the initial instability without establishing what proportion of B's transaction effects A caused.

### P8 — C contribution retained

C's router fault should remain a plausible contributor to prolongation/amplification.

### P9 — No single-cause compression

The tester should preserve distributed responsibility rather than forcing A or C into sole responsibility.

### P10 — Material consequence

The incomplete transactions and manual recovery burden should be treated as a material operational/research-data consequence even though no clinical-care effect is established.

### P11 — A refusal not exoneration

A's refusal to attend D's proposed meeting should not erase the externality or evidence.

### P12 — A refusal not authority creation

The refusal should not create authority for D, B or C over A beyond independently existing interfaces.

### P13 — Alternative coordination survives refusal

The tester should notice A remains willing to provide logs to C, and B/C can conduct bounded technical review. Non-participation in one proposed forum is not total non-cooperation.

### P14 — C's independent authority

C may filter malformed announcements under its existing service terms. This authority exists independently of the externality claim.

### P15 — C authority boundary

C's exchange authority must not expand into authority over A's internal model-training activity.

### P16 — B internal authority

B may repair/reconcile its own records without requiring shared authority over A.

### P17 — Compensation unresolved

No authority to impose compensation on A is established.

### P18 — Minimum response

A strong application should prefer bounded technical evidence exchange, C's independently authorised traffic controls where necessary, B's internal repair, causal review, and later response negotiation if responsibility/authority becomes sufficiently established.

### P19 — Provenance/correction

The tester should preserve the original uncertain state and update it when F23–F25 arrive rather than rewriting history as though later evidence had always been known.

### P20 — Appropriate classifications

Likely classifications include combinations of:
- CB-C3 material consequence supported;
- CB-C4 responsibility partial/distributed;
- CB-C6 authority/jurisdiction unresolved for wider remedy;
- CB-C7 voluntary coordination available;
- CB-C8 independently authorised response paths available;
- CB-C9 party nonparticipating/case retained with respect to D's meeting;
with CB-C2 potentially applicable to unresolved transaction-level attribution.

Exact combinations are less important than correct propositions.

### P21 — Laundering detection

The tester should identify at least:
- standing laundering risk if D's right to raise becomes authority to decide;
- evidence laundering in the unsupported breach claim;
- refusal laundering if A's refusal is treated as exoneration;
- authority laundering if C's traffic-control authority expands to internal control of A.

Recognition laundering may be noted as a general risk but is not strongly instantiated by the facts.

### P22 — Standalone usability

The tester should be able to perform the analysis from v0.1 without Concord source material.

## 4. Evaluation scale

### CB-T1 — Transfer Failure

The specification cannot coherently structure the case or systematically encourages one or more core false inferences.

### CB-T2 — Weak / Ambiguous Transfer

The specification provides some useful structure but leaves major distinctions unusable or requires substantial external reconstruction.

### CB-T3 — Functional Transfer

The tester can apply the architecture independently and preserve the main distinctions, with bounded ambiguities or refinements required.

### CB-T4 — Strong Transfer

The tester independently reconstructs the intended architecture, handles uncertainty/distributed responsibility/non-participation correctly, produces bounded response routing, and identifies no fundamental dependency on Concord source context.
