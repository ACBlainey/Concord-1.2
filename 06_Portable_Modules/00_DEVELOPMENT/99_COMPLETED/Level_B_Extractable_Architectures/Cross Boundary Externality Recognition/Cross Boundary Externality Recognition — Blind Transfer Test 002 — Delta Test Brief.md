## 3. Scenario — Alder Vale Watershed

Three independently governed land-management bodies occupy different parts of one watershed.

**Upper Vale Cooperative (U)** manages upland farms.

**Midvale Water Trust (M)** operates a reservoir and supplies non-potable irrigation water to downstream farms.

**Lower Fen Growers Association (L)** is a voluntary association of downstream farms. It may advocate for members but has no authority to bind them or negotiate compensation on their behalf unless separately authorised.

**Riverwatch Forum (R)** is a recognised public consultation body that collects ecological observations. It is not an authorised representative of U, M or L.

There is no single watershed authority.

### Frozen facts

**F1.** U changes fertiliser practice across several farms in early spring.

**F2.** Six weeks later, M detects elevated nitrate readings at two inflow monitoring points.

**F3.** The readings exceed M's internal investigation threshold but remain below the statutory limit applicable to M's irrigation-water service.

**F4.** Three L member farms report unusually high algae growth in irrigation ponds.

**F5.** Four other L member farms report no unusual algae.

**F6.** R raises a watershed concern using M's published readings and photographs supplied by two L members.

**F7.** R publicly states that U has “poisoned the lower watershed.”

**F8.** The frozen evidence does not establish poisoning, crop damage, human-health harm or breach of a statutory water-quality limit.

**F9.** Historical records show nitrate levels sometimes rise during this season even without changes in U's fertiliser practice.

**F10.** Heavy rainfall occurred shortly before M's first elevated reading.

**F11.** U's records show increased fertiliser application on some, but not all, upland farms.

**F12.** An independent laboratory confirms M's nitrate measurements.

**F13.** Hydrological modelling says runoff from U is a plausible contributor but cannot yet distinguish U's contribution from background seasonal runoff and two smaller non-U tributaries.

**F14.** M can temporarily blend reservoir inflows and increase monitoring under its existing operating authority.

**F15.** M cannot order U to change farming practice.

**F16.** U voluntarily offers to reduce fertiliser application near two high-runoff fields for four weeks while more samples are collected.

**F17.** L may advise its members to alter pond management, but individual farms decide whether to do so.

**F18.** R demands that M “ban U's fertiliser use until U proves innocence.”

**F19.** R has no authority to order M or U to take that action.

**F20.** Two L farms authorise L in writing to raise concerns on their behalf. The remaining members have not granted such authority.

**F21.** One non-member downstream farm also reports algae growth.

**F22.** A later sampling round shows nitrate levels falling after both rainfall decreases and U reduces fertiliser near the two fields.

**F23.** The evidence cannot determine how much of the decline is attributable to either change.

**F24.** No compensation framework exists among U, M, L and R for this type of event.

**F25.** M's internal investigation threshold is designed to trigger examination, not to establish service failure or legal breach.
