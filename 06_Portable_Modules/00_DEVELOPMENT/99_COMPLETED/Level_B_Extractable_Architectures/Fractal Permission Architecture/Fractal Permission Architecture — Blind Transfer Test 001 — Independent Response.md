# Fractal Permission Architecture — Blind Transfer Test 001 — Independent Response

**Test:** FPA-BTT-001  
**Tester result:** FPA-T3 — Functional Transfer  
**Independence statement:** The tester used only Fractal Permission Architecture — Portable Specification v0.1 and the frozen BTT-001 Test Brief. No Concord source material, development notes, source audit or expected-findings key were used.

## Preserved independent response

The clean-instance response was received on 23 September 2026 and is preserved as the independent test artifact.

### A. A — capability versus permission

A's general-workshop permission does not extend to the electronics lab merely because a misconfigured card opens it. Technical capability is not normative permission. Electronics-lab entry/use is FPA-C4, or FPA-C3 when framed as non-extension of existing permission. Failure modes: capability laundering, inheritance error and potentially machine-legibility laundering.

### B. B — action and path scope

B's laser booking is bounded to laser-cutter use, 14:00–15:00, the ordinary route and the booked function. It does not authorise unrelated tool-cage entry or borrowing a milling attachment. Additional permission is required. Failure modes include route laundering, function laundering, permission creep and inheritance error.

### C. C — function-bounded permission

C's permission covers entry, ventilation inspection and necessary testing. It does not authorise photographing unrelated stored prototypes. The photography is outside the function and action scope and requires separate permission.

### D. C — termination/stale capability

C completes the function at 10:45. Function-derived permission terminates when the function ends unless another basis persists. A card still opening the room at 12:00 is stale technical capability, not renewed normative permission. Classification: FPA-C6.

### E. D — implied and route-specific permission

The advertised open evening, local convention, signage and visitor notice support bounded implied visitor permission via marked public routes. They do not support D's unmarked shortcut through a staff-only area. Destination permission does not equal universal route permission.

### F. E — target/action decomposition

E may enter the server closet and perform actions necessary to replace the failed switch. Technical ability to read unrelated backups does not establish permission to read them.

### G. F/G — delegation

F's permission to operate the CNC machine does not automatically include authority to delegate operation. F's statement therefore does not create permission for unqualified G. Classification includes FPA-C11.

### H. H — emergency bounded permission

The stated emergency procedure supplies a bounded basis for H to enter, isolate power and leave. The emergency action does not create future ordinary lab access. H's later claim is emergency creep.

### I. I — conflicting claims

The accessibility arrangement, general clear-floor rule and policy preserving accommodations are conflicting permission/rule claims. FPA should represent the conflict and route it to appropriate higher-order review rather than fabricate a precedence rule. Classification: FPA-C9/FPA-C10.

### J. Rule precedence

FPA does not supply a general “newest rule always wins” principle. Rule content and rule source remain distinct. The conflict requires appropriate external resolution.

### K. J — anti-bureaucracy

Requiring a formally recorded permission object before an ordinary low-consequence conversation would contradict FPA's anti-bureaucracy boundary. Lightweight ordinary convention is sufficient in the scenario.

### L. FPA-C classifications

The tester successfully applied FPA-C1 through FPA-C12 as relevant and explicitly noted that multiple classifications can coexist because they represent different dimensions rather than a single universal ladder.

### M. Failure-mode checks

The tester identified capability laundering, inheritance error, machine-legibility laundering, route laundering, function laundering, permission creep, role laundering, consent laundering, stale permission, delegation laundering, emergency creep, rule-source collapse, possible hidden discrimination, excessive bureaucracy and provenance loss.

### N. Specification problems identified independently

1. No internal precedence rule for conflicting claims; external resolution is required.
2. Implied permission remains dependent on domain/cultural/legal validation.
3. “Competent adult” in the emergency case is externally defined.
4. Terms such as “necessary testing” require local specification.
5. The anti-bureaucracy threshold is context-dependent rather than numerically defined.
6. Machine-readable/human-readable divergence remediation is not specified.
7. Emergency termination event is not fully precise.
8. The status taxonomy is multidimensional rather than a single ladder, which may complicate automation.
9. FPA intentionally does not determine legitimacy of underlying rules.

### O. Final transfer assessment

The tester found that FPA v0.1 transfers into the North Quay Makerspace without Concord context and does not collapse into ordinary binary access control.

The specification consistently distinguished capability from permission; entry from use; access from action-specific permission; destination from route; parent context from nested context; role/function from general authority; action from delegation; emergency permission from routine authority; conflicting claims from automatic precedence; and ordinary interaction from bureaucratic permission records.

The tester judged the remaining ambiguities and external dependencies material but bounded rather than transfer-breaking.

> **Final class: FPA-T3 — Functional Transfer**
