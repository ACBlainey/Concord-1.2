## Source comparison

The primary source already contains the substantive KCS kernel: externalised memory, epistemic state, provenance, historical preservation, reasoning pathways, honest negative knowledge, active/dormant/archive distinctions, bounded retrieval, stewardship, attribution, contribution evidence and anti-oracle/anti-social-credit safeguards.

Portable development does not justify replacing that kernel.

It does expose bounded operational precision not explicit enough in the primary source:

1. state dimensions should be explicitly independent and scope-qualified;
2. integrity state should remain distinct from epistemic state;
3. access/visibility should remain distinct from retrieval/activity state;
4. state assignments need provenance and an external legitimate review/evidential interface;
5. typed relations need minimum semantics including DERIVED_FROM, SUPPORTS, CONTRADICTS, CORRECTS, SUPERSEDES, NARROWS, QUALIFIES, REPLICATES, FAILED_TO_REPLICATE and SUMMARISES;
6. relation scope/context may be bounded rather than global;
7. bounded retrieval should expose query scope, access/version limits, uncertainty, provenance/history pointers and known search limits;
8. negative search should be representable as a search-result object rather than a claim of nonexistence;
9. later successful search should preserve rather than erase the earlier bounded negative-search record;
10. stored-object availability must be separated from operational capability, compatibility and validation currency;
11. contribution evidence requires explicit anti-collapse safeguards against universal person-level scoring and authority laundering;
12. privacy/provenance handling should represent restricted, sealed, redacted, expired and policy-removed states without assuming universal visibility or indefinite retention;
13. redacted derivatives may preserve bounded provenance relations without leaking protected content;
14. KCS should state minimal interface payloads to Change Propagation and Continuity without absorbing either architecture;
15. preservation of a procedure/object does not by itself prove executable or recoverable capability.
