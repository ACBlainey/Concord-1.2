# 1. Headline Result

EKC-02 produces **very strong convergence at the level of authority legitimacy and bounded-power architecture**, while leaving much of the detailed V1.2 governmental machinery underdetermined.

The most striking convergence is structural rather than terminological.

The frozen U1–U8 derivation concluded:

> **Authority is derivative from legitimate function, not inherent in office, identity, intelligence, status or institutional existence.**

The later-reopened V1.2 Authority Justification Chain states, in substance:

> legitimacy flows toward function, need and bounded authority rather than authority proving its own legitimacy.

The V1.2 Bounded Contextual Authority work separately holds that authority exists only to the extent required by a legitimate function in a valid context and ends when the function/context that generated it ends.

This is exceptionally close to the architecture independently frozen in EKC-02.

The primary classification is therefore:

> **C1 — Strong Architectural Convergence**

with important:

> **C2 — Functional Convergence / Architectural Variation**  
> **C8 — Non-Kernel Dependency**

and a strong contamination warning.

No direct tension was identified.

# 2. Authority Source

## U1–U8 counterfactual

Authority is justified only by a real ethically legitimate cooperative function. Existing office, status, intelligence or institutional history is insufficient.

## V1.2

The Authority Justification Chain explicitly rejects the direction:

**Authority → Function → Legitimacy**

and instead develops:

**Ethical Kernel → Rights → Legitimate Civil Objective → Legitimate Function → Functional Need → Bounded Contextual Authority.**

It also distinguishes function legitimacy from authority necessity.

## Comparison

This is near-direct structural convergence.

Both architectures reject circular self-justifying authority and require an independent chain from legitimate problem/function to bounded power.

### Classification

**C1 — Strong Architectural Convergence**

# 3. Minimum / Proportionate Authority

## Counterfactual

The existence of a collective problem justifies only the minimum authority reasonably necessary to perform the legitimate function.

## V1.2

The Authority Justification Chain explicitly moves through functional need to **minimum proportionate authority**. It rejects the proposition that a legitimate objective makes any effective means legitimate.

Bounded Contextual Authority similarly limits power to what the function requires.

## Comparison

Very strong convergence.

### Classification

**C1**

---

# 4. Scope Tracks Function

## Counterfactual

Authority scope tracks function. Expansion requires renewed justification.

## V1.2

Bounded Contextual Authority distinguishes being a person with general authority from being authorised to perform a particular function. The work repeatedly treats context-derived permissions as bounded to action, object, space, time and conditions.

## Comparison

Strong convergence.

### Classification

**C1**

---

# 5. Inherent Sunset / Institutional Mortality

## Counterfactual

The frozen derivation concluded:

- institutions have no intrinsic claim to persistence;
- when a function disappears, attached authority presumptively contracts or terminates;
- necessary functions may persist while institutions are replaced;
- failed authority should permit transfer or regeneration.

## V1.2

Bounded Contextual Authority explicitly identifies an **inherent sunset**: authority derived solely from a context terminates when the function or context terminates.

The wider V1.2 governance-development corpus also treats historical authority as insufficient proof of present justification.

## Comparison

Strong convergence at the authority-sunset level.

The counterfactual frames this more broadly as institutional mortality and functional regeneration. Some of that broader continuity architecture lies elsewhere in V1.2 and should not be treated as absent from the Concord.

### Classification

**C1 / C2**

---

# 6. Expertise and Capability

## Counterfactual

Expertise grants task-relevant epistemic weight and can justify specialised responsibility, but not unrestricted standing or general sovereignty.

## V1.2

The canonical governance architecture contains specialised governance roles and extensive epistemic machinery, while the wider Concord consistently distinguishes competence/function from participant worth and standing.

## Comparison

Functional convergence is strong, but the detailed V1.2 mechanisms are substantially more specified than anything uniquely generated by U1–U8.

### Classification

**C1 / C8**

# 7. Accountability and Corrigibility

## Counterfactual

Consequential authority must be attributable, challengeable and epistemically corrigible. Greater power requires stronger accountability.

## V1.2

Canonical governance contains substantial oversight, review, provenance and corrective architecture. Active-development work explicitly attacks circular authority and separately audits the Authority Justification Chain and Bounded Contextual Authority.

## Comparison

Strong convergence in function.

The specific oversight topology remains a downstream architectural choice.

### Classification

**C1 / C8**

# 8. Majority Limits and Minority Protection

## Counterfactual

Majority preference cannot by itself legitimate severe unnecessary harm or domination of minorities.

## V1.2

The Concord's constitutional structure does not treat simple numerical preference as sufficient to override its ethical/rights constraints. Peaceful Heterogeneity work further tests the minimum common constraints required while preserving variation and rejects majority preference as a definition of rationality.

## Comparison

Strong functional compatibility and convergence, though the counterfactual does not independently select the exact V1.2 decision machinery.

### Classification

**C1 / C2**

# 9. Peaceful Heterogeneity

## Counterfactual

Where multiple arrangements can coexist without serious unjustified externalities, governance should not impose uniformity merely for administrative simplicity.

## V1.2

Peaceful Heterogeneity explicitly begins from the question of what minimum common constraints are necessary while leaving everything outside them free to vary. It seeks the largest defensible interior space for autonomous variation.

## Comparison

Strong functional convergence.

The counterfactual reaches this through U3 plus harm/externality constraints rather than beginning from an explicit named doctrine of peaceful heterogeneity.

### Classification

**C1**

---

# 10. Externalities and Local Autonomy

## Counterfactual

Local autonomy ends where serious unjustified external domination begins.

## V1.2

The Peaceful Heterogeneity work explicitly distinguishes self-regarding variation from involuntary external effects and asks which constraints must remain common where one community's rules would dominate others.

## Comparison

Strong convergence.

### Classification

**C1**

# 11. Consent and Authority

## Counterfactual

Consent strengthens legitimacy but cannot be the sole governance test. Unanimity is not always possible where genuine externalities exist, while nominal consent under domination is insufficient.

## V1.2

The reopened governance-development material treats consent as important but context-sensitive and explicitly distinguishes consent, harm, capacity and external effects. It does not reduce all legitimate authority to simple individual permission.

## Comparison

Functional convergence.

# 12. V1.2 Machinery Not Independently Regenerated

The canonical V1.2 governance document contains extensive concrete architecture beyond the frozen U-derived result.

Depending upon the section, this includes detailed institutional roles, procedural relationships, operational governance structures and interfaces with other Concord systems.

EKC-02 did **not** independently regenerate a unique equivalent of every such mechanism.

That absence is important.

U1–U8 generated a strong **legitimacy architecture**, but not a complete constitutional-governmental blueprint.

This supports the frozen underdetermination result.

### Classification

Predominantly **C8 — Non-Kernel Dependency**, with **C2** where alternative mechanisms could perform equivalent functions.

The detailed V1.2 machinery appears to depend on additional design work, empirical assumptions, systems engineering, constitutional choices and historical development.

# 13. Does U1–U8 Generate Democracy?

No such conclusion is supported by EKC-02.

The kernel generated:

- participant agency;
- affected-participant input;
- challenge;
- accountability;
- limits on domination;
- bounded authority;
- correction;
- minority protection;
- functional legitimacy.

Those requirements could constrain a democratic architecture, but the experiment did not establish that one particular voting or representation system uniquely follows.

This is an important negative result and should be preserved.

# 14. U8 Assessment

The comparison strengthens the pre-comparison conclusion.

V1.2 already contains function-sensitive sunset and authority termination despite being developed from the earlier kernel.

Therefore U8 is **not uniquely necessary** to produce bounded authority or inherent sunset.

However, U8 makes a broader distinction particularly explicit:

> preserve the legitimate cooperative function, not necessarily the institution currently performing it.

That strengthens succession, regeneration and long-horizon continuity reasoning.

Thus EKC-02 suggests:

- **bounded authority:** recoverable without an explicit U8 root;
- **authority sunset:** also recoverable compositionally;
- **functional continuity across institutional replacement:** especially strengthened by U8.

This distinction should be carried forward to EKC-08.

# 17. Contamination Assessment

EKC-02 has unusually high contamination risk.

The existing Concord contains documents whose titles and concepts closely resemble what the U1–U8 derivation generated, and the assisting reasoner had prior familiarity with this work.

Therefore this case should **not** be treated as strong independent evidence that an unfamiliar reasoner would necessarily derive the same authority architecture.

What the case does establish internally is narrower:

> given the frozen neutral problem and U1–U8 reasoning recorded before comparison, the resulting architecture is highly congruent with existing V1.2 authority-legitimacy work.

This makes EKC-02 a high-priority candidate for later fresh-instance or cross-model replication.

# 18. Cross-Case Signal After EKC-01 and EKC-02

Two cases are now complete.

Both show a similar pattern:

1. U1–U8 regenerates a large fraction of the **functional constraints** of V1.2.
2. U1–U8 does not uniquely regenerate all **implementation machinery**.
3. Some V1.2 mechanisms appear to be secondary systems innovations rather than direct ethical deductions.
4. U8 strengthens temporal and regenerative reasoning, but functions resembling U8 can already emerge compositionally from the earlier ethical architecture.
5. No direct ethical tension has yet appeared.

Two cases are far too few for aggregate conclusions, but this recurring distinction between **ethical architecture** and **implementation architecture** should be monitored prospectively.

It must not become an assumption imposed on later cases.
