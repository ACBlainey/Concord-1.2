# 2. Purpose of Adjudication

## U1–U8

Adjudication seeks warranted truth, protection, attributable accountability, proportionate response, repair, recurrence reduction and correction.

## V1.2

The Judiciary describes justice as more than verdict. It includes process, evidence, reasoning, authority, rights, participation, challenge, independence, accessibility and civilisational learning.

It distinguishes ordinary adjudication from constitutional adjudication and gives the Judiciary both an immediate adjudicative function and an enduring stewardship function.

## Comparison

Very strong functional convergence.

V1.2 is more constitutionally elaborated because it must interface with Governance, constitutional interpretation and public authority.

### Classification

**C1 / C8**

# 3. Evidence, Allegation and Uncertainty

## U1–U8

Frozen requirements included:

- allegation ≠ finding;
- evidence proportionate to consequence;
- visible uncertainty;
- evidence power asymmetry safeguards;
- decision provenance;
- correction when new evidence appears.

## V1.2

The Judiciary explicitly distinguishes evidence, allegation, inference, assumption, interpretation, expert opinion, model output, probability and speculation.

It requires evidential provenance and distinguishes what evidence exists from what the Court infers.

It explicitly rejects manufactured certainty while also rejecting paralysis through impossible certainty requirements.

## Comparison

Exceptionally strong convergence.

# 4. Finality and Correctability

## U1–U8

Finality cannot convert known error into legitimate truth. Material new evidence must permit correction.

## V1.2

The Judiciary explicitly distinguishes **procedural finality** from **epistemic certainty** and states:

> Finality is a procedural property, not proof of infallibility.

It then develops controlled reopening for new evidence, fraud, corruption, procedural failure, systemic evidential failure, jurisdictional invalidity and defective technological/forensic systems.

## Comparison

Strong convergence in principle.

V1.2 contains a more developed balance between endless litigation and correction.

### Classification

**C1 / C2**

# 10. Judicial Independence

The U derivation required independent truth-seeking and accountability but did not spontaneously develop a detailed doctrine separating:

> judicial judgement

from:

> judicial architecture.

V1.2 does.

It protects judgement from political, economic, institutional, technological and internal judicial command while preserving reviewability of the architecture itself.

This is an important additional safeguard.

Without such a distinction, “accountability” could become a route for outcome control.

### Classification

**C2 / C8**

This is a significant resilience addition.

# 11. Independent Reasoning and Correlated Failure

- common evidence from common interpretation;

# 15. Safeguard Accumulation Failure

> **Every safeguard can become a failure mechanism.**

This directly addresses the counter-hypothesis that “more safeguards” might make V1.2 worse.

## RD-4 — Functional Necessity

Several additions address genuine vulnerabilities not adequately resolved merely by stating the root ethical requirement.

Examples:

- “be epistemically corrigible” does not itself prevent three judges from sharing one defective source;
- “be accountable” does not by itself prevent accountability from becoming political outcome control;
- “allow appeal” does not prevent wealthy actors weaponising endless appeals;
- “add oversight” does not prevent the overseer being captured;
- “add safeguards” does not prevent safeguard accumulation from making justice inaccessible;
- “use independent judges” does not prevent common AI or evidence infrastructure from correlating their errors.

Therefore at least some V1.2 additions appear functionally non-trivial.

## RD-5 — Alternative Solution

U1–U8 does not uniquely require V1.2's mechanisms.

For example:

- correlated failure could be addressed without triads;
- dissent could be preserved through other epistemic architectures;
- capture could be constrained by alternative institutional designs;
- continuity could use different distributed recovery structures.

Thus the evidence supports **resilience-function convergence as a possibility**, not uniqueness of V1.2 mechanisms.

## RD-6 — Safeguard Cost

V1.2 itself identifies costs and failure risks from its safeguards:

- complexity;
- delay;
- procedural burden;
- conformity incentives;
- centralisation;
- accessibility loss;
- recursive oversight;
- technology dependence;
- duplicated procedure.

This is important evidence against treating safeguard quantity as resilience.

## RD-7 — Maturity Attribution

The observed differential is presently better explained by **developmental maturity** than by root-kernel difference.

The first-pass U architecture generated many ethical constraints and several safeguards.

V1.2 has subsequently been exposed to repeated rounds of:

- edge cases;
- failure analysis;
- capture analysis;
- technology analysis;
- continuity analysis;
- adversarial reasoning;
- recursive correction.

EKC-03 therefore supports the hypothesis that mature resilience architecture can be a second-order product of repeated failure exposure.

It does **not** establish that Blainey's Laws intrinsically generate more resilience than U1–U8.

# 23. Resilience-Hypothesis Status After EKC-03

**Provisional support increased.**

EKC-03 is the first prospective case and shows exactly the pattern predicted:

1. the roots regenerate much of the ethical architecture;
2. V1.2 contains additional failure safeguards;

Later EKC cases must continue collecting the same RD-1 through RD-7 observations.

A particularly important future test would be to subject a frozen U-derived architecture to a deliberately comparable adversarial-development cycle and determine whether it independently regenerates equivalent resilience functions.
