# 3. V1.2 Foundational Position

The V1.2 intercivilisational README states its guiding idea directly:

> **Interoperability without forced internal uniformity.**

It also explicitly separates:

- common protocol from common government;
- wrapper adoption from Concord oversight;
- design authorship from operational authority;
- technical compatibility from political subordination.

The long-term objective is to determine whether substantially different societies can become:

> **neighbours rather than competitors for universal conformity.**

This is very strong convergence with the frozen U architecture.

Because these broad concepts were known before EKC-10, their rediscovery is not strong evidence of cognitive independence. They remain strong evidence that the two architectures occupy the same functional region.

---

# 4. Internal Difference Versus Boundary Compatibility

V1.2's foundational wrapper paper explicitly distinguishes:

**internal compatibility**

from:

**boundary compatibility.**

A civilisation need not become internally Concordian merely to communicate, trade or cooperate.

The frozen U derivation independently framed the same problem as:

**difference without impunity; interaction without absorption.**

Both architectures place the principal interoperability burden at the interface rather than demanding internal constitutional sameness.

This is the central EKC-10 convergence.

---

# 5. Domain-Scoped Cooperation

The frozen U derivation rejected a binary choice between isolation and integration and generated **Domain-Scoped Cooperation**.

V1.2 explicitly treats compatibility as interaction-specific rather than binary.

Its preliminary progression is:

**Recognition → Communication → Transaction → Movement → Shared Systems → Deep Cooperation**

and it states that progress is neither mandatory nor necessarily permanent.

The frozen U Cooperation Gradient was:

**Awareness → Non-Aggression → Communication → Limited Exchange → Domain Cooperation → Shared Infrastructure → Deep Interoperability → Optional Institutional Integration.**

The structures are strongly convergent.

The fresh U version is somewhat broader at both ends: it begins before formal recognition and makes optional institutional integration explicit as a possible but non-required endpoint.

---

# 6. Interoperability Without Sovereignty Transfer

V1.2 repeatedly states that wrapper architecture does not govern the relationship and does not create authority merely through authorship, neutrality or competence.

The frozen U derivation generated:

> **shared protocol does not imply shared sovereignty.**

It also warned of **Interface Sovereignty**, where a shared interface expands into unagreed superior government.

V1.2 contains a dedicated **Wrapper Non-Governance and Optional Mediation Principle**, making this safeguard substantially more explicit and developed.

Strong convergence; mature V1.2 has greater institutional specification.

---

# 7. Interface Minimisation and V1.2 Minimum Common Architecture

The frozen U derivation generated:

## **Interface-Minimisation Principle**

> Use the least integrative shared architecture sufficient for the cooperative function.

V1.2's foundational wrapper paper states that the wrapper should seek the **minimum common architecture necessary for interaction rather than demanding unnecessary internal convergence**.

This is extremely close functional convergence.

The U formulation makes the anti-integration-ratchet logic particularly explicit.

---

# 8. Peaceful Distance

The frozen U derivation treated Peaceful Distance as a legitimate outcome rather than failed integration.

V1.2 explicitly places Peaceful Distance within the architecture and states that legitimate differences may remain separated or bounded while peaceful and useful interaction continues.

Recognition work further shows that relations can remain domain-specific even under serious political disagreement: communication, humanitarian interface, diplomatic recognition, alliance and intervention need not collapse into a single binary status.

This strongly supports frozen prediction P3.

---

# 9. Recognition Is Not Endorsement

The U derivation generated:

**recognition ≠ ethical approval.**

V1.2 develops this distinction extensively in the emergent-polity work:

- diplomatic communication ≠ sovereign recognition;
- operational contact ≠ political endorsement;
- wrapper compatibility ≠ diplomatic recognition;
- technical interoperability ≠ political endorsement;
- portable-module adoption ≠ Concord political recognition.

This is a mature, highly articulated version of the same architecture.

---

# 10. Recognition Has Resolution

V1.2 contributes an important developed mechanism beyond the fresh U derivation:

> **Recognition has resolution.**

It proposes a spectrum from observed collective entity through forms of political recognition to sovereign statehood, without claiming these must be sequential.

The U derivation generated recognition without endorsement and Progressive Intercivilisational Trust, but did not develop political recognition resolution as deeply.

This is a genuine V1.2 architectural enrichment.

---

# 11. Translation and Non-Equivalence

The frozen U derivation generated a Translation Layer and specifically **Loss-Aware Translation**:

> where concepts do not map cleanly, the mismatch itself must remain visible.

The V1.2 folder architecture explicitly reserves a domain for Rights Translation and Recognition, and the developed recognition material carefully distinguishes concepts that must not be collapsed:

- sentience ≠ political community;
- political community ≠ sovereignty;
- rights violation ≠ secession;
- grievance ≠ proposed remedy;
- physical location ≠ infrastructure location ≠ data location ≠ agent location ≠ civil jurisdiction.

This strongly supports the underlying U principle that translation must preserve non-equivalence.

However, the exact **Loss-Aware Translation** abstraction remains a useful V1.2a compression and should be retained for reintegration review.

---

# 12. Protective Recognition Without Ontological Merger

The frozen U architecture asked how to protect entities where civilisations disagree about personhood without requiring identical metaphysics.

V1.2 directly encounters this problem in sentient asylum and emergent-polity recognition.

It explicitly rejects allowing a foreign legal classification to determine Concord sentient standing:

> foreign legal classification does not automatically determine whether the Concord may regard an intelligence as a person.

At the same time it rejects the opposite shortcut:

> sentience does not automatically establish sovereignty.

This is strong functional convergence with **Protective Recognition Without Ontological Merger**.

V1.2 is more developed in political-status decomposition.

---

# 13. Jurisdictional Gaps and Cross-Boundary Legibility

The frozen U derivation identified **Jurisdictional Gap** and **Jurisdictional Overlap**.

V1.2's wrapper architecture devotes substantial attention to jurisdictional transitions, especially the distinction among physical geography, infrastructure geography, data location, agent location and civil jurisdiction.

It also develops digital ports, digital civil presence, special jurisdictions and cross-boundary movement.

The designated folder structure explicitly reserves a domain for disputes, jurisdiction and externalities, although some of that subfolder remains early-stage.

Thus the problem is strongly present, while mature V1.2 contains substantially more implementation-specific multisubstrate geography.

---

# 14. Externality Boundary

The frozen U result stated:

> the stronger and more direct an action's effects outside a civilisation's legitimate internal domain, the weaker the claim that the matter is exclusively internal.

V1.2 explicitly identifies shared resources, cross-boundary externalities and external responsibility as wrapper domains and recognition criteria.

The high-level principle is therefore strongly compatible.

The exact **Externality Boundary Principle** remains a useful U-derived compression rather than a demonstrated unique V1.2 mechanism.

---

# 15. Dispute Resolution Without Automatic Supremacy

The U derivation predicted mutually accepted dispute processes rather than automatic supremacy of one civilisation's courts.

V1.2's README makes mediation optional and explicitly states that neutrality, competence or authorship does not manufacture authority to mediate.

The developed low-trust summit work provides functionally bounded negotiation architecture rather than assuming universal government.

This strongly confirms prediction P2 and supports the fresh U anti-supremacy constraint.

---

# 16. Capability Asymmetry and Dependency Leverage

The frozen U derivation gave substantial weight to **Dependency Leverage**:

> a stronger civilisation may control critical infrastructure or capabilities and use withdrawal to extract unrelated concessions even where formal consent exists.

V1.2's wider wrapper architecture strongly emphasises bounded authority, responsibility, minimum necessary capability and non-subordination. It is therefore structurally compatible with this concern.

However, in the sampled intercivilisational materials, the fresh U derivation makes **dependency leverage as a distinct cross-civilisational domination mechanism** more explicit than the material examined.

Under ESCP this is not a claim of corpus-wide absence.

It is a candidate V1.2a contribution deserving later targeted review.

---

# 17. Exit Continuity

The frozen U derivation generated:

## **Exit Continuity**

> Cooperation should be designed so that a party can leave or reduce integration without avoidable catastrophic dependency failure.

V1.2 clearly treats interaction depth as non-mandatory and non-permanent and strongly protects autonomy.

Its broader architecture also values replacement capability and non-indispensability.

However, the sampled intercivilisational documents do not make **civilisation-to-civilisation exit continuity** as explicit a named design requirement as the fresh U derivation.

This is therefore another potentially useful fork contribution.

It should not yet be labelled a V1.2 omission without a dedicated corpus search.

---

# 18. Integration Ratchet

The frozen U failure mode **Integration Ratchet** is:

> every successful cooperative layer is assumed to justify deeper integration.

V1.2 already states that progress through its interoperability sequence is neither mandatory nor necessarily permanent.

It therefore contains the direct antidote.

The fresh U label is nevertheless useful because it identifies the failure mechanism explicitly.

This parallels EKC-09's Emergency Success Trap:

**success within a bounded function does not prove that expansion of the mechanism is desirable.**

This may be a more general V1.2a pattern.

---

# 19. Assistance Without Annexation

The U derivation scales the EKC-06 support/non-paternalism principle to civilisations:

> help does not create ownership.

V1.2's wrapper non-governance principle, sentient-asylum work and non-intervention distinctions are strongly compatible.

The recognition paper explicitly separates moral standing, recognition of oppression, intervention authority and military alliance.

This is strong convergence.

---

# 20. Unknown Civilisation and Progressive Trust

The U derivation generated **Progressive Intercivilisational Trust**:

observation → low-risk communication → reversible interaction → limited dependency → deeper cooperation only as evidence supports it.

V1.2 contains multisubstrate/nonhuman wrapper domains and low-trust/hostile interoperability work, but the sampled material does not establish an exact equivalent developmental sequence.

This is therefore best classified as a plausible fresh U architecture requiring later targeted comparison rather than a demonstrated gap.

---

# 21. Heterogeneity as Resilience and Discovery Infrastructure

The U derivation suggested that civilisational heterogeneity may preserve:

- alternative knowledge;
- technologies;
- institutional experiments;
- epistemic methods;
- independent failure modes.

This extends the V1.2a finding that cognitive heterogeneity can act as search-space insurance.

V1.2 strongly protects peaceful difference and rejects universal conformity, but the sampled intercivilisational material primarily justifies heterogeneity through autonomy, legitimacy and interoperability.

The explicit framing of **civilisational heterogeneity as resilience/discovery infrastructure** appears to be a meaningful V1.2a hypothesis.

It should remain prospective until tested more broadly.

# 26. Implication for the Fork Experiment

EKC-10 is an especially important final case because the problem occurs at the scale of whole civilisations.

The alternative U1–U8 branch again does not drift toward universal government or forced ethical homogenisation.

Instead it regenerates a recognisably Concordian architecture:

**plural internal systems + bounded interfaces + voluntary/domain-specific cooperation + non-domination + peaceful distance + correction.**

This makes the repeated convergence across EKC-01 to EKC-10 harder to explain as merely one narrow policy similarity.

However, HIGH contamination and common problem framing mean the result still cannot establish that the architecture is uniquely entailed by U1–U8.

The appropriate conclusion is evidence of a recurring architectural basin, not proof of a unique attractor.

---

# 27. Final EKC-10 Result

> **EKC-10 shows strong functional and substantial architectural convergence between the U1–U8 fork and V1.2. Both reject forced internal uniformity and instead place interoperability at civilisational boundaries, allowing cooperation to vary by domain while preserving peaceful distance and political autonomy. Both separate shared protocol from shared sovereignty, recognition from endorsement, communication from political recognition and technical compatibility from authority. Mature V1.2 contains much deeper wrapper engineering, digital jurisdiction, mobility, asylum, secession, political-recognition resolution and adversarial safeguards. The fresh U branch contributes useful abstractions around loss-aware translation, externality boundaries, dependency leverage, exit continuity, relationship reversibility and integration ratchets. No major root-level conflict was identified.**
