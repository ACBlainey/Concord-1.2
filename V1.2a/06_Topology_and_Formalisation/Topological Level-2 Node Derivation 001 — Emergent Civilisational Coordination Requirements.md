# Topological Level-2 Node Derivation 001 — Emergent Civilisational Coordination Requirements

**Status:** V1.2a FROZEN PRE-COMPARISON DERIVATION
**Date:** September 2026
**Canonical effect:** NONE
**Comparison state:** Concord coverage deliberately not assessed in this document
**Parent:** Topological Level-1 Node Derivation 001 — Cross-System Civilisational Requirements
**Evaluation convention:** For later coverage testing, the current V1.2a topology/development-control architecture is treated as if already part of the civilisation being evaluated.

# 1. Purpose

Level 0 identified ten root architectural positions. Level 1 derived twelve cross-system positions. Subsequent analysis indicates all twelve positions are occupied when the present V1.2a topology/development-control architecture is included, although some remain unevenly mature or dependency-blocked.

This document moves outward one further abstraction level and asks:

> **When the root systems and their cross-system coordination architecture operate together as a civilisation, what further persistent functions become necessary to keep the civilisation coherent, corrigible, plural, adaptive and non-sovereign at the whole-system level?**

This is a derivation, not a search for things already present in Concord.

# 2. Derivation and Admission Rule

Let L0 = {A1,...,A10} and L1 = {L1-01,...,L1-12}. Derive L2 = Phi(L0,L1,E,U), where E contains materially relevant interactions and U is the U1-U8 ethical kernel.

L2 candidates arise from coordination-of-coordination, whole-civilisation consequence, cross-scale coherence, or persistent emergent requirements not adequately reducible to one L0/L1 owner.

A candidate is promoted only if it is **necessary, irreducible, architecturally distinct, persistent and non-duplicative**. A relation, invariant, constraint, state or mode is not automatically a new system.

# 3. L2-01 — Whole-Civilisation Coherence and Contradiction Resolution

Individually legitimate systems can produce mutually incompatible outputs: conflicting obligations, continuity versus legitimate change, resource allocation versus participant protection, emergency versus ordinary participation, or incompatible corrected states.

**Required function:** detect and route materially incompatible civil states, obligations, authorities or system outputs toward legitimate resolution without allowing one coordination layer to become universally sovereign. It must distinguish genuine contradiction, different scopes/contexts, legitimate plurality, temporal supersession and unresolved conflict.

# 4. L2-02 — Cross-Scale Invariant Preservation

Civilisation operates across participant, local system, institution, system family, civilisation and intercivilisational scales. A safeguard can survive locally but disappear at aggregation.

**Required function:** preserve applicable civilisational invariants across changes of scale while allowing implementations to differ. Candidate invariants include bounded authority, non-domination, contestability, correction, provenance, replaceability, uncertainty representation and legitimate standing.

# 5. L2-03 — Plural Model Reconciliation and Shared Civil Reference

Topology, state, epistemic, translation and dependency systems may maintain legitimate but non-identical representations. Coordination sometimes nevertheless requires sufficiently shared reference.

**Required function:** permit plural legitimate models while establishing the minimum shared civil reference required for a particular consequence, without making one model metaphysically sovereign.

# 6. L2-04 — Consequence-Relevant Civil Reconciliation

Distributed systems can act legitimately under partial information, delay, partition, divergent state or different local ordering. Reconnection can expose incompatible but defensible civil histories.

**Required function:** reconcile divergent civil histories only to the degree necessary for legitimate consequence, preserving provenance, concurrency, uncertainty, branch history, partial ordering, contestability and bounded consequence.

# 7. L2-05 — Civilisational Legibility Without Total Surveillance

The development-control, state, dependency, failure and learning functions require information about civilisation. If this requires comprehensive observation, the architecture undermines agency and privacy.

**Required function:** make civilisation sufficiently legible to itself for coordination, correction, learning and continuity while preserving purpose limitation, privacy and distributed information boundaries. Required Visibility != Total Visibility.

# 8. L2-06 — Distributed Trust and Verifiability

A plural civilisation cannot assume one universally trusted observer, metric provider, state authority, translator, provenance service or topology owner, yet consequential claims sometimes require verification.

**Required function:** permit sufficient verification without universal trust in one actor/service, including provenance verification, bounded evidence, independent corroboration, contestability, uncertainty, privacy-preserving proof where required and replaceable trust providers.

# 9. L2-07 — Civilisational Change Legitimacy

L1 development systems can identify deficits and develop solutions, but Ability to Improve != Authority to Change.

**Required function:** distinguish diagnosis, proposal, experimentation, validation, legitimate adoption, constitutional change, implementation and reversal, ensuring self-development does not become self-authorising government.

# 10. L2-08 — Diversity Preservation Under Optimisation

A civilisation capable of measuring itself and selecting priorities can optimise away minority needs, unusual participants, low-frequency risks, alternative architectures, local variation, experimental branches and low-connectivity/high-consequence problems.

**Required function:** preserve justified civil diversity and option space while permitting coordinated development.

# 11. L2-09 — Civilisational Reversibility and Safe Experimentation

A self-developing civilisation must test uncertain changes. Correction after deployment can be insufficient where intervention destroys prior state, eliminates alternatives or creates irreversible dependency.

**Required function:** provide bounded experimentation, reversibility, rollback, containment and explicit treatment of irreversible transitions. Irreversibility triggers stronger evidence and legitimacy requirements.

# 12. L2-10 — Multi-Horizon Stewardship and Intergenerational Continuity

Immediate participant need, institutional stability, infrastructure replacement, ecological stewardship, knowledge continuity and long-term survival operate on different timescales.

**Required function:** coordinate materially different temporal horizons without treating present participants or future continuity as automatically overriding, representing short-term need, medium-term development, long-term dependency, irreversible depletion, delayed harm, future capability and uncertainty.

# 13. L2-11 — Civilisational Identity, Membership and Boundary Continuity

A corrigible, replaceable, multisubstrate civilisation undergoing institutional change requires a way to determine continuity through transformation. Participation, exit, migration, new substrates and intercivilisational relations also create membership/standing questions.

**Required function:** maintain sufficiently stable civil identity and membership continuity without freezing institutional form, distinguishing civilisation from institution, participant identity from substrate, membership from location, exit from erasure, succession from capture and legitimate transformation from unrelated replacement.

# 14. L2-12 — Civilisational Self-Limitation and Non-Sovereign Coordination

The combined L1 architecture is powerful. Mapping dependencies, measuring maturity, representing state, coordinating interfaces, prioritising development, tracking failure and supporting correction could become a de facto command layer.

**Required function:** prevent informational/developmental centrality from becoming general civil authority. Knowledge of the Civilisation != Sovereignty over the Civilisation. Development Leverage != Civil Authority.

# 15. L2-13 — Unknown-State and Epistemic Boundary Management

At whole-civilisation scale, some dependencies, states, effects, identities, translations and future consequences remain unknown. Integrated representations can create a dangerous appearance of completeness.

**Required function:** preserve explicit boundaries between known, inferred, disputed, stale, inaccessible, not applicable, unknown and unknown-but-material across topology, state, metrics, governance, development and continuity.

# 16. L2-14 — Civilisational Coordination Under Partial Failure

The L1 coordination architecture itself can fail: topology can be stale, state maps unavailable, communications partitioned, metrics unreliable, planners unavailable, translation incomplete, dependency graphs wrong or oversight delayed.

**Required function:** define graceful degradation and bounded local autonomy when whole-civilisation coordination infrastructure is incomplete/unavailable, plus reconnection and later reconciliation.

# 17. Candidates Not Promoted

Machine readability, universal ontology, global clock, central civil database, single identity system, single optimiser, universal decision-maker and one authoritative world model are not promoted. They are implementations or architectures the topology should avoid requiring.

**Machine readability is specifically downstream of a sufficiently mature topology/formalisation model.** It is not an independent L2 root requirement. Emergency likewise remains a mode rather than being duplicated at L2.

# 19. Dependency-Aware Coverage States

The later comparison should use: PRESENT-EXPLICIT; PRESENT-DISTRIBUTED; INTERFACE-SATISFIED; PRESENT / DEPENDENCY-BLOCKED; PARTIAL; CANDIDATE-ABSENT; UNKNOWN; DISPUTED.

PRESENT / DEPENDENCY-BLOCKED is necessary because an occupied node may be unable to complete its next developmental step until a legitimate upstream capability matures. This must not be confused with architectural absence.

# 20. Coverage Boundary

The later L2 coverage test evaluates the civilisation as C* = V1.2 + current topology/formalisation/development-control architecture in V1.2a.

This is an explicit experimental convention. It prevents falsely declaring absent the very capability being evaluated. It does not make every speculative V1.2a invention canonical by assumption.
