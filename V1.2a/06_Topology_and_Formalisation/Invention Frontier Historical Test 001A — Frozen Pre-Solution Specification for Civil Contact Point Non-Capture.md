# 1. Purpose

This document performs the blinded derivation phase of Historical Test 001.

The question is:

> Using only the earlier Civil Contact Point architecture and its stated unresolved problems, what functional properties would a solution need in order to prevent civil reachability infrastructure from becoming a monopolistic, coercive or fragile dependency?

The resulting specification is frozen before the later Citizen ID and communications architecture is compared against it.

# 2. Source-Grounded Starting Architecture

The Civil Contact Point source already establishes:

- persistent participant identity;
- a portable civil interface;
- current contact point(s);
- access to relevant services and participation;
- transferability between contact points;
- possible multiple contact points;
- participation that can decrease as well as increase;
- record transfer questions;
- continuity when changing contact points;
- a fallback contact point during disruption;
- Governance, Judiciary and constitutional interfaces.

It also explicitly separates:

Participant Identity
≠ Contact Point
≠ Current Location
≠ Participation Role.

# 3. Source-Grounded Failure Conditions

The source explicitly identifies:

- service dependency;
- political coercion;
- economic abuse;
- contact-point monopolisation;
- digital exclusion;
- surveillance;
- infrastructure collapse.

It states that these require development before implementation.

It also asks:

> Which institution administers contact status?

and:

> How is abuse prevented?

Therefore the unresolved problem is not speculative reconstruction. It is source-recognised.

# 4. Derived Failure Chain

The source permits the following conditional structural chain:

Civil Reachability Depends on Contact Infrastructure
→ Contact Infrastructure Mediates Services / Notices / Participation
→ Administrative or Provider Control Becomes Concentrated
→ Participant Becomes Dependent on the Controlled Interface
→ Exit / Transfer / Privacy / Access Can Become Practically Constrained
→ Service Dependency Can Become Coercion
→ Civil Reachability and Participation Become Vulnerable to Capture or Failure.

This is conditional. The source does not establish that capture must occur.

# 5. Required Preserved Function

The required preserved function is:

> **A participant must remain reliably civilly reachable and able to preserve civil continuity without becoming unnecessarily dependent upon one physical location, one contact endpoint, one provider, one administrator or one technical implementation.**

Call this function:

**Q-CCP-01 — Non-Capturable Civil Reachability.**

# 6. Frozen Functional Specification

A satisfying architecture should demonstrate, where technically and constitutionally possible:

## S1 — Persistent Identity Independence

Civil identity must survive replacement or movement of the contact mechanism.

Required relation:

Identity ≠ Current Contact Mechanism.

## S2 — Endpoint / Contact Replaceability

The participant must be able to change the active contact endpoint without reconstructing civil identity.

## S3 — Multiple or Alternative Reachability Paths

The architecture must not require one structurally indispensable contact path where alternatives are feasible.

This may be satisfied by simultaneous plurality, rapid substitution, fallback paths or another mechanism providing equivalent non-dependence.

## S4 — Transferability of Participant-Controlled Civil State

Changing contact mechanisms must not unnecessarily destroy correspondence, relevant records, continuity or legitimate participation state.

## S5 — Failure Continuity

Loss or failure of a current contact mechanism must not automatically cause loss of civil identity or civil relationship.

## S6 — Provider / Administrator Non-Ownership

Operating a contact mechanism must not give the operator ownership of participant identity, civil standing or the participant's continuing relationship with civilisation.

## S7 — Bounded Administrative Authority

Any institution administering contact status must have explicitly bounded authority.

Control of the interface must not silently expand into general civil authority.

## S8 — Anti-Coercive Exit

A participant must be able to reduce, transfer or terminate the contact relationship without arbitrary deprivation of unrelated civil protections.

## S9 — Privacy Against Unnecessary Location Exposure

Civil reachability should not require unnecessary exposure of current physical location.

This follows directly from the source's privacy research question and separation of identity, contact point and location.

## S10 — Non-Surveillance by Default

The communication/contact function should not unnecessarily become a general surveillance mechanism.

## S11 — Independent Challenge / Remedy

Where administration of the contact interface materially affects identity, notices or rights, the participant requires access to an independent dispute/remedy path.

The source already identifies Judiciary as the relevant layer for such disputes.

## S12 — Operator / Mechanism Replaceability

If a provider, administrator or implementation fails, becomes abusive or becomes captured, civilisation should be able to replace the form while preserving the civil function.

This is stronger than participant transfer alone.

## S13 — Accessibility / Non-Digital Exclusion

The solution must not make practical civil reachability dependent on a mode inaccessible to participants who cannot use a particular digital system.

## S14 — Provenance Across Transfer

Changes of contact mechanism should preserve enough provenance to maintain legitimate continuity and resolve disputes without making the participant permanently traceable beyond necessity.

## S15 — Technology / Implementation Independence

The required civil function should not be unnecessarily coupled to one physical-address model or one communications implementation.

This is derived from the source's existing separation between persistent identity and changing contact points and its application across locations, jurisdictions, planets and disruptions.

# 7. Constraint Set

A candidate solution is inadequate if it achieves reliable reachability only by:

- creating a permanent universal surveillance point;
- binding civil identity to one physical location;
- making one provider indispensable;
- preventing meaningful exit;
- making operator authority equivalent to civil authority;
- destroying continuity when endpoints change;
- requiring exclusive allegiance where unnecessary;
- or excluding participants unable to use one technical modality.

# 8. Minimum Success Pattern

A later solution need not implement one specific technology.

A high-fit solution should provide most of:

Persistent Identity
→ Replaceable Contact/Delivery Mechanism
→ Alternative or Fallback Reachability
→ Continuity Across Change
→ Privacy From Unnecessary Location Exposure
→ Bounded Operator Knowledge/Authority
→ Independent Challenge
→ Replaceability Under Failure/Capture
→ Provenance.

# 9. What This Specification Does Not Predict

The source does not justify predicting any specific mechanism such as:

- quantum communication;
- cryptographic relays;
- particular network protocols;
- blockchain;
- specific identifier formats;
- specific resolver architectures.

Those would be inventions or implementations.

The topology predicts required **properties**, not the later mechanism.
