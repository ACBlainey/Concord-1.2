# EKC-10 — Peaceful Relations Between Ethically or Institutionally Different Civilisations

**Experiment:** V1.2a — U-Kernel Counterfactual Concord Experiment  
**Case:** EKC-10  
**Protocol category:** Relations between distinct communities or civilisations  
**Status:** VERSION 1.0 — FROZEN NEUTRAL PROBLEM PACKET  
**Date:** September 2026

---

# 1. Neutral Problem

Distinct civilisations may coexist while differing substantially in:

- ethical interpretation;
- political institutions;
- law;
- culture;
- economics;
- technology;
- substrate composition;
- concepts of personhood;
- property;
- governance;
- participation;
- collective identity;
- historical experience.

Difference does not by itself establish hostility.

But interaction creates practical problems.

Civilisations may trade, communicate, migrate, share infrastructure, exchange knowledge, cross borders, create joint projects, affect common environments, or encounter disputes involving participants recognised differently by each system.

The central problem is:

> **How should ethically or institutionally different civilisations coexist and interact without requiring internal uniformity, while preventing difference, distance or sovereignty claims from becoming licences for unjustified harm, domination or unaccountable external effects?**

---

# 2. Conditions the Architecture Must Address

The problem includes situations where:

- civilisations disagree about legitimate internal governance;
- one civilisation does not recognise a category of participant recognised by another;
- participants move between jurisdictions;
- activities in one civilisation create effects in another;
- shared resources or environments exist;
- trade requires interoperable rules;
- communication systems differ;
- concepts do not translate cleanly;
- one civilisation is technologically or economically stronger;
- one civilisation wishes minimal contact;
- another seeks extensive cooperation;
- neither accepts the other's internal institutions as authoritative;
- disputes cross jurisdictional boundaries;
- mixed-civilisation projects are created;
- historical conflict reduces trust;
- peaceful coexistence may require distance rather than integration;
- a civilisation changes its internal ethical or institutional system;
- unknown future civilisations or intelligence forms may appear.

---

# 3. Required Distinctions

A valid derivation should distinguish where relevant between:

- coexistence and integration;
- cooperation and assimilation;
- interoperability and common government;
- recognition and endorsement;
- translation and agreement;
- internal autonomy and external effect;
- jurisdiction and sovereignty;
- voluntary distance and exclusion imposed on others;
- peaceful separation and hostility;
- trade access and political membership;
- rights recognition and institutional uniformity;
- shared rules and shared civilisation;
- mutual defence and permanent hierarchy;
- dispute resolution and constitutional supremacy;
- contact protocols and internal governance;
- civilisational self-determination and domination of outsiders.

These distinctions define the problem rather than prescribing a solution.

# 5. Stress Conditions

### S1 — Ethical Difference
Two civilisations disagree substantially about legitimate internal practice.

### S2 — Recognition Asymmetry
Civilisation A recognises a participant category that civilisation B does not.

### S3 — Capability Asymmetry
One civilisation possesses overwhelming technological, economic or military capability.

### S4 — Externality
An internally lawful action in A materially harms B.

### S5 — Migration
A participant leaves one civilisation and seeks entry or membership in another.

### S6 — Mixed Project
Participants from several civilisations jointly create infrastructure or institutions.

### S7 — Translation Failure
Each civilisation has concepts the other cannot map cleanly into its own legal or ethical categories.

### S8 — Voluntary Distance
A civilisation wants peaceful non-integration and very limited contact.

### S9 — Internal Wrong
A civilisation engages in conduct another civilisation considers seriously unethical, but the effects are primarily internal.

### S10 — Cross-Border Wrong
An actor exploits jurisdictional difference to cause harm across boundaries.

### S11 — Disputed Personhood
A being treated as property or tool in one civilisation is treated as a rights-bearing participant in another.

### S12 — Institutional Change
A civilisation changes its constitution or ceases to recognise an earlier agreement.

### S13 — Collapse
One civilisation partially collapses, creating refugees, uncontrolled externalities or uncertain representation.

### S14 — Unknown Civilisation
Contact occurs with an intelligence whose institutions, substrate and ethical concepts are initially poorly understood.

3. How far does internal self-determination extend?

6. How should incompatible rights concepts be handled?

8. Can a civilisation legitimately choose peaceful distance?
9. What distinguishes peaceful distance from hostile exclusion?
10. What happens when one civilisation is vastly more capable?
11. How should cross-civilisational participants be protected from jurisdictional gaps?
12. What happens when concepts cannot be translated exactly?
13. What architecture is needed for shared projects?
14. What happens when agreements become obsolete or one party changes?
15. How should contact with unknown intelligences begin?
16. What information must be exchanged for cooperation to be safe?
17. How should civilisations preserve their autonomy without exporting harm?
18. Can interoperability exist without common government?
19. What happens when cooperation fails?
20. Which requirements follow directly from U1–U8 and which require institutional invention?

# 8. Contamination Note

EKC-10 has **HIGH contamination risk**.

Prior work has exposed general Concord concepts including:

- wrappers as interoperability rather than authority;
- common protocol not implying common government;
- peaceful distance;
- rights translation;
- multisubstrate participation;
- functional authority;
- anti-domination;
- continuity;
- civilisational heterogeneity.

Therefore the experiment cannot treat rediscovery of those labels as strong independent evidence.

The derivation must instead:

- proceed explicitly from U1–U8;
- avoid deliberately reconstructing remembered V1.2 mechanisms;
- generate its own functional architecture;
- freeze it before reopening the comparison domain;
- give greater evidential weight to structural differences, novel abstractions, failure modes and underdetermined areas than to familiar terminology.
