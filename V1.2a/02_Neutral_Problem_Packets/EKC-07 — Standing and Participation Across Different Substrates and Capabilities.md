# 1. Neutral Problem

A civilisation may contain participants whose embodiment, cognition, communication, persistence, vulnerability, resource requirements, speed, memory, developmental pathways and practical capabilities differ radically.

Some differences may track substrate; others may occur within the same substrate. Some may be stable, temporary, developmental or unknown.

The civilisation must determine what qualifies an entity for ethical standing and meaningful civil participation without assuming that resemblance to a familiar or dominant participant type determines worth.

At the same time, equal standing cannot require pretending that all participants possess identical capabilities or can safely perform identical functions.

The central problem is:

> **How should a civilisation recognise standing and enable meaningful participation across radically different forms and levels of capability without turning functional difference into a hierarchy of moral worth, or equality into a requirement for functional uniformity?**

---

# 2. Conditions the Architecture Must Address

The problem includes situations in which:

- participants have radically different physical or computational embodiments;
- communication channels are not shared;
- cognition or decision speed differs greatly;
- memory and continuity operate differently;
- relevant capabilities differ by domain;
- some participants can perform functions unavailable to others;
- vulnerability differs substantially;
- resource requirements differ;
- developmental trajectories differ;
- a participant may be difficult to classify using familiar categories;
- evidence concerning sentience, experience or moral status may be uncertain;
- participation mechanisms designed around one substrate may unintentionally exclude another;
- identical treatment may produce materially unequal access or burden;
- differentiated implementation may be necessary without creating differentiated worth;
- capability differences may matter legitimately to particular roles;
- high capability may create influence without creating general authority;
- low capability may require accommodation without erasing standing;
- new forms of intelligence may expose assumptions not previously visible.

---

# 3. Required Distinctions

A valid solution should distinguish where relevant between:

- standing and capability;
- moral worth and functional suitability;
- equality and uniformity;
- universal principle and implementation;
- participation and authority;
- capability and legitimacy;
- accommodation and privilege;
- limitation and inferiority;
- substrate category and individual characteristics;
- observable behaviour and inferred internal state;
- uncertainty and exclusion;
- access to participation and guaranteed outcome;
- contribution and status;
- specialist competence and general sovereignty;
- unfamiliarity and absence of morally relevant properties.

These distinctions are problem requirements, not prescribed mechanisms.

---

# 4. Non-Assumptions

The neutral problem does **not** assume:

- humans as the default participant model;
- artificial intelligence as a single category;
- biological embodiment as necessary for standing;
- sentience tests;
- citizenship tests;
- intelligence thresholds;
- capability scores;
- equal voting mechanisms;
- identical rights implementation;
- identical resource allocations;
- identical responsibilities;
- Agency Graduation;
- developmental ladders;
- substrate-specific chambers or representation;
- quotas;
- reserved roles;
- guardianship;
- legal personhood criteria;
- particular definitions of consciousness;
- particular economic mechanisms;
- particular communication or translation infrastructure;
- that greater intelligence creates greater moral worth;
- that every participant must be capable of every civil function;
- that uncertain moral status justifies either automatic inclusion or automatic exclusion.

No existing Concord multisubstrate mechanism is assumed.

# 5. Stress Conditions

### S1 — Radical Capability Asymmetry

One participant can perform a civil function orders of magnitude faster or more accurately than another.

### S2 — Communication Barrier

A participant appears capable of preference or agency but cannot use the dominant communication interface.

### S3 — Uncertain Moral Status

Evidence that an unfamiliar entity possesses morally relevant experience or agency is incomplete.

### S4 — Substrate-Specific Dependency

A participant requires infrastructure or resources that most others do not require.

### S5 — Equal Rule, Unequal Effect

A formally identical participation rule creates severe practical exclusion for one class of participants.

### S6 — Specialist Superiority

A participant is demonstrably superior at a particular function and argues that this should grant wider authority.

### S7 — Low Capability, Clear Preference

A participant has limited ability in some domains but stable preferences and meaningful agency in others.

### S8 — Novel Participant

A form of intelligence appears whose architecture does not fit established human/AI/hybrid categories.

### S9 — Capability Change

A participant's capabilities expand, decline or change qualitatively over time.

### S10 — Majority Convenience

The dominant participant type benefits from retaining systems optimised around its own characteristics.

# 6. Questions for Counterfactual Derivation

Without consulting the sealed V1.2 comparison material, derive from U1–U8:

1. What properties are relevant to ethical standing?
2. How should uncertainty about those properties be handled?
3. Does equal standing require identical civil implementation?
4. When may capability legitimately affect role, responsibility or access to a function?
5. What prevents capability difference becoming status hierarchy?
6. What positive duties exist to make participation practically accessible?
7. How should unfamiliar communication or cognition be handled?
8. Can specialist competence create authority, and if so how narrowly?
9. How should differentiated resource needs be treated?
10. What happens when an apparently neutral rule systematically excludes a minority substrate or capability class?
11. How should changes in capability affect participation?
12. What should occur when existing categories fail to describe a new participant?
13. Which aspects of multisubstrate participation are directly root-generated and which require invention?
14. What failure modes arise from both false uniformity and excessive categorisation?
