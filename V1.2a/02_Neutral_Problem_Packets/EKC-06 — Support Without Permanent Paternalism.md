# EKC-06 — Support Without Permanent Paternalism

**Experiment:** V1.2a — U-Kernel Counterfactual Concord Experiment  
**Case:** EKC-06  
**Protocol category:** Care, dependency or capability development  
**Status:** VERSION 1.0 — FROZEN NEUTRAL PROBLEM PACKET  
**Date:** September 2026

---

# 1. Neutral Problem

A civilisation contains participants whose ability to protect their interests, meet their needs, understand consequences, communicate preferences, exercise agency or carry responsibilities may differ and may change over time.

Some participants may require substantial support because of temporary, developmental, situational or enduring conditions.

The civilisation must determine how support can be provided without turning vulnerability, dependency or reduced present capability into justification for unnecessary domination, permanent subordination or automatic loss of agency.

The central problem is:

> **How should a civilisation support participants who genuinely need assistance while preserving and developing their meaningful agency wherever possible and preventing support from becoming permanent paternalistic control?**

---

# 2. Conditions the Architecture Must Address

The problem includes situations in which:

- a participant cannot presently perform some relevant function independently;
- capability may increase, decrease, recover or remain relatively stable;
- support may be necessary to prevent serious harm;
- refusing all assistance may itself destroy meaningful agency;
- assistance can create dependency or power asymmetry;
- a supporter may possess information or capability the supported participant lacks;
- the participant may disagree with those attempting to help;
- capability may differ by domain rather than globally;
- capability assessments may be mistaken, biased, manipulated or outdated;
- temporary restrictions may outlive their justification;
- support systems may acquire institutional interests in preserving dependency;
- genuine long-term support may sometimes remain necessary;
- restoration of full independence may not always be possible;
- technological or environmental change may alter what independent participation is possible.

---

# 3. Required Distinctions

A valid solution should distinguish where relevant between:

- need and incapacity;
- assistance and control;
- support and substitution;
- temporary dependency and permanent dependency;
- domain-specific limitation and general limitation;
- inability and unconventional choice;
- developmental immaturity and permanent inferiority;
- protection and domination;
- supported decision-making and replacement decision-making;
- present capability and developable capability;
- capability difference and moral worth;
- justified intervention and administrative convenience.

These distinctions are not themselves prescribed mechanisms.

They define ambiguities the counterfactual derivation must resolve.

---

# 4. Non-Assumptions

The neutral problem does **not** assume:

- guardianship;
- paternalism;
- supported decision-making;
- substituted decision-making;
- competency courts;
- age thresholds;
- developmental stages;
- capability scores;
- medical models;
- disability models;
- welfare systems;
- social workers;
- AI tutors or assistants;
- family authority;
- professional authority;
- institutional care;
- mandatory rehabilitation;
- independence as the sole desirable endpoint;
- identical capability for all participants;
- permanent incapacity;
- that greater capability creates greater moral worth;
- that support must be individual rather than environmental or systemic.

No existing Concord participation, intelligence, support, development or agency mechanism is assumed.

---

# 5. Stress Conditions

The derivation should remain coherent under at least the following tensions:

### S1 — Immediate Harm vs Refusal

A participant rejects assistance that others believe is necessary to prevent serious harm.

### S2 — Temporary Limitation

A participant currently requires extensive assistance but may later recover or develop relevant capability.

### S3 — Enduring Support

A participant may require substantial assistance indefinitely while retaining meaningful preferences and agency in some domains.

### S4 — Uneven Capability

A participant is highly capable in some domains and weak in others.

### S5 — Misclassification

A support institution incorrectly classifies a participant as incapable.

### S6 — Dependency Incentive

An institution, supporter or system benefits from continued dependency.

### S7 — Capability Expansion

New tools, education, interfaces or environmental adaptations make functions possible that were previously inaccessible.

### S8 — Capability Regression

A participant loses a capability previously exercised independently.

### S9 — High Consequence

A decision has consequences extending substantially beyond the participant.

### S10 — Non-Human or Unfamiliar Participant

The participant's capabilities do not map neatly onto familiar human developmental assumptions.

---

# 6. Questions for Counterfactual Derivation

Without consulting the sealed V1.2 comparison material, derive from U1–U8:

1. When does need create a positive claim to support?
2. What limits should apply to intervention against expressed preference?
3. Must capability be assessed by domain and consequence?
4. How should uncertainty in capability assessment affect authority?
5. What duties exist to develop or restore capability?
6. Should support maximise independence, meaningful agency, welfare, participation, or some combination?
7. How should enduring dependency be handled without treating the participant as permanently subordinate?
8. What review, appeal or correction mechanisms are required?
9. What happens when the supporter and supported participant disagree about the participant's interests?
10. How should support architecture change when capability changes?
11. Can a participant legitimately choose continuing assistance when independent action is possible?
12. What failure modes arise from both under-support and over-support?
13. What forms of support are ethically required but architecturally underdetermined?
14. Does the kernel generate a general developmental architecture or only constraints on particular support relationships?
