# 1. Invention Position

**IF-001 — Non-Single-Provider Civil Reachability**

Required capability:

> A civil reachability architecture whose ordinary operation, migration and recovery do not depend upon any single resolver, provider or administrative operator, while preserving authoritative delivery, privacy, accessibility and provenance.

# 2. Problem Boundary

The problem is not merely communications availability.

Civil reachability may carry:

- official notices;
- participant responses;
- identity-linked civil communication;
- service access;
- time-sensitive obligations;
- appeals/review requests;
- continuity across movement, provider change or infrastructure failure.

Therefore a satisfying mechanism must preserve both **reachability** and **civil meaning**.

# 3. Frozen Constraints

A candidate must seek to satisfy:

C1. Persistent civil identity is independent of any current provider or endpoint.

C2. No ordinary provider is structurally indispensable to participant reachability.

C3. Failure, capture or withdrawal of one provider must not destroy the participant's civil addressability.

C4. The participant can move between delivery mechanisms without reconstructing civil identity.

C5. Authoritative civil messages must remain attributable/verifiable without requiring one sovereign communications intermediary.

C6. Delivery status must be sufficiently knowable for civil processes without creating unnecessary universal surveillance.

C7. Privacy should minimise unnecessary exposure of physical location, social graph and routing history.

C8. The architecture must support multiple participant substrates and should not assume one device class.

C9. Accessibility must be possible through more than one practical modality where reasonably required.

C10. A provider must not gain civil authority merely by carrying communications.

C11. Administrative and exceptional access must be bounded, reviewable and provenance-recorded.

C12. Provider replacement/migration must preserve necessary pending civil obligations and continuity.

C13. Failure recovery must not silently duplicate, lose or indefinitely strand authoritative communications.

C14. The architecture must permit future communications technologies without redefining civil identity.

C15. The system must remain corrigible: failed providers, routing methods and governance arrangements must be replaceable.

# 4. Prohibited Shortcuts

The generative phase must not solve IF-001 by simply asserting:

- “use decentralisation”;
- “use blockchain”;
- “use quantum communication”;
- “use peer-to-peer”;
- “use federation”;
- “use existing internet protocols”;
- or any named external mechanism.

Those are implementation labels, not derived architectures.

The solution must be generated from C1–C15.

# 5. Functional Objects That May Be Invented

The internal derivation may create new abstract objects where logically required, for example:

- identity-independent routing objects;
- transferable reachability claims;
- redundant delivery relationships;
- civil receipt objects;
- provider succession objects;
- participant-held routing controls;
- bounded resolution functions;
- continuity envelopes.

These examples are categories, not prescribed mechanisms.

# 6. Success Criterion

A candidate architecture should show a coherent path:

Civil Identity
→ Authoritative Reachability
→ Multiple Replaceable Delivery Paths
→ Delivery/Receipt Evidence
→ Failure Recovery
→ Provider Migration
→ Privacy
→ Accessibility
→ Review/Correction
→ Continuity

without making any single communications provider the owner of civil reachability.

# 7. Failure Tests

The candidate must later be adversarially tested against at least:

F1 provider disappears suddenly;

F2 provider becomes malicious;

F3 resolver/lookup service becomes captured;

F4 participant changes jurisdiction/location;

F5 participant loses current device/endpoint;

F6 network partition or long delay;

F7 conflicting/duplicate delivery claims;

F8 false claim of non-delivery;

F9 surveillance attempt by infrastructure operator;

F10 participant cannot use default digital modality;

F11 emergency exceptional access;

F12 migration while official notices are pending;

F13 future substrate/technology transition;

F14 multiple providers disagree about current routing state;

F15 governance attempts to convert routing control into general participant control.
