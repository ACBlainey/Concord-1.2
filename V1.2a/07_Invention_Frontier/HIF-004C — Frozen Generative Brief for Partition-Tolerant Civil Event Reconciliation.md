# HIF-004C — Frozen Generative Brief for Partition-Tolerant Civil Event Reconciliation

**Status:** V1.2a SECOND-ORDER GENERATIVE TEST / FROZEN PRE-SOLUTION SPECIFICATION  
**Date:** September 2026  
**External knowledge state:** E0 — NO EXTERNAL SEARCH PERFORMED  
**Canonical effect:** NONE

# 1. Problem

Two or more legitimate civil actors or systems may begin from a sufficiently shared civil state and then temporarily lose the ability to observe one another's current events.

During that period, each may continue performing actions that are locally legitimate.

When communication resumes, civilisation must not assume that:

- one history is automatically false;
- the later-observed history is automatically subordinate;
- duplicated records imply duplicated entitlement;
- one operator may silently choose the authoritative past;
- reconciliation permits historical erasure;
- infrastructure failure may simply be assigned to participants as fault.

# 2. Required Preserved Function

**Q-HIF004-01 — Legitimate Divergence Reconciliation**

> Preserve and reconcile independently legitimate consequential civil events created during temporary state divergence while preventing silent loss, duplicate consequence, fabricated agreement, unilateral history control and unjust participant consequence arising solely from infrastructure failure.

# 3. Frozen Functional Requirements

R1 — **Independent History Preservation**  
Each legitimate event history remains recoverable until reconciliation is complete.

R2 — **Event Provenance**  
Material events retain origin, actor/system, relevant authority, time/context and evidentiary status.

R3 — **No Silent Overwrite**  
Reconciliation cannot make a conflicting material event disappear without an explicit disposition.

R4 — **Legitimate Divergence ≠ Fraud**  
Difference created by partition/delay is not itself evidence of wrongdoing.

R5 — **Claim-Specific Consequence**  
Shared or duplicated history does not automatically duplicate exclusive property, authority, obligation, payment, status or other scarce/consequential civil effects.

R6 — **Infrastructure-Fault Protection**  
A participant should not suffer avoidable adverse civil consequence merely because authorised infrastructure could not maintain synchrony.

R7 — **Bounded Provisional Operation**  
Where safe, necessary civil functions may continue during divergence under explicitly bounded/provisional status rather than forcing total civil paralysis.

R8 — **Reconnection Detection**  
The architecture must recognise when previously divergent histories become mutually observable enough to trigger reconciliation.

R9 — **Conflict Classification**  
Post-reconnection differences must be classifiable at least into compatible, duplicate, competing-exclusive, inconsistent, disputed, invalid/unauthorised, and unresolved classes.

R10 — **Non-Sovereign Reconciliation**  
No ordinary node/operator acquires permanent sovereignty merely because it performs reconciliation.

R11 — **Contestability**  
Affected participants can inspect/challenge consequential reconciliation decisions to an appropriate degree.

R12 — **Correction Without Erasure**  
Later correction modifies civil consequence while preserving the historical record of prior states and decisions.

R13 — **Irreducible Conflict Escalation**  
Conflicts not safely resolvable by bounded rules are routed to competent review/adjudication rather than silently forced into automated agreement.

R14 — **Continuity During Pending Resolution**  
The system identifies which functions can safely continue and which state-changing consequences must pause while a conflict remains unresolved.

R15 — **Function-over-Implementation**  
The civil reconciliation function must not depend constitutionally upon one technical substrate or provider.

R16 — **Privacy Proportionality**  
Reconciliation uses no more participant information than required to resolve the consequential conflict.

R17 — **Reconciliation Provenance**  
The reconciliation itself records inputs, classifications, rules, unresolved uncertainty, decisions, corrections and review path.

R18 — **Recursive Anti-Capture**  
The reconciliation mechanism is itself reviewable, correctable, replaceable and bounded.

# 4. Inherited Internal Concord Relations

The generative attempt may use these already-established internal relations:

- Provenance ≠ Truth.
- Evidence ≠ Authority.
- Shared History ≠ Shared Liability.
- Authentication Success ≠ Unique Current Entitlement.
- Function should outlive implementation.
- Temporary need should normally produce temporary capability.
- Correction ≠ Erasure.
- Divergence can be legitimate.
- No majority / insufficient authority should not silently create a state-changing action.
- Contestability requires an effective route into review.
- Infrastructure and oversight functions do not acquire sovereignty merely through operational importance.

# 5. Prohibited Shortcuts

The generative phase must not:

- search external distributed-systems literature;
- import named external reconciliation algorithms or protocols;
- begin from a known external technical architecture and relabel it;
- assume perfect synchrony;
- solve the problem by appointing one permanently authoritative master node;
- solve the problem by discarding one legitimate history;
- solve the problem by freezing all civil action during every communication interruption;
- equate duplicate records with duplicate civil rights/consequences;
- conceal unresolved conflict.

# 6. Required Adversarial Cases

A candidate architecture must later be tested against at least:

C1 two compatible non-exclusive events;
C2 duplicated recording of one event;
C3 two locally authorised claims to one exclusive resource;
C4 conflicting official notices issued during partition;
C5 payment/obligation performed on one side but not visible on the other;
C6 participant changes contact/identity-routing state during partition;
C7 one side acts maliciously while another diverges legitimately;
C8 clocks/order information disagree;
C9 one history is incomplete after reconnection;
C10 reconciliation operator is compromised;
C11 reconnection occurs only partially;
C12 participant cannot access the default reconciliation channel;
C13 emergency action occurs during partition;
C14 later evidence invalidates an earlier reconciliation;
C15 multisubstrate branch/copy creates shared historical evidence but distinct current participants.

# 7. Success Condition

A generated candidate is promising only if it can explain, from the frozen requirements, how civil consequences are preserved, classified and reconciled without requiring either:

**Permanent Sovereign State Authority**

or

**Uncontrolled Divergent Civil Reality.**
