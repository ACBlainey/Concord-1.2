---

# 1. Purpose

The Concord was developed from an earlier ethical foundation represented in its present constitutional architecture by **Blainey’s Laws — The Ethical Kernel**.

Subsequent work in the Unified Paths of Light programme independently examined multiple religious and traditional corpora through experiments E2–E9. Increasingly controlled derivation, holdout, comparison, compression and falsification procedures ultimately produced a new cross-experimental synthesis:

## Unified Paths Minimum-Stable Root Ethical Kernel — U1–U8

1. **U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm**
2. **U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity**
3. **U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination**
4. **U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need**
5. **U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship**
6. **U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair**
7. **U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development**
8. **U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function**

This later kernel is strongly compatible with the ethical field represented by Blainey’s Laws, but it does not partition that field in exactly the same way.

This creates a new research opportunity.

The purpose of this experiment is **not to replace Blainey’s Laws**, revise the Concord, or assume that the later kernel is superior.

The purpose is to ask:

> **If the Concord had instead been developed from U1–U8, would substantially the same civilisational architecture have emerged?**

The experiment therefore tests the **generative sensitivity of the Concord to its ethical starting architecture**.

V1.2a is the isolated workspace for this experiment. No result produced here modifies V1.2 unless a later, explicit integration process is undertaken.

---

# 2. Primary Research Question

> **Does replacing the original Concord Ethical Kernel with the independently derived Unified Paths U1–U8 kernel materially alter the civilisational architecture generated from otherwise comparable problems and conditions?**

---

# 3. Secondary Research Questions

1. Does U1–U8 independently regenerate major existing Concord principles, constraints and architectures?
2. Where the same functions are recovered, does U1–U8 reach them through the same reasoning or through a different ethical route?
3. Does U1–U8 generate functions or constraints that are absent, weakly represented or differently represented in the existing Concord?
4. Does U1–U8 fail to regenerate any important function presently contained in the Concord?
5. Does U1–U8 produce alternative architectures compatible with the same underlying ethical objectives?
6. Which existing Concord systems appear strongly determined by root ethics, and which depend more heavily upon secondary assumptions, contingent design decisions or historical development?
7. Does **U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function** materially affect civilisational design?
8. Do the more explicit formulations of positive care, capability-sensitive development and non-domination materially alter downstream architecture?
9. Are apparent differences genuine ethical divergences, or differences in ethical resolution and partition geometry?
10. Does the experiment support the Concord's proposition that multiple legitimate **HOWs** may emerge beneath compatible **WHYs**?

---

# 4. What This Experiment Does Not Test

This experiment does not establish that:

- U1–U8 is metaphysically true;
- U1–U8 is superior to Blainey’s Laws;
- Blainey’s Laws are invalid;
- the existing Concord is invalid;
- any regenerated architecture is empirically effective;
- convergence proves that either kernel is universally correct;
- divergence proves that the existing Concord requires redesign.

The experiment addresses a narrower question:

> **How sensitive is Concord-like civilisational design to this change in ethical starting geometry?**

Any later question of amendment, redesign, replacement or branching must be treated separately.

---

# 5. Preservation of the Existing Concord

The existing V1.2 Concord is the **reference architecture**.

It must not be modified in response to intermediate experimental findings.

During this experiment:

- Blainey’s Laws remain the canonical Ethical Kernel of the existing Concord;
- U1–U8 remains a research result of the Unified Paths programme and the experimental starting kernel of V1.2a;
- existing Concord systems remain unchanged;
- counterfactual architectures remain experimental;
- no V1.2a result is silently integrated into canonical V1.2 material.

This separation is necessary because modifying the reference architecture during testing would contaminate later comparisons.

---

# 6. Experimental Principle

The experiment uses **counterfactual regeneration**.

For a selected Concord domain:

1. identify the underlying civilisational problem;
2. remove the existing Concord solution from the active derivation space as far as practicable;
3. retain relevant factual and environmental conditions;
4. apply U1–U8;
5. derive ethical requirements;
6. derive necessary constraints;
7. derive candidate mechanisms;
8. derive an architecture only as far as the ethical and factual premises justify;
9. freeze the result;
10. reopen the existing Concord solution;
11. compare the two.

The intended sequence is:

**Problem → Conditions → U1–U8 → Requirements → Constraints → Mechanisms → Candidate Architecture → Freeze → Existing Concord Comparison**

This is deliberately different from:

**Existing Concord → reinterpret through U1–U8**

The second procedure tests compatibility.

The first tests **generativity**.

---

# 7. The Counterfactual Firewall

Perfect cognitive blinding is impossible. The researcher and assisting AI systems may already possess knowledge of the Concord architecture.

This limitation must be explicitly recorded rather than concealed.

A procedural firewall will therefore be used.

## Stage A — Problem Extraction

A selected Concord system is reduced to a neutral problem statement describing relevant agents, environmental conditions, dependencies, asymmetries, risks, scarce resources where relevant, temporal constraints, coordination requirements, capabilities and uncertainty.

The problem statement must avoid naming or describing the existing Concord solution wherever possible.

## Stage B — Solution Closure

Once the neutral problem statement has been produced, the existing Concord implementation is not consulted during regeneration.

## Stage C — U-Kernel Derivation

U1–U8 are applied independently to the problem. Each root must be considered explicitly, including the possibility that it imposes no material requirement in that case.

## Stage D — Freeze

The resulting ethical requirements, constraints, mechanisms and architecture are recorded before comparison.

No retrospective modification is permitted merely to increase similarity with the Concord.

## Stage E — Reopening

Only after freezing is the relevant existing Concord architecture reopened for direct comparison.

---

# 8. Derivation Discipline

A critical distinction must be preserved:

> **Principle ≠ Constraint ≠ Mechanism ≠ Architecture ≠ Outcome**

U1–U8 are ethical roots. They must not be treated as if they directly specify institutions.

A root may generate requirements; requirements may generate constraints; constraints may permit multiple mechanisms; mechanisms may support multiple architectures.

The experiment must therefore avoid falsely claiming that a particular Concord mechanism follows uniquely from a root ethic when several implementations remain possible.

---

# 9. Underdetermination Is a Valid Result

The experiment must not force U1–U8 to generate a complete solution.

If the kernel establishes constraints but does not uniquely determine an implementation, the result is **UNDERDETERMINED**.

This may indicate dependence upon empirical assumptions, engineering choices, environmental constraints, historical circumstances, cultural preferences, secondary principles, optimisation choices, risk tolerance or implementation practicality.

Underdetermination must not be counted as failure.

---

# 10. Sampling Strategy

The experiment is not intended initially to regenerate the entire Concord.

A **bounded heterogeneous sample** will be used.

The sample must cross substantially different civilisational functions so convergence cannot be produced merely by repeatedly examining closely related systems.

The initial sampling frame is fixed to include cases involving:

1. individual agency, rights or boundaries;
2. governance and legitimate authority;
3. judgement, accountability or repair;
4. epistemic integrity and correction;
5. resources, allocation or economic coordination;
6. care, dependency or capability development;
7. multisubstrate participation;
8. continuity, succession or regeneration;
9. emergency or exceptional authority;
10. relations between distinct communities or civilisations.

These categories are fixed before individual test cases are selected.

---

# 11. Deliberate Stress Cases

Some cases should be selected specifically because differences between the kernels might matter.

These include problems where:

- **U4** may impose positive care, reciprocity or support obligations beyond merely avoiding harm;
- **U7** may affect treatment of differing capabilities, developmental states or dependencies;
- **U8** may affect persistence, succession, institutional continuity, recovery, adaptation or intergenerational function;
- **U3** may distinguish beneficial intervention from domination, paternalism or unnecessary removal of agency.

These are selected for discriminatory power, not to demonstrate divergence.

---

# 12. Control Cases

The sample should also contain domains where strong convergence is expected, including problems dominated by unnecessary harm, truthful information, responsible resource use, and accountability for consequential action.

If the process produces radical divergence even in straightforward controls, the derivation procedure itself should be examined.

---

# 13. Per-Case Experimental Record

Every sampled case uses the same structure:

## A. Case Identifier
Unique experimental number.

## B. Domain
Relevant civilisational functional area.

## C. Neutral Problem Statement
The problem without the Concord solution.

## D. Environmental and Factual Conditions
Only facts necessary to reason about the problem.

## E. U1–U8 Activation Map
For each root: strongly active; active; weakly active; not materially active; or uncertain. This is descriptive, not numerical ethical weighting.

## F. Derived Ethical Requirements
What must be protected or achieved?

## G. Derived Constraints
What must a legitimate solution not violate?

## H. Candidate Mechanisms
What mechanisms could satisfy those requirements?

## I. Counterfactual Architecture
What system, if any, emerges?

## J. Underdetermined Elements
Which decisions cannot be derived from U1–U8 and supplied conditions?

## K. Frozen Counterfactual Result
Explicit closure point before comparison.

## L. Existing Concord Architecture
Only now reopen relevant V1.2 material.

## M. Comparison
Record similarities, differences and omissions.

## N. Divergence Classification
Apply the predefined classification.

## O. Kernel Attribution
Identify which differences appear plausibly caused by changed ethical kernel and which arise from other assumptions.

## P. Confidence and Contamination Note
Record uncertainty and likely influence from prior knowledge of the Concord.

# 14. Predefined Outcome Classes

## C1 — Strong Architectural Convergence
U1–U8 independently generates substantially the same ethical requirements and major architecture.

## C2 — Functional Convergence / Architectural Variation
Different mechanisms or structures emerge, but they protect substantially equivalent ethical and civilisational functions.

## C3 — U-Kernel Addition
U1–U8 generates a substantive requirement or architectural feature not materially represented in the sampled Concord system.

## C4 — Concord-Only Function
The existing Concord contains a substantive function not regenerated from U1–U8 under the supplied conditions.

## C5 — Direct Tension
The counterfactual derivation and existing Concord architecture impose materially incompatible requirements.

## C6 — Different Ethical Emphasis
Both remain compatible, but U1–U8 materially changes priority, scope, threshold or institutional emphasis.

## C7 — Underdetermined
U1–U8 constrains the solution but does not determine the existing architecture or a clear alternative.

## C8 — Non-Kernel Dependency
A Concord feature appears to derive primarily from empirical, engineering, historical or secondary design considerations rather than directly from the Ethical Kernel.

These classes are descriptive and do not imply a hierarchy.

---

# 15. Difference Attribution

A difference is not automatically evidence that the kernels caused it.

For each divergence, test:

- **Kernel causation:** would the difference plausibly disappear if the original ethical kernel were restored?
- **Resolution difference:** are both kernels protecting the same function at different ethical layers?
- **Implementation freedom:** do both kernels permit both architectures?
- **Environmental assumption:** did the counterfactual use a different factual assumption?
- **Historical contingency:** is the Concord mechanism one path chosen during development?
- **Missing premise:** does V1.2 rely on an additional principle, observation or module absent from the counterfactual packet?

Only after these alternatives are considered should divergence be provisionally attributed to kernel change.

---

# 16. Special U8 Test

Because **Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function** is the major substantive addition produced by the final E2–E9 compression, it requires explicit testing.

For every case ask:

> **If U8 were removed while U1–U7 remained, would the same architecture still emerge?**

Where the answer appears to be no or materially uncertain, conduct a local ablation:

**U1–U8 derivation**

versus

**U1–U7 derivation**

This distinguishes architecture merely compatible with U8, strengthened by U8, and actually dependent upon U8.

Particular attention should be paid to institutional succession, constitutional continuity, civilisational memory, infrastructure maintenance, resilience, regeneration, intergenerational stewardship, peaceful preservation of cooperative relationships and recovery following institutional failure.

---

# 17. Compound-Root Analysis

Some existing Concord principles may not correspond to any single root.

Where a feature emerges only through interaction among several U roots, record it as a **compound derivation**.

Possible hypotheses for testing include justice, peaceful coexistence, safe spaces and legitimate governance. Their actual root topology must be allowed to emerge from cases rather than being predetermined.

---

# 18. Falsification Conditions

The experiment must be capable of challenging current expectations.

Potential disconfirming observations include:

- **F1:** U1–U8 repeatedly fails to regenerate important Concord functions.
- **F2:** U1–U8 repeatedly produces architectures directly incompatible with existing Concord systems.
- **F3:** U8 produces little or no observable difference even in continuity-sensitive cases.
- **F4:** Apparent convergence depends heavily on importing unstated Concord assumptions into neutral problem statements.
- **F5:** Different derivations from the same U1–U8 problem generate radically incompatible architectures, indicating weak generative constraint.
- **F6:** Both kernels prove too abstract to explain much existing architecture without extensive secondary assumptions.
- **F7:** The experiment cannot reliably distinguish kernel-derived architecture from historical design inheritance.

None of these outcomes should be suppressed.

---

# 19. Aggregate Interpretation

Possible aggregate patterns include:

### Pattern A — High Convergence
Most systems regenerate the same architecture or close functional equivalents. This supports relative robustness to kernel repartitioning, but does not prove the architecture correct.

### Pattern B — Same Functions, Different Civilisation
The same ethical functions survive but different institutional architectures emerge. This supports multiple coherent civilisational HOWs beneath substantially shared ethical WHYs.

### Pattern C — Localised Divergence
Most architecture converges but particular domains systematically change, identifying where ethical partition geometry has downstream consequences.

### Pattern D — Systematic Alternative Architecture
Differences accumulate across independent domains into a coherent alternative civilisational pattern, justifying a larger independent regeneration exercise.

### Pattern E — Weak Generativity
U1–U8 is insufficient to generate much architecture without substantial additional assumptions, indicating that the ethical kernel constrains the Concord but does not strongly determine it.

---

# 20. No Redesign Threshold During Initial Sampling

The initial experiment must not contain a rule such as “if X cases differ, redesign the Concord.”

The initial decision threshold is only:

> **Is there sufficient evidence of systematic kernel-dependent divergence to justify a larger counterfactual regeneration programme?**

---

# 21. ESCP Constraint

The Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem applies directly.

Failure to find a Concord analogue for a U-derived function does not establish that the function is absent from the Concord. Before classifying a result as **C3 — U-Kernel Addition**, the wider relevant V1.2 corpus must be searched.

Likewise, failure of one counterfactual derivation to regenerate a Concord function does not establish that U1–U8 cannot generate it.

Claims must remain bounded to the examined derivation and search space.

---

# 22. Provenance Requirement

Every case must preserve:

- source problem;
- source Concord files;
- neutralisation procedure;
- U-root reasoning;
- frozen counterfactual result;
- later comparison;
- identified contamination risks;
- unresolved questions;
- subsequent replication where performed.

Negative and unexpected results must be preserved alongside positive results.

---

# 23. Replication

Important divergences should eventually be repeated by a fresh AI instance where possible, a different model family where practical, a human reviewer where useful, or a derivation using a separately prepared neutral problem statement.

The purpose is not consensus. It is to determine whether results depend strongly upon the particular reasoner.

---

# 24. Experimental Stop Rule

The first phase continues until the predefined functional categories have been represented and additional cases cease to reveal major new divergence classes.

The experiment should not continue merely to accumulate confirmations.

If early cases reveal a systematic alternative architecture, that pattern should become a separate targeted experiment rather than simply expanding the original sample indefinitely.

---

# 25. Expected Value of Either Result

If the architectures converge, the Concord gains evidence that major structures are relatively insensitive to the precise partitioning of a strongly overlapping ethical root field.

If they diverge, the research may gain evidence that different minimum ethical kernels can generate distinct but potentially viable civilisational architectures.

The objective is not to make the new kernel reproduce the Concord.

The objective is to discover what it actually generates.

---

# 26. Protocol Freeze

With Version 1.0 accepted, the following central rules are frozen throughout the first sampling phase:

- the primary research question;
- the U1–U8 kernel;
- the heterogeneous sampling categories;
- the outcome classifications C1–C8;
- the requirement to freeze derivations before V1.2 comparison;
- acceptance of underdetermination;
- the U8 ablation requirement where relevant;
- the ESCP constraint;
- preservation of negative results;
- separation of V1.2a from canonical V1.2.

Minor procedural improvements must be recorded prospectively with version history and must not silently alter completed cases.

---

# 27. Experimental Sequence

**Phase 1 — Build the sampling register.**

Select V1.2 systems representing the predefined heterogeneous categories without performing their counterfactual derivations.

**Phase 2 — Prepare neutral problem packets.**

**Phase 3 — Perform and freeze U1–U8 derivations.**

**Phase 4 — Reopen V1.2 and compare.**

**Phase 5 — Cross-case synthesis.**

Only after those stages should the project ask whether evidence justifies a larger U-kernel civilisational regeneration experiment.

---

# Closing Principle

> **Do not make the existing Concord win this experiment. Do not make the new kernel win it either.**

The relevant question is whether two closely related but independently derived ethical foundations generate the same civilisational solution.

If they do, that is evidence.

If they do not, that is evidence too.
