## Stronger Recursive Prediction

Where a canonical system exercises oversight, epistemic, representational or other meta-level authority, it should apply substantial parts of this grammar to **itself**, not only to its object.

Expected examples include:

- bounded mandate;
- review of the reviewer;
- correction of the correction mechanism;
- replacement/revision of the institution;
- provenance of its own decisions;
- protection against capture by the meta-system itself.

## Evaluation

For each system:

- **Present** — relation is explicit/substantial;
- **Partial** — relation is indirect, limited or context-specific;
- **Absent** — no material support found in reviewed source.

Absence must not be converted automatically into architectural defect.

## Prediction Threshold

The hypothesis will be strengthened if:

1. most systems instantiate a substantial majority of the grammar;
2. meta-level systems show meaningful self-application;
3. recurrence is not dependent on identical vocabulary;
4. any failures/non-matches are preserved rather than reinterpreted into agreement.

The hypothesis will weaken if the grammar appears only after broad interpretive stretching, if key relations are routinely absent, or if meta-systems systematically exempt themselves from the constraints they impose.
