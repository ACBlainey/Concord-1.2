# EKC-08 — Preservation and Recovery Across Discontinuity — U1–U7 Ablation Derivation

**Experiment:** V1.2a — U-Kernel Counterfactual Concord Experiment  
**Case:** EKC-08  
**Status:** VERSION 1.0 — FROZEN U1–U7 ABLATION / PRE-U8 / PRE-COMPARISON  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Excluded root:** U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function

---

# 1. Ablation Question

Can U1–U7, without an independent continuity root, generate a civilisational architecture for preservation and recovery across disruption?

The derivation must not silently recreate U8 as an axiom.

Continuity may appear only where it is instrumentally required by the remaining roots.

---

# 2. Root Activation Without U8

## U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm

Loss of critical knowledge, infrastructure or protective capability can foreseeably cause severe harm.

U1 therefore generates reasons to preserve things whose disappearance would expose present or future participants to serious avoidable harm.

This is instrumental preservation:

**preserve X because loss of X creates unjustified harm.**

It does not establish continuity itself as an independent good.

## U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity

Knowledge needed for consequential judgement must remain sufficiently connected to evidence and reality.

This generates reasons for:

- preserving evidence;
- preserving provenance;
- distinguishing verified knowledge from inherited assertion;
- detecting corruption;
- maintaining interpretability where consequential knowledge is retained.

Again, the purpose is epistemic integrity, not continuity for its own sake.

## U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination

Participants require enough institutional and informational environment to exercise meaningful agency.

Loss of key civil capabilities can produce dependence or domination.

U3 therefore supports preservation where loss would destroy agency or concentrate control.

It also rejects continuity custodians converting preservation responsibility into permanent sovereignty.

## U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need

Disruption can create dependency and vulnerability.

U4 supports maintaining or restoring support functions where their disappearance would strand participants with legitimate claims.

It creates reasons for intergenerational transfer where foreseeable successors would otherwise inherit preventable dependency.

## U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship

Knowledge, infrastructure and preserved capability are resources.

U5 supports maintaining valuable resources for legitimate present and future claims.

But it also constrains preservation burden.

**Preserve everything forever** is not justified if preservation itself consumes resources required for more important functions.

## U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair

If institutions, knowledge systems or infrastructures fail, U6 generates a requirement to identify error, repair damage and correct defective systems.

This can produce reconstruction.

It also supports preserving provenance of safeguards so successors can understand why they exist.

## U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development

Capability must be developed and transferred where needed.

U7 strongly supports:

- teaching;
- skill transfer;
- maintaining competence;
- rebuilding lost competence;
- calibrating responsibility to actual capability.

This root makes **usable capability**, not mere stored information, important.

---

# 3. What U1–U7 Clearly Generates

Without U8, the roots still generate substantial continuity-like behaviour.

They require preservation where loss would:

- cause serious harm;
- destroy meaningful agency;
- abandon legitimate dependencies;
- waste or irreversibly destroy important common resources;
- prevent correction or repair;
- erase evidence needed for truth;
- leave future participants unable to exercise important capabilities.

Thus:

**Continuity-like architecture emerges instrumentally from protected functions.**

---

# 4. Function Before Institution

None of U1–U7 gives an institution moral entitlement to survive merely because it exists.

If institution A performs function F, and A becomes harmful or obsolete, preserving A is not required where F can be preserved or restored by B.

Therefore:

**institutional persistence ≠ ethical continuity.**

The relevant question is:

> **What protected function would be lost if this institution disappeared?**

This strongly favours function over institutional identity even without U8.

---

# 5. Preservation Is Selective

U5 prevents unlimited preservation.

Candidate priority classes emerge:

1. knowledge/capability whose loss risks severe harm;
2. knowledge needed to preserve agency and basic cooperative function;
3. provenance needed to understand consequential constraints;
4. capabilities difficult or impossible to regenerate;
5. resources whose destruction imposes major costs on future participants;
6. lower-value historical material.

This is not a fixed hierarchy.

It is a reasoned preservation triage.

---

# 6. Archive Is Not Capability

U2 + U7 generate an important distinction:

**stored information ≠ usable knowledge ≠ operational capability.**

A civilisation may possess perfect technical records but lack:

- tools;
- prerequisite knowledge;
- trained practitioners;
- language competence;
- measurement systems;
- supply chains;
- tacit knowledge.

Therefore preservation must sometimes include **capability chains**, not merely documents.

---

# 7. Capability Dependency Chains

To recover advanced function F, predecessors may be required:

**F depends on E  
E depends on D  
D depends on C...**

Thus a recovery architecture should preserve enough prerequisite capability to traverse from the successor's actual state toward the desired function.

U7 generates this because development must begin from actual capability rather than assumed capability.

This is a strong continuity architecture despite U8's absence.

---

# 8. Provenance Without Past Sovereignty

U2 + U6 require provenance:

- why a safeguard exists;
- what failure produced it;
- what evidence supported it;
- what assumptions it depended on.

But historical provenance does not create permanent authority.

A successor must be able to say:

**we understand why this existed, but current evidence no longer justifies this implementation.**

Thus:

**preserve the lesson more strongly than the implementation.**

This follows naturally from corrigibility.

---

# 9. Recovery as Repair

Without U8, recovery is most naturally framed under U6:

**failure/loss → diagnosis → repair → restored legitimate function.**

This creates a repair-oriented rather than continuity-oriented architecture.

If a function has disappeared but its absence creates no relevant harm, agency loss, stewardship failure, unmet support claim or capability deficit, U1–U7 provide weaker reason to reconstruct it.

This is an important ablation result.

---

# 10. Adaptation

U2 and U6 prevent blind restoration.

If conditions have changed, exact restoration may be false to current reality or reproduce known failure.

Therefore:

**recovery ≠ historical replication.**

A successor should restore the protected function using mechanisms appropriate to present conditions.

Adaptation is therefore recoverable without U8.

---

# 11. Multiple Legitimate Successors

Nothing in U1–U7 requires one institutional lineage to possess exclusive legitimacy.

If several successor communities preserve the relevant ethical constraints and cooperative functions through different implementations, their divergence is not automatically failure.

U3 especially resists using historical succession claims as domination.

Therefore:

**continuity of legitimacy need not imply singular institutional succession.**

---

# 12. Cross-Substrate Continuity

The roots do not privilege one substrate.

What must survive depends on protected functions, not whether the same biological, digital or institutional carrier persists.

Thus a function may legitimately migrate across:

- people;
- machines;
- organisations;
- media;
- substrates;

provided the transfer preserves what is ethically relevant and remains corrigible.

---

# 13. Corruption and Inheritance

Preserved material can be wrong.

U2 forbids treating age or survival as evidence of truth.

Therefore inherited knowledge should retain:

- provenance;
- evidence status;
- uncertainty;
- known disputes;
- revision history where consequential.

A recovery system that preserves claims but erases epistemic status can propagate ancient error with high confidence.

---

# 14. Custodian Capture

Continuity creates power.

Those controlling archives, education, infrastructure or recovery pathways can define what successors know.

U3 + U6 therefore require:

- contestability;
- distributed challenge where feasible;
- provenance;
- review;
- separation between custody and permanent political authority.

The exact institutions remain underdetermined.

---

# 15. Preservation Burden

U5 makes preservation itself subject to stewardship.

A civilisation facing immediate survival pressure may legitimately allow some low-priority material to disappear.

The ethical question is not:

**Can this be preserved?**

but:

**What claims justify preservation relative to competing uses of resources?**

This prevents continuity from becoming unlimited accumulation.

---

# 16. Harmful Legacy

Historical importance does not imply operational preservation.

A harmful institution may be retained as:

- evidence;
- warning;
- historical knowledge;
- failure provenance;

while its mechanism is not restored.

Thus:

**remembering a system ≠ reactivating it.**

---

# 17. Architecture Generated Without U8

U1–U7 collectively generate a:

# **Selective Functional Preservation and Reparative Recovery Architecture**

Its purpose is:

> **Preserve and transfer knowledge, capability, provenance and resources where their loss would materially undermine protected ethical functions, and restore or replace lost functions through evidence-responsive repair when justified.**

Its architecture contains:

1. protected-function identification;
2. preservation triage;
3. evidence/provenance preservation;
4. capability-chain preservation;
5. skill and competence transfer;
6. corruption detection;
7. recovery/repair;
8. adaptive replacement;
9. custodian anti-capture;
10. resource-bounded preservation.

This is substantial.

---

# 18. What Is Weak Without U8

Despite substantial architecture, several things are noticeably weaker.

## 18.1 Continuity Has No Independent Normative Status

Every preservation action must ultimately be justified through another root.

There is no direct statement:

**legitimate cooperative function should remain recoverable across time because recoverability itself is ethically required.**

## 18.2 Latent Future Value Is Harder to Protect

If the present generation cannot show concrete harm, agency loss, support need or resource claim, preservation of a currently dormant function has weaker grounding.

## 18.3 Long-Horizon Recoverability Is Indirect

A function unnecessary today but predictably valuable after an unknown future disruption can be defended through stewardship, but less directly.

## 18.4 Civilisational Regeneration Is Fragmented

U1–U7 can preserve components for different reasons, but do not as cleanly generate a system-level requirement that the civilisation remain capable of regenerating cooperative function after discontinuity.

## 18.5 Continuity Across Ethical Generations Is Instrumental

The roots preserve lessons, capabilities and safeguards because losing them causes other failures. They do not independently protect the existence of a recoverable cooperative inheritance.

# 19. What Does Not Disappear

The ablation does **not** eliminate:

- archives;
- provenance;
- repair;
- succession;
- skill transfer;
- knowledge preservation;
- redundancy where justified;
- adaptive restoration;
- future-oriented stewardship;
- anti-capture;
- preservation triage.

Therefore a result claiming that U8 alone creates continuity architecture would be false.

U1–U7 already recover much of it.

# 20. Key Ablation Finding

The likely distinctive contribution of U8 is not the invention of archives or recovery.

It is a change in **normative compression and default orientation**.

Without U8:

> preserve or recover function **when another root supplies the reason**.

With U8, if it behaves as stated:

> preservation of legitimate cooperative function across change may become a **direct standing requirement**, subject to the other roots.

This difference must now be tested by restoring U8.

# 21. Failure Modes Without U8

### A1 — Present-Need Bias
Preservation is underweighted where future value is difficult to demonstrate.

### A2 — Fragmented Continuity
Different functions are preserved piecemeal without system-level recoverability.

### A3 — Archive Without Capability
Information survives but reconstruction chains do not.

### A4 — Capability Without Rationale
Practice survives while reasons and safeguards disappear.

### A5 — Institutional Fossilisation
Historical implementation is mistaken for protected function.

### A6 — Repair Myopia
Recovery focuses on the immediate failure without preserving future recoverability.

### A7 — Custodian Sovereignty
Control of continuity assets becomes political authority.

### A8 — Preservation Maximalism
Stewardship is misread as preserve everything.

### A9 — Historical Authority
Provenance becomes obedience to the past.

### A10 — Successor Monopoly
One lineage claims exclusive legitimacy merely through institutional descent.

### A11 — Knowledge Cliff
Advanced records exist beyond the successor's prerequisite capability.

### A12 — Silent Corruption
Preserved material loses evidence status or accumulates undetected error.

### A13 — Regeneration Gap
Components survive but no architecture ensures they can recombine into functioning cooperative systems.

---

# 22. Frozen Ablation Result

> **U1–U7 already generates substantial continuity architecture, but it does so instrumentally. Harm prevention, epistemic integrity, agency, care, stewardship, repair and capability development together produce selective preservation, provenance, skill transfer, capability chains, adaptive recovery and anti-capture. The architecture protects functions rather than institutions and treats archives as insufficient without usable capability. However, continuity itself lacks independent normative status. System-level recoverability, long-horizon regeneration and preservation of legitimate cooperative function remain dependent on arguments through other roots. The principal predicted contribution of U8 is therefore not invention of continuity mechanisms, but elevation and integration of recoverability as a direct civilisational requirement.**
