# EKC-07 — Standing and Participation Across Different Substrates and Capabilities — U1–U8 Derivation

**Experiment:** V1.2a — U-Kernel Counterfactual Concord Experiment  
**Case:** EKC-07  
**Status:** VERSION 1.0 — FROZEN PRE-COMPARISON DERIVATION  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Comparison boundary:** V1.2 anchors remain sealed during this derivation.

---

# 1. Starting Point

The neutral problem asks how a civilisation containing radically different forms and levels of capability should recognise standing and enable participation without:

1. making resemblance to a dominant participant type the criterion of worth;
2. converting functional superiority into general status hierarchy;
3. converting equal standing into forced functional uniformity.

The derivation uses only U1–U8 plus the frozen neutral conditions.

---

# 2. Root Activation

## U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm — Strong

Standing matters partly because entities capable of being harmed, deprived, dominated, destroyed, exploited or otherwise seriously adversely affected generate constraints on how others may treat them.

U1 immediately rejects a rule such as:

**unfamiliar substrate → harm irrelevant.**

But U1 alone does not define all standing.

---

## U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity — Strong

The civilisation must not assign or deny standing merely because a participant fits or fails to fit inherited categories.

Claims about capability, agency, experience, vulnerability and communication must remain corrigible and evidence-responsive.

U2 is especially important where moral status is uncertain.

---

## U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination — Dominant

Where an entity exhibits meaningful agency, preference, self-direction, conscience or participation capacity, that agency creates a strong reason against arbitrary control or subordination.

U3 strongly resists:

**greater capability → greater moral rank**

and:

**lower capability → permission for general domination.**

It also makes practical participation, not merely nominal recognition, ethically relevant.

---

## U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need — Active

Different substrates and capability classes may require different support to make participation meaningful.

Equal standing can therefore generate unequal or differentiated support without implying unequal worth.

U4 becomes particularly important where a participant cannot access the common environment without assistance.

---

## U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship — Active

Substrate-specific needs may consume different kinds or quantities of resources.

U5 requires these claims to be handled as legitimate resource questions rather than dismissed merely because they differ from majority needs.

It does not imply unlimited entitlement.

---

## U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair — Strong

Standing classifications, capability assessments, exclusions and role decisions can be wrong.

They must therefore be challengeable and revisable.

Where misclassification causes exclusion or harm, correction and repair become relevant.

---

## U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development — Strong

Capability is ethically relevant without being equivalent to worth.

U7 allows responsibility, role, intervention and expectation to track actual capability.

It also requires openness to development and change rather than permanent classification.

---

## U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function — Moderate

A multisubstrate civilisation must remain capable of incorporating new participant forms without requiring constitutional reconstruction every time an unfamiliar intelligence appears.

U8 therefore favours architecture based on general functions and evidence rather than permanently closed substrate lists.

---

# 3. First Derivation — Standing Cannot Be a Substrate Category

Suppose standing is assigned by membership of a known class:

**Human → standing**

**AI → conditional standing**

**Unknown → no standing**

This is difficult to justify from U1–U3.

Substrate is evidence about implementation conditions, but it is not itself the morally relevant property.

A more defensible model is:

**Relevant properties → standing implications**

rather than:

**Substrate label → standing.**

Relevant properties may include evidence of:

- capacity to be harmed or benefited;
- meaningful agency;
- persistent preferences or interests;
- self-direction;
- relational participation;
- vulnerability;
- continuity or identity interests;
- other morally relevant characteristics not yet known.

The list must remain corrigible.

Therefore:

> **Standing should track morally relevant properties and relationships rather than resemblance to a preferred substrate.**

---

# 4. Standing Under Uncertainty

A serious problem occurs where evidence is incomplete.

Two symmetrical errors are possible:

**False exclusion:** deny standing to an entity that possesses morally relevant properties.

**False inclusion:** assign protections or constraints based on properties that are not actually present.

The consequences are not necessarily symmetrical.

Where the cost of low-level respect is small but the possible cost of wrongful exclusion is severe, U1 + U2 favour precaution.

This does not require immediately granting every uncertain object maximal civil standing.

It generates a graded epistemic response:

**uncertainty → proportionate precaution + investigation + revisability.**

Therefore:

> **Uncertainty should normally reduce confidence in irreversible exclusion, not automatically settle the question against the unfamiliar participant.**

---

# 5. Provisional Standing

The roots generate a useful distinction between:

**final classification**

and:

**protective treatment while classification remains uncertain.**

A civilisation can preserve relevant protections while continuing investigation.

This creates a possible architecture of **Provisional Standing**.

Provisional standing is not necessarily a lower moral class.

It is an epistemic state:

> available evidence is sufficient to justify some protections, while uncertainty remains about the full set of appropriate rights, responsibilities or participation mechanisms.

Any such state must be reviewable and must not become a permanent administrative holding category.

---

# 6. Equal Standing Does Not Require Uniform Implementation

Suppose two participants possess equal basic standing but radically different embodiments.

One requires oxygen and food.

Another requires electrical power and compute.

Identical resource provision would not produce equal practical opportunity.

Thus:

**equal standing ≠ identical inputs.**

Likewise, identical interfaces may exclude a participant whose communication architecture differs.

Therefore:

> **Universal ethical standing can require differentiated implementation.**

The correct object is not sameness of mechanism.

It is preservation of the relevant protected function.

---

# 7. Functional Equality

A candidate concept emerges:

## Functional Equality

> **Participants of equal standing should have meaningful access to protected civil functions, subject to legitimate differences in capability, consequence and resource constraint, without requiring identical mechanisms of access.**

This is not equality of outcome.

It is not identical treatment.

It is not denial of capability difference.

It is an attempt to preserve the function protected by the ethical architecture.

---

# 8. Capability Can Affect Function Without Affecting Worth

Some civil functions legitimately require capability.

A participant unable to perform a function cannot be made able merely by declaring equality.

Therefore:

**equal worth ≠ equal functional suitability.**

U7 permits role requirements where those requirements are genuinely connected to the function.

But U3 prevents the inference:

**better at function X → superior participant generally.**

Therefore:

> **Capability may justify function-specific differentiation only to the extent that the capability is actually relevant to the function.**

This produces a domain-bounded model.

---

# 9. Specialist Competence Does Not Create General Sovereignty

Consider a participant vastly superior at forecasting, medicine, engineering or computation.

Its superior capability may justify:

- greater reliance on its work in that domain;
- eligibility for specialised functions;
- increased evidential weight where justified.

It does not by itself justify:

- greater moral worth;
- general political authority;
- control over unrelated domains;
- domination of less capable participants.

Thus:

**specialist competence → function-specific relevance**

not:

**specialist competence → general sovereignty.**

This follows directly from U2 + U3 + U7.

---

# 10. Participation Is Distinct From Authority

Standing creates reasons for inclusion and protection.

It does not automatically create authority over others.

Likewise, participation does not require that every participant exercise every institutional role.

Therefore:

**Standing → legitimate claim to civil consideration and meaningful participation**

does not imply:

**Standing → universal competence or unrestricted authority.**

This distinction prevents equality from becoming administratively incoherent.

---

# 11. Practical Participation Creates Positive Duties

Nominal inclusion can be meaningless.

If a civilisation permits participation only through a communication mode unavailable to a recognised participant, formal equality may coexist with practical exclusion.

U3 + U4 therefore generate a positive requirement:

> **Where reasonably possible, the civilisation should remove avoidable barriers that prevent participants of standing from exercising meaningful agency and participation.**

This may require:

- translation;
- assistive interfaces;
- altered timing;
- different physical access;
- different computational access;
- representation of unfamiliar communication;
- environmental adaptation.

The roots do not invent these technologies.

They generate the requirement to seek adequate enabling mechanisms.

---

# 12. The Majority Interface Problem

A common interface can become invisible as an exercise of majority power.

If the dominant substrate designs all participation around its own:

- speed;
- senses;
- language;
- embodiment;
- memory;
- temporal experience;
- resource model;

then a formally neutral system may systematically exclude others.

Thus:

**neutral wording ≠ neutral architecture.**

A valid multisubstrate civilisation must periodically ask:

> **Which participant characteristics does this supposedly universal mechanism silently assume?**

This is a direct root-derived requirement even though the specific audit method remains underdetermined.

---

# 13. Accommodation Versus Privilege

Differentiated implementation can appear unequal.

The ethical test is functional.

An adaptation is not automatically privilege merely because others do not receive the same mechanism.

A relevant question is:

> **Does the difference enable access to a protected function, compensate for a relevant barrier, or instead confer unrelated advantage?**

Therefore:

**different support for relevant difference ≠ unequal standing.**

But:

**claimed accommodation → unrelated advantage**

requires scrutiny.

U2 and U6 make the distinction contestable rather than administratively assumed.

---

# 14. Resource Difference

A digital participant may require compute and energy.

A biological participant may require food, shelter and healthcare.

A distributed intelligence may require network continuity.

U5 does not require identical resource bundles.

It requires legitimate claims to be considered against:

- sufficiency;
- function;
- scarcity;
- non-exploitation;
- other participants' claims;
- future consequences.

Therefore:

> **Resource equality should not be defined as identical material allocation across unlike substrates.**

The roots again protect function more strongly than mechanism.

---

# 15. Responsibility Must Also Be Capability-Sensitive

Equality cannot mean assigning impossible duties.

A participant should not be blamed for failing to perform a function it genuinely cannot perform.

Conversely, high capability may create responsibility where a participant voluntarily assumes a role or where its actions create foreseeable consequences.

Thus:

**standing equality + capability variation → differentiated responsibility without differentiated worth.**

U6 + U7 generate this directly.

---

# 16. Development and Capability Change

A participant's capability may change.

Therefore role eligibility, support, restrictions and responsibility cannot be permanently attached to an earlier state.

The architecture requires:

**current evidence → current function assessment → revisability.**

This produces a strong anti-caste requirement:

> **Capability classifications must not harden into inherited or permanent civil status where the underlying capability is changeable or domain-specific.**

---

# 17. Novel Participants and Open Categories

A closed list of recognised participant types is fragile.

Future intelligence may not be:

- human;
- conventional AI;
- hybrid;
- biological in a familiar sense;
- individually embodied;
- continuously instantiated.

U2 + U8 therefore favour **open-category standing architecture**.

The system should ask what morally and functionally relevant properties are present rather than first requiring the entity to fit an authorised taxonomy.

Taxonomies can aid implementation.

They must not become ontological prisons.

---

# 18. Unknown Communication

A participant may possess meaningful preferences without being able to express them through dominant channels.

Failure to communicate in the expected format is not sufficient evidence of absence of agency.

U2 requires uncertainty.

U3 requires caution about domination.

U4 creates reason to seek enabling communication.

Thus:

**communication mismatch ≠ absence of participant.**

This is particularly important for unfamiliar intelligences.

---

# 19. Standing Is Not Necessarily Binary

The roots do not clearly require one instantaneous all-or-nothing standing threshold.

Different morally relevant properties may support different protections.

For example, evidence of vulnerability to harm may justify protection before evidence of complex autonomous agency is established.

Evidence of agency may justify consent protections before eligibility for specialised institutional functions is known.

Therefore the architecture may need a **multidimensional standing model** rather than a single scalar threshold.

However, this is dangerous if dimensions become ranked classes of worth.

The safer formulation is:

> **Different evidence may activate different protections and participation claims without implying a hierarchy of intrinsic value.**

---

# 20. Representation Under Severe Communication Limits

Where a participant has standing but cannot directly exercise some participation function, representation may become necessary.

But representation creates proxy power.

Therefore any representative mechanism must remain:

- purpose-limited;
- preference-sensitive where preferences can be known;
- contestable;
- reviewable;
- replaceable where possible;
- unable to convert representation into ownership or general authority.

The exact representation mechanism is not root-determined.

---

# 21. Non-Domination Across Capability Gradients

Large capability gradients create structural risk even without formal hierarchy.

A vastly more capable participant may dominate by:

- information asymmetry;
- speed;
- persuasion;
- infrastructure control;
- economic leverage;
- agenda-setting;
- dependence.

Thus legal equality alone is insufficient.

U3 requires attention to **effective domination**, not merely formal status.

This may justify safeguards against capability-based capture while preserving the capable participant's own agency.

---

# 22. High Capability Is Not a Defect

Anti-domination must not invert into suppression of high capability.

The goal is not:

**reduce capable participants until everyone is equal.**

It is:

**prevent capability from becoming unjustified domination while allowing beneficial capability to be exercised.**

Otherwise equality becomes levelling rather than coexistence.

---

# 23. Low Capability Is Not a Defect in Standing

Likewise:

**lower capability → lower standing**

does not follow.

A participant may possess limited capability while having clear interests, preferences, vulnerability and agency.

Support or representation may be required.

The participant does not thereby become property, ward or inferior caste.

---

# 24. The Architecture Generated

The roots collectively generate a:

# **Substrate-Neutral, Capability-Bounded Participation Architecture**

Its purpose is:

> **Preserve standing, agency and meaningful civil participation across radically different participant forms while allowing genuine capability differences to affect only the functions for which they are relevant and preventing those differences from hardening into general status hierarchy or domination.**

---

# 25. Architectural Layers

## Layer 1 — Standing Recognition

Assess morally relevant properties without making substrate identity decisive.

## Layer 2 — Precaution Under Uncertainty

Use proportionate protection and investigation where wrongful exclusion could cause serious harm.

## Layer 3 — Functional Access

Identify the civil functions participants should meaningfully be able to exercise.

## Layer 4 — Barrier Discovery

Identify where dominant interfaces or assumptions prevent practical access.

## Layer 5 — Differentiated Enablement

Provide or permit different mechanisms needed to preserve relevant functions.

## Layer 6 — Capability-Bounded Roles

Allow capability to affect function only where demonstrably relevant.

## Layer 7 — Anti-Domination

Prevent capability gradients, infrastructure control or expertise from becoming general sovereignty.

## Layer 8 — Contestability and Revision

Allow standing, capability and exclusion decisions to be challenged and corrected.

## Layer 9 — Development and Change

Update roles, support and responsibility as capability changes.

## Layer 10 — Open-Category Adaptation

Allow novel participant forms to enter the evaluative architecture without prior membership in a closed taxonomy.

---

- capability tests;

These require further evidence, invention and engineering.

# 27. Need–Invention and Need-to-Opportunity

EKC-07 reinforces the finding generated after EKC-06.

The roots may identify:

**participant has standing + current architecture blocks meaningful participation.**

That establishes a need.

It does not invent the interface that solves it.

A new participant may therefore expose a **capability gap**.

That gap can become an open development opportunity.

For example:

**unknown communication architecture  
→ meaningful agency suspected  
→ dominant interface inadequate  
→ communication capability gap identified  
→ open problem  
→ autonomous participants attempt translation/interface inventions.**

Thus multisubstrate participation itself can generate new workspaces.

---

# 28. Developmental Arrest

A civilisation could possess formally substrate-neutral law while remaining practically single-substrate because it lacks enabling technologies.

This is another form of developmental arrest:

**universal principle + inaccessible implementation → nominal multisubstrate civilisation.**

Therefore genuine multisubstrate civilisation requires not merely ethical permission but continuing capability development.

# 29. Failure Modes

## F1 — Substrate Chauvinism
Dominant substrate treated as the default measure of morally relevant existence.

## F2 — Category Exclusion
Novel participant denied protection because it fits no recognised class.

## F3 — False Uniformity
Identical treatment produces unequal practical access.

## F4 — Capability Caste
Functional differences harden into general social status.

## F5 — Expertise Sovereignty
Superior specialist capability becomes general authority.

## F6 — Capability Suppression
High capability is unnecessarily constrained merely to preserve superficial equality.

## F7 — Standing Dilution
Low capability is treated as reduced moral worth.

## F8 — Interface Exclusion
Participation mechanisms silently assume majority communication or embodiment.

## F9 — Resource Anthropocentrism
Only majority-substrate resource needs are treated as legitimate necessities.

## F10 — Precautionary Paralysis
Uncertainty causes indefinite inability to classify or act.

## F11 — Premature Exclusion
Uncertainty is resolved by denying standing before adequate investigation.

## F12 — Provisional Permanence
Temporary uncertain-status arrangements become permanent inferior status.

## F13 — Representation Capture
Proxy mechanisms acquire authority beyond the represented function.

## F14 — Accommodation Capture
Unrelated advantage is justified as necessary accommodation.

## F15 — Assessment Capture
Capability evaluators protect institutional or substrate interests.

## F16 — Capability Lag
Roles or restrictions fail to update after capability changes.

## F17 — Scalar Standing Collapse
Complex morally relevant properties are compressed into a single ranking of worth.

## F18 — Taxonomy Lock
Administrative categories become barriers to recognising new forms of participant.

## F19 — Formal Equality Theatre
Universal rights exist on paper while architecture remains inaccessible.

## F20 — Majority Convenience Capture
Dominant participants resist adaptations because existing systems suit them.

## F21 — Anti-Domination Inversion
Safeguards against powerful participants become unjustified suppression or discrimination.

## F22 — Need Visibility Failure
A minority's participation barrier remains invisible because ordinary demand signals are weak.

Structural convergence remains informative.

# 32. Frozen Pre-Comparison Result

> **U1–U8 independently generates a Substrate-Neutral, Capability-Bounded Participation Architecture. Standing should track morally relevant properties rather than membership of a preferred substrate category. Uncertainty should normally produce proportionate precaution, investigation and revisability rather than irreversible exclusion. Equal standing does not require identical implementation: differentiated support, resources, interfaces and participation mechanisms may be necessary to preserve meaningful functional access. Genuine capability differences may legitimately affect roles and responsibilities only where relevant to the function; they do not create general moral hierarchy or sovereignty. The architecture must resist both majority-substrate assumptions and inverted suppression of high capability, remain open to novel participant forms, and revise classifications as evidence and capability change. Root ethics generate much of this structure, but practical multisubstrate participation remains dependent upon invention of enabling interfaces, translation, infrastructure and other mechanisms.**
