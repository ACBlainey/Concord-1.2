# 1. Derivation Question

Given only the frozen U1–U8 ethical kernel and the EKC-04 neutral problem:

> **How should a civilisation remain capable of acting on its best available understanding while preserving the ability to discover that understanding is wrong?**

No existing Concord epistemic methodology is assumed.

---

# 2. Root Activation

## U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm

**Active.**

False beliefs can cause harm, but attempts to suppress error can also cause harm.

U1 therefore requires epistemic practices proportionate to the consequences of being wrong.

## U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity

**Dominant / strongly active.**

This root directly requires belief, representation, communication and consequential judgement to remain corrigibly related to relevant reality and evidence.

EKC-04 is therefore a direct stress test of U2.

## U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination

**Active.**

Control over accepted belief can become control over participants.

Epistemic authority must not become arbitrary authority over what may be questioned.

## U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need

**Conditionally active.**

Participants with less expertise, access or capability may require support to participate meaningfully in consequential epistemic processes.

## U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship

**Active.**

Investigation consumes time, attention, computation, material resources and opportunity.

Not every possibility can receive unlimited investigation.

## U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair

**Strongly active.**

Beliefs, models, decisions and institutions must remain challengeable and correctable.

Consequences of earlier error may require repair.

## U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development

**Strongly active.**

Epistemic capability can improve.

The civilisation should support development of judgement, evidence interpretation and error recognition rather than merely classifying actors as competent or incompetent.

## U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function

**Active.**

Civilisational learning must survive turnover, institutional change and correction.

Useful discarded knowledge and the provenance of past error may retain future value.

# 3. Derived Principles

## P1 — Reality Has Priority Over Institutional Commitment

No belief becomes true because:

- an institution adopted it;
- experts endorsed it;
- a majority accepted it;
- resources were invested in it;
- it is old;
- or changing it would be inconvenient.

Where sufficiently strong evidence conflicts with established belief, the belief must remain correctable.

## P2 — Consequential Confidence Must Track Evidential Support

The stronger the consequence of acting on a claim, the more important it becomes to distinguish:

- observation;
- inference;
- assumption;
- prediction;
- uncertainty;
- and unresolved alternatives.

Confidence should not be represented more strongly than evidence warrants.

## P3 — Uncertainty Is Information

Uncertainty should not be treated merely as a defect to hide.

Where uncertainty materially affects decisions, it should remain visible.

## P4 — Competing Explanations Should Remain Distinguishable

Where several explanations remain compatible with available evidence, the civilisation should not collapse them into one merely for administrative convenience.

This does not require equal belief or equal resource allocation.

It requires preserving meaningful distinction until evidence justifies stronger resolution.

## P5 — Dissent Has Evidential, Not Automatic Moral, Priority

A minority claim is not correct because it is a minority claim.

A majority claim is not correct because it is a majority claim.

Relevant dissent should receive treatment according to evidence, explanatory value and consequence.

## P6 — Expertise Alters Evidential Weight, Not Reality

Expertise can legitimately affect how testimony or analysis is weighted.

It does not create sovereignty over truth.

Expert conclusions remain evidence-bearing claims rather than self-validating facts.

## P7 — Decisions May Be Necessary Before Certainty

Ethical corrigibility does not require paralysis.

Where waiting itself has consequences, action may be justified using the best available evidence.

The decision should preserve appropriate reversibility where uncertainty remains material.

## P8 — Error Correction Must Reach Consequences

Correcting a record while leaving avoidable consequences of the error untouched is incomplete where repair is feasible and proportionate.

## P9 — Epistemic Systems Must Be Able to Detect Their Own Failure

A system that only detects errors anticipated by its existing theory is incomplete.

Persistent mismatch between prediction and observation should itself become evidence that the representational space may be inadequate.

## P10 — Institutional Reputation Cannot Override Correction

Embarrassment, sunk cost or loss of prestige cannot ethically justify maintaining a materially false belief.

## P11 — Information Lineage Matters

Where a conclusion depends on evidence, measurements, models or testimony, relevant provenance should be preservable.

Apparently independent agreement may otherwise conceal common-source failure.

## P12 — Learning Requires Preservation of Error History

Superseded beliefs should not necessarily disappear without trace.

Their provenance, supporting evidence, failure conditions and reasons for correction can remain useful civilisational knowledge.

## P13 — Metrics Remain Representations

A metric is evidence about a target phenomenon, not the phenomenon itself.

If incentives or environmental change alter the relationship, the metric must remain challengeable.

## P14 — Epistemic Authority Must Be Function-Bounded

Institutions may legitimately perform specialised epistemic functions.

Such functions do not create unrestricted authority over belief, speech or unrelated domains.

## P15 — Correction Must Not Require Institutional Collapse

A civilisation should be able to revise important beliefs without making admission of error existentially threatening to the institution that held them.

Architecture should therefore reduce incentives to defend falsehood merely to preserve institutional continuity.

# 4. Derived Constraints

A civilisational epistemic architecture should therefore satisfy at least:

**C01.** Consequential claims remain traceable to relevant evidence where feasible.

**C02.** Material uncertainty must not be silently represented as certainty.

**C03.** Observation and interpretation should remain distinguishable.

**C04.** Competing live explanations should not be erased merely because one is institutionally preferred.

**C05.** Alternative explanations need not receive equal resources where evidence differs.

**C06.** Relevant new evidence must be capable of changing accepted conclusions.

**C07.** Expertise must not make claims immune from challenge.

**C08.** Low-status sources must not be dismissed solely because of status.

**C09.** High-status sources must not be accepted solely because of status.

**C10.** Consequence and reversibility should influence evidential thresholds for action.

**C11.** Decisions made under uncertainty should record material assumptions where proportionate.

**C12.** Epistemic institutions should preserve routes for challenge and correction.

**C13.** Correlated sources should not be mistaken for independent confirmation where dependency is known.

**C14.** Measurements and metrics should remain contestable representations.

**C15.** Failure to fit observed reality should be capable of triggering model or framework reconsideration.

**C16.** Historical error should remain recoverable where useful for future learning.

**C17.** Correction mechanisms should consider downstream consequences created by earlier error.

**C18.** Investigation resources should be allocated proportionately rather than equally across every conceivable claim.

**C19.** Epistemic participation should be made practically accessible where participant input is materially relevant.

**C20.** No epistemic institution should acquire general coercive authority merely because it performs a truth-seeking function.

# 6. Counterfactual Architecture

The U1–U8 derivation produces:

# **Provisional, Plural and Correctable Knowledge Architecture**

Its core rule is:

> **Civilisation should act from the best warranted understanding presently available while preserving the evidential distinctions, competing possibilities, provenance and correction pathways required to discover when that understanding is wrong.**

The architecture has nine layers.

## Layer 1 — Observation

Preserve relevant observations and their provenance before collapsing them into explanation.

## Layer 2 — Representation

Claims should state what is asserted and, where consequential, distinguish evidence from inference and assumption.

## Layer 3 — Alternative Explanations

Maintain materially live competing explanations while evidence remains insufficient to resolve them.

## Layer 4 — Evaluation

Compare explanations against evidence, predictive performance, coherence and relevant counterevidence without granting status-based immunity.

## Layer 5 — Provisional Commitment

A civilisation may adopt a best-supported working conclusion for action without declaring all alternatives impossible.

## Layer 6 — Consequence Calibration

Higher-cost and less-reversible decisions require stronger epistemic safeguards.

Where delay itself is dangerous, provisional action may still be justified.

## Layer 7 — Challenge and Correction

Participants and institutions require routes to introduce contradictory evidence, challenge assumptions and revise conclusions.

## Layer 8 — Memory

Preserve enough provenance of rejected, superseded and failed explanations to support future re-evaluation and learning.

## Layer 9 — Meta-Correction

When persistent observations do not fit available explanations, the architecture must permit reconsideration of its own categories, assumptions and methods.

# 7. Treatment of the Edge Cases

## Correct Minority

Status does not determine truth.

The minority hypothesis remains available if it retains evidential relevance.

When later evidence supports it, the architecture can update without requiring the minority's earlier social validation.

## Persuasive False Consensus

Source lineage and dependency matter.

Ten reports ultimately derived from one defective measurement do not provide ten independent confirmations.

## High-Cost Delay

Action may proceed provisionally.

The architecture should record uncertainty and preserve correction or reversal where feasible.

## High-Cost Error

Greater consequence increases the importance of independent verification, provenance, uncertainty visibility and reversible action.

## Institutional Investment

Sunk cost and reputation do not change reality.

Where possible, institutional continuity should be separated from doctrinal infallibility so correction does not require self-destruction.

## Unresolved Competition

Both explanations may remain live with unequal confidence.

The system is not ethically required to manufacture a winner.

## Model Success Without Understanding

Predictive success is evidence of usefulness.

It does not automatically establish that the model's internal explanation is ontologically correct.

Operational use and explanatory confidence may therefore differ.

## Measurement Distortion

Metrics remain representations.

Evidence that behaviour has changed the metric-target relationship requires recalibration, replacement or reduced reliance.

## Lost Knowledge

U8 supports preservation of superseded reasoning and evidence sufficient for later recovery.

## Novel Failure

Persistent unexplained mismatch should create an explicit unresolved state rather than forcing observations into existing categories.

# 11. Failure Modes Created by This Architecture

The counterfactual design itself creates risks.

## F1 — Hypothesis Hoarding

Preserving alternatives can become refusal to discard explanations that have lost meaningful evidential support.

## F2 — Uncertainty Paralysis

Making uncertainty visible can be misused to avoid decisions.

## F3 — Manufactured Doubt

Actors may strategically generate weak alternatives to obstruct well-supported conclusions.

## F4 — Provenance Overload

Excessive lineage requirements can make ordinary reasoning administratively impossible.

## F5 — Permanent Provisionality

Institutions may refuse to make usable commitments because every conclusion remains technically revisable.

## F6 — Expertise Flattening

Anti-status principles may be misread as requiring equal evidential weight for unequal competence.

## F7 — Correction Instability

Excessively sensitive updating may cause oscillation in response to weak new evidence.

## F8 — Archive Burden

Preserving superseded knowledge indefinitely consumes resources and may bury current understanding.

## F9 — Challenge Capture

Challenge mechanisms can be overwhelmed by strategic, repetitive or bad-faith claims.

## F10 — Meta-Scepticism

Permission to question frameworks can become indiscriminate rejection of any shared epistemic standard.

These failures imply that corrigibility itself requires proportionate architecture.

# 13. Pre-Comparison Result

The counterfactual architecture is:

> **Provisional, Plural and Correctable Knowledge Architecture**

It strongly constrains the epistemic functions a civilisation should preserve but leaves the exact reasoning methodology highly underdetermined.

This creates a useful control for comparison.

If V1.2's Reality Trees and Blaineyan Reasoning perform substantially the same functions through more specific methods, the result would support functional convergence with methodological variation.

If the existing V1.2 architecture contains functions not recoverable from these constraints, those differences should be recorded rather than retrofitted into this derivation.
