# EKC-09 — Exceptional Power During Severe Threat — U1–U8 Derivation

**Experiment:** V1.2a — U-Kernel Counterfactual Concord Experiment  
**Case:** EKC-09  
**Status:** VERSION 1.0 — FROZEN PRE-COMPARISON DERIVATION  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Comparison boundary:** V1.2 Constitutional Emergency anchor remains sealed.

---

# 1. Starting Question

Can U1–U8 justify temporary exceptional power when ordinary civil processes cannot adequately address a severe threat?

If so, the architecture must solve two opposed dangers:

**too little exceptional capacity → preventable catastrophic harm**

and:

**too much exceptional capacity → domination, capture and permanent constitutional distortion.**

The problem is therefore not simply whether emergency power should exist.

It is how to permit **necessary exceptional action without normalising exceptional rule**.

---

# 2. Root Activation

## U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm — Dominant

A sufficiently serious and imminent threat can create positive reason to act even where ordinary procedure would delay effective protection.

U1 therefore makes exceptional action possible in principle.

But the same root constrains emergency action because the response itself can cause serious harm.

Thus U1 operates on both sides:

**harm of inaction ↔ harm of intervention.**

---

## U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity — Dominant

Emergency authority cannot legitimately rest merely on assertion.

Threat claims, uncertainty, forecasts and response effectiveness must remain tied to evidence as far as conditions allow.

Emergency urgency may reduce available certainty.

It does not make truth irrelevant.

U2 therefore opposes both paralysis through impossible evidential standards and arbitrary action through evidential abandonment.

---

## U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination — Dominant

Exceptional power is intrinsically dangerous because it often expands one actor's control over others.

U3 does not make all emergency restriction impossible.

It requires that restrictions remain justified by the threat rather than becoming general domination.

This produces a central rule:

> **Emergency necessity may justify bounded interference with agency; it does not create general ownership of participants or civilisation.**

---

## U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need — Strong

Emergencies create vulnerability and dependency.

Response architecture must not focus only on coercive power.

It also generates duties of:

- rescue;
- support;
- shelter;
- assistance;
- protection of disproportionately affected participants;
- maintenance of essential care.

Thus emergency architecture is partly a support architecture.

---

## U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship — Strong

Severe threat may require rapid reprioritisation of scarce resources.

U5 permits exceptional allocation where necessary for legitimate protective function.

But emergency resource control remains stewardship, not ownership.

Scarcity does not erase legitimate claims.

---

## U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair — Dominant

Emergency decisions can be wrong.

Therefore exceptional authority must remain:

- attributable;
- reviewable;
- correctable;
- capable of repair.

The greater the exceptional power, the stronger the need for post-action accountability where real-time review is impossible.

---

## U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development — Active

Emergency roles may legitimately depend on actual capability.

Relevant expertise can matter greatly.

But expertise does not automatically create unlimited authority.

U7 supports function-specific emergency competence while resisting status-based emergency sovereignty.

---

## U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function — Strong

Severe disruption may make ordinary structures temporarily unusable.

U8 supports temporary alternative mechanisms where required to preserve legitimate cooperative function.

But it also generates the restoration requirement:

**exceptional mechanism → preserve function → return or adapt toward legitimate ordinary operation when possible.**

Emergency architecture is therefore transitional rather than self-justifying.

---

# 3. Exceptional Authority Can Be Legitimate

U1 and U8 together make a categorical ban on exceptional authority difficult to justify.

Suppose:

- catastrophic harm is imminent;
- ordinary process cannot act in time;
- a capable actor can prevent the harm;
- the intervention is proportionate.

Refusing all exceptional action merely because ordinary procedure cannot complete would itself permit avoidable harm and potentially destroy cooperative function.

Therefore:

> **Exceptional authority can be ethically justified where ordinary authority is materially incapable of responding adequately to a sufficiently serious threat.**

This is not yet a mechanism.

It is a conditional permission.

---

# 4. The Necessity Gap

Exceptional authority becomes relevant only where there is a **Necessity Gap**:

> **the difference between what legitimate ordinary processes can achieve in the available time and what must be achieved to prevent or contain the severe threat.**

If ordinary processes can adequately respond, the necessity gap is absent.

Then exceptional power lacks its principal justification.

This yields:

**exceptional authority should fill the necessity gap, not exceed it.**

---

# 5. Threat Severity Does Not Alone Determine Power

A catastrophic threat does not automatically justify every possible intervention.

Legitimacy depends on at least:

- severity;
- probability;
- imminence;
- evidence quality;
- reversibility;
- expected effectiveness;
- collateral harm;
- available alternatives;
- time available;
- distribution of burden;
- uncertainty.

Thus:

**large threat ≠ unlimited power.**

The relevant relationship is proportional and functional.

---

# 6. Minimum Necessary Exceptional Power

U1 + U3 generate:

> **Use no more exceptional power than is reasonably necessary to close the demonstrated necessity gap.**

This can be called the **Minimum Necessary Exceptional Power principle**.

It does not require choosing the weakest intervention.

It requires choosing an intervention adequate to the threat without adding unrelated authority.

---

# 7. Purpose-Bounded Authority

Emergency power must attach to a specific protective function.

If authority is granted to contain threat T, it does not thereby justify action concerning unrelated objective X.

Therefore:

**Threat → Necessary Function → Exceptional Authority**

not:

**Threat → General Sovereignty.**

This is one of the strongest root-derived constraints.

---

# 8. Epistemic Threshold Under Urgency

Emergency decisions often occur before certainty is possible.

A rigid demand for certainty can cause catastrophic delay.

But abandoning evidence enables manufactured emergencies.

Therefore U2 generates a variable evidential requirement.

As urgency rises, action may need to occur with lower certainty.

But as power, irreversibility and collateral harm rise, evidential burden also rises.

Conceptually:

**Required justification increases with coerciveness and irreversibility, while permissible uncertainty may increase with genuine urgency and severity.**

No simple numerical threshold follows from the roots.

---

# 9. Reversibility Preference

Under uncertainty, reversible interventions should generally be preferred where they can adequately address the threat.

Why?

Because error can be corrected.

Thus:

**uncertainty + comparable effectiveness → prefer more reversible action.**

This is not absolute.

A reversible response that cannot prevent irreversible catastrophe may be inadequate.

---

# 10. Emergency Rights Constraint

The roots do not generate a simple rule that rights disappear during emergency.

Instead:

- U1 can justify temporary interference to prevent severe harm;
- U3 continues constraining domination;
- U6 continues requiring accountability;
- U2 continues requiring epistemic integrity.

Therefore:

> **Emergency can alter the permissible balance of constraints without nullifying the ethical roots themselves.**

Exceptional action must remain ethically legible.

---

# 11. Non-Derogable Ethical Boundary

Because the roots remain active during emergency, some actions cannot be justified merely by invoking emergency status.

For example, emergency status cannot by itself legitimate:

- unrelated domination;
- fabricated evidence;
- permanent ownership of participants;
- arbitrary punishment unrelated to protection;
- deliberate destruction without relevant necessity;
- indefinite power merely because it was once useful.

The exact legal catalogue is underdetermined.

The principle is not.

---

# 12. Emergency Authority Is Function, Not Rank

An emergency role does not elevate a participant's moral or civic status.

Authority exists because a function must be performed under unusual conditions.

Thus:

**emergency competence → emergency role**

not:

**emergency competence → superior participant.**

When the function ends, its special authority loses its basis.

---

# 13. Duration Must Track Justification

A fixed time limit may be useful, but the roots do not generate one universal duration.

Instead:

> **Exceptional authority remains legitimate only while the necessity conditions that justified it remain materially present.**

This creates two dangers:

- automatic expiry while catastrophe continues;
- indefinite continuation because no one declares the threat fully over.

Therefore duration requires periodic reassessment.

---

# 14. Burden of Renewal

Once exceptional authority exists, institutional inertia favours continuation.

The architecture should therefore reverse the presumption.

Normal condition:

**ordinary authority is legitimate by ordinary constitutional basis.**

Exceptional condition:

**continuation requires renewed justification.**

Thus:

> **Emergency power should not continue merely because termination has not been proven necessary. Its continuation should require evidence that exceptional necessity persists.**

This is a powerful anti-ratchet constraint.

---

# 15. Review During Emergency

Where feasible, emergency action should remain subject to review while occurring.

But review itself must not make urgent action impossible.

This suggests separation between:

**permission to act quickly**

and:

**permission to act without later scrutiny.**

The first may be justified.

The second generally is not.

---

# 16. Delayed Accountability

If real-time review is impossible, U6 strengthens the requirement for later review.

Therefore:

**reduced ex ante constraint → increased ex post accountability.**

This is not compensation for every possible abuse.

It is a structural response to unavoidable emergency speed.

Records should survive sufficiently to reconstruct:

- what was known;
- what was uncertain;
- who acted;
- what authority was claimed;
- what alternatives were considered;
- what consequences followed.

---

# 17. Emergency Provenance

U2 + U6 + U8 generate an **Emergency Provenance Record** as a functional requirement, though not necessarily a specific institution.

It should preserve enough information for later civilisation to ask:

- Why was emergency action considered necessary?
- What evidence existed at the time?
- What uncertainty existed?
- Which powers were exercised?
- Against whom?
- For how long?
- With what results?
- Which harms occurred?
- What should change next time?

This turns emergency into learnable civilisational experience rather than constitutional amnesia.

---

# 18. Secrecy

Some emergency information may need temporary restriction where disclosure would materially worsen the threat.

But secrecy creates epistemic and capture risks.

Therefore:

**operational secrecy ≠ permanent unaccountability.**

Where immediate disclosure is dangerous, the architecture should preserve information for later review or permit bounded review by appropriately positioned actors where feasible.

The exact mechanism requires institutional engineering.

---

# 19. Distributed Review Under Institutional Failure

If ordinary oversight collapses, review cannot simply be declared impossible forever.

The roots suggest preserving **review plurality** where possible.

Possible functions include:

- independent evidence holders;
- separate operational and evaluative roles;
- distributed records;
- later reconstruction;
- multiple competent observers.

These are functional requirements, not prescribed institutions.

The aim is to avoid one emergency actor becoming simultaneously:

**threat detector + evidence owner + decision-maker + executor + reviewer + historian.**

---

# 20. Emergency Resource Stewardship

Resource control may need to change rapidly.

But emergency allocation should remain connected to:

- survival;
- protection;
- essential function;
- vulnerability;
- future recovery;
- proportionality.

Emergency cannot convert common or participant resources into unrestricted spoils of authority.

Temporary control should not silently become permanent ownership.

---

# 21. Unequal Burden

Some interventions may burden one group more than another.

Identical burden is not always possible.

But concentrated cost requires stronger justification.

The architecture should ask:

- Is the burden necessary?
- Is it connected to the threat?
- Is a less concentrated alternative available?
- Can harm be mitigated?
- Can losses later be repaired?
- Is the burden being shifted because the group is politically weak?

U1 + U3 + U4 + U6 make this unavoidable.

---

# 22. Repair After Emergency

Ending emergency authority is not sufficient.

Emergency measures may leave:

- lost property;
- damaged livelihoods;
- wrongful restrictions;
- institutional distortion;
- trauma;
- corrupted data;
- displaced populations;
- unequal burdens.

U6 therefore generates **restorative exit**.

The post-emergency task includes identifying and repairing avoidable harms where possible.

---

# 23. Restoration Is Not Necessarily Exact Reversion

U8 prevents a simplistic return-to-before rule.

The emergency may reveal that ordinary architecture was inadequate.

Therefore:

**exit emergency → restore legitimate ordinary function**, not necessarily **restore every pre-emergency mechanism unchanged.**

Permanent reform may be justified.

But reform should pass ordinary legitimacy processes rather than being smuggled into permanence through temporary emergency authority.

---

# 24. Emergency Learning Without Emergency Permanence

A successful exceptional mechanism may contain useful innovations.

The civilisation should be able to retain the lesson without retaining the exceptional authority.

Thus:

**successful emergency mechanism → evaluate → extract useful function → ordinary review → possible normal architecture**

rather than:

**successful emergency mechanism → permanent exceptional power.**

This is an important distinction.

---

# 25. Recurrent Emergencies

Repeated crises create a danger:

**temporary exception + repeated renewal → de facto normal government.**

The architecture should therefore treat recurrent emergency as evidence that either:

1. ordinary architecture lacks a capability it should possess; or
2. the threat has become a persistent condition requiring a new ordinary mechanism.

Thus:

> **A recurring emergency should trigger architectural diagnosis, not merely endless exceptional renewal.**

This is a strong root-derived result.

---

# 26. Chronic Threat

If a threat becomes permanent, the justification for exceptional governance cannot remain permanently exceptional.

The civilisation must redesign ordinary architecture to handle the persistent condition while restoring ordinary constraints as far as possible.

Otherwise:

**emergency becomes constitutional bypass.**

---

# 27. Multiple Emergencies

Where several threats compete for resources, no single emergency automatically dominates.

U1 + U5 require comparative stewardship.

The architecture must consider:

- severity;
- probability;
- time horizon;
- reversibility;
- dependency;
- resource conflict;
- systemic consequences.

This again resists single-metric emergency optimisation.

---

# 28. Emergency During Constitutional Collapse

If normal constitutional structures fail, U8 requires preservation or regeneration of legitimate cooperative function.

Temporary authority may therefore operate under reduced institutional support.

But the absence of ordinary institutions does not erase the roots.

The emergency actor should preserve:

- minimal ethical constraints;
- evidence;
- provenance;
- restoration pathways;
- future contestability.

This is emergency authority as **bridge function**, not successor sovereignty.

---

# 29. Emergency Bridge Architecture

A candidate abstraction emerges:

## **Emergency Bridge**

> A temporary exceptional arrangement whose legitimacy derives from maintaining or restoring protected cooperative function across a period in which ordinary mechanisms are materially inadequate.

The bridge should connect:

**Ordinary Constitutional Function  
→ Severe Disruption  
→ Exceptional Bridge  
→ Restored or Adapted Ordinary Function.**

A bridge that becomes the destination has failed its constitutional purpose.

---

# 30. Emergency Success Trap

One of the most dangerous cases is not failed emergency authority but successful emergency authority.

If concentrated command performs extremely well, participants may conclude that ordinary constraints were inefficient.

But emergency effectiveness under one narrow threat does not prove general legitimacy.

Therefore:

**effective emergency coordination ≠ evidence for permanent general authority.**

This is a direct application of ESCP:

> success within the emergency evaluation space does not prove superiority across the full civilisational evaluation space.

---

# 31. Manufactured Emergency

Because emergency authority can be valuable, actors may manufacture the conditions used to justify it.

U2 and U6 therefore require examination of:

- evidence provenance;
- incentive to exaggerate;
- independent signals;
- alternative explanations;
- threat-model manipulation.

The emergency architecture must treat **claiming emergency** as a consequential act subject to accountability.

---

# 32. Emergency Metric Capture

If emergency status depends on metrics, actors can optimise or manipulate them.

Examples could include threat levels, resource thresholds or incident counts.

Thus:

**metric → emergency trigger**

creates incentive around the metric.

The roots therefore favour multiple evidence channels and contextual judgement rather than blind threshold automation.

---

# 33. Exceptional Power Must Be Legible

Emergency authority should be sufficiently explicit that participants can determine:

- what power exists;
- why;
- for what function;
- over whom or what;
- under what constraints;
- how it can end;
- how it will later be reviewed.

Hidden exceptional authority is difficult to contest.

Legibility is therefore part of anti-domination.

---

# 34. Architecture Generated

U1–U8 collectively generate a:

# **Necessity-Bounded Emergency Bridge Architecture**

Its purpose is:

> **Permit the minimum exceptional authority reasonably necessary to prevent or contain severe harm when ordinary mechanisms are materially inadequate, while preserving epistemic integrity, agency constraints, accountability, continuity and a credible path back to legitimate ordinary governance.**

---

# 35. Architectural Layers

## Layer 1 — Threat Recognition
Establish evidence that a severe threat may exist.

## Layer 2 — Necessity-Gap Assessment
Determine what ordinary mechanisms cannot accomplish in time.

## Layer 3 — Exceptional Function Definition
Specify the protective function that must be performed.

## Layer 4 — Minimum Necessary Authority
Grant no broader exceptional power than reasonably required.

## Layer 5 — Burden and Reversibility Assessment
Prefer adequate measures with lower irreversible harm where possible.

## Layer 6 — Operational Action
Allow sufficiently rapid execution.

## Layer 7 — Continuing Evidence and Review
Reassess threat, effectiveness and necessity.

## Layer 8 — Provenance and Accountability
Preserve evidence and decisions for challenge and learning.

## Layer 9 — Renewal or Contraction
Continue only where renewed justification exists; reduce power as necessity shrinks.

## Layer 10 — Restorative Exit
Terminate exceptional authority and repair avoidable emergency harms.

## Layer 11 — Architectural Learning
Convert useful lessons into ordinary mechanisms through ordinary legitimacy processes.

## Layer 12 — Recurrence Diagnosis
If emergency repeatedly returns, redesign ordinary architecture rather than normalising exception.

---

- exact emergency duration;
- exact renewal interval;
- voting thresholds;
- courts;
- executive structures;
- emergency councils;
- command chains;
- secrecy rules;
- classified review systems;
- emergency elections;
- resource requisition formulas;
- compensation mechanisms;
- emergency communication systems;
- exact non-derogable legal rights;
- military organisation;
- automated emergency triggers.

These require engineering, testing and institutional design.

# 37. Failure Modes

### F1 — Emergency Capture
Temporary power becomes a route to unrelated control.

### F2 — Manufactured Emergency
Threat is fabricated or exaggerated to obtain authority.

### F3 — Necessity Inflation
More power is claimed than required to close the actual necessity gap.

### F4 — Threat Persistence Bias
Residual risk is used to justify indefinite continuation.

### F5 — Renewal Inertia
Exceptional power continues because no actor bears the burden of re-justifying it.

### F6 — Emergency Success Trap
Effective exceptional governance is mistaken for generally superior governance.

### F7 — Review Paralysis
Oversight becomes so burdensome that urgent protection fails.

### F8 — Review Elimination
Urgency is used to abolish all accountability.

### F9 — Epistemic Monopoly
Emergency authority controls evidence, action and review.

### F10 — Secrecy Persistence
Temporary operational secrecy becomes permanent unaccountability.

### F11 — Minority Burden Capture
Politically weak participants absorb disproportionate emergency costs.

### F12 — Irreversible Overreaction
Uncertain threat produces permanent damage where reversible alternatives existed.

### F13 — Underreaction by Proceduralism
Ordinary procedure is followed despite being incapable of preventing imminent catastrophe.

### F14 — Expertise Sovereignty
Emergency expertise becomes general political authority.

### F15 — Resource Appropriation
Temporary stewardship becomes permanent ownership.

### F16 — Restoration Failure
Emergency power ends but damaged ordinary function is not restored.

### F17 — Reform Smuggling
Permanent constitutional change is enacted through temporary emergency authority without ordinary legitimacy.

### F18 — Recurrent Exception
Repeated emergencies create permanent exceptional government.

### F19 — Chronic-Threat Lock
A persistent threat prevents return to ordinary constraints indefinitely.

### F20 — Provenance Loss
Future reviewers cannot reconstruct why emergency action occurred.

### F21 — Metric Trigger Capture
Emergency indicators are manipulated or Goodharted.

### F22 — Emergency Bridge Sovereignty
The temporary bridge claims legitimacy as the permanent destination.

### F23 — Competing-Emergency Blindness
One threat monopolises resources while other severe risks are ignored.

### F24 — Repair Neglect
Wrongful or avoidable emergency harms remain unaddressed after the threat.

# 40. Frozen Pre-Comparison Result

> **U1–U8 generates a Necessity-Bounded Emergency Bridge Architecture. Exceptional authority can be legitimate where a severe threat creates a demonstrable gap between what ordinary mechanisms can achieve in time and what is necessary to prevent or contain serious harm. The authority should be no broader than reasonably necessary to close that gap, remain tied to a defined protective purpose, preserve the ethical roots rather than suspend them, and contract as necessity declines. Urgency can justify rapid action and reduced ex ante process but not permanent exemption from evidence, provenance, review and repair. Emergency authority is a temporary bridge across failure of ordinary mechanisms, not a new source of general sovereignty. Recurrent or chronic emergency should trigger redesign of ordinary architecture rather than indefinite normalisation of exception. Successful emergency power does not prove that exceptional governance is generally legitimate.**
