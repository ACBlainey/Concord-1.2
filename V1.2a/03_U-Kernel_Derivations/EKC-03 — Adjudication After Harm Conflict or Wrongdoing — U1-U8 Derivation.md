# 1. Derivation Question

> **How should a civilisation determine relevant facts, responsibility and legitimate response after alleged harm, conflict or wrongdoing while protecting participants from both unresolved abuse and wrongful judgement?**

---

# 2. U1–U8 Activation Map

## U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm

**STRONGLY ACTIVE**

Adjudication exists partly because unresolved wrongdoing can expose participants to continuing harm.

But adjudication itself can cause severe harm through false accusation, excessive response, exclusion, deprivation or irreversible judgement.

U1 therefore constrains both failure to act and wrongful action by the adjudicative system.

---

## U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity

**STRONGLY ACTIVE**

An allegation is not equivalent to an established fact.

A judgement must remain related to available evidence, uncertainty, alternative explanations and the possibility of error.

U2 therefore makes epistemic discipline central rather than optional.

---

## U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination

**STRONGLY ACTIVE**

Both harmed and accused participants retain standing and agency.

Powerful actors must not be able to convert institutional influence into unquestionable truth.

Likewise, an accused participant cannot invoke autonomy to gain unrestricted permission to impose unjustified harm on others.

---

## U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need

**ACTIVE**

Harmed participants may require support independent of whether responsibility has yet been established.

Participants with limited capacity may require assistance to participate meaningfully.

U4 also resists reducing the entire process to blame while ignoring repair and vulnerability.

---

## U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship

**MODERATELY ACTIVE**

Adjudication consumes resources and can redistribute them through repair.

Resources should not be used arbitrarily or disproportionately, and remedies involving shared resources must remain legitimate.

U5 also becomes relevant where institutional or resource architecture contributed to harm.

---

## U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair

**STRONGLY ACTIVE**

This root is central.

Where responsibility can reasonably be attributed, actors should remain answerable to consequences.

Adjudication must also itself remain correctable.

Repair is not merely optional decoration: where wrongful consequences can feasibly be repaired, U6 creates a positive reason to do so.

---

## U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development

**STRONGLY ACTIVE**

Responsibility cannot be assessed independently of actual capability, understanding, control and developmental condition.

The response to wrongdoing should also consider whether future capability and conduct can change.

U7 therefore resists treating responsibility as a permanently fixed identity.

---

## U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function

**ACTIVE**

Civilisation requires stable ways to resolve serious disputes and preserve learning across cases.

But continuity cannot require preservation of erroneous judgements or defective rules.

U8 supports institutional memory, recurrence detection and correction of systemic conditions while allowing adjudicative architecture to adapt.

---

# 3. What Adjudication Is For

The roots do not generate a single purpose such as punishment.

They generate several functions:

1. establish the best warranted account of relevant events;
2. protect participants from ongoing unjustified harm;
3. attribute responsibility where justified;
4. constrain arbitrary blame;
5. repair consequences where feasible;
6. reduce recurrence;
7. preserve meaningful agency and standing;
8. correct defective decisions, rules and systems;
9. maintain cooperative trust by making serious disputes resolvable.

This produces:

> **Adjudication is a truth-constrained process for protection, attributable accountability, proportionate response, repair and future risk reduction.**

No independent root requires suffering to be imposed for its own sake.

# 4. Derived Ethical Requirements

## R1 — Allegation Must Remain Distinct From Finding

A claim can justify investigation or temporary precaution without becoming established truth.

**Roots:** U1, U2, U3.

## R2 — Consequential Findings Require Evidence Proportionate to Consequence and Uncertainty

The more serious and irreversible the imposed consequence, the stronger the epistemic justification should be.

U1 and U2 jointly create this gradient.

The kernel does not determine a numerical or named standard of proof.

## R3 — Relevant Uncertainty Must Remain Visible

An adjudicative process should not erase uncertainty merely to produce administrative finality.

**Roots:** U2, U6.

## R4 — Accuser and Accused Both Retain Standing

Protection of one participant does not erase the agency and dignity-relevant interests of another.

**Roots:** U1, U3, U4.

## R5 — Responsibility Requires Attribution

Responsibility should track relevant causal contribution, capability, knowledge, foreseeability, control and available alternatives.

**Roots:** U2, U6, U7.

## R6 — Harm and Wrongdoing Must Be Distinguished

Harm does not automatically establish wrongdoing.

Wrongdoing may exist even when chance prevents the expected harm.

**Roots:** U1, U2, U6.

## R7 — Response Must Be Proportionate to Legitimate Function

A response may protect, constrain, repair, develop capability or reduce recurrence.

It should not exceed what these legitimate functions can justify.

**Roots:** U1, U3, U6, U7.

## R8 — Repair Has Independent Ethical Importance

Where consequences can feasibly be repaired or mitigated, accountability should not end with classification of blame.

**Roots:** U1, U4, U6.

## R9 — Protection May Precede Final Finding Where Risk Is Serious

Temporary protective action may sometimes be justified under uncertainty where potential harm is sufficiently severe.

Such action must not be misrepresented as proof of guilt and should remain proportionate and reviewable.

**Roots:** U1, U2, U3, U6.

## R10 — Power Asymmetry Requires Compensating Safeguards

Where one party controls evidence, resources or institutional access, the process requires stronger means to preserve independent truth-seeking and meaningful participation.

**Roots:** U2, U3, U6.

## R11 — Responsibility May Be Distributed

A harmful outcome may be attributable to multiple actors, institutional rules, incentives or architecture.

Adjudication must not force every failure into a single-person blame model.

**Roots:** U2, U5, U6, U8.

## R12 — Rule Compliance Is Not a Complete Ethical Defence

If a rule itself creates unjustified harm, the rule may require correction even where the participant reasonably followed it.

Individual responsibility and systemic responsibility must be separable.

**Roots:** U1, U2, U6, U8.

## R13 — Participant Change Matters

Past responsibility remains historical fact, but present treatment should respond to relevant evidence of reform, changed capability and current risk.

**Roots:** U2, U6, U7, U8.

## R14 — Adjudication Must Be Correctable

Strong new evidence must be capable of reopening or correcting materially erroneous outcomes.

Finality has value, but cannot convert known error into legitimate truth.

**Roots:** U2, U6, U8.

## R15 — Adjudicative Power Must Itself Be Accountable

Investigators, adjudicators and enforcement functions are not outside the ethical system.

**Roots:** U2, U3, U6.

# 5. Derived Constraints

### C01 — No automatic conversion of allegation into guilt.

### C02 — No severe consequential judgement without proportionate epistemic justification.

### C03 — No hiding material uncertainty merely to create apparent certainty.

### C04 — No responsibility attribution detached from causal contribution and relevant capability.

### C05 — No response whose severity lacks a legitimate protective, reparative, corrective or recurrence-reducing justification.

### C06 — No permanent identity reduction of a participant to a past wrongful act where present change is materially relevant.

### C07 — No institutional immunity from challenge.

### C08 — No evidential privilege merely because a claimant is powerful or official.

### C09 — No rule may immunise itself from ethical correction merely because it existed before the case.

### C10 — No temporary protective restriction should be represented as a final finding.

### C11 — No single-actor blame requirement where causation is genuinely distributed.

### C12 — No repair architecture that ignores the agency or legitimate interests of those affected.

# 6. Candidate Mechanisms

The roots permit but do not uniquely require:

## M1 — Independent Fact-Finding

A process separated enough from the parties to evaluate conflicting evidence.

## M2 — Evidential Provenance

Preservation of source, transformation, uncertainty and chain of relevant evidence.

## M3 — Response Gradient

Different responses according to harm, responsibility, capability, recurrence risk and repair possibility.

## M4 — Temporary Protective Measures

Narrow restrictions under serious uncertainty where necessary to prevent credible severe harm, without converting precaution into guilt.

## M5 — Participant Representation or Assistance

Support for participants unable to navigate the process unaided.

## M6 — Review / Reopening Mechanism

A route for materially new evidence or demonstrated procedural error.

## M7 — Repair Process

Restoration, compensation, correction, reconciliation or other feasible mitigation according to context.

## M8 — Capability-Development Response

Where relevant, intervention directed toward reducing future harmful behaviour by improving capability rather than merely imposing cost.

## M9 — Systemic-Cause Review

Cases can trigger examination of defective rules, incentives, infrastructure or institutions.

## M10 — Separation of Protective and Final Judgement

Urgent safety action can be operationally distinct from final responsibility determination.

## M11 — Decision Provenance

Consequential findings preserve enough reasoning to permit later scrutiny and correction.

## M12 — Recurrence Monitoring

Patterns can be detected without assuming that every repeated allegation is automatically true.

# 7. Counterfactual Architecture

The roots generate:

# Evidence-Constrained, Reparative and Correctable Adjudication

Central proposition:

> **A legitimate adjudicative system seeks the best warranted account of what occurred, attributes responsibility only where justified, protects participants against both unresolved harm and wrongful judgement, applies proportionate responses directed toward legitimate future functions, repairs consequences where feasible, and remains capable of correcting itself.**

---

# 8. Architectural Layers

## 8.1 Intake Layer

Claims can enter the system without being treated as established fact.

## 8.2 Immediate Protection Layer

Where credible severe risk exists, narrowly bounded temporary protection may occur before final adjudication.

Protection and guilt remain conceptually separate.

## 8.3 Epistemic Layer

Evidence, uncertainty, inference, conflicting accounts and alternative explanations are examined.

## 8.4 Attribution Layer

The system asks:

- what occurred;
- what caused it;
- who or what contributed;
- what could reasonably have been known;
- what control existed;
- what alternatives were available;
- what capability was present.

## 8.5 Judgement Layer

Findings remain proportional to evidential warrant.

## 8.6 Response Layer

The legitimate response may combine:

- protection;
- constraint;
- repair;
- restitution;
- capability development;
- recurrence reduction;
- systemic correction.

The roots do not independently generate retribution as a necessary function.

## 8.7 Review Layer

Material error, new evidence or demonstrated process failure can trigger correction.

## 8.8 System-Learning Layer

A case can expose defective rules or institutional conditions.

The architecture therefore learns upward from individual disputes rather than assuming every failure belongs only to the immediate participant.

## 8.9 Temporal Layer

Historical responsibility may be preserved without freezing the participant's present identity.

Current restrictions require current justification where their purpose concerns current risk or capability.

# 9. Edge-Condition Results

## A. False Accusation

The allegation can justify investigation but not automatic guilt. Wrongful judgement itself activates U1/U3/U6.

## B. True Harm, No Wrongdoing

Repair or support may still be justified, but blame should not be manufactured where responsibility is absent.

## C. Wrongdoing, Little Immediate Harm

Responsibility can still exist because intent, attempted consequence and boundary violation matter independently of chance outcome. Response remains proportionate.

## D. Shared Responsibility

Responsibility can be distributed among participants and systems according to causal and functional contribution.

## E. Powerful Accused Actor

Greater power increases the need for independent evidence preservation and scrutiny; it does not establish guilt.

## F. Vulnerable Accused Participant

Capability-sensitive support is required. Responsibility should reflect actual understanding and control.

## G. Reformed Participant

Past wrongdoing remains part of history, but present treatment must account for genuine change where relevant.

## H. Dangerous but Uncertain Case

Temporary protective action may be justified without a final finding. The burden of uncertainty must not silently become permanent punishment.

## I. Defective Rule

The case should be capable of propagating correction upward to the rule or architecture.

## J. Adjudicative Error

Strong new evidence requires a route to correction. Institutional finality cannot ethically transform known error into truth.

# 10. Is Punishment a Root Requirement?

No.

U1–U8 can justify imposed adverse consequences where they serve legitimate functions such as:

- immediate protection;
- prevention of recurrence;
- proportionate accountability;
- repair;
- restoration of violated boundaries;
- capability development;
- preservation of cooperative function.

But the frozen derivation does not produce a separate requirement to inflict suffering because suffering is deserved.

This does not prove that every punitive mechanism is illegitimate.

It means punishment is not independently generated as a root-level purpose in EKC-03.
