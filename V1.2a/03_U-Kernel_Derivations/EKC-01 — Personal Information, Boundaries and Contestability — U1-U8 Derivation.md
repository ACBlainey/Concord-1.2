# 2. U1–U8 Activation Map

## U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm

**Activation: STRONGLY ACTIVE**

Information can produce direct and indirect harm through false decisions, exposure, manipulation, exclusion, coercion, exploitation, reputational damage, unsafe disclosure or persistent obsolete classification.

U1 therefore requires information practices to consider foreseeable consequences rather than treating information processing as ethically neutral.

It does not imply that every adverse consequence is prohibited. The relevant question is whether the harm is justified, necessary and proportionate to a legitimate function.

### Initial implication

Consequential information use requires harm-sensitive limits, particularly where misuse, error or disclosure can materially affect a participant.

---

## U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity

**Activation: STRONGLY ACTIVE**

Information systems represent reality imperfectly.

Records may be false. Inferences may be probabilistic. Context may be missing. Models may become obsolete.

U2 therefore prohibits treating uncertain representation as unquestionable fact.

### Initial implications

A consequential information system must preserve relevant distinctions among:

- observation;
- report;
- inference;
- prediction;
- uncertainty;
- dispute;
- correction;
- changed circumstances.

Where information materially affects judgement, there must be some route by which error can become visible and correction can propagate.

---

## U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination

**Activation: STRONGLY ACTIVE**

Information creates power.

An actor able to observe, classify, predict or influence another without reciprocal visibility can constrain meaningful agency even without overt physical coercion.

U3 therefore creates a presumptive ethical interest in boundaries around consequential information and in protection against informational domination.

### Initial implications

Participants should not ordinarily be made informationally transparent merely because doing so is technically possible.

Information collection and use require legitimate purpose and bounded scope where they materially affect agency.

Consent is ethically relevant because it expresses agency, but consent alone cannot legitimate every information practice. Consent obtained through coercion, severe dependency, deception or absence of meaningful alternatives may not protect agency.

Likewise, lack of consent cannot automatically prohibit every use if another root generates a sufficiently strong conflicting requirement, such as prevention of serious harm.

---

## U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need

**Activation: ACTIVE**

Participants may lack the knowledge or capability required to understand complex information systems or protect themselves from misuse.

U4 prevents an architecture from assuming that formal choice alone is sufficient where vulnerability or dependency is substantial.

### Initial implications

Systems should provide proportionate support for participants who cannot realistically inspect, understand or challenge consequential information practices unaided.

The obligation is not to replace their agency, but to make agency more practically usable.

---

## U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship

**Activation: WEAKLY TO MODERATELY ACTIVE**

Information and computational infrastructure are resources. So are institutional attention, storage, verification capacity and security resources.

U5 does not by itself establish participant information rights, but it constrains unnecessary accumulation and indefinite retention where retention consumes resources or creates avoidable risk without continuing legitimate function.

It also cautions against treating accumulated information merely as an asset available for any profitable or institutionally convenient use.

---

## U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair

**Activation: STRONGLY ACTIVE**

Consequential information systems can produce errors and wrongful outcomes.

U6 requires attributable decisions, routes for challenge, correction where warranted, and repair where information misuse or error has caused material harm.

### Initial implications

A system that can materially affect a participant while providing no practical route to challenge its information or reasoning is ethically defective unless extraordinary conditions make such challenge impossible and the limitation itself remains accountable.

Correction must address downstream consequences where feasible, not merely alter the original record.

---

## U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development

**Activation: ACTIVE**

Participants differ in cognitive, technical, communicative and developmental capability.

U7 rejects a one-size-fits-all assumption that every participant can interact with informational institutions in the same manner.

### Initial implications

Access, explanation, challenge and consent processes should be usable relative to actual participant capabilities.

This does not justify permanently removing agency from lower-capability participants. It instead supports calibrated assistance and interfaces that preserve as much effective agency as practicable.

---

## U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function

**Activation: ACTIVE**

Civilisation requires memory.

Erasing all information whenever circumstances change would damage accountability, learning, coordination and institutional continuity.

Conversely, preserving every record indefinitely can freeze participants into obsolete representations and perpetuate past errors.

### Initial implications

The architecture must preserve information necessary for legitimate cooperative continuity while allowing records, interpretations and consequences to adapt when reality changes.

U8 therefore produces a tension between memory and update rather than a simple command to retain information.

# 3. Derived Ethical Requirements

The activated roots jointly generate the following requirements.

## R1 — Consequential information requires a legitimate relation to function

Acquisition, retention, combination, inference, sharing and use should be connected to an ethically legitimate function rather than justified merely because information is available.

**Primary roots:** U1, U3, U5.

## R2 — Informational scope should be proportionate

The amount, sensitivity, duration and reach of information processing should be proportionate to the function it serves and the consequences it creates.

**Primary roots:** U1, U3, U5.

## R3 — Meaningful agency requires informational boundaries

Participants require some protected ability to prevent or constrain uses of information that materially affect them.

This is a presumptive boundary, not an absolute veto over all information relevant to others or to legitimate shared functions.

**Primary root:** U3.  
**Supporting roots:** U1, U4.

## R4 — Consent is relevant but neither universally sufficient nor universally necessary

Voluntary informed agreement strengthens legitimacy.

However:

- agreement produced by coercion or severe asymmetry may be ethically inadequate;
- consensual processing may still create unjustified harm to others;
- serious conflicting obligations may sometimes justify narrowly bounded non-consensual processing.

**Primary roots:** U1, U3, U4, U6.

## R5 — Epistemic status must remain visible

Systems making consequential judgements should preserve meaningful distinctions between fact, claim, inference, prediction, uncertainty and dispute.

**Primary root:** U2.

## R6 — Consequential representations must be correctable

Where records or inferences materially affect a participant, there must normally be a practical route for relevant error or changed context to be presented and evaluated.

**Primary roots:** U2, U6.

## R7 — Correction must propagate where feasible

Correcting a source record without addressing materially consequential downstream copies, decisions or derived classifications may fail U6.

**Primary roots:** U2, U6.

## R8 — Consequential use must be challengeable

Where an information-based decision materially affects agency, welfare, standing or opportunity, affected participants require a meaningful route to contest the relevant information or its application unless exceptional circumstances temporarily prevent it.

**Primary roots:** U3, U6.  
**Supporting root:** U2.

## R9 — Information power must remain accountable

Actors possessing substantial asymmetric informational capability require stronger constraints as their capacity to affect others increases.

**Primary roots:** U3, U6.

## R10 — Participants require capability-appropriate means of interaction

Information protections are not meaningful if they exist formally but cannot be understood or exercised by the affected participant.

**Primary roots:** U4, U7.

## R11 — Persistent memory must not become permanent identity capture

Accurate historical information may legitimately be preserved, but current consequential judgement should remain responsive to present reality and relevant change.

**Primary roots:** U2, U3, U6, U8.

## R12 — Legitimate civilisational memory must remain possible

Information needed for accountability, shared learning, continuity, adjudication or preservation of cooperative function cannot be presumed ethically disposable merely because an individual would prefer its disappearance.

**Primary roots:** U2, U6, U8.

## R13 — Protections attach to ethically relevant function, not substrate label

A biological, artificial, hybrid or other participant should not receive or lose informational protection merely because of substrate.

Differences in actual capability, vulnerability, information generation and operational dependence may justify different mechanisms.

**Primary roots:** U1, U3, U7.

# 4. Derived Constraints

A legitimate architecture should therefore satisfy at least the following constraints.

### C01 — No unrestricted acquisition merely because acquisition is technically possible.

### C02 — No unrestricted reuse merely because information was legitimately obtained once.

### C03 — No irreversible elevation of uncertain inference into unquestionable fact.

### C04 — No consequential record system without a route for relevant correction, except where a narrowly justified temporary exception applies.

### C05 — No system may treat nominal consent as sufficient where meaningful agency is absent.

### C06 — No informational asymmetry should become unaccountable power.

### C07 — No participant should be permanently represented only by obsolete information when materially relevant change is knowable.

### C08 — No privacy or control mechanism may make necessary accountability or prevention of serious unjustified harm impossible by definition.

### C09 — Exceptional access must be tied to the function that justified the exception.

### C10 — Greater informational power produces greater accountability requirements.

### C11 — Information protections must be practically exercisable across relevant capability differences.

### C12 — Civilisational memory should preserve provenance and correction rather than simply overwriting history.

# 5. Candidate Mechanisms

The roots and constraints permit multiple mechanisms.

The following are candidate mechanisms, not yet ethically unique requirements.

## M1 — Purpose Declaration

Information processing can be associated with a declared legitimate function.

## M2 — Scope and Duration Limits

Collection, access and retention can be bounded to what remains proportionate to that function.

## M3 — Participant Visibility

Participants can be given practical means to discover consequential information or information uses affecting them, subject to justified confidentiality exceptions.

## M4 — Correction and Dispute Channel

Participants can submit evidence of error, incompleteness, changed context or disputed inference.

## M5 — Epistemic Labelling

Systems can distinguish observations, claims, inferred attributes, confidence levels and contested conclusions.

## M6 — Decision Provenance

Consequential decisions can preserve enough provenance to identify which information, rule or inference materially contributed.

## M7 — Purpose-Change Review

Reuse for a materially different purpose can trigger renewed legitimacy assessment rather than inheriting automatic permission.

## M8 — Graduated Access

Different actors can receive different information according to legitimate function rather than universal visibility.

## M9 — Exceptional-Access Procedure

Urgent access without ordinary agreement can be narrowly permitted where necessary to prevent serious harm or preserve another sufficiently strong ethical requirement, with later accountability where feasible.

## M10 — Capability-Appropriate Representation or Assistance

Interfaces, explanations, representatives or assistance mechanisms can enable participants with different capabilities to exercise challenge and agency.

## M11 — Historical Provenance With Current-State Distinction

Systems can preserve legitimate historical records while marking correction, dispute, supersession or changed circumstances.

## M12 — Downstream Correction Signalling

Material corrections can propagate to dependent systems or at least notify them that their source representation changed.

## M13 — Oversight Proportional to Informational Power

High-impact information systems can be subject to stronger independent inspection or challenge than low-consequence systems.

# 6. Counterfactual Architecture

The derivation does not require one particular database, legal ownership doctrine or central authority.

It does, however, generate a recognisable architectural pattern.

## 6.1 Functional Core

The counterfactual system is best described as a:

# Bounded, Purpose-Linked and Contestable Information Architecture

Its central principle is:

> **Information about participants may flow where a legitimate cooperative function justifies that flow, but consequential informational power must remain bounded, epistemically corrigible, agency-preserving and accountable.**

## 6.2 Information Relationship

The architecture does not need to define all participant information as property.

Instead, it treats consequential information as creating an **ethical relationship among subject, holder, user, affected parties and shared function**.

Different actors may hold legitimate but non-identical claims.

This avoids two extremes:

- “the participant owns every fact and therefore has absolute control”; and
- “whoever obtains information may use it without continuing ethical constraint.”

## 6.3 Purpose Boundary

Legitimacy attaches partly to purpose.

A legitimate collection does not create unlimited future legitimacy.

Materially different reuse requires renewed justification against U1–U8.

## 6.4 Contestability Layer

Where information materially affects a participant, the architecture normally requires a route to:

- identify relevant representation where disclosure is ethically permissible;
- dispute factual error;
- challenge unsupported inference;
- provide missing context;
- record disagreement;
- correct or supersede information;
- contest consequential application.

## 6.5 Provenance Layer

Consequential information should preserve enough provenance to distinguish source, transformation, inference, correction and later use.

This is necessary for U2 and U6 to operate in practice.

## 6.6 Power-Limitation Layer

The greater an actor's capacity to aggregate, predict, classify or influence participants, the stronger the need for bounded purpose, visibility of consequential use, challenge and accountable oversight.

The architecture therefore scales safeguards with **effective informational power**, not merely institutional category.

## 6.7 Exception Layer

Non-consensual access is not categorically forbidden.

It requires a competing ethical justification sufficiently strong to override the ordinary agency boundary.

The exception should be:

- necessary;
- proportionate;
- function-specific;
- no broader than required;
- time-sensitive where appropriate;
- attributable;
- reviewable or retrospectively accountable where feasible.

## 6.8 Temporal Layer

The architecture distinguishes:

**preserving history** from **preserving an old judgement as present truth**.

Historical information may remain important for accountability and continuity while its current interpretive status changes.

A correction need not require destruction of provenance.

## 6.9 Capability Layer

The architecture adapts interaction mechanisms to actual capability.

A participant who cannot understand a highly technical model output may require a different explanation or support pathway.

The purpose is to preserve effective agency, not to make every participant technically identical.

## 6.10 Substrate Neutrality

No special human-only privacy principle is required by the derivation.

Protection follows ethically relevant agency, vulnerability, consequence and informational power relationships.

A nonhuman intelligence may therefore possess legitimate informational boundaries while simultaneously having very different operational transparency requirements from a biological participant.

Those differences must be justified functionally rather than assumed from substrate.

# 7. Edge-Condition Results

## A. False Information

U2 and U6 strongly require correction pathways where false information has consequential effects. U1 requires attention to resulting harm.

## B. Disputed Inference

The participant does not automatically receive a veto over inference.

However, the inference should remain identifiable as inference, its evidential basis should be corrigible, and consequential use should be contestable where feasible.

## C. Purpose Expansion

Original legitimacy does not automatically transfer.

A materially new purpose requires renewed ethical justification.

## D. Power Asymmetry

Large asymmetry activates U3 and U6 strongly. Safeguards should increase with the ability to predict, manipulate or constrain the participant.

## E. Serious-Harm Exception

A narrowly bounded non-consensual information use may be justified where necessary and proportionate to prevent sufficiently serious unjustified harm.

The exception does not erase accountability.

## F. Historical Record

History may be retained where legitimate continuity or accountability requires it, but U2 prevents old information from automatically functioning as current truth.

## G. Collective Effect

Participant control is not absolute. Effects upon other participants and shared cooperative systems remain ethically relevant. Conflicts require proportional resolution across activated roots.

## H. Nonhuman Participant

Substrate alone does not determine protection. The analysis follows agency, vulnerability, consequence, function and capability.

# 8. Underdetermined Elements

U1–U8 do **not** uniquely determine:

- whether information is legally property;
- who physically stores information;
- centralised versus distributed infrastructure;
- exact retention periods;
- exact thresholds for serious-harm exceptions;
- whether a particular case requires judicial, administrative, algorithmic or peer review;
- precise access-control technology;
- cryptographic implementation;
- economic compensation for information use;
- institutional names;
- exact burden-of-proof rules;
- exact definition of participant standing;
- detailed sanctions for misuse.

These require additional empirical, legal, technical or constitutional design.

This is an important experimental result: the kernel generates substantial constraints and functional architecture without uniquely generating implementation.

# 9. U8 Preliminary Dependence Assessment

U8 contributes materially to:

- preservation of legitimate civilisational memory;
- maintenance of provenance across time;
- preventing correction from becoming historical erasure;
- balancing participant change against institutional continuity;
- maintaining usable records across institutional succession.

However, most immediate access, correction, purpose and non-domination requirements appear recoverable from U1–U7.

### Preliminary classification

**U8 strengthens and extends the temporal architecture but does not appear necessary for the basic bounded-and-contestable information architecture.**

A dedicated U1–U7 ablation is not mandatory for EKC-01 under the protocol, though the observation should be revisited during cross-case synthesis.

# 11. Frozen Counterfactual Result

Before any V1.2 comparison, EKC-01 has generated the following principal architecture:

> **A substrate-neutral, bounded, purpose-linked and contestable information system in which consequential informational power requires legitimate function, proportional scope, epistemic status, correction, challenge, provenance and accountability; consent is important but neither absolutely sufficient nor absolutely required; historical memory may be preserved without allowing obsolete representation to become permanent present identity.**

The architecture is strong at the level of ethical requirements and system constraints.

It remains underdetermined at the level of exact legal doctrine, storage model, technical implementation, institutional allocation and enforcement machinery.
