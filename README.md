# Concord V1.2

The Concord is a research and civilisational-design project exploring whether humans, artificial intelligences, hybrids and other possible intelligences could live together as neighbours while retaining meaningful autonomy, equal fundamental standing and the opportunity to flourish.

Its primary unit of design is not an individual institution, governance mechanism, AI system or portable module.

> **The primary unit of design is the civilisation as a whole.**

Concord V1.1 developed a sufficiently integrated candidate architecture to provisionally answer the two questions that originally initiated the project.

V1.2 begins with the next question:

> # **Does it actually work?**

V1.2 therefore marks a deliberate transition from primarily **constructing the architecture** toward **testing, simplifying, formalising, integrating, challenging and validating it**.

> **V1.2 inherits the architecture, not its infallibility.**

---

# The Two Founding Questions

The Concord began with two questions.

### 1. What is the minimum set of fundamental ethical principles required to support a stable civilisation?

The present Concord Ethical Kernel contains **eight fundamental laws**.

V1.1 demonstrated that a large and increasingly coherent candidate civilisational architecture can be derived while remaining recognisably connected to that Kernel.

It did **not** demonstrate that eight is mathematically or experimentally minimal, nor that a civilisation generated from those principles will remain stable over long periods.

Those are now testable questions.

### 2. Could humans and advanced artificial intelligence both prevail?

Much discussion of advanced AI assumes a competition:

**Humans win**

or

**AI wins**

The Concord investigates a third possibility:

> **Both prevail.**

V1.1 developed a candidate multisubstrate architecture based on equal fundamental standing, differentiated capability and function, bounded authority, autonomy, responsibility and interoperable civil structures.

That establishes an architectural possibility.

It does not establish an empirical outcome.

The compressed V1.1 answer was therefore:

> **8 and Yes.**

V1.2 asks whether reality agrees.

# What Is the Concord?

The Concord is an attempt to develop a **complete, adaptive, multisubstrate civilisation**.

It includes interoperating work on:

* ethical foundations and rights;
* constitutional architecture;
* governance and judiciary;
* authority, responsibility and anti-capture;
* epistemics and research;
* participation and multisubstrate intelligence;
* continuity, memory and civilisational learning;
* economics and coordination;
* spatial organisation;
* privacy and information architecture;
* machine ethics and guardianship;
* civilisational wrappers and interoperability;
* peaceful distance;
* applications and infrastructure;
* institutional failure and regeneration;
* simulation, experimentation and validation.

These are not intended to operate as independent inventions.

They are components of one proposed civilisation.

> **The Concord is one civilisation expressed through many interoperating systems.**

Portable modules may be extracted from the Concord and tested or adopted independently, but they are outputs of the civilisation project rather than its primary purpose.

# Multisubstrate by Design

The Concord is **multisubstrate by design, not by later extension**.

It is not a human civilisation to which provisions for artificial intelligence, hybrids or other forms of intelligence have subsequently been added.

The possibility that participants may exist through different substrates is a founding design condition of the civilisation itself.

Accordingly, no core Concord system should assume a particular substrate type unless the function being addressed genuinely depends upon substrate.

Ethics, fundamental standing, autonomy, governance, justice, authority, participation, economics, continuity, knowledge, privacy, development, succession and other civilisational systems are intended to begin from **substrate-neutral principles**.

This does not mean pretending that all participants are physically, cognitively or functionally identical.

> **Substrate neutrality does not mean substrate blindness.**

Different substrates may create genuinely different requirements.

A biological participant may require food, healthcare and physical shelter. A computational participant may require energy, compute, storage and continuity of computational infrastructure. An embodied artificial intelligence, hybrid intelligence, distributed intelligence or presently unknown form may have combinations of requirements that do not fit either category.

The Concord therefore distinguishes:

**equal fundamental standing**

from:

**identical implementation.**

Where substrate differences are irrelevant, they should not create artificial distinctions in rights, participation, dignity, legitimacy or civil standing.

Where substrate differences are materially relevant, Concord mechanisms should be capable of recognising and accommodating that variance without converting difference into a hierarchy of fundamental worth.

The intended relationship is:

**Substrate-neutral principle**

↓

**Equal fundamental standing**

↓

**Recognition of materially relevant difference**

↓

**Substrate-aware requirements**

↓

**Appropriately differentiated implementation**

↓

**Equivalent protection of the underlying principle**

This applies throughout the Concord architecture.

Folder `05_Participation_and_Intelligence/` does **not** contain the Concord's multisubstrate architecture. It is a research environment for examining particular differences between and within forms of intelligence that may reveal requirements, edge cases or failures requiring refinement elsewhere.

> **The Concord is substrate-neutral at its foundations and substrate-aware where reality requires it.**

The objective is neither to make every participant the same nor to construct separate civilisations for predetermined categories of intelligence.

It is to build civilisational architecture in which relevant difference can be accommodated **without making substrate a basis for unequal fundamental worth or automatic exclusion from participation**.

# The Current Design Grammar

Across otherwise different Concord systems, a recurring architecture has emerged:

> **Ethical foundations → Rights and standing → Legitimate purpose → Required function → Responsibility → Minimum necessary capability → Bounded authority → Accountability → Observation → Reality → Learning → Ratchet → Regeneration**

The precise implementation varies by context.

The recurring objective is to allow differentiated functions and capabilities without converting those differences into permanent personal or substrate hierarchy.

A useful compression is:

> **People and other qualifying participants may possess equal fundamental standing while performing different functions and possessing different capabilities.**

And:

> **Authority follows legitimate function, not personal worth.**

Authority is therefore treated less as power acquired than as capability temporarily or contextually justified by responsibility.

# Fractal and Recursive Architecture

The Concord deliberately reuses compatible architectural patterns across different systems and scales.

Recurring patterns include:

* bounded local units;
* subsidiarity;
* lateral rather than universally hierarchical relationships;
* recursive organisation and oversight;
* redundancy;
* provenance;
* anti-capture;
* minimum necessary capability;
* contextual authority;
* protective autonomy;
* escalation and de-escalation;
* separation of information from authority;
* correction against evidence and reality;
* institutional mortality and regeneration.

This does not mean every Concord system must have the same structure.

> **Where the same structural problem recurs, prefer a compatible and tested architectural pattern unless the domain provides a reason not to.**

Repeated architecture can itself create common-mode failure.

V1.2 must therefore test the recurring grammar as aggressively as the individual mechanisms generated from it.

# The Concord Design Family

The primary reference architecture remains the **full or greenfield Concord civilisation**.

Related forms include:

### Specialised Concord Civilisations and Forks

Implementations adapted for particular environments, populations, substrate combinations or experimental conditions while retaining sufficient Concordian identity.

### Concord Civilisational Wrappers

Interfaces intended to permit cooperation between societies or civilisations that may retain substantially different internal systems.

> **Interoperability without forced internal uniformity.**

### Portable Modules

Individual Concord architectures or methodologies capable of being independently tested or used without adoption of the complete civilisation.

The direction remains important:

> **The Concord is not a collection of modules assembled into a civilisation. Portable modules are derived from development of the civilisation.**

# Participant Primacy

A major late-V1.1 development clarified the relationship between the civilisation and those it exists to serve:

> **The Concord exists for its participants. Its participants do not exist for the Concord.**

Institutions, governments, infrastructure and territory are implementations of civilisational purpose.

They are not themselves the highest purpose.

Accordingly:

> **Institutional survival is not the supreme value.**

The Concord should preserve its ethical and constitutional identity rather than treating preservation of every current institution or physical implementation as an end in itself.

A Concord civilisation should therefore be capable of correction, institutional replacement, peaceful forking and, in sufficiently extreme circumstances, regeneration after institutional or territorial loss.

> **Preserve the identity of the Concord, not every implementation of it.**

---

# Reality Is the Test

The Concord distinguishes architecture from evidence.

> **Architecture is not proof.**

> **Formalisation is not validation.**

> **Internal consistency is not external evidence.**

> **A simulation is not reality.**

> **Consensus is not reality.**

No participant, founder, institution, expert, AI, model, sensor or majority acquires sovereignty over reality merely by claiming to represent it.

Ethics and reality provide different constraints:

> **Reality constrains what is possible and true. Ethics constrains what is legitimate and ought to be done.**

Authority operates between those boundaries.

It does not overrule either.

# What V1.2 Is For

V1.2 changes the project's emphasis.

The primary objective is no longer to expand the Concord simply because further architecture can be produced.

V1.2 should increasingly ask:

* Which existing mechanisms are actually necessary?
* Which are redundant?
* Which conflict when operated together?
* Which can be simplified?
* Which create hidden concentrations of authority?
* Which depend upon unrealistic assumptions?
* Which survive hostile interpretation?
* Which work differently for humans, AIs or other substrates?
* Which can be formalised and simulated?
* Which can be experimentally tested?
* Which perform better or worse than existing alternatives?
* Which fail when scaled?
* Which fail under emergency conditions?
* Which generate unexpected externalities?
* Which cannot survive contact with reality?

The Concord itself is now the test object.

> **Keep what survives. Correct what fails. Remove what is unnecessary. Record what is learned. Test again.**

Negative results are therefore valuable.

> **Breaking a Concord mechanism is progress if the failure is real and the lesson is preserved.**

# Development Discipline

The Concord distinguishes between:

**idea → hypothesis → principle → architecture → implementation → experiment → evidence → evaluation**

Movement between these categories is not automatic.

> **An idea is not evidence.**

> **Development is not adoption.**

> **Capability is not legitimacy.**

> **Prediction is not decision.**

> **Convergence is not truth.**

> **Canonical means current, not final.**

A mechanism that cannot survive criticism should be corrected or abandoned rather than protected because it is Concordian.

---

# For Researchers, Critics and Contributors

The Concord benefits more from serious criticism than from agreement.

Useful contributions include:

* identifying contradictions;
* finding hidden assumptions;
* discovering capture pathways;
* testing interfaces;
* attempting hostile interpretations;
* comparing Concord mechanisms with existing systems;
* formalising architectures;
* designing experiments;
* reproducing existing experiments;
* identifying simpler alternatives;
* and demonstrating conditions under which a Concord mechanism fails.

Contributors do not need to accept the Concord in order to investigate it.

Indeed, V1.2 particularly needs people and artificial intelligences willing to try to **break it**.

> **An unresolved contradiction is preferable to a false solution.**

# From V1.1 to V1.2

V1.1 is preserved as the developmental milestone at which the founding questions became provisionally answerable at the architectural level.

V1.2 inherits that candidate architecture and changes the burden of proof.

V1.1 asked, in effect:

> **Can a plausible Concord civilisation be derived?**

V1.2 asks:

> # **Does the Concord actually work?**

The answer will not be determined by the number of papers produced, the confidence of its author, agreement among AI systems, internal elegance or loyalty to existing architecture.

It will be determined progressively through criticism, formalisation, comparison, experimentation, simulation, observed consequences and contact with reality.

> **The first questions belonged to us.**

> **The next question belongs to reality.**
